> I used to think confident prayer meant praying loud.
>
> Turns out it's the opposite. Volume is usually a cover for disconnection. What actually builds confidence is proximity — showing up close to God, again and again, until it's normal.

## B — Family B, #20 Problem Illumination · 394 characters

> If you sit down to pray and your mind just won't stop racing — jumping from your to-do list to random memories to worry about tomorrow — it's not that you're bad at this.
>
> Nobody ever gave you a fence. Meditation isn't clearing your head, it's picking one thing and holding your mind there on purpose. Without it you're not focused, you're just watching everything go by and landing on nothing.

**Source:** Session 12 fence quote — "There must be a fence, otherwise you'll just be seeing everything... you perceive everything and land on nothing."

## C — Family B, #22 Diagnostic with Branching Outcomes · 321 characters

> You know you're actually meditating on something when your mind keeps drifting back to it on its own, hours later.
>
> If it's a verse, great — that's the Word taking root.
> If it's a worry, that's fine too — bring it back and give it to God.
> Either way, something's finally getting your full attention instead of half of it.

**Source:** the note's "give it back to God" (RESTING's final step) + the general premise that meditation means one thing holding your attention, not many.

## D — How-To + Bullets (your proven structure) · 406 characters

> Most of us read a verse and move straight to the next one. Meditating on it is different — here's how to actually do it:
>
> - Find somewhere quiet, phone away
> - Read one line, slowly
> - Say it out loud, soft, a few times
> - Picture it as you go
> - Give it back to God when your mind wanders
>
> Five steps. Two minutes a day. That's genuinely the whole thing — no candles, no chanting, no special posture required.

**Source:** raw "how to prepare" list, condensed from 10 steps to the strongest 5.

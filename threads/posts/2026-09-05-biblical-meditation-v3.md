> The more I look into what meditation actually means, the less it looks like the version I grew up with.
>
> It's not about clearing your mind. It's about filling it — with one thing, on purpose — until there's no room left for anything else.
>
> You need a fence around your focus too, otherwise you're just seeing everything and landing on nothing. Set what you're meditating on, then hold your mind there. That's when the still small voice actually gets through.

**Why it works:** Uses the account's own documented recurring opener ("The more I…") instead of a Definition Flip label. Stacks two raw-source realisations (filling not emptying; the fence) the way real posts often stack two connected insights in one post.

## Option 4 — How-To + Bullets (your proven structure) · ONE POST · 406 characters · grade 7.0

> Most of us read a verse and move straight to the next one. Meditating on it is different — here's how to actually do it:
>
> - Find somewhere quiet, phone away
> - Read one line, slowly
> - Say it out loud, soft, a few times
> - Picture it as you go
> - Give it back to God when your mind wanders
>
> Five steps. Two minutes a day. That's genuinely the whole thing — no candles, no chanting, no special posture required.

**Why it works:** Condensed from the raw "how to prepare" list (10 steps down to the strongest 5), framed as direct address rather than "how I do it" — avoids implying a personal practice claim that isn't confirmed.

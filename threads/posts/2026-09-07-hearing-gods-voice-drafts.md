## A — The crowded receiver

*Structure: Family C · #2 Definition Flip · ONE POST · 191 characters*

<!-- POST START A -->
> God's voice isn't always absent when prayer feels quiet.
>
> Sometimes the signal is there, but the receiver is crowded.
>
> The answer isn't always more volume.
>
> Sometimes it's stillness.
<!-- POST END A -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Turns the raw source's transmitter-receiver model into a tight definition flip, then lands on its repeated teaching that God's frequency is stillness. Drawn from `Hearing the Voice of God Part 3`, sections 2, 5, and `How to Train Your Mind to Enter the Spirit`, "How do we operate on God's frequency?"

**Posted version · ONE POST · 180 characters:**

> God isn’t always silent when prayer feels quiet.
>
> Sometimes He is speaking, but your mind is too full to hear.
>
> The answer isn’t to get louder. Sometimes you just need to be still.

**Rewrite:** Replaced the technical signal/receiver language with simpler human wording and joined the final explanation and instruction into one paragraph.

## D — Pray like you're catching a dove

*Structure: Family B · #20 Problem Illumination · ONE POST · 449 characters*

<!-- POST START D -->
> If prayer feels noisy and you can't make out what God is saying, it doesn't mean He has stopped speaking.
>
> The transmitter hasn't moved. The receiver is crowded by hurry, distraction or offence.
>
> Before you ask God to speak louder, become still.
>
> “Be still, and know that I am God.” - Psalm 46:10
>
> Pray like you're trying to catch a dove. Slow down enough to notice what usually gets drowned out.
>
> Connection matters more than volume.
<!-- POST END D -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Names the felt problem, explains it through the receiver mechanism, then makes the correction concrete with the source's dove demonstration. The transmitter, distraction/offence, and frequency model appear in `Hearing the Voice of God Part 3`; the verified Psalm 46:10 wording appears in `How to Train Your Mind to Enter the Spirit`; the dove line appears in `Prophet School - Session 12 - August 26.md`.

## E — Train your sensitivity

*Structure: Family C · #9 Stacked Directive · ONE POST · 480 characters*

<!-- POST START E -->
> If you want to become more sensitive to God's voice:
>
> • Remove external noise
> • Anchor your mind in Scripture
> • Slow your breathing
> • Pray without rushing
> • Pay attention to repeated impressions
> • Write after you pray
>
> Don't stomp towards stillness. Pray like you're trying to catch a dove.
>
> When you feel yourself going faster than God, slow down.
>
> God's voice isn't made clearer by volume. The receiver becomes clearer through stillness, attention and use.
<!-- POST END E -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Converts the raw training into a saveable checklist. Removing stimulation, using an anchor verse, slowing the breath, and writing after prayer come from `How to Train Your Mind to Enter the Spirit`; repeated impressions, sensitive prayer, the dove image, and slowing down when moving faster than God come from `Prophet School - Session 12 - August 26.md`.

**Posted version · ONE POST · 359 characters:**

> If you want to become more sensitive to God’s voice:
>
> • Get into a quiet place
> • Anchor your mind in Scripture
> • Slow your breathing
> • Never rush, ignore the clock
> • Pay attention to impressions & thoughts
> • Write after you pray
>
> Don’t stomp towards stillness. Pray like you’re trying to catch a dove.
>
> When you feel yourself NEEDING an answer now, slow down.

**Rewrite:** Made the checklist more physical and immediate (`Remove external noise` → `Get into a quiet place`; `Pray without rushing` → `Never rush, ignore the clock`), widened `repeated impressions` to `impressions & thoughts`, changed the final diagnostic from moving faster than God to needing an answer now, and cut the abstract receiver/volume restatement entirely.

## C — The missing piece

*Structure: Family C · #19 Cleared Suspect · ONE POST · 155 characters*

<!-- POST START C -->
> Not having everything the mission needs isn't disobedience.
>
> Refusing to prepare what you already have is.
>
> Abraham brought the wood. God provided the ram.
<!-- POST END C -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Acquits the obvious problem—lacking part of what the mission requires—and places responsibility on preparing what is already available. Both sides of the contrast come directly from the raw source.

## D — The Lord Will Provide

*Structure: Family B · #23 Narrative Parallel (Typology) · ONE POST · 429 characters*

<!-- POST START D -->
> Abraham loaded his donkey, took Isaac and two servants, and walked for three days.
>
> On the way, Isaac asked, “Where is the lamb?”
>
> “God Himself will provide the lamb for the burnt offering, my son.” - Genesis 22:8
>
> Abraham built the altar, bound Isaac and took the knife.
>
> Then God stopped him. A ram was caught in the thicket.
>
> Abraham called the place The Lord Will Provide.
>
> Obedience often begins before provision is visible.
<!-- POST END D -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Keeps the concrete Genesis 22 sequence at the centre—three-day journey, Isaac's question, Abraham's answer, the altar, the knife and the ram—before extracting one restrained principle. The quoted line appears verbatim in the raw source; its verse reference is Genesis 22:8.

## E — Before every piece is in place

*Structure: Family B · #20 Problem Illumination · ONE POST · 469 characters*

<!-- POST START E -->
> When what God has asked of you feels bigger than what you have, the instinct is to wait until every missing piece is in place.
>
> Genesis 22 gives a different picture.
>
> Abraham didn't have the sacrifice he would ultimately offer. But he had wood to carry and an altar to build.
>
> He prepared what he could. Then God provided what Abraham could not.
>
> Faith isn't pretending nothing is missing.
>
> It's obeying with what is already in your hands while the rest remains unseen.
<!-- POST END E -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Names the paralysis created by an under-resourced mission, then explains the source's mechanism without promising a specific outcome to the reader: Abraham prepared what he had before the missing provision became visible.

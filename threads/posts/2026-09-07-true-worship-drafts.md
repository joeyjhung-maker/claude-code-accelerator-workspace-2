## A — The life behind the song

*Structure: Family C · #2 Definition Flip · ONE POST · 155 characters*

<!-- POST START A -->
> Worship isn’t the song. It’s the life behind it.
>
> You can sing every word and still miss the point.
>
> God hears your heart before He hears your voice.
<!-- POST END A -->

**Why it works / source trace:** A plain definition flip drawn from the raw source's repeated distinction between activity and posture, plus its claim that God starts His assessment with the worshipper's heart.

## B — Jesus stays the focus

*Structure: Family B · #22 Diagnostic with Branching Outcomes · ONE POST · 160 characters*

<!-- POST START B -->
> You know worship has become real when Jesus stays the focus.
>
> If the answer comes, worship Him.
>
> If it doesn’t, worship Him.
>
> He was always the reason.
<!-- POST END B -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Defines worship by its object rather than its outcome, with both branches returning to Jesus. Drawn from the raw source's "audience of one" teaching and its warning against making salary, promotion, or breakthrough the true object of worship.

## C — The order of worship

*Structure: Family A · Personal-processing register · ONE POST · 180 characters*

<!-- POST START C -->
> The more I understand worship, the more I realise how little of it has to do with music.
>
> Your heart comes first.
>
> Then your words.
>
> Then your life.
>
> Then your service.
<!-- POST END C -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Uses one documented Joey opener and makes the source's four-step pattern easy to absorb. The exact sequence—heart, words, life, service—is repeated throughout the raw teaching.

## D — Worship cannot be switched on

*Structure: Family B · #20 Problem Illumination · ONE POST · 432 characters*

<!-- POST START D -->
> If worship has become something you switch on when the music starts, the problem isn’t your singing.
>
> Worship begins with the heart, then words, then life, then service.
>
> When we skip the first three and jump straight to the song, the outside can look right while the inside stays untouched.
>
> God is not looking for a better performance. He is looking for a surrendered person.
>
> The song is the last step, not the first.
<!-- POST END D -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Names a familiar habit, reframes the problem, and explains it through the raw source's four-step order. Its closing distinction comes directly from the teaching that activity only gains value when the worshipper's heart posture is right.

## E — The first worship in Scripture

*Structure: Family C · #6 Research Reveal · ONE POST · 437 characters*

<!-- POST START E -->
> The first time worship appears in Scripture, nobody is singing.
>
> Abraham is walking up a mountain with Isaac. He tells his servants they are going to worship, but what follows is sacrifice and obedience.
>
> That first mention gives us the pattern: worship begins before the song.
>
> It starts when your heart bows, your will gives way and your life says yes to God.
>
> Music can express worship. Surrender is what makes it worship.
<!-- POST END E -->

**Why it works / source trace:** Uses the source's Genesis 22:5 law-of-first-mention study and keeps the concrete scene at the centre. The raw teaching explicitly introduces worship through Abraham's sacrifice and obedience, then uses it to establish posture before activity.

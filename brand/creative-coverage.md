# **Creative Coverage: The Full Operational Playbook**

---

## **The Problem**

Most advertisers make ads based on gut feeling. They come up with an idea, make the ad, launch it, and hope it works. When it doesn't, they come up with another idea and try again.

The problem with this approach is that you're randomly wandering through a massive landscape of possible ads, hitting the same spots over and over while ignoring entire territories that might contain your biggest winners. You end up with blind spots you don't even know exist. Maybe you've been crushing it with problem-aware busy moms using belief concepts through a contrarian angle in UGC style. But you've never once talked to men over 50 at the unaware level using a desire concept through a personal story angle in a talking head style. That's a whole territory you've never explored, and it might contain a massive winner.

Creative coverage is the antidote to gut-feel randomness. It's a systematic way of mapping out every possible type of ad you could make for your product so that you can be intentional about what you're testing and where you still have gaps.

---

## **The Grid**

Think of it like a multi-layered grid. Each layer is a different dimension of an ad, and when you cross them all together, you get a map of every possible ad combination.

**Creative coverage = Segments × Awareness Levels × Concepts × Angles × Styles × Hooks**

Every unique combination across those six dimensions is a genuinely different ad. The total number of possible ads is enormous — and that's the point. You're not supposed to make all of them. You're supposed to see the full landscape so you can make intentional choices about where to focus, and more importantly, so you can see where you have gaps.

The grid makes the invisible visible. It turns "I don't know what ad to make next" into "here are the 40 cells I haven't tested yet."

Nobody fills every cell. It's a theoretical ceiling. But understanding it changes how you think. Instead of randomly guessing what to make, you look at your grid, see where you have coverage and where you don't, and make a deliberate decision about what to test next. Over time, you systematically explore more of the landscape, find more winners, and become very hard to compete against because your creative diversity is so much wider than anyone else in your market.

---

## **The Six Layers**

---

### **Layer 1: Segments**

Not "our target market" as one blob — the specific sub-groups within that market. Each segment has different language, different identity markers, different emotional triggers. An ad that crushes with busy moms might completely miss with men over 50.

Define each segment by the overlap of:

* **Demographics** — age, gender, income, location  
* **Life situation** — new mom, recently divorced, just promoted, just retired, empty nester  
* **Psychographics** — what they believe, fear, value, aspire to  
* **Behavioral** — what they've tried, where they spend time, what they consume, what competitors they've used

**Example segments for a weight loss supplement:**

* Women 35-50 who've tried 3+ diets and failed  
* Men 40+ whose doctor just warned them about cholesterol  
* New moms trying to lose postpartum weight  
* Former athletes who let themselves go  
* Desk workers with no time who are gaining weight slowly

Each segment gets its own creative track. Same product, different everything else.

---

### **Layer 2: Awareness Levels**

For each segment, people sit at different levels of awareness about their problem and your solution. The way you talk to someone who doesn't even know they have a problem is completely different from the way you talk to someone who's been to your website three times.

* **Unaware** — doesn't know they have a problem. You're competing with all content on the internet, not just other ads. Your job is to interrupt their scroll, make them realize something is off, and pull them into awareness. You're selling the problem.  
* **Problem-Aware** — knows something is wrong but doesn't understand the real cause, doesn't know why past attempts failed, doesn't know what kind of solution to look for. Your job is to name the real enemy, validate their experience, and teach the mechanism. You're selling understanding.  
* **Solution-Aware** — knows solutions exist, has probably tried some, may be comparing options. Your job is to explain why your mechanism is different and superior, demonstrate results, and position as the clear best choice. You're selling differentiation.  
* **Product-Aware** — knows your product, hasn't bought. Something specific is stopping them. Your job is to address every objection, prove it works for people like them, remove risk, and make saying yes feel safe. You're selling confidence.  
* **Most-Aware** — ready to buy, just needs a reason to act now instead of "later." Your job is to provide urgency, scarcity, risk removal, and value stacking. You're selling the moment.

So now you've got segments multiplied by awareness levels. If you have 5 segments and 5 awareness levels, that's already 25 distinct "cells" — 25 different types of people in different states of mind you could be talking to.

---

### **Layer 3: Concepts**

For each of those cells, what is the ad actually about? What core idea is it built around?

There are five meta-categories of concepts that cover the most important ground:

* **Attention concepts** — things already in the cultural zeitgeist that grab focus. What's trending, what's controversial, what's already on people's minds that you can tap into.  
* **Belief concepts** — about shifting what someone currently believes. They believe calories are all that matter, or they need to exercise harder, or they're just lazy. You identify the belief and challenge or reframe it.  
* **Constraint concepts** — what's actually stopping someone from acting or getting results. They think they don't have time, it's too expensive, they've tried everything, nothing works for them specifically.  
* **Desire concepts** — what someone actively wants. Not the surface want but the deeper emotional desire underneath — feeling confident, being seen as attractive, having energy to play with their kids.  
* **Excuse concepts** — the stories people tell themselves about why they can't or won't do something. The rationalizations keeping them stuck. The ad calls out the excuse and dismantles it.

Within those meta-categories, each awareness level has specific concepts that work best. These are detailed in the next section.

---

### **Layer 4: Angles**

The angle is the psychological doorway you use to deliver the concept. Same concept, different angle = genuinely different ad. You could have the same concept (insulin resistance is why you can't lose weight) but deliver it through a conspiracy angle ("what your doctor isn't telling you"), a new-science angle ("2024 study reveals hidden cause"), a contrarian angle ("everything you've been told about dieting is wrong"), a personal story angle ("I spent 15 years blaming myself until I found this"), or an authority angle ("as an endocrinologist with 30 years of experience").

There are dozens of proven angles. The point is that for each concept, you run it through multiple different angles to see which psychological entry point makes it land hardest with that segment at that awareness level.

---

### **Layer 5: Styles**

Style is the creative execution — how the ad actually looks and feels. This includes format (static image, video, carousel) and within those formats, specific visual treatments (ugly ad, talking head, UGC, green screen, text-on-screen, podcast clip).

The same concept and angle can perform completely differently depending on whether it's a polished talking head or a raw selfie-style UGC clip. Some audiences trust authority figures in lab coats. Some trust people who look like them. Some just want text they can read without turning on sound.

---

### **Layer 6: Hooks**

The hook is the first thing someone sees or hears. The first 3 seconds of video. The headline on a static. The pattern interrupt that earns the right to deliver the rest of the message. For any given ad, you test multiple hooks while keeping the body identical. This is the most granular and cheapest level of testing — pure scroll-stopping power.

---

## **The Sources Layer**

On top of the structural grid, there's one more dimension that works differently. It's not about what type of ad you're making — it's about where the ideas feeding into those ads come from.

Eight core sources for ad ideas:

* **Swipes** — studying what competitors and other markets are running  
* **Templates** — proven ad structures you fill in with your specifics  
* **Organic** — stealing hooks and ideas from organic creators who are getting engagement  
* **Research & Comments** — mining comment sections, Reddit, reviews, support tickets for language and ideas  
* **Matrix/Coverage** — the systematic approach described in this document  
* **Internal Vectors** — pulling buried ideas from your own winning ads (what else can this winner teach us?)  
* **New Style/Format** — finding fresh creative formats and fitting existing ideas into them  
* **Gambits** — wild card ideas, gut feelings, random inspiration

This isn't a layer you cross-multiply against the grid. That would be unmanageable. Instead, it's a diagnostic layer — a way to audit your creative process. You periodically zoom out and ask whether you're pulling ideas from all eight sources or whether you've gotten lazy and only work from swipes and templates. If all your ads come from the same two sources, your creative will feel samey and stale no matter how well you cover the structural grid.

The coverage grid is the map of every ad you could make. The sources are the different wells you draw from to fill those cells with actual ideas. The grid tells you what to make. The sources make sure you're not always going to the same well to figure out how to make it.

## **Concepts by Awareness Level**

### **Unaware Concepts**

The job: interrupt their scroll, make them realize something is off, pull them into awareness. No selling. Compete with all content, not just other ads. The quality test: would someone share this if it sold nothing?

* **Hidden Villain** — something they use/trust daily revealed as harmful or surprisingly beneficial

  * *"That 'healthy' oatmeal you eat every morning spikes your blood sugar higher than a Snickers bar"*  
  * *"I've been getting paid $1,000 a month just from this app on my phone"*  
  * *"Your plastic cutting board is flooding your food with synthetic estrogen"*  
* **Impossible Question** — two true facts that seem to contradict each other

  * *"Why do French women eat butter, cheese, and croissants daily and still weigh half what American women do?"*  
  * *"Marathon runners have more arterial plaque than couch potatoes"*  
  * *"How are 2,500-year-old teeth perfectly preserved after millennia in dirt while ours are rotting with modern dentistry?"*  
* **Body Check** — give them something to test on themselves right now

  * *"Press your thumbnail for 3 seconds and let go. If the color doesn't return within 2 seconds, your circulation is already declining."*  
  * *"Stand up quickly right now. If you get dizzy, that's not normal — that's your adrenals failing."*  
  * *"If you're type 2 and take metformin, check your feet and legs for these two things."*  
* **Demonstration** — show what a common substance actually does when examined

  * *"Watch what nuts do to our body"* → walk through cashews (poison ivy family), walnuts (same fat as avocados), oats (basically pure sugar)  
  * *"Here's what your tap water looks like under a microscope"*  
* **Quick Quiz** — yes/no symptom questions forcing self-identification

  * *"Do you crash at 2pm? Wake at 3am? Crave salt after meals? Feel wired but tired at bedtime? If you said yes to three or more..."*  
* **Warning List** — numbered signs or signals

  * *"5 signs your metabolism is broken"*  
  * *"3 things your child needs to get into a top university — and it's not GPA or SAT scores"*  
* **Strange Fact** — pure curiosity, no obvious product connection

  * *"Why you wake up at 3:17am every night"*  
  * *"If your pee smells like popcorn, watch this"*  
  * *"What your second toe length actually means about your health"*  
* **Unusual Method** — weird origin or format signals novelty

  * *"Weird Japanese method melts 3 pounds overnight"*  
  * *"The Alpine ice hack that dissolves fat"*  
  * *"The breakfast food that blocks fat storage"*  
* **Expert Contradiction** — credentialed person does the opposite of expected

  * *"The cardiologist who promotes high cholesterol"*  
  * *"The gym teacher who can't do a pushup"*  
  * *"The minimalist who owns 500 pairs of shoes"*  
* **Pattern Recognition** — identical inputs, different outputs, unexplained

  * *"These two dogs are the same age, eat the same food, exercise the same — yet one has grey fur and can barely walk while the other plays like a puppy. What makes the difference?"*  
* **Secret Shortcut** — discovered opportunity framing

  * *"The $5 item that flips for $500"*  
  * *"Why millionaires read their email backwards"*  
* **Magic Phrase** — extremely specific verbal promise

  * *"Seven words to make anyone obsessed with you"*  
  * *"The four-word text that makes him chase"*  
  * *"Say this one thing at your next job interview"*

### **Problem-Aware Concepts**

The job: name the real enemy, validate their experience (it's not their fault), explain why past solutions were doomed, teach the mechanism that actually drives the problem. Install the beliefs that there's a hidden mechanism, past solutions missed it, and the problem is getting worse.

* **The Real Cause** — name the hidden mechanism *(most important concept at this level — always test first)*

  * *"It's not calories — it's insulin resistance"*  
  * *"It's not laziness — it's lymphatic blockage at the neck preventing your brain from cleaning itself at night"*  
  * *"It's not your spending — it's childhood money trauma creating a self-sabotage loop"*  
* **Why Nothing Else Worked** — explain why every past attempt was doomed

  * *"Here's why cutting carbs never worked — your body compensated by downregulating your metabolism. Here's why the gym didn't help — exercise increases cortisol, which worsens insulin resistance when your hormones are disrupted. Here's why willpower failed — you're fighting brain chemistry with discipline, which is like holding your breath forever."*  
* **Flipped Cause** — reverse the causal direction they assume

  * *"You're not tired because you're lazy — you're 'lazy' because your mitochondria produce 40% less energy than they should"*  
  * *"You don't avoid exercise because you're fat — you're fat because something is making exercise feel impossible"*  
  * *"You don't overspend because you lack discipline — you lack discipline because your nervous system is dysregulated from childhood trauma"*  
* **The Metaphor** — make the mechanism tangible and visual

  * *"Your metabolism is like a furnace with a broken thermostat — eating less just makes it burn colder"*  
  * *"Your fat cells are like vaults with broken locks — insulin is jamming them shut"*  
  * *"Your money trauma is like a thermostat set to 'broke' — no matter how much you earn, the system pulls you back to the set point"*  
* **Authority Confirmation** — credentialed person validates the real cause

  * *"As a dermatologist with 24 years of experience, what I found recently changed everything I thought I knew about aging skin"*  
  * *"As a cardiologist, I've been telling my patients to take statins for decades. I was wrong."*  
* **Demonstration Through Story** — show the mechanism working through someone's experience

  * *"I gave my 79-year-old dad adaptogenic mushrooms and what happened next blew me away. Within a week, he was sleeping through the night for the first time in years."*  
* **Compounding Damage** — the problem gets worse every day they wait

  * *"Each hour of lost sleep increases tomorrow's cortisol by 15%, which guarantees worse sleep tomorrow, which accelerates aging by three times"*  
  * *"Every month of untreated inflammation causes more permanent joint damage that becomes irreversible"*  
  * *"Your debt isn't staying flat — interest is compounding. Every month you wait, the hole gets $400 deeper."*  
* **Suppressed Truth** — information was withheld by the system

  * *"Why psychiatrists never check your Vitamin D levels — it would put them out of business"*  
  * *"The supplement that's banned in America but required in Japan"*  
* **Bold Statement** — big claim immediately backed by mechanism

  * *"This will make you stop snoring tonight."* → then explain the airway mechanism  
  * *"Your fatigue ends in 72 hours."* → then explain the lymphatic mechanism  
* **Origin Story** — full arc: pain → discovery → transformation

  * *"'Your son has no future' — that's what his teacher told me at parent-teacher night. Three weeks later, he smiled while doing homework for the first time in two years."*  
* **Hidden Bottleneck** — the one specific limiter they didn't know existed

  * *"Your visual processing speed caps your golf game at a 15 handicap — perfect form can't overcome slow neural firing"*  
  * *"Your pancreatic beta cells are the bottleneck — until they're reset, no amount of dieting will work"*  
* **Domino Chain** — problem connects to seemingly unrelated life areas

  * *"Bad posture triggers submission hormones → reduces confidence → limits career growth → increases stress → worsens posture"*  
  * *"Gut bacteria control neurotransmitter production → affects mood → changes food choices → alters gut bacteria"*

### **Solution-Aware Concepts**

The job: explain why your mechanism is different and superior, demonstrate concrete results, position as the clear best choice. Complete the unique mechanism belief, begin the personal authority belief, start removing constraints.

* **Full Mechanism Explanation** — deep dive on how and why your approach works

  * *"We don't count calories — we address insulin resistance at the cellular level through a specific combination of spices that increase metabolic flexibility. Here's the science behind why it works and what happens in your body when you take it."*  
* **Fastest Simplest Path** — position as lowest-friction option

  * *"It takes 90 seconds for this NASA-engineered device to cool your room"*  
  * *"30-second morning ritual replaces hours of meal prep"*  
  * *"Just 5 minutes a day — no gym, no equipment, no complex tracking"*  
* **Side-by-Side** — direct comparison against known alternatives

  * *"Ozempic costs $1,000/month, requires injections, and you gain the weight back when you stop. This costs $30/month, takes 30 seconds in the morning, and the results are permanent because it fixes the root cause."*  
  * *"Dave Ramsey's system addresses behavior but not trauma. It takes 2 years and runs on shame. Ours addresses the trauma first, takes 30 days, and includes your partner."*  
* **Discovery Journey** — how the solution was found

  * *"After my wife's third miscarriage at 35, I found an obscure MIT study on CoQ10 and egg quality. The first version of the protocol tasted like dirt and didn't absorb. It took 14 months to figure out that adding black pepper increased absorption by 2,000%."*  
* **Proof Through Testing** — empirical comparison where yours wins

  * *"I tested every popular diet for 30 days each — here's what won"*  
  * *"We gave 1,000 people this supplement and tested their blood work before and after. Here's what happened."*  
* **Specific Transformation** — detailed case study with numbers and timeline

  * *"Jim and Carol went from bankruptcy to $50,000 saved in eight months. His gambling addiction was addressed. Her shopping habits healed. They funded their kids' college."*  
* **Evidence Stack** — multiple independent sources all validating your approach

  * *"Stanford ran a 20-year study on money scripts that validated our approach. The Japanese have used a version of this method called Kakeibo since 1904. The VA has validated somatic experiencing — the technique we use — for trauma recovery."*  
* **Transformation Timeline** — concrete milestones

  * *"Week 1: detox symptoms and cravings may appear. Days 8-21: energy surge and mental clarity. Days 22-45: visible changes that others start noticing. Days 46-90: full metabolic flexibility."*  
* **The One Thing Nobody Else Does** — true differentiator

  * *"The only system combining somatic money healing with practical financial systems"*  
  * *"The first protocol that addresses egg quality naturally instead of just hormones"*  
  * *"The only program that includes your partner at no extra charge"*  
* **Outsider Who Cracked It** — authority from unexpected domain

  * *"Two engineers who worked on thermal control systems for NASA space missions built this portable AC"*  
  * *"A prison psychologist's method for beating addiction — not a rehab counselor, not a therapist, a prison psychologist"*  
* **Credibility Stack** — story + credentials + track record + social proof combined

  * *"After 10 years of chronic pain, trying 47 different treatments, spending $200,000, and eventually helping 3,400 other people recover, I know exactly what works and what doesn't."*  
* **The "What If" Reframe** — bridge from frustration to your approach

  * *"What if the reason nothing worked before is that you were solving the wrong problem? What if you don't need more willpower, a better diet, or a stricter budget — what if you need to heal the mechanism that's been sabotaging everything?"*

### **Product-Aware Concepts**

The job: address every objection, prove it works for people like them, remove risk, make saying yes feel safe and obvious. Complete constraint removal, reinforce personal authority, activate risk reversal, remind them of escalating cost.

* **Barrier Destroyer** — address every reason they haven't bought

  * Time: *"Takes 5 minutes daily, not hour-long sessions"*  
  * Money: *"Less than your morning coffee"*  
  * Complexity: *"No spreadsheets or complex tracking — one simple visual system"*  
  * Identity: *"You don't need to be a 'fitness person' — this was built for normal people"*  
  * Capability: *"No gym required. No equipment. No lifestyle overhaul."*  
  * Past failure: *"Works especially well if you've failed at everything else — because those failures weren't your fault"*  
  * Partner: *"Your partner is included free"*  
* **Person Just Like Me** — testimonial matching their exact segment

  * *"I'm a 52-year-old divorced mom of two. I work full-time. I've tried seven diets. I paid off $67,000 in 18 months and my marriage was saved."*  
* **Transformation Proof** — multi-dimensional before/after

  * *"Before: 287 pounds, size 22, prediabetic, couldn't play with my kids without getting winded. After: 163 pounds, size 8, perfect bloodwork, training for a triathlon, and my husband lost 45 pounds doing nothing different."*  
* **Objection Crusher** — take top 3-5 objections head-on

  * *"You're probably thinking 'I've tried supplements before and they didn't work.' Here's why this is different — those supplements treated symptoms. This addresses the mechanism that creates the symptoms. It's like the difference between mopping the floor and fixing the leak."*  
* **Safety Net** — lead with the guarantee as the whole message

  * *"Use it for 60 full days. If your snoring doesn't stop completely, send it back for a complete refund, no questions asked. Keep the bonuses regardless."*  
* **Founder's Story** — why trust this specific person

  * *"I spent $500,000 and 10 years finding the answer to chronic pain. I tried 47 treatments. I was told it was all in my head. I nearly lost my marriage. Then I found an obscure study that changed everything."*  
* **Value Inventory** — walk through every component

  * *"Here's exactly what you get: daily somatic exercises (the core system), a visual tracking system, emergency protocols for when something triggers you, your partner's access included free, plus the investment basics course, the tax optimization guide, and the kids' money training bonus."*  
* **Segment Match** — narrow the audience to increase conversion

  * *"This works especially well if you've failed at 3+ diets, you're over 40, your hormones feel off, and you have less than 30 minutes a day."*  
* **The Numbers** — hard metrics

  * *"5,247 members. $8.3 million in collective debt eliminated. 87% reach their goals within six months. 91% satisfaction rate."*  
* **Permission Slip** — release the emotional shame

  * *"It's okay if you've failed before — that wasn't your fault. It's okay if you're skeptical — you should be after what you've been through. It's okay if you don't have much time — the system was built for that."*  
* **Cost of Waiting** — reframe inaction as the expensive choice

  * *"Every day you wait costs you $147 in lost productivity"*  
  * *"Your fertility is declining — not slowly, not eventually, right now, every cycle"*  
  * *"Each month without treatment is another month of permanent damage"*

### **Most-Aware Concepts**

The job: give them a reason to buy today. No more education, no more mechanism, no more proof. Just urgency, scarcity, risk removal, and value stacking.

* **Real Deadline** — must be genuine

  * *"Enrollment closes Friday at midnight"*  
  * *"Price increases $200 next month"*  
  * *"Next group starts in 7 days — miss it and the next one isn't until March"*  
* **Real Limit** — must be genuine

  * *"I can only properly coach 50 people at a time. 38 spots are filled."*  
  * *"They've sold out twice — they can only produce limited quantities each month"*  
* **Price Reframe** — compare to daily cost, alternative cost, or problem cost

  * *"That's less than $3 a day — less than your morning coffee"*  
  * *"Compare $497 to the $30,000 you've already spent on IVF cycles that didn't work"*  
  * *"Your current problem costs you $147/day in lost productivity. This pays for itself in 72 hours."*  
* **Bonus Pile** — stack value with dollar amounts

  * *"Order today: partner's access ($497 value), kids' money training ($197 value), investment basics course ($297 value), tax optimization guide ($397 value). Total added: $1,388 — yours free today."*  
* **Regret Prevention** — future emotional projection

  * *"Six months from now, you'll wish you started today"*  
  * *"The people who saw this same ad last year and didn't act — where do you think they are now?"*  
* **Guarantee Spotlight** — guarantee is the entire ad

  * *"Here's our deal: use it for 60 full days. Follow the protocol. If it doesn't work, email us and get every penny back. No questions. No hassle. You keep the bonuses. We take 100% of the risk."*  
* **Momentum** — social proof of action

  * *"Join the 10,000 people who already transformed"*  
  * *"247 people signed up this week"*  
* **Daily Cost of Doing Nothing** — reframe delay as the most expensive choice

  * *"Every day with poor sleep ages your brain by two days"*  
  * *"This problem compounds at 15% per month. The math only gets harder."*  
* **Last Objection** — find the ONE thing and eliminate it

  * If money → show payment plan or daily cost breakdown  
  * If time → show how little is required  
  * If "will this work for ME" → show testimonial from their exact situation  
* **Special Deal** — limited-time offer

  * *"For a limited time, buy one get one free"*  
  * *"First 48 hours only: this bonus disappears after Monday"*

## **Styles**

### **Static**

* **Ugly Ad** — raw text on plain background, intentionally undesigned, looks like content not an ad  
* **Bold Statement** — big clean typography, one strong claim, minimal design  
* **Fake Post** — looks like a tweet, Reddit comment, Facebook post, or Google result  
* **Phone Screenshot** — looks like a text convo, email, app screen, or payment notification  
* **Product Hero** — product front and center, clean, benefit-driven  
* **Multi-Slide Carousel** — each slide reveals next piece of info

### **Video**

* **Real Person** — regular person filming with phone, raw, authentic — highest trust style  
* **Expert on Camera** — structured talking head, more polished  
* **Founder on Camera** — CEO/creator speaking directly  
* **Show-and-Tell** — person on camera + green screen showing evidence behind them  
* **Text Video** — background footage with text overlays for sound-off  
* **Raw Clip** — handheld phone, no edits, looks like someone just hit record  
* **Conversation** — two people talking, podcast clip format  
* **Reaction** — split screen reacting to another video or competitor claims

## **Hooks**

The first 1-3 seconds or first line. Multiple hooks get tested against the same ad body. This is the cheapest and most granular form of creative testing.

### **Curiosity Hooks**

* *"Why do smokers in Japan outlive health-conscious Americans?"*  
* *"Nobody's talking about this"*  
* *"I wasn't supposed to share this"*  
* *"What happens when you [simple action] for 30 days"*  
* *"This is the [thing] they don't want you to see"*

### **Call-Out Hooks**

* *"If you wake up at 3am every night, watch this"*  
* *"Women over 40 with stubborn belly fat — this is for you"*  
* *"You're probably making this mistake right now"*  
* *"POV: you just found out your oatmeal is worse than candy"*  
* *"This is for everyone who's tried everything and given up"*

### **Authority Hooks**

* *"Harvard cardiologist says stop taking statins immediately"*  
* *"After treating 10,000 patients, here's what I never tell them"*  
* *"I've spent 20 years studying this and this is the #1 thing"*  
* *"New Stanford study just proved what we've been saying"*

### **Proof Hooks**

* *"I tested 12 popular diets for 30 days each — here's what actually worked"*  
* *"Sarah lost 47 pounds in 4 months doing this one thing"*  
* *"Watch what happens when I put this under a microscope"*  
* *"Here's my blood work after 90 days"*  
* *"Before and after — same person, 6 months apart"*

### **Emotion Hooks**

* *"I wish someone had told me this 10 years ago"*  
* *"My doctor looked at my labs and said 'we need to talk'"*  
* *"The moment I realized everything I'd been taught was wrong"*  
* *"I almost didn't post this"*  
* *"This changed my life and I'm not being dramatic"*

### **Contrarian Hooks**

* *"Stop taking probiotics — they're making it worse"*  
* *"Your protein powder is a scam and here's proof"*  
* *"Everything you've been told about cholesterol is backwards"*  
* *"That 'healthy' smoothie is destroying your gut"*

### **Urgency Hooks**

* *"This deal is gone Friday at midnight"*  
* *"247 people signed up in the last 24 hours"*  
* *"Last time we opened this, it sold out in 6 hours"*  
* *"If you've been waiting for a sign, this is it"*

## **Amplifiers That Boost Any Hook**

Certain references consistently boost performance by borrowing pre-existing emotional charge:

* **Authority references** — NASA, MIT, Harvard, Stanford, Mayo Clinic, NIH, "peer-reviewed," "new study," "declassified"  
* **Cultural/tribal references** — Okinawans, Sardinians, Maasai warriors, Tibetan monks, Italian grandmothers, Japanese centenarians  
* **Historical references** — "your grandmother," "before refrigeration," "ancient Egyptians," "Victorian doctors," "Depression-era families"  
* **Elite performer references** — Navy SEALs, Olympic athletes, astronauts, fighter pilots, Silicon Valley CEOs  
* **Blue Zone references** — Okinawa, Sardinia, Ikaria, Nicoya Peninsula, Loma Linda

## **The Three-Tier Rollout**

The key principle: **every tier covers every segment and every awareness level.** That's the whole point of creative coverage — you never leave entire territories unexplored. What scales across tiers is the *depth* per cell: the number of concepts, angles, styles, and hook variations you test within each segment × awareness level combination.

Think of it like painting a wall. Tier 1 is one thin coat across the whole surface — you can see where the paint catches and where it doesn't. Tier 2 is a second coat, heavier in the spots that worked. Tier 3 is full coverage, multiple coats everywhere, thickest where results are strongest.

---

### **TIER 1: STARTER (Weeks 1-4)**

**Goal:** One thin coat across the entire grid. Hit every segment at every awareness level with your single best-guess concept, one angle, and one style. Find which cells light up.

**Target volume: 50-75 ad skeletons → 150-225 testable variations (with 3 hook variations each)**

#### **Step 1 — Map Your Full Grid**

List out all your segments (let's say 3-5) and all 5 awareness levels. Every combination is a cell. If you have 4 segments and 5 awareness levels, you have 20 cells. Every cell gets at least one ad.

**Example grid for a weight loss supplement with 4 segments:**

|  | Unaware | Problem-Aware | Solution-Aware | Product-Aware | Most-Aware |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Women 35-50, failed diets | Cell 1 | Cell 2 | Cell 3 | Cell 4 | Cell 5 |
| Men 40+, doctor warnings | Cell 6 | Cell 7 | Cell 8 | Cell 9 | Cell 10 |
| New moms, postpartum | Cell 11 | Cell 12 | Cell 13 | Cell 14 | Cell 15 |
| Desk workers, no time | Cell 16 | Cell 17 | Cell 18 | Cell 19 | Cell 20 |

20 cells. Every single one gets creative in Tier 1.

#### **Step 2 — Pick 1-2 Concepts Per Cell**

Choose the single strongest concept for each awareness level, then adapt it to each segment's language and pain.

* **Unaware** → Hidden Villain + Body Check *(these two consistently have the highest scroll-stop rate)*  
* **Problem-Aware** → The Real Cause + Why Nothing Else Worked *(the foundational concepts)*  
* **Solution-Aware** → Fastest Simplest Path + Specific Transformation *(simplicity + proof)*  
* **Product-Aware** → Barrier Destroyer + Person Just Like Me *(remove friction + social proof)*  
* **Most-Aware** → Safety Net / Guarantee Spotlight + Price Reframe *(remove risk + reframe cost)*

#### **Step 3 — Pick 1 Angle Per Concept**

Don't overthink this at Tier 1. Match to your audience's most obvious emotional state:

* **Unaware** → Everyday Threat *(widest reach)*  
* **Problem-Aware** → Myth Buster *(challenges existing belief, opens the mechanism door)*  
* **Solution-Aware** → Experiment *(empirical credibility)*  
* **Product-Aware** → Confession *(vulnerability earns trust from skeptical prospects)*  
* **Most-Aware** → Countdown *(urgency)*

#### **Step 4 — Pick 1 Style Per Format**

* **Static:** Ugly Ad *(highest-performing first test in most markets — doesn't look like an ad)*  
* **Video:** Real Person *(highest trust)*

That's it. Two styles total. Every other style is a Tier 2 test.

#### **Step 5 — Adapt Each Ad to Each Segment**

Same concept, same angle, same style — but the specific language, pain expressions, demographic signals, and testimonials match the segment.

**Example — Problem-Aware / The Real Cause / Myth Buster / Ugly Ad, adapted across segments:**

* **Women 35-50, failed diets:**  
   *"You've tried keto, Weight Watchers, Noom, and counting every calorie. None of it stuck. It's not your willpower — it's insulin resistance. Your body is locked in fat-storage mode, and no diet addresses that. Here's what actually does."*

* **Men 40+, doctor warnings:**  
   *"Your doctor said lose 40 pounds or go on medication. So you hit the gym and cut carbs. Still nothing. It's not effort — it's insulin resistance. Your metabolism is working against you. Here's the one thing that actually unlocks it."*

* **New moms, postpartum:**  
   *"You expected to bounce back after the baby. It's been 14 months and nothing fits. It's not the breastfeeding, it's not the lack of sleep, it's not 'just give it time' — it's postpartum insulin resistance. Here's what nobody tells you."*

* **Desk workers, no time:**  
   *"You sit 10 hours a day and your metabolism has flatlined. You don't have time for hour-long workouts or meal prep Sundays. Good — you don't need them. The real problem is insulin resistance, and fixing it takes 5 minutes a day."*

Same concept. Same angle. Four genuinely different ads because each is written in a different person's language about their specific life.

#### **Step 6 — Write 3 Hook Variations Per Ad Skeleton**

Pull from different hook categories. Same body, different opening line.

**Example — the Women 35-50 Problem-Aware ad above:**

1. *(Call-Out)* *"If you've tried every diet and nothing sticks, it's not your fault — watch this"*  
2. *(Contrarian)* *"Stop counting calories. It's making you fatter."*  
3. *(Emotion)* *"I wish someone had told me this before I wasted 15 years on diets that were designed to fail"*

#### **Step 7 — Launch and Measure**

**What you're looking for:**

* **Which cells light up?** Which segment × awareness level combinations produce the best cost-per-acquisition?  
* **Which concepts pull?** Does The Real Cause outperform Why Nothing Else Worked? Does Hidden Villain outperform Body Check?  
* **Which hooks stop scrolls?** Look at hook rate / thumb-stop rate across the 3 variations per ad.  
* **Which segments respond?** Are men 40+ dramatically cheaper than new moms? Or the reverse?  
* **Which awareness levels convert?** Is Problem-Aware cheaper than Solution-Aware for cold traffic? Does Most-Aware retargeting pay for itself?

Tier 1 gives you a heat map of your entire grid. You now know where the warmest zones are.

---

### **TIER 2: EXPANSION (Weeks 5-12)**

**Goal:** Go deeper in every cell. More concepts per cell. More angles per concept. More styles. More hooks. Thickest coverage in the cells that lit up in Tier 1, but still expanding the cold cells too — sometimes a cell didn't work because the concept or angle was wrong, not because the cell itself is dead.

**Target volume: 150-400 ad skeletons → 500-1,500+ testable variations**

**Production cadence: 10-20 new variations per week**

#### **Step 1 — Multiply Your Winners**

For every winning ad from Tier 1 (any cell that hit performance benchmarks):

* Run the winning concept through **3 new angles** you didn't test before  
* Run the winning concept through **2 new styles** you didn't test before  
* Write **5 new hook variations** from different hook categories

**Example — if "The Real Cause" + "Myth Buster" + "Ugly Ad" won for Women 35-50 at Problem-Aware:**

New angles on same concept:

* Confession: *"I've been a nutritionist for 22 years and I've been giving the wrong advice. The real cause of stubborn weight isn't what you eat — it's insulin resistance."*  
* Hidden Truth: *"There's a reason your doctor never explains why diets fail. They don't learn about insulin resistance in medical school. Here's what they're not telling you."*  
* Paradox: *"Why do some women eat pizza and wine and stay thin while you gain weight eating salads? It's not genetics. It's insulin resistance."*

New styles on same concept:

* Fake Post (looks like a Facebook comment or Reddit post)  
* Real Person (video version of the same message)

New hooks:

1. *"Everything you've been told about weight loss is backwards"*  
2. *"New Stanford study reveals why 93% of diets fail — and it has nothing to do with willpower"*  
3. *"The moment I realized every diet I'd tried was designed to fail"*  
4. *"If your belly fat won't budge no matter what you eat, this is why"*  
5. *"I almost didn't post this because it goes against everything the fitness industry teaches"*

#### **Step 2 — Expand Concepts in Every Cell**

Scale from 1-2 concepts per cell to **3-5 concepts per cell**.

**Unaware — add:**

* Impossible Question  
* Warning List  
* Demonstration

**Problem-Aware — add:**

* Flipped Cause  
* Compounding Damage  
* Authority Confirmation

**Solution-Aware — add:**

* Side-by-Side Comparison  
* Discovery Journey  
* Evidence Stack

**Product-Aware — add:**

* Objection Crusher  
* Value Inventory  
* Cost of Waiting

**Most-Aware — add:**

* Real Deadline  
* Bonus Pile  
* Regret Prevention

#### **Step 3 — Expand Angles to 2-3 Per Concept**

Every concept now gets tested through 2-3 angles matched to the segment's psychology.

**Example — Unaware / Hidden Villain concept, across 3 angles:**

* Everyday Threat: *"That 'healthy' oatmeal you eat every morning spikes your blood sugar higher than a Snickers bar"*  
* Sacred Cow: *"You were told oatmeal is the healthiest breakfast. It's not. It spikes your blood sugar higher than a candy bar."*  
* Warning Sign: *"If you're always hungry an hour after breakfast, check what you're eating. Oatmeal spikes your blood sugar higher than a Snickers."*

#### **Step 4 — Expand Styles to 4-5**

Add to your rotation:

* **Static:** Bold Statement, Phone Screenshot, Multi-Slide Carousel  
* **Video:** Show-and-Tell (green screen), Text Video, Expert on Camera

Test new styles against proven concept × angle combinations. Same message, different format — pure style testing.

#### **Step 5 — Expand Hooks to 3-5 Per Ad Skeleton**

Each ad skeleton now gets tested with 3-5 hook variations instead of 3.

#### **Step 6 — Deepen Segment Personalization**

In Tier 1 you adapted the language. Now go deeper:

* Segment-specific testimonials (Person Just Like Me that actually matches each segment)  
* Segment-specific objections (the reasons a new mom hasn't bought are different from the reasons a desk worker hasn't bought)  
* Segment-specific transformation proof (before/after from someone in their demographic)  
* Segment-specific metaphors (the desk worker's metabolism is "like a computer in sleep mode" vs. the new mom's metabolism is "like a thermostat that pregnancy reset to the wrong temperature")

**Example — Product-Aware / Person Just Like Me, 4 segment versions:**

* Women 35-50: *"I'm 47. Three kids. Full-time job. I tried keto, Weight Watchers, Noom, and Ozempic. Nothing stuck. This is the first time in 20 years I've kept weight off for more than 6 months."*  
* Men 40+: *"I'm 53. My doctor said lose 40 pounds or go on medication. I lost 52 in 5 months. No gym. No meal prep. My blood work came back perfect."*  
* New moms: *"My baby just turned one and I finally fit into my pre-pregnancy jeans. I couldn't exercise with a newborn. I didn't have time to cook special meals. This worked around my actual life."*  
* Desk workers: *"I sit at a desk 10 hours a day. I gained 35 pounds in 2 years. I don't have time for the gym. I lost 38 pounds doing this for 5 minutes every morning before work."*

#### **Step 7 — Re-Test Cold Cells**

For cells that didn't perform in Tier 1, don't assume they're dead. Test with:

* A completely different concept (if Hidden Villain didn't work for Unaware, try Impossible Question or Strange Fact)  
* A completely different angle (if Myth Buster didn't work, try Story or Confession)  
* A completely different style (if Ugly Ad didn't work, try Raw Clip video)

Sometimes a cell looks dead because you tried the wrong combination, not because the cell itself has no potential.

#### **What You're Measuring at Tier 2**

* **Concept-level performance** — which concepts consistently win across different angles and styles?  
* **Angle performance** — which angles consistently win across different concepts?  
* **Style performance** — are certain styles beating others regardless of content?  
* **Segment response patterns** — do different segments respond to different concepts or angles?  
* **Awareness level efficiency** — where does the best cost-per-acquisition live at scale?  
* **Creative fatigue curves** — how long do winners last before performance degrades?  
* **Cell-level heat map update** — which cells are now warm that were cold in Tier 1?

---

### **TIER 3: FULL COVERAGE (Month 4+, Ongoing)**

**Goal:** The machine. Every cell in the grid has deep coverage. Every winning combination is fully explored. Every losing combination has been tested and eliminated. You're optimizing and refreshing, not discovering.

**Target volume: 300-1,500+ ad skeletons active → 1,000-3,000+ variations in rotation**

**Production cadence: 20-40 new variations per week**

#### **All Segments × All Awareness Levels at Full Depth**

Every cell in the grid is active. Budget is allocated based on performance data from Tier 2:

* **40-50%** to the highest-performing cold traffic cells (usually Problem-Aware and Solution-Aware for your top 1-2 segments)  
* **20-30%** to Unaware across all segments for top-of-funnel expansion  
* **15-20%** to Product-Aware retargeting across all segments  
* **5-10%** to Most-Aware conversion

#### **8-12 Concepts Active Per Cell**

Every concept from the framework gets tested at its designated awareness level, for every segment. Winners get multiple angle variations, multiple styles, and deep hook testing. Losers get retired but revisited quarterly with fresh angles in case the market shifted.

#### **Full Angle Rotation Per Concept**

At Tier 3, each concept gets systematically tested through 3-5+ angles.

**Example — Full angle rotation for one Problem-Aware concept across one segment:**

Concept: The Real Cause (insulin resistance), Segment: Women 35-50

* Myth Buster: *"Everything you've been told about weight loss is backwards. It's not calories — it's insulin resistance."*  
* Confession: *"I've been a dietitian for 22 years. I was wrong. The real cause isn't what you eat — it's insulin resistance."*  
* Hidden Truth: *"There's a reason your doctor never tests for insulin resistance. Here's what they're not telling you."*  
* Paradox: *"Why do thin people eat more than you and stay thin? It's not metabolism. It's insulin resistance — and yours is fixable."*  
* Story: *"In 2019, I weighed 230 pounds and had tried every diet. Then my naturopath ran one test my doctor never did: fasting insulin. The number changed everything."*  
* Warning Sign: *"If you gain weight in your midsection first, that's not aging — that's insulin resistance. Here's what it means and what to do."*

Six genuinely different ads from one concept for one segment. Now multiply that across 12 concepts and 4 segments.

#### **Full Style Rotation**

All 6 static styles and all 8 video styles in play. Each winning concept × angle combination gets tested across 3-4 styles. Styles also rotate to fight creative fatigue — same message, fresh visual format.

**Example — One concept × one angle tested across styles:**

Concept: Hidden Villain (oatmeal blood sugar spike), Angle: Everyday Threat

* Ugly Ad: plain text, *"That 'healthy' oatmeal you eat every morning spikes your blood sugar higher than a Snickers bar. Here's what happens..."*  
* Fake Post: formatted as a tweet or Reddit comment with the same message  
* Bold Statement: large text on dark background — *"YOUR OATMEAL IS WORSE THAN CANDY"* with supporting copy below  
* Real Person: woman in her kitchen holding a bowl of oatmeal, filmed on phone  
* Text Video: b-roll of someone making oatmeal with text overlays delivering the message  
* Show-and-Tell: person on camera with CGI/green screen showing blood sugar charts behind them

Six ads. Same message. Completely different scroll-stopping visual experience.

#### **Hook-Line Testing at Scale**

Proven ad bodies get 5-10 different opening hooks tested systematically. A single winning body with 10 hook variations is 10 testable ads for the production cost of one.

**Example — One winning ad body gets 10 hooks:**

1. *"Stop eating oatmeal"*  
2. *"That 'healthy' breakfast is spiking your blood sugar"*  
3. *"Why do you crash at 10am every day? It's your breakfast."*  
4. *"I tested my blood sugar after oatmeal. I was shocked."*  
5. *"Your oatmeal is worse than a candy bar and here's the proof"*  
6. *"If you're always hungry by 10am, watch this"*  
7. *"Nobody's talking about what oatmeal actually does to your body"*  
8. *"New study: the 'healthiest' breakfast is the worst for your blood sugar"*  
9. *"POV: you just found out oatmeal spikes your blood sugar more than Snickers"*  
10. *"I wish someone had told me this before I ate oatmeal every morning for 15 years"*

#### **The "Multiply" Treatment for Every Winner**

Every ad that hits performance benchmarks gets expanded along all dimensions:

* Same concept × **new angle**  
* Same concept × same angle × **new style**  
* Same concept × same angle × same style × **new hook set**  
* Same concept × same angle × same style × **new segment**

#### **Ongoing Creative Refresh**

At Tier 3, creative fatigue is your main enemy. Winners degrade over time. The refresh cycle:

* Weekly: 20-40 new variations entering rotation (hook swaps, style swaps, angle swaps on proven concepts)  
* Monthly: new concepts entering testing at each awareness level  
* Quarterly: full grid audit — which cells have gone cold? Which retired concepts deserve another shot with new angles?

#### **Build a Creative Scorecard**

Track performance by each variable independently:

* Which concepts win most often?  
* Which angles win most often?  
* Which styles win most often?  
* Which hooks win most often?  
* Which segment × awareness level cells are hottest?  
* Which segment × concept pairings outperform?

Use the scorecard to prioritize production. The framework tells you what to make. The data tells you what to keep.

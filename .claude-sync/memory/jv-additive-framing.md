When writing copy for a JV partner's audience (e.g. Ryan Magdanz's Leadbase / PPL operators), NEVER dunk on their existing business model. The offer is an ADDITION that makes their core business STRONGER — never a replacement that implies their model is broken.

**Why:** Joey killed a squeeze-page rewrite that opened "stop living and dying by ad spend… that treadmill gets OLD fast." The partner's audience IS the partner's livelihood and customer base — trashing their model (paid ads / lead gen) insults the partner and the reader. This is the sharper, more general form of the rubric's "name the complement" note.

**How to apply:** Frame every line as "on top of what you already do," not "instead of." Show the synergy — the new offer makes their existing service convert better (e.g. the Androids work the leads their lead gen already generates). No "escape the grind," no "your ads suck." Lives in the copy-rubric PERSUASION section + living log (2026-07-03).

**Refinement (2026-07-03, Ryan email 3):** the sharpest additive angle for a lead-gen / PPL audience is RETENTION, not just "a new side income." Lead with the churn stakes — the leads they already send get dropped by the client's team, don't close, and the AGENCY gets blamed for "bad lead quality," so the client churns. Position the AI system as protecting that existing revenue first (stop the churn, more revenue per client without more work), then the new income cut as the bonus. "Make your existing clients stickier" beats "earn a new side income."

When a decision is put to Joey, show him the actual thing to look at — a rendered visual, a mockup, two concrete examples side by side. Do NOT describe two abstract options in prose and ask him to imagine the difference. He can't decide on what he can't see.

**Why:** Joey said (2026-07-11) "there's a problem with the way you ask your questions — my brain cannot process it. I can't see it and therefore can't make a decision." This is exactly how his whole workspace already says he works: [[the-script]] — "Show me an example before writing. I work from swipes and past winners, not blank prompts" and "If you are unsure what good looks like, ask for an example before guessing." Abstract either/or questions are the wrong shape for a visual creative director.

**How to apply:** Before asking him to choose, render it — an SVG/mockup (the visualize tool, an artifact, a screenshot), or at minimum two concrete filled-in examples. For an ad Image choice, draw both versions. For copy, show the two lines, not "punchy vs formal." Reserve non-visual micro-decisions (proof-type flags, filenames) for me to default and just report — don't make him adjudicate the small stuff. Proven out: he instantly picked "B" from a rendered A/B of the Lead Coma static after being unable to decide from the prose version.

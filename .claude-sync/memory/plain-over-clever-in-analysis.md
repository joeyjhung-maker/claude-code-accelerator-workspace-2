Joey's "plain over clever" rule governs **analysis writing too** — audits, findings, strategy notes — not just ad copy.

He stopped this line in the Tobi Arayomi teardown:

> "the £49 product is sold into a community that's a rounding error against the audience that already exists"

His response: *"I literally dont understand what you mean."*

The plain version: **he has 281,000 followers on Instagram, only 7,500 are in the Telegram group, and the £49 membership only ever gets offered to those 7,500.**

**Why:** analysis-speak *sounds* like rigour, so metaphors and abstractions slip past the voice rules that would catch them instantly in an ad. An unclear finding is worth nothing however true it is — and a finding he can't repeat out loud in a meeting can't be pitched.

**How to apply:** write every finding as a sentence that survives being read aloud to the prospect. Banned in analysis as well as copy: "rounding error," "load-bearing," "downstream of," "the seam," "inverted curve," "X, not opinion," "structurally capped." Doctrine/framework names go in citations, never in the sentence doing the explaining. If a finding can't be said in one plain sentence, it isn't understood yet.

Joey asked Travis directly how he builds community/engagement, ahead of taking over content for
all four of Dan's Skool groups. Full notes and B2B Leads Lab cross-references:
`mentors/travis-sago/2026-08-20-content-engagement-call.md`.

The core mechanics, in Travis's own frame:

- **Run people at their actual next obstacle, never a reward further downstream.** His "Evan in a
  straitjacket" story: someone locked in a straitjacket doesn't care that you know a great steak
  place outside — free them from the straitjacket first, let them see the next locked door, THEN
  sell the key to that. Selling "Hooters across the street" while they're still in the straitjacket
  is why engagement stalls.
- **"The map is not the terrain."** Test any assumed customer-journey/obstacle sequence against
  real people (a poll, direct engagement) before building content or an offer around it — his own
  AI Royalty Rockstars community mapped 4 pathways, then found a real segment (people who'd rather
  broker than build) the map hadn't fully predicted, only by testing it live.
- **"Diagnose but withhold" is really about finding their CURRENT plan, not concealing the
  answer.** He wants to see how someone is ALREADY trying to solve the problem outside a sales
  context — telling them the answer directly doesn't stick ("they'll forget it in 5 minutes"), so
  either he waits until they've hit the wall themselves, or he gets the audience to pre-sell each
  other.
- **Consensus polls make the audience do the selling.** His real example: a 2-option poll ("gaining
  skills" vs "gaining assets") where nearly everyone answered the same way — the visible consensus
  pre-sells anyone who answered differently ("there must be something to this"), AND it surfaces a
  free, self-identified lead list (people who gave the "right" answer but aren't yet in his paid
  group).
- **"Chum the water" — short probe posts across several angles, then go long only on the one that
  gets bites.** This is explicitly how he manages content across many groups at once without
  writing full long-form posts everywhere — exactly Joey's situation from Sept 1 onward.

**How to apply:** before building the Sept content calendar for AAA Ninjas/ROYA/MDAD/B2B Leads
Lab, run a real consensus poll to test the assumed audience journey rather than assuming it; keep
posts short by default and only go long on a confirmed-resonant angle; never present the paid
program/next offer to someone who hasn't yet cleared their actual current obstacle.

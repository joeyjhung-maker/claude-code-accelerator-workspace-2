When a piece of copy uses an outside analogy as the angle (e.g. "this is like what hedge fund managers do"), a faithful mad-lib of a swipe ad that only NAMES the comparison isn't enough — the reader needs the actual connecting logic: what specifically makes the analogy true, spelled out, before it gets mapped onto the offer.

For the Matt Leitz/BotBuilders JV reg-email 1 (2026-08-05), the first draft mad-libbed Dan's "hedge fund manager" FB post near-verbatim. Joey rejected it: it named the hedge fund comparison but never explained the mechanism (hedge funds get rich because they take a % of PROFITS — "2 and 20" — not a flat fee, so there's no ceiling on their upside), and it never showed how that same mechanism let Dan scale to real money. It also opened with "My friend Dan" in line 1 — proof/name before the reader had any reason to care.

**Why:** A swipe mad-lib preserves STRUCTURE but can accidentally drop the ARGUMENT if the source ad was written in a compressed, cascading-beat style (each line assumes the reader already gets the connection). When repurposing that structure for a colder or different audience, the connective logic has to be written back in explicitly, not assumed.

**How to apply:**
- When an analogy is the angle, don't just state the comparison — explain the actual mechanism that makes it true, in plain terms, before applying it to the offer.
- Build the reader's investment in the IDEA first. Don't name-drop the expert/spokesperson (Dan, or any other) in the opening lines — earn the reveal after the hook has landed, per [[jv-additive-framing]] and the partner-voice-template's "open on a hook, never proof" rule, sharpened here to also mean "never a name."
- If a swipe mad-lib comes back thin on logic, don't keep patching the mad-lib — go back to a fresh write (mariobot, regular body route) using the swipe only as a proof/mechanism REFERENCE, not a structural template.
- Also watch for accuracy drift when fleshing out a compressed source ad: don't invent relationship labels (e.g. calling a real named result "one of his students" when the source only ever called him "a friend") or unverified scale claims (e.g. "built a multi-million pound business") that aren't in any locked primer.

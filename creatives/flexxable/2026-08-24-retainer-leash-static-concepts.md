## Ranked concepts (7, best to weakest scroll-stop read)

1. **Phone face-up on a couch armrest, Slack notification lit, TV remote right beside it.**
   The remote is the gut-punch detail — you were right there, doing nothing, and now you
   can't be.
2. **Extreme close-up, white-knuckle grip on a phone, incoming call on screen.** No context
   needed — the physical "knot in your stomach" made visible before the viewer even reads
   anything.
3. **Home office desk at night, laptop glow, a full wine glass gone warm and untouched.**
   The untouched glass says: you tried to stop. Something pulled you back anyway.
4. **A crumpled invoice/contract stuffed in a junk drawer that won't close.** The "supposed
   to be a win" that got shoved away instead of celebrated.
5. **Shoes still on at the front door, bag dropped mid-entry.** You almost made it to "off
   mode" before the phone went.
6. **A sticky note reading "DO NOT DISTURB — FAMILY TIME," stuck on an open laptop.** The
   contradiction tells the whole story with zero copy needed.
7. **Overhead shot of a bed at 11pm — laptop open on one side, the other side untouched.**
   The destination the 9pm ping leads to; the empty side of the bed is the unspoken cost.

**My read:** #2 (white-knuckle grip) is the most viscerally "vicious" per the hook-
amplification framework — pure physical sensation, no scene-reading required. #6 (sticky
note) is the cleanest single-glance story. #7 (laptop in bed) carries the highest stakes
but is the most emotionally loaded — worth checking against Dan's actual comfort level
before running it, since it implies a relationship cost specifically.

**Lessons for [[kie-render-reference]]:**
1. Compound edits (fix pose + fix text + add a person, all at once) reliably fail —
   each pass tends to satisfy some instructions and silently drop or corrupt others.
   One targeted fix per edit pass, chaining off the best prior output, works far better
   than trying to fix everything in one shot.
2. `--ref` + `--model nano-banana-2` together IS supported (undocumented until now) —
   but combining a strong pose-reference constraint with the text-quality model seems to
   raise the odds of the model deviating from the reference more freely than `--ref`
   alone on the base/edit model. Trade-off, not a strict win.
2b. When a real reference photo doesn't match verbal description well enough after 1-2
   tries, stop re-describing and pass the actual photo via `--ref` immediately — this
   session burned 2 verbal-only attempts before reaching for the reference photo that
   was available from the start.
3. Fixing "make the text legible" as an isolated edit pass tends to also silently drift
   OTHER elements (room details, added accessories) even when the prompt says "keep
   everything else unchanged" — treat any edit pass as a full re-roll of everything
   ELSE too, not just the field being targeted, and re-check the whole frame after.

**Lesson for [[kie-render-reference]]:** the base model's text-garbling issue isn't limited
to primarily-text concepts (Notes app, screenshots) — it also hits otherwise-photographic
concepts that include ANY legible UI text as a secondary element (a notification bubble,
a lock screen). Default to `nano-banana-2` whenever any readable text appears anywhere in
frame, not just when text IS the concept.

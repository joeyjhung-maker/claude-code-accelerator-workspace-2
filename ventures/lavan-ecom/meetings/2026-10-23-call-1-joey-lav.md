## Top of mind
- No structured decision-making criteria exists yet: selections are currently driven by instinct and vibes
- Core strategic tension: copy what is already scaling vs. shift one element to open a new segment
- Agreed framing: treat this like launching an offer, not building a brand from scratch
- Priority for 2026: get a store live, sell something, prove the system works before scaling complexity
- ADHD is the strongest instinct-backed niche candidate: growing market, personal legitimacy, content volume

## Updates and wins
- Reviewed a WinningHunter spreadsheet of top scaling brands, filtered by revenue, ad count and traffic trends
- Two-axis framework for product selection: (1) everyday product type, (2) target niche
- Key pattern across successful brands: take a proven product, reposition it for a specific buyer
- Examples reviewed (WinningHunter figures, not verified): IM8 (3,500 active ads, AG1 equivalent), Gruns, Tradesman Nutrition ($450K–800K/mo, fat loss and energy for tradesmen), blyss mop soap (~$2M/mo, female-skewed), Uproot (client; $6–11M/mo, 2.0 Trustpilot from ~1,900 reviews, so demand outpaces quality), HydroJug (visits up 75% in 6 months, female-skewed), Brick (phone-locking device, ~$40M/mo, owns its niche), WANDER+ (travel pillow, $1–2M/mo), pheromone diffuser for pets (refillable, so subscription possible)
- Patterns: same product new buyer; proven product renamed for a specific situation; older buyer (45+) underserved; hidden mechanism as the ad angle; refills and subscriptions first
- ADHD and sleep overlap flagged: ADHD commonly comes with sleep issues, and poor sleep worsens ADHD
- Pillows high-interest: ads everywhere, both have bought them, staying power. Angle: stress and energy rather than neck pain; Japanese sleep techniques as a branding reference
- Men's HydroJug: tactical or rugged bottle for gym bros or office tech workers
- Dumb phone as "the ADHD phone": exciting to Lav, instinct only, too complex to manufacture for launch 1
- Photo book subscription: dismissed (software, fulfilment, shipping)
- An 18-year-old founder in Lav's Discord ($300K/mo in supplements) offered a call

## Challenges and blockers
- No decision criteria yet, so picks default to gut feel
- Supplements: formulation, claims liability, legal exposure. Most operators ignore it and scale anyway, but it's a hesitation
- Subscription is hard to attach to one-off products like pillows
- Branding-heavy products (men's bottle, luxury soap) need custom packaging, inventory and capital, which conflicts with a lean first launch
- Complex fulfilment ideas need software and logistics
- Brick owns its niche; whether a knockoff could be repositioned for a sub-segment is unresolved
- Risk of using the same product-finding methods as everyone else

## Mutual feedback

- Lav: our edge is marketing craft, specifically creative strategy
- Joey: keep instinct, but put a ranking layer on top so it's repeatable
- Both: health and beauty top the revenue, but home and kitchen and pets are strong with less compliance risk
- Both: this stage is about dropshipping something and proving we can sell. Breaking even on product 1 is a win
- Lav: wants to feel at least a bit excited about it. Old-person-smell soap and washing machine tablets don't meet that bar
- Both: review each product manually first, then use AI to reposition after narrowing down

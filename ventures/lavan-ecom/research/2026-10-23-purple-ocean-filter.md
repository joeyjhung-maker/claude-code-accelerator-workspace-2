# Purple ocean filter

A fixed scorecard for narrowing the store list without arguing from gut feel. Built from Mark Builds Brands, "Zero to $1,000/day" (2026), sections 5 and 9, applied to the 80 stores on tab 1 of the daily.ecommerce sheet.

Related: [[2026-10-23-store-teardown]] [[research-playbook]] [[project]]

## Mark's rules, as gates

Pass all three or it's out. No exceptions, no "but I like it".

| Gate | Mark's rule | How we test it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Proven demand** | At least one competitor doing $300K+/month selling something similar. 300K+ visits trending up, or 100+ active Meta ads, or 30K+ monthly searches | Est. revenue ≥ $300K/mo, or visits ≥ 300K, or ≥ 100 active ads (from the sheet) |
| **2. Margin** | Selling price minus product cost incl. shipping ≥ $30, **and** price ≥ 3x that cost | Price ≥ $45 passes (at a typical ⅓ cost that leaves $30). $25–$44 passes **only with a bundle** that gets the order to $45+. Under $25 fails |
| **3. Purple ocean** | Proven, red-ish market, carved down to a **hyper-specific demographic** (Primal Queen: carnivore supplements for postpartum and perimenopausal women) | Score the carve-out on the 5 questions below |

## The purple ocean score (1 to 3 each, 15 max, 11+ to shortlist)

| | Question | 1 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Specific:** can you name them in 4 words, and would they call themselves that? | "busy people" | "night-shift nurses" |
| B | **Different problem:** does their version of the problem make the generic product a poor fit? | Same problem as everyone | A real reason the normal product doesn't work for them |
| C | **Reachable:** do they have an identity, community or life event Meta can find? | Invisible | Groups, pages, a clear life stage |
| D | **Unserved:** is nobody on our list (or obvious in the market) already talking to them? | Already someone's whole brand | Nobody |
| E | **Margin fit:** does the product clear gate 2 for them? | Bundle-only | $45+ single, or natural refill |

Score it on paper first, then talk. If two people score the same carve-out differently, the gap is the conversation, not the vibe.

### Why the top ones score high
- **GLP-1 users** (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro) are a huge, fast-growing, self-identifying group with side effects the generic product ignores: hair shedding and muscle loss are both widely reported. They gather in large Facebook groups and on Reddit. A hair vitamin or creatine "for GLP-1 users" is the Primal Queen move exactly.
- **CPAP users** need power through the night or their machine stops. A generic power bank doesn't say "runs your CPAP for 8 hours". Check unit cost first: a CPAP-capable battery costs far more to make than a phone power bank.
- **Rescue adopters** have a named, time-limited problem (the first weeks in a new home) and rescue groups to reach them through.
- **Night-shift nurses** tie the Trends finding (weighted sleep masks rising) to the shift-worker kit idea in the Notion list.

## What this doesn't prove yet
- Demand is proven at the **market** level, not for the carve-out. Before picking, check each top carve-out in the Meta Ad Library: is anyone already selling to them? If yes, how many ads?
- Margins assume product cost is about a third of price. Get real quotes for the finalist (see [[2026-10-23-supplier-trace]]).
- Scores A to E are judgement. The point is that both of you score the same way, then compare.

## How to use this every time
1. New store goes on the sheet.
2. Gate 1 and gate 2 fill themselves from the numbers.
3. If it passes, write 2 or 3 carve-outs for its market and score each A to E, separately, then compare.
4. Anything 11+ goes to the Ad Library check. Anything under 11 is parked, not argued about.

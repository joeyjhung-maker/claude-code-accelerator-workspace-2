# Organic Hooks

Organic moves faster than ad accounts. A hook that's blowing up this week proves one thing: people stop for it. It proves nothing about whether they buy. So this only ever supplies the *top* of an ad. The bottom always comes from something you already know converts. **Never run a viral hook into an unproven body.**

Five moves: **burner → prime → spot → pull → write.** Thirty minutes to set up, fifteen minutes a day after that.

## 1. Make the burner

A new Instagram account on a new email, on your phone. Neutral name, no bio, no posts, no photo. Don't link it to your main account, don't sync contacts, don't find friends. It never posts. One burner per market. It's a clean pair of eyes, nothing else.

## 2. Prime the feed

**Day one.** Search five to ten terms and open the Reels tab on each: the outcome in the market's words (*belly fat*, *can't sleep*, *low testosterone*), the product category, two or three competitor names, one or two adjacent identities (*dad bod*, *over 40*). Watch every relevant reel to the end. Like, save, follow. Twenty to thirty follows.

**Days two and three.** Scroll Reels. In your market: watch to the end, like, save. Not your market: swipe past instantly. The feed tightens in about three sessions.

**After that.** Fifteen minutes a day. Every few weeks, search two new terms so it doesn't go stale.

## 3. Spot the outliers

You're not collecting good reels. You're collecting reels that did far better than their creator normally does. **Views ÷ what that creator normally gets.** Open the creator's Reels grid; most of their views sit in a band. Five to ten times that band is an outlier; twenty times is a breakout. Save it.

Freshest first: a reel posted this week and already past the creator's normal is the one nobody has copied yet. A months-old reel still climbing has a durable structure. Likes and comments are not the signal. Views decide. And a 20× reel from a different market is a structure to steal, not a reel to throw away.

Five or more outliers a week means the feed works. Zero means it isn't primed yet, or try TikTok with the same method.

## 4. Pull the hooks, word for word

Every reel carries up to three hooks. Pull all three exactly as written or said. Don't clean them up.

1. **Spoken**: the first sentence out of their mouth.
2. **On-screen**: the text overlay in the first frame.
3. **Caption**: the first line before "more".

Then write the **pattern**: the hook with its swappable parts as blanks. *"I'm 43, I tried {thing} for {duration} — here's what happened."* That's what you'll reuse. Note the reel's URL and the multiple next to it. Do it the same day; the Saved tab is a shoebox.

## 5. Write: the weld

A viral hook is a proven opener. It is not an ad. The move is **proven hook on top, proven body underneath.** Take an ad you already know converts and freeze it. Then paste this into any AI:

```
The hook below already earned its audience on organic social. It is not a draft to improve.

HOOK, VERBATIM: <the hook>
ITS PATTERN: <the hook with blanks>
PROVEN BODY (frozen; this ad already converts): <the ad, minus its current opener>
WHO THE BODY IS FOR AND WHAT THEY ALREADY KNOW: <one line>

1. Run it verbatim. Keep the hook exactly as written; change only the subject word to what this brand sells. Add one to three bridge sentences into the frozen body. Body untouched. CTA as is.
2. Then ten more hooks that run the same PATTERN with different fills, each one the brand can honestly say, each one leading cleanly into the same body.
Say in one line whether the hook's awareness matches the body's. If it doesn't, stop.
```

Three rules: **don't improve the hook** (every edit past the product swap throws away the one thing you know works) · **match the awareness** (a cold-scroller hook belongs on a cold-traffic body, not an offer ad) · **lead with the hook type that won** (spoken, on-screen, or caption).

Launch the verbatim one first. If it wins, the body has a new proven opener and the hook now has conversion proof. Back to the feed.

# Timeline ads — reference

The doctrine behind `/timeline-ad`, in my own words. Source: the Genesis
"Constructing Timeline Ads" training and its 14-step build guide (links in
`SKILL.md`). The numbers come from that training's read of 296 timeline ads
from three supplement brands, plus 183 more from 17 other brands. Open the
originals for the worked examples and real ad lines.

## Why the container works (five elements)

| Element | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Earned ownership** | He rehearses using the product before he's asked for money: the taste, the routine, his real day, his own objection said for him |
| **Gradualization** | Claims start below his doubt and grow only as fast as his belief. The calendar is the excuse |
| **Built-in open loop** | Each checkpoint raises a question the next one answers |
| **Plausibility in place of proof** | A cause attached to each rung does the work a testimonial would |
| **Witness, not judge** | Everything inside the days happens *to* someone. The brand doesn't speak until the last day has landed |

## The claim ladder (eight rungs, in order)

| # | Rung | Typical timing | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Day 1 | Nothing felt, or invisible work under the surface |
| 2 | Sensation | 24 hours to week 2 | A private feeling, usually an absence |
| 3 | Behaviour | Days to week 3 | Something he stops or starts doing |
| 4 | Appearance | Week 2–3 | Visible to himself, in the mirror |
| 5 | Measurement | Week 2–4 | An object reports it, not a feeling |
| 6 | Social | Week 2–4 | Others notice. Spouse first, then kids, then coworkers |
| 7 | Identity | Final | Who he is again, in his own words |
| 8 | Life scene | Final | The outcome as a picture, with an optional number |

Rungs 2 and 3 often swap. Internal wins come first: the mirror arrives about
44% of the way through, social about 47%, identity and any number about 80%.
Most winning ads open Day 1 on "nothing happened" or on invisible work.

**Negative timeline:** the same ladder run downward. Harm from a habit grows
over years, and the product gets one short beat at the end.

## The ten positions

0. **Frame and tease.** Who, habit swapped, horizon. Then name the last stage as
   the prize.
1. **Day 1.** Ease, the mirror with nothing in it, his objection.
2. **The turn.** First signals, framed as things that stop.
3. **Sensation with a cause attached.**
4. **Appearance and measurement.** Hedged.
5. **Identity and the life scene.** Loosest clock in the ad.
6. **Bridge and differentiator.** The villain is the cheap version, not him.
7. **The seal.**
8. **Proof.** A count. Nothing more.
9. **Offer, guarantee, stock check.**

Timeline is about three quarters of the script. Milestones number 3 to 7, with
4 the most common. Dense early, sparse late.

## Clocks

| Clock | Use when |
|---|---|
| Hard days (1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 14) | Fast products. Feels like realism |
| Weeks | The default for a 30-day arc |
| Hours | A speed hook |
| Soft sequence (at first, then, eventually) | Numbers can't be defended |
| Ingredient stack | Multi-part products. Each part owns a result |
| Product consumed | Presupposes the purchase |
| Named stages | A day number would be an undefendable claim |
| Years | Negative timelines only |
| Within one day | Same-day effects |

**Horizon by claim size:** cravings, energy, calm: 7–14 days. Belly, skin,
joints, sleep: 30 days. Body composition or a relationship: 60 days. Harm:
years. Mixing clocks is fine. "About a week" is doing hedge work.

## Mechanisms

- **The villain (problem mechanism).** Named early (hook or Day 1). Explained
  late (first big visible rung, or the seal). Strongest when physical, active,
  and absolving.
- **The hero (ingredient or component).** Lives inside the rungs, one per rung,
  fastest first, same order every time. About three quarters of winners attach
  one to every stage.
- **Four weights for a "because":** name only (small early rungs); name plus
  job (most rungs); name plus picture (the rung you most need believed); the
  full version with science and day-to-day (only for avatars who want to feel
  smart).
- **Recap or not.** Presenter frames attach causes per rung and skip the recap.
  Person frames (diary) run the story first and recap after.

## Frames

| Frame | Reach for it when |
|---|---|
| Hypothetical ("what would happen if…") | No customer story. Deniable. The most common |
| Testimony | You want it to read as a report |
| Diary | Emotional payoffs. First person, present tense |
| Skeptic who tried it | Jaded markets. Each rung is a grudging concession |
| Yapper friend | Younger or male audiences who'd roll their eyes at a diary |
| Friend of a user | The buyer can purchase for someone else |
| False warning | The real want is something he'd never say out loud |
| Body-part narrator | The problem narrates its own retirement |
| Negative timeline | The cost of not acting is the argument |
| Wrong-product fork | A false solution gets the harm ladder first |
| Conditional ladder | Presenter style, no avatar, pure benefit stack |

The frame changes the opener and the pronouns. The rungs don't move.

## Beats

**The five that can't be skipped:** a cause at each rung, absence framing, the
witness, the skeptic beat or the invisible-work line, and the mirror or identity
line.

**Underused, worth trying:** a recognition line that names the normalised
decline; "coincidence, then pattern"; the connected-problems reveal at the seal;
the skeptic narrator; the wrong-product fork.

## The sales block (standard order)

1. Bridge, one line
2. Mechanism recap (only if the arc ran without causes)
3. Differentiator, pointing back at the spec planted in a rung
4. Quality marks, fast
5. Proof, as a count
6. Price anchor
7. Offer
8. Guarantee, made easy to picture
9. Stock check
10. Call to action

Proof is thin on purpose. The arc is the proof. If the sales block runs past a
quarter of the script, it is arguing with someone already convinced.

**The training's own warning:** hard numbers, fake-precise stock counts and
conspiracy lines are the weak spot in the library. An invented count that
appears in two ads tells the viewer it's invented. Copy the order. Fill it with
claims that can be defended.

## What breaks it

- The brand's voice inside the days
- A stalled clock (several sentences of argument at one checkpoint)
- A reason before the event it explains
- A big claim too early
- The mirror before the internal wins
- No cause at any rung, so it reads as a list of hopes with dates

## Porting to a coaching or info offer (my adaptation, untested)

The training's data is all consumables. This table is a guess at the mapping.
Treat the first non-supplement run as an experiment and log what happens.

| In the training | For a coaching or info offer |
|---|---|
| Ingredient | A component of the method, a tool, a script, a session |
| Taste or ease on Day 1 | How easy the first step is: ten minutes, one template, no tech |
| Nothing felt on Day 1 | No result yet, and he feels he wasted his money |
| Invisible work | What has been set in motion that he can't see yet |
| Sensation | Relief, clarity, less dread |
| Behaviour | What he stops doing: chasing, refreshing, discounting |
| Appearance | What he can see himself: the calendar, the inbox |
| Measurement | An object that reports it: a dashboard, a booked call, an invoice |
| Social | Who notices: a partner, a client, a peer |
| Identity and life scene | The same |

**Extra care here.** Income and time-to-result claims are the most scrutinised
claims there are. Every rung with a number or a timescale must come from the
client's real results. The hypothetical frame adds deniability, and the claim
still has to be one the client can stand behind.

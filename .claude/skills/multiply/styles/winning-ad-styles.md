# Winning Ad Styles — the list

A **style** is the filter the ad is rendered through. Same script, same claims, same offer, same structure, different picture: Pixar, camcorder, Notes app, courtroom sketch. Nothing the buyer has to believe changes when you swap it, which is why it's the cheapest swap there is. You are not writing a new ad. You are re-rendering a proven one.

The one-second test: could you re-render the exact same script in this and change nothing but the picture? Yes → style. No, the script has to change shape → that's a conceit (workflow 3). Entries marked *(both)* are honestly both.

**Three rules.** 1. Match the skin to the awareness: cold traffic wants native, ugly, low-fidelity (camcorder, Notes app, raw UGC); product-aware traffic can carry polish. 2. Style breaks are a tool: cutting from cartoon to real footage at the product reveal says "the story part is over, this part is real." 3. Some styles do the targeting for you: a reggae track, a country kitchen, an anime skin pick the audience before a word lands.

### A. Live-action, captured looks
The camera itself is the filter.

1. **Raw iPhone UGC** — handheld, front camera, bad lighting, no edit · the default native skin; unaware traffic, anything that needs to feel like a person not a brand.
2. **FaceTime / video call** — call UI framing, the little self-view window, slight lag · confessions, "I need to tell you something"; makes a monologue feel overheard. *(both)*
3. **Camcorder / VHS 90s** — 4:3, tracking lines, date stamp in the corner · nostalgia buyers, "before" footage, anything about time passing.
4. **Security / CCTV** — high angle, timestamp, grain, black and white · "caught on camera" moments, demonstrations, humor.
5. **Bodycam / GoPro POV** — fisheye, chest height, motion · in-use product demos, "a day with," anything physical.
6. **Dashcam** — windshield frame, road, timestamp · driving monologues, commuter segments, "on my way to."
7. **Ring-light beauty** — flat bright light, bathroom mirror, close crop · skincare, supplements, anything with a face-based before/after.
8. **Webcam low-fi** — laptop-height angle, room behind, compression artifacts · founder honesty, "I'm not a marketer" energy.
9. **Documentary handheld** — natural light, slow pans, subject not looking at camera · authority messengers, origin stories, farms and labs.
10. **Stock b-roll montage** — faceless polished stock clips (kitchens, walks, ingredients) with one-word karaoke captions, anatomy inserts between · the cheapest video that exists; carries a VO script with no talent at all.
11. **Cinematic / anamorphic** — widescreen bars, shallow focus, lens flare, graded · product-aware and brand traffic, premium positioning.
12. **Glossy commercial / studio founder** — bright even light, shallow focus, crisp 4K, a clean neutral room · founder and expert presenters; the "trustworthy insider" look that gets intercut with raw UGC inserts.
13. **Black-and-white flashback** — the past is desaturated, the present is in color · one filter that separates "before" from "after" without a word; failed-treatment montages.
14. **Film noir** — black and white, hard shadows, venetian-blind light · detective-style investigations of a problem, "the case of."
15. **Super 8 home movie** — soft grain, warm, flicker, silent with music · family, memory, "what you'll miss."
16. **Archival meme-clip cut-in** — grainy 70s and 80s film or TV clips (boxers, action heroes) cut into modern footage as reactions · comedy timing and nostalgia without shooting anything.
17. **Night vision** — green, grainy, IR glow · sleep products, "what happens while you sleep," night sweats.
18. **Thermal camera** — heat map colors on a body or object · inflammation, circulation, cooling products, anything invisible made visible.
19. **Microscope slide footage** — real microscope video: worms, eggs, cell debris, with circle callouts and keyword captions · parasites, gut, skin; the gross-out is real, which beats a render.
20. **Slow-motion macro** — extreme close-up, 240fps, texture · food, liquids, skin, ingredients dissolving.
21. **Overhead gloved bench demo** — top-down camera, dark bench, blue nitrile gloves testing the product on a prop · lab credibility for a consumer product; teeth, skin, stains.
22. **Timelapse** — hours in seconds, clouds moving, plants growing · transformations, "30 days in 30 seconds," routines.
23. **Hidden camera** — obscured, angled, subjects unaware · social experiments, reactions, "we asked strangers." *(both)*
24. **Photobooth strip** — four vertical frames, flash, white border · before/after in one image, timelines, couples.
25. **Mirror selfie** — phone visible, bathroom or gym, arm's length · body transformations, fitness, outfit and physique.
26. **Drone / aerial** — top-down, sweeping · scale, land, "we grow this here," origin and sourcing.
27. **Screen-recorded Zoom** — gallery view, mute icons, recording dot · leaked calls, "internal meeting," team reveals. *(both)*
28. **Podcast studio** — big microphone in frame, two-camera setup, warm studio light · authority without a lab coat; the most native-feeling place to say something controversial. *(both)*
29. **Conference stage clip** — a speaker on a lit stage, audience silhouettes, phone-filmed from the crowd · borrowed authority, "he said this on stage," often under a reactor.
30. **As-seen-on broadcast clip** — a real daytime-TV segment in a box with a persistent "Featured On" badge bar under it · borrowed mainstream trust; the bar does the work even when the clip is short.
31. **80s local news broadcast** — VHS grain, color bleed, station ident with light flares, chyrons, vox-pop street interviews · nostalgic authority plus humor; makes a kitchen-cupboard ingredient feel like a news story. *(both)*
32. **Reality-TV confessional** — subject alone on a stool, lower-third name card, slightly too-bright · testimonials that need drama, "at first I thought." *(both)*
33. **Sports broadcast** — scoreboard overlay, replay wipes, commentator VO · competitions, comparisons, "the matchup." *(both)*
34. **Green-screen react** — creator in a corner over a stat, article, or clip · commentary on proof, "look at this study." *(both)*
35. **Reactor over clip (top/bottom stack)** — a real person in the bottom half reacting to a clip in the top half · a peer vouches for the clip; the hook lives in the reactor, the pitch lives in the clip.
36. **Duet side-by-side split** — two vertical panels side by side, the TikTok duet layout: a clip on one side, a person reacting or demonstrating on the other · same vouching move as the stack, more face on screen.
37. **AI-dubbed lookalike** — real broadcast footage of a public figure, AI voice replaced to say the script · a known winner runs on it; legally and platform-risky, so it's listed, not recommended.
38. **AI avatar presenter** — a photoreal generated nurse or doctor on a rendered set, speaking to camera, later composited over b-roll · an authority messenger you don't have to cast; watch the uncanny valley.

### B. 3D and rendered
Everything is built, nothing is filmed.

39. **Pixar / Dreamworks 3D** — big eyes, soft lighting, expressive characters · story-led VSLs, sensitive topics people won't watch a real face say.
40. **AI spectacle costume** — a Pixar-grade character in an absurd era or outfit (a mummy in ancient Egypt, a googly-eyed skeleton, a knight, an astronaut) while the script plays it completely straight · the costume is the pattern interrupt; the testimonial underneath doesn't change. A proven winner shape.
41. **Hyperreal AI animal-people** — photoreal cats or lions with human bodies, human clothes, human kitchens, filmed like a prestige drama · lets a sensitive domestic story play without a real face; huge stop rate.
42. **Dark fantasy creature** — the tired man rendered as a monster, zombie, or stone golem in a video-game world · the metaphor is the visual; transformation arcs, energy and vitality products.
43. **Blue-skin / alien-tinted humans** — normal people, normal scenes, skin rendered blue or otherwise off-world · one dial turn on a live-action look that makes the frame impossible to scroll past.
44. **Claymation** — fingerprints in clay, stop-motion jitter · gut, digestion, anything squishy; disarms gross subjects.
45. **Wooden mannequin stop-motion** — jointed artist mannequins acting the story, warm wood tones, handcrafted feel · anything about fabric, materials, the body as an object.
46. **LEGO / brick** — minifigs, studs, stop-motion · assembly and mechanism explanations, "how it works," dad humor.
47. **Muppet / felt puppet** — fabric, googly eyes, hand-operated · adult topics said by something harmless; absurd contrast.
48. **Action figure / toy diorama** — plastic figures on a set, packaging visible · founder as a toy, "collect all three," ironic premium.
49. **Isometric miniature / tilt-shift** — tiny world, everything looks like a model · systems, routines, "your day from above."
50. **Video-game cutscene** — Unreal-engine look, HUD, health bars · energy, testosterone, performance; gamer segments.
51. **Low-poly PS1** — blocky, jittery textures, 1998 · nostalgia, irony, "we're not a real brand."
52. **Low-poly cel-shaded (modern casual game)** — clean gradients, sharp geometry, bright sunlit palette, bouncing text · energy and focus products; younger and gamer segments without the irony of PS1.
53. **The Sims** — plumbob, needs bars, pictographic speech · routines and needs, "your hunger bar," relationship humor.
54. **Hyper-real product CGI** — floating product, exploding ingredients, macro water · product-aware and retargeting, ingredient stories.
55. **Hyperreal AI cinematic** — photoreal AI-generated scenes with slight morphing, glossy light, impossible camera moves · the default substrate for acted-story VSLs now; cheap, fast, slightly uncanny in a way that holds.
56. **Hyperreal industrial / dystopian factory** — smoking pipes, bubbling vats, analog gauges, grocery shelves · "how it's really made" exposés; the villain's factory tour.
57. **AI historical reenactment** — photoreal village, market, or era scenes that never had a camera · provenance beats: the herbalist in Ghana, the 1937 hearing, the ancestral kitchen.
58. **3D anatomy** — medical model, transparent body, highlighted organ · mechanism explanations, "here's what's actually happening inside."
59. **Gross-out anatomy** — intestines clogged with sludge, wriggling parasites, glowing biofilm · the insert track under half the supplement winners in the library; shock plus mechanism in one shot.
60. **Glass skeleton / see-through body** — a translucent glossy figure with glowing organs inside, day-counter on screen · timeline ads where the change is internal.
61. **Metallic specimen render** — a chrome head or body part on a dark grid, glowing deposits, UI crosshairs and rotation arrows · "this is a 3D render of the tissue under your eyes"; reads as clinical, no spokesperson needed.
62. **Wax figure / uncanny** — slightly wrong human, museum lighting · fake vs real comparisons, "the imitation."
63. **Architectural render** — clean white model, blueprint edges · systems and plans, "the framework."

## How to use this tonight

1. Take a winner, or anything with real signal. Freeze the script.
2. Pick five styles from the list your account has never run.
3. Brief each one: same words, same order, same offer; only the rendering changes. Note where a style break belongs, if anywhere.
4. Render and launch. Every one is one swap from proven, so you know what the test measures.
5. A style wins → it's the next winner in; pick five more.

Public examples of most styles, longest-running first, are in `style-references.md`.

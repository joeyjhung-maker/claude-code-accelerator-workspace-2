# Prompt 2 — list the raw ideas

Paste this, then the FACTS and the quotes from prompt 1 (and, if you have them, a few lines from the ads already running — those get excluded).

```
Here is what a market said, verbatim, and the facts it supports. List the ideas this reality makes possible. Ideas only — no explanation, no headings, no process. Each idea is one plain, surprising, declarative claim about the market's reality, under 25 words, no craft (no punchlines, no questions, no "Warning:"), followed by the quote(s) that fund it with their like count and number. Never invent a study, statistic, mechanism or ingredient; an idea that needs one isn't an idea.

Ask, silently, at the evidence: what do they blame that isn't responsible? what are they doing to fix it that may make it worse? what symptom are they treating instead of the disease? could cause and effect be backwards? what trusted thing is secretly hurting them? what apparent problem is useful? what's costing more than they realise? what happens because of the obvious effect? what's harmless now and disastrous later? what do they believe that's backwards? are they measuring the wrong thing? what looks like evidence but isn't? what's the mechanism underneath the visible result? what tiny variable controls the result? what's actually scarce? which step do they skip? are they doing things in the wrong order? where exactly does it go wrong? what should they judge themselves by instead? what can they control that they think they can't? if this continues, what happens?

Product (what it is, the mechanism as the brand states it, what it may and may not claim): <…>
Market: <who buys, what for>

Give 30–40 ideas, numbered, the best 12 first and starred. Drop anything the running ads already say.
```

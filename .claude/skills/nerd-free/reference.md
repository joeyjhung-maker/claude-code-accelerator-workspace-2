# /nerd-free — reference

The ideas behind the skill, in my own words. Source: Mario Castelli's Genesis
call of 31 August 2026 and the three resources that ship with it (slides, a
diagnosis workbook, a swipe file). Links are in `SKILL.md`. Open the originals
for the worked examples and the real ads.

## The core claim

- An ad has two layers. The **idea** is the proposition the reader newly
  considers. The **intensification** is everything that makes it hit harder.
- Most writers only work the second layer, so their hooks get louder without
  getting better.
- A strong idea barely needs good writing. Picking it is most of the job.
- The two layers are independent. The workbook chose 36 ads purely on idea
  quality, then tested their sharpest lines for vagueness. 30 of 36 failed.
  Strong ideas ship inside weak lines every day and still run.

## Why AI writes weak hooks by default

It thinks in a straight line and stays literal. Good copywriters work in the
space between the literal fact and the compelling idea, without lying. Giving
the model this process, and permission to work in that space, is the fix.

## The twelve idea types (from the workbook)

| Type | The move |
|---|---|
| **Valence flip** | Something filed as good turns out bad, or the reverse. The stronger the prior belief, the bigger the jolt |
| **Traitor** | The trusted thing does its job until something changes, then does the opposite |
| **Broken model** | They did what was supposed to solve it. It happened anyway |
| **Hidden culprit** | The real cause is something nobody has put in the frame. Same symptom, new accused |
| **Evidence reframe** | Something they see every day and have explained away means something else |
| **Category enemy** | Every product that failed them failed for one shared reason |
| **Causal reversal** | They have the arrow backwards. The supposed cause is the consequence |
| **Outcome paradox** | The better they complied, the worse it got |
| **Identity reframe** | What they read as a character flaw is a mechanism. Absolution and an enemy in one move |
| **Distant association** | Two things the market has never put side by side |
| **Permission slip** | Side with them against the friction they hate |
| **Self-biology twist** | Something reasonable they do every day feeds the problem. Surprise without blame |

**The failure pile.** Ads with no idea at all: a product description with
intensifiers bolted on. Learn to spot it in your own drafts.

## Grading an idea (the workbook's seven dimensions)

Surprise · human consequence · prediction error · specificity potential ·
associative distance · explanation debt · defensibility.

## Intensifiers (the second layer)

Specificity · contrast · scene · authority · identity consequence · absolution ·
enemy · taboo · visceral verb · exact time · implied agency · open loop · named
phenomenon · coined phrase · social proof · urgency.

These go on *after* the idea is chosen.

## Vague-line detectors

The workbook scores each line against ten detectors. The ones that show up in
its answer key:

1. The sentence opens on a bare pronoun.
2. A placeholder noun.
3. A bare "the [abstraction]" doing a noun's job.
4. The template test: no concrete noun, so the sentence's shape could be moved
   to any product.

## Idea families from one advertiser (the swipe file)

Mario scraped about 9,000 long-form ads from one operation and reduced them to
934 ideas in roughly 22 families. The ones he walks through:

- **The managed-number lie.** The doctor says the number is fine while damage
  carries on underneath. About a fifth of that advertiser's spend, re-skinned
  across blood pressure, cholesterol, kidneys, nerves, joints and blood sugar.
- **Starving, not broken.** Something believed to be worn out is only short of
  a resource. That makes it fixable.
- **The cure is the decline.** The trusted treatment causes the deterioration
  everyone blames on age.
- **The perfect-patient paradox.** Did everything the doctor said. Got the bad
  outcome anyway.
- **Testable evidence.** Something visible every day, read as proof.

## Angles, and how to test them

An angle is **an accepted cause plus a consequence the market badly wants to
avoid**.

1. Test baseline ideas for signal.
2. When one lands, run the same idea through different consequences.
3. When a consequence lands, hold it and find other causes that lead to it.

A side effect of the thing people already take is a way into a segment. People
on one treatment who suffer a known side effect become a door to the
replacement.

## Unaware story hooks

- Connect a mundane commercial truth to a human event with far more emotional
  charge.
- The product doesn't have to cause the event. It can be exposed by it,
  discovered because of it, or relevant to how bad it was.
- **The opening shows A and Z and hides B through Y.** Show the strange event.
  Show the outcome the market cares about. Hold back how one led to the other.
- Keep the product out of the opening. Earlier drafts gave it away too soon.
- Which version to use depends on awareness. If they already know they need
  the product type, name it. If they only know the problem, leave it open.

## The one question

If this boring fact is true, what becomes possible that would anger, scare,
embarrass, excite, shock, inconvenience or fascinate the customer? And what
would that look like in their actual life?

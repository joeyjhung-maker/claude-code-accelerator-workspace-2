# Workflow: loose mariobot briefs beat constraint-stacks

Related: [[Promotion]] [[copy-rubric]] [[always-use-mariobot-to-write]]

## The lesson
A heavily-constrained mariobot instruction — a growing list of "do NOT include X," "avoid Y,"
"hard constraint: N words," stacked up over a session — produces technically-compliant but flat,
forgettable copy. A short, open brief closer to a plain facts-dump (what you'd actually type to
brief a person) gets measurably more vivid, funnier, more sellable lines out of the *same* model
on the *same* facts. Confirmed twice in one session, same underlying source material both times:

1. **LinkedIn automation tool teaser.** A long, bulleted, multi-constraint brief produced safe,
   spec-sheet-flat copy. Re-briefed with something close to how Joey actually prompts ChatGPT —
   short paragraph, real facts, audience context, a handful of plain "don't"s — and the result was
   sharper and more natural, even though it still didn't fully match a competing model's output on
   raw comedic swing (see the open question below).
2. **Two waitlist-push emails** (Yeti-only UK launch angle, then the faster/easier/safer angle) —
   both briefed loosely from the start this time, both landed well on the first pass with only a
   real accuracy fix needed, not a voice rewrite.

## The corollary: judge accuracy and voice as two separate passes
A hyperbolic illustrative image ("drop-kicked your account into the sun," "like doing your own
dentistry") is not the same category of risk as an invented business fact (a fake proof number, a
feature that isn't built yet, a price that was never confirmed). Treating both as "unverified
content to flag" quietly sands the jokes off every draft — the vivid, absurd, memorable lines are
exactly the ones that read as risky to an over-literal accuracy check. Keep flagging invented
facts about the business hard. Stop flagging bold hypothetical/comedic exaggeration as if it were
the same thing.

## Also fold into every accuracy pass: continuity, not just single-post correctness
A post can be internally accurate and still be wrong if it contradicts the *current state* of an
ongoing campaign — e.g. a draft describing a waitlist as "not open yet, DM me for first dibs" when
the same waitlist has already shipped with 30+ real signups. Check new copy against what's already
gone out in the same sequence, not just against the raw facts handed to this draft.

## How to brief loose, in practice
- Give the real facts as a plain paragraph or bullet dump, not a rules document.
- State the audience/avatar in one line (who's reading this, what they want) — this alone changes
  tone more than most structural rules do.
- Keep hard "don't"s to the few that actually matter for this piece (unconfirmed price, planned-
  not-live features, named competitors) rather than restating every rule already living in
  `mariobot-style-contract.md`.
- Judge the output afterward for real accuracy problems and genuine AI-tells (antithesis-flips,
  fragment-stacks) — but leave bold, vivid, hyperbolic lines alone unless they cross into a
  specific claim about the business.

## Open question, not resolved
Even the loosened mariobot briefs didn't fully match a competing model's raw comedic instinct on
this same task (a "ChatGPT 5.6 SOL" draft, prompted directly by Joey with the same facts, still
had the funnier headline and the more vivid image). Worth testing on more tasks before concluding
whether that's a prompting gap that closes with more looseness, or a genuine model-capability gap
for this specific "hype/humor-driven product tease" genre that a better brief alone won't fix.

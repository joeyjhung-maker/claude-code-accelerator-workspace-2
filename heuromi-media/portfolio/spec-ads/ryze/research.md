## The short version

- RYZE is the category leader. Everyone in the market has already seen the ads.
- So the job is not "what is mushroom coffee". The job is the fence-sitter who has scrolled past RYZE fifty times.
- Buyers do not talk about mushrooms. They talk about **taste, energy, and bloating**. In that order.
- The #1 reason people hate it is also taste. 65% of 1-star reviews.
- RYZE had to drop its hard health claims in September 2025. Spec ads that sell without those claims are the portfolio flex.

## 1. The offer (live on site, 2026-10-07)

Sale name: **Early Halloween Sale**. Countdown timer on the page.
Homepage banner: "40% Off Bundles + Gifts worth $40".

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single Bag | 30 servings | $27 | $45 | $0.90 | $36.00 |
| Starter Kit | 2 bags, 60 servings | $49.50 | $80 | $0.82 | $66.00 |
| Ritual Set ("best deal") | 2 bags + 2 vanilla creamers | $57.60 | $115 | $0.82 | $80.01 |

**Free gifts**
- Starter Kit: Mush Magnet ($5), Spoon & Scoop ($10), free shipping. Pick 2 more: frother, pumpkin spice creamer, tumbler.
- Ritual Set: pumpkin spice creamer ($14), Mush Magnet ($5), Spoon & Scoop ($10), acacia jar ($25), free shipping. Pick 2 more: frother, vanilla latte sticks, tumbler.

**Terms**
- Free US shipping. US only.
- "Adjust shipments or cancel anytime."
- Every bundle on the page is a subscription. The refill price is higher than the first order.
- "30-Day Money Back Guarantee. If you don't love it, you get your money back. No questions asked."

**The guarantee fine print (matters for copy)**
- The "Love-It Guarantee" on an opened bag covers the **first subscription order only**, and **one** opened bag.
- One-time purchases must be returned unopened and sealed.
- You have to message them within 30 days and mail the bag back with their label.
- The most-upvoted 1-star review on the site (288 votes) is about exactly this.
- **Rule for our copy:** say "30-day money-back guarantee". Do not write "return the empty bag" or "no questions asked on any order".

Notes on reading this:
- These are keyword counts on short reviews (average 157 characters). Good for ranking, not exact.
- Almost nobody names a mushroom. They buy a feeling, not an ingredient.
- Ages people stated: 20, 20, 29, 30, 30, 41, 42, 50, 52, 60, 65, 69, 72, 76, 80. Only 15 people, so treat it as a hint. It leans 40+.
- Reviewer names lean heavily female. **My read, not a stat.**

### The people I can see in the reviews

**A. The coffee lover whose coffee turned on her**
Decades of coffee. Loves the ritual. Now gets the stomach, the jitters, the 3pm crash.
She does not want to quit coffee. She wants to keep the cup.
> "I have a been a coffee addict for decades… what followed was a slightly upset stomach and jitters. I would wake up the next day and do it again"
> "It's almost like a hug I can drink."
> "looking for something gentler than regular coffee"

**B. Bloated at 45–55**
Eats well, moves, still puffy by the end of the day. Perimenopause is in the background.
> "my shirts are no longer tight making me look pregnant at 50!"
> "By the end of the day I felt like a puff ball."
> "my stomach bloating is gone"

**C. Running on fumes**
Mums, carers, shift workers. Two coffees then a Red Bull at 3pm.
> "I was surviving off fumes."
> "I have energy after 12 hour shifts"
> "Id usually have a cup or two of coffee in the morning, and a redbull by 3pm"

**D. The sceptic who finally gave in**
Has seen the ads for months. Assumed it was hype. Bought anyway.
> "FINALLY, I gave in to the Ryze ads"
> "Surprised! I thought it was BS! Happy to be wrong!"
> "I was doing what you're probably doing right now… reading through the reviews."

**E. The adder, not the switcher**
Does not replace coffee at all. Stirs a scoop into the coffee they already make.
> "I haven't had to give up my usual coffee routine. I still drink my coffee with my typical creamer and syrup"
> "I use Ryze in my coffee daily."

**F. The 60+ health keeper**
Older, managing aches and energy. Talks about joints and inflammation.
High claims risk. Park this one.

In their words:
- "It tastes like bad instant coffee prepared in a cup that was just used for broth."
- "I'm sure it's very good for you, because it tastes awful."
- "no jolt whatsoever in the morning, i swear there is no caffine"
- "The ad… said that it's going to bring a lot of energy and health and so many other great things, but… I have not noticed any changes"
- "They make some pretty extraordinary claims about this stuff"
- "I can buy a bag of good normal for less than $.20 per cup"
- "They wouldn't honor their 30 day money back 'guarantee' because im not a subscriber"

**What this means for the ads**
- Taste is the fight. It is the top reason to love it and the top reason to bin it.
- The honest version already exists in the reviews: odd for two days, hooked by day three, creamer helps.
- "Felt nothing" pairs with "2–3 weeks". Nobody is told to wait, so they quit in week one.
- Outside critics make the same point. A dietitian liked it for a week and still would not keep buying: price, and claims she called unproven.
- One reviewer site notes the studies used 5–10g of a single mushroom. RYZE is 2g across six, split not disclosed.

## 7. What we must not say

**September 2025:** the BBB's National Advertising Division looked at RYZE's ads.
RYZE permanently dropped these as direct claims:
> "all-day energy, sharper focus, healthier digestion, better immune support" and "better sleep"

They also dropped matcha ads that hinted at GLP-1-style appetite effects.
The site now says "supports" on everything.

**Lawsuit:** Younger et al. v. Ryze Superfoods, Central District of California. Listed on Truth in Advertising as a class action about subscription terms and cancelling.
Another source describes it as being about mushroom ingredient claims.
I did not read the court filing. **UNVERIFIED** on which it is.

**Correction, 2026-10-07 (later the same day):** the rules below were tighter than RYZE's own live ads, and they stripped the benefits out of the first six drafts of the timeline script. RYZE's active library still talks about fog, bloating, the 3pm crash and stress. It words each one as "helps support [job], so [everyday moment] happens less". So the working rule is: **hedge the wording, keep the content.** Still out: the five dropped phrases word for word, disease and medical words, weight loss, any number on a result, "send back the empty bag".

**Rules for the spec ads** (original, see correction above)
- "Supports", never "gives", "fixes", "boosts", "cures".
- No disease or condition words. No blood sugar, cholesterol, inflammation, prostate, kidney, weight loss.
- No numbers on a result. Numbers only on the offer and the proof.
- Customer feelings are fine as customer feelings. Do not turn a review into our claim.
- Do not invent a testimonial. Only quotes from `reviews-raw.json`.
- Most of the RYZE swipes already in this workspace (red flags, blood sugar, three signs) are the style that drew the scrutiny. Good to study. Wrong to copy for a 2026 spec.

## 9. Lanes worth writing into (ideas, not copy)

These are directions for the bot. The hooks get written and stress-tested later.

1. **Keep the cup.** For the coffee lover. You are not being asked to give anything up. (Persona A)
2. **The scoop, not the swap.** Stir it into the coffee you already make. Almost nobody is running this. (Persona E)
3. **Day one tastes odd. Day three you're hooked.** Lead with the thing every hater says, and own it. (Objection 1)
4. **"I finally gave in to the ads."** Written to the person who has scrolled past fifty times. (Persona D)
5. **The 3pm Red Bull.** The second caffeine hit of the day is the thing that goes. (Persona C)
6. **One review every 21 minutes.** 67 a day on a single product. Pure proof, no health claim. (Proof)
7. **Half the caffeine, and you still get your afternoon cup.** Permission, not restriction. (Product fact: ~48mg)
8. **Give it 21 days.** Sets the expectation nobody sets. Kills "felt nothing". (Objection 2)

Lanes 2, 3 and 8 are the ones I'd back for a portfolio.
They come straight out of the data and they don't need a single health claim.

## 0. Before signing: screen the client

Five questions. A bad answer caps what the ads can earn.

- [ ] **Pipeline speed.** How many days from brief to live? Good is 3–5. Bad is 2–3 weeks.
- [ ] **Backlog.** How many ideas from last month are still waiting to be made?
- [ ] **Admin load.** Who manages editors and UGC creators? If the answer is "you", that's 15 hours a week gone.
- [ ] **Data access.** Will they share the ad account and new-customer numbers?
- [ ] **Back end.** Is the product pleasant to keep using, and do they know their LTV?

Then the deal.

- [ ] Base that lets me focus (Kam's range for a good strategist: $5–8k a month).
- [ ] A percentage on my own ads that hit agreed numbers.
- [ ] Agree the tiers in writing. Kam's example: ~2% once an ad holds its numbers to $1k spend, 4–5% once it holds to $10k.
- [ ] Agree what "net new" and "iteration" mean before agreeing a ratio.

## 1. Day one: what to ask for

- [ ] Ad account access. Triple Whale (or whatever tracks new customers) too.
- [ ] **The NC-CPA target, by platform and by format.** Video and native will have different numbers.
- [ ] The ads the team calls winners, and why.
- [ ] The ads that brought the customers who stayed longest.
- [ ] Which avatars they've been running.
- [ ] Current split: net new vs iteration, video vs static.
- [ ] Account-wide new-visitor percentage against their target.
- [ ] Who edits, who handles creators, and the real turnaround.

**If they won't share data:** build around the ads they keep calling winners. Offer middle- and bottom-of-funnel iteration without data. Say top of funnel needs the numbers.

**If they ask for everything by Friday:** say yes, and say what moves to next week. Let them pick.

**Read the client too.** This sets how much risk the first batch can carry.

- [ ] Are they hungry for winners, or for fresh ideas?
- [ ] How patient are they? Listen to how they talk about the last person's misses.
- [ ] Am I replacing someone, their first outside hire, or one of several being tested?
- [ ] Do they understand testing, or will I need to teach as I go?

## 2. First two hours in the account: read it

Sort by all-time spend. Read the top 15–30 ads. Answer four questions and write the answers down.

- [ ] **Avatar.** Which one has the best NC-CPA? Which one takes the most spend? If they're different, that gap is the opportunity.
- [ ] **Format.** Which structures work at top of funnel? Which only at the middle?
- [ ] **Hook structure.** Hook stack, condition call-out, symptom call-out, or a new problem?
- [ ] **Body.** What's the evergreen body and mechanism? Reuse it. Don't rewrite it.

Then two more passes.

- [ ] **The emotion under the winners.** Is it vindication, shame, fear? Most winners in an account share one.
- [ ] **Time windows.** Top 5–7 spenders at 7, 14, 30, 60 and 90 days. Recent shows what works now. Old shows what holds.

**Tag each winner's genome.** This is the frame for the whole read. See [[Ad Genome]].

Take the top five winners. One line per layer, bottom up.

| Layer | Ask |
|---|---|
| 1. Segment + sub-segment | Who is it for? Outcome, person, and why they're here |
| 2. Awareness | What do they already know? |
| 3. Concept, angle, format | What's the claim, the frame and the skeleton? |
| 4. Conceit | What is it pretending to be? |
| 5. Actor, style, look | Who's on screen and what does it look like? |
| 6. Copy beats | What's the hook, and what are the beats? |

- [ ] Line the winners up side by side. A value that repeats across three is a pattern.
- [ ] A layer where every winner has the same value is a gap everywhere else.
- [ ] For each winner, name the one layer doing the heavy lifting.
- [ ] Lay the client's segments out as a tree: brand → outcomes → sub-outcomes → who and why → the one-second line. The diagram to copy is in [[Ad Genome]] (diagram 5).

**Then write down patterns and gaps.**

- [ ] **Patterns.** One plain sentence each. Three winners sharing a trait is a pattern. One is an anecdote.
- [ ] **Gaps.** Walk the six layers and look for empty slots. The usual three: segments nobody's talking to, formats nobody's using, awareness levels nobody's writing for. Unaware is usually empty.
- [ ] **Weigh each gap.** How big is it, how big is the prize, is it timely, and does it connect to anything proven?

## 4. The first ten concepts

**First, set the size and the dial.**

- [ ] Make more than they ordered. Hired for 10, make 13.
- [ ] About three ideas for every ad. Split them: spear (hunting a named gap or pattern) and net (best idea, open search).
- [ ] Set the mix from the client read:

| Client | Bankers | Bridges | Bets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impatient, burned before | 8 | 3 | 2 |
| Not sure yet | 6–7 | 4 | 2–3 |
| Patient, wants creativity | 5 | 4 | 4 |

- [ ] Bankers: format swap on the top spender, segment transplant, competitor swipe.
- [ ] Bridges: concept or angle remix, organic swipe, bottom of funnel, internal vectors.
- [ ] Bets: true net new, usually an unaware ad aimed at the biggest gap.
- [ ] Put the Bets in the extra ads they didn't pay for.

**Step 1. Lock in early wins with category buyers.**

- [ ] 2–4 concepts aimed at people already buying the category.
- [ ] The "better way" ad: you already use X, here's a faster or safer way.
- [ ] The branded "us vs them" format fits here.

**Step 2. Then go broad, on proven structures.**

- [ ] Use the five universal formats: red flag / three signs, timeline, transformation, lifestyle, us vs them.
- [ ] Vary the avatar, not the wording of the hook.
- [ ] Stack 3–4 real symptoms in the call-out. Only ones this avatar has.
- [ ] Recombine parts the account has already proven separately.

**The split.**

- [ ] About 80% on something proven. About 20% genuine risk.
- [ ] If other brands already run it, it's a test with evidence, not a wild card.
- [ ] Trendy formats (claymation, AI talking head, sliding text) sit inside the 20%.
- [ ] Mostly video. Natives are penny stocks.

**Label every concept** before it goes to the client: Banker, Bridge or Bet, and which move.

- [ ] **One written hypothesis per ad.** What it is, why it should work, what a miss would teach.
- [ ] Tag each ad with the genome layer it changes. One layer per ad. Top layers (style, hook) are cheap. Bottom layers (segment, awareness) are the big bets.

## 5. The brief

- [ ] Hook carries proof, a mechanism tease, the promise, curiosity and tension inside 5–15 seconds.
- [ ] The USP lands before most viewers have left.
- [ ] One line per element telling the editor what it looks like on screen.
- [ ] Supply the assets: the study screenshot, the clip, the reference ad.
- [ ] Read the script out loud in character before sending it.
- [ ] Any time frame ("four weeks") is true for this product.

Wiki: `front-loaded-hooks-and-the-qualified-click`, `ad-body-as-belief-bridge`, `front-loading-proof-and-demonstration`

## 6. Weeks one to four

- [ ] **Send the story with the batch.** What I found, the patterns, the top three gaps, how the batch is split and why. Say some ads are built to win fast and some to win big or miss.
- [ ] **Launch the Bankers first**, so a win shows up while the Bets are still learning.
- [ ] Send short updates without being asked. Never go quiet long enough for them to wonder.
- [ ] Ship the first batch fast. An early win settles the client's nerves about hiring.
- [ ] Judge top-of-funnel ads on NC-CPA, against the benchmark for their format.
- [ ] Tag winners level 1 and level 2 so the media buyer knows what to scale.
- [ ] After a hit, work the cells next to it: same format on other avatars, then a second format.
- [ ] At week four, ask which ads brought customers who reordered. A cheap customer who leaves is not a win.
- [ ] Bring one proven structure from outside the account to each weekly call.

**Reading the results for batch two.**

| What hit | What it means | Batch two |
|---|---|---|
| A format swap | Messages fine, creative worn out | Run every proven message through the format gaps |
| A segment transplant | New segment validated | Treat it as a mini-account |
| Remixes, not swaps | Messaging has headroom | More Bridges |
| A Bet | New territory, lots of trust | Go a notch more aggressive. Iterate on the new winner |
| Nothing | It happens | Lead with what each miss showed and what changes |

## If they ask "what's your testing process?"

It comes up in interviews and on sales calls. The answer is this checklist in thirty seconds:

1. Ask what they're optimising for, their split, and where new-customer cost sits.
2. "I read every ad as six layers, tag the winners, and find the patterns and gaps."
3. "I test one layer at a time. Cheap top layers first, big bottom layers next."
4. "Every ad ships with a hypothesis, so a miss still teaches us something."

The full version, and how to dissect an ad out loud, is in [[Ad Genome]].

## Open points for Joey

1. **Kam's order vs NHB.** Kam starts with category buyers. NHB says scale lives with the unaware majority. The wiki treats Kam's as a starting sequence. Does that match how you'd want to open an account?
2. **The split.** Kam says 80% net new. Volkwyn and Ioannis say 80% iteration. Genesis doesn't fix a ratio. It sets a Banker / Bridge / Bet dial from the client read. Step 4 now leads with the Genesis dial and keeps "80% proven, 20% risk" as the rough guide. Tell me if you'd rather it be one or the other.
3. **Proof first.** Kevin Just puts proof in the hook. The Script says never open with student proof. Step 5 follows Kevin for e-com video. That may not carry to coaching offers.
4. **Rates.** The numbers in step 0 are Kam's, from mid-2026 e-com. They aren't tested against the clients you'd pitch.
5. **Kam's "safe first move" vs Genesis's.** Kam opens with category-buyer ads. Genesis rates bottom-of-funnel ads as low-ceiling and makes a format swap on the top spender the safest move. Step 4 keeps both, since both are cheap. Worth deciding which you'd lead with.
6. **This overlaps your own skills.** `/account-read` already ends in a production plan, and `/storm` and `/brief` already carry a hypothesis per ad. I haven't merged this checklist with them. It should probably point at those commands at stages 2 and 4.

## 8. What the audit turned up that the teardown missed

**A. The lead magnet downloads without opting in.** `[OBSERVED — verified]`
The PDF link sits in the store page's own HTML. Fetched directly: **HTTP 200, application/pdf, 399,350 bytes, 7 pages.** No email required.

Most humans won't view source — but it means the file is publicly fetchable and indexable, and the opt-in exchange isn't enforced at the file level.

**B. The checkout asks for a full postal address** for a digital subscription. Name, address, country, before payment. Every field is friction ([[checkout-friction-and-optin-placement]]), and a postal address for a login is friction with no purpose the buyer can see.

**C. "Subscribe to our email list" appears as a separate opt-in on the checkout.** `[INFERRED]` If it isn't pre-ticked, **a paying member can complete purchase and not be on the list** — which, given there's one email a week and no sequences, means the highest-value people may be the least contacted.

**D. Zero risk reversal, verified not assumed.** Across the store page source: `guarantee` 0 · `refund` 0 · `trial` 0 · `money back` 0 · `cancel anytime` 0 · `risk-free` 0.

**E. Three typos still live on the money page.** "CIRRICULUM" · "Watchen" (for Watchmen) · "INTERCESORS". One of them misspells the audience's own identity word.

**F. The lead magnet spends part of its 1,453 words on political commentary.** Point 2 ("Instinct") moves into COVID/Wuhan and media-trust material inside an ebook promising *7 Ways God Speaks*.
*Flagging the mechanics only:* the magnet's job is to deliver its promise and build trust toward Prophet School, and a political detour narrows who completes that journey. **His theological and political positions are his own and not mine to assess** — this is noted as an offer-mechanics observation and a judgement call for you, not a recommendation.

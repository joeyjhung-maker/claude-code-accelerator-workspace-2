# HOOK BRIEF: opening line for the Topa launch video (mini VSL, voice-over)

Write hooks for the FIRST LINE of a 2.5 minute launch video for Topa, a LinkedIn automation tool (topa.io). The line is spoken by a narrator and sits on screen as the first frame, so it must work read aloud AND as a still.

## Who is watching
Agency owners, consultants, coaches and B2B service sellers who sell to other business owners. They believe the big-ticket buyers are on LinkedIn. They have tried DMing decision makers and got ignored. They have hundreds or thousands of LinkedIn connections they have never messaged.

## Aim (what is already stored in their head)
- Suspicion they already carry: LinkedIn DMs are a waste of time, everyone is pitch-slapping the same people, and the big deals go to somebody else.
- Enemy that gets the rock: the "send 100 cold DMs a day" crowd and the copy-paste pitch.
- Past failure that needs justifying: they DMed decision makers and heard nothing back.
- Identity under threat: "I'm a professional, not a spammer."

## The idea, as one ugly sentence
The $250k to $1m+ deals are on LinkedIn, everyone is fighting for them by DMing strangers and almost nobody closes, while the few who do close work the connections they already have and take the conversation off LinkedIn.

## Where the script goes after the hook (so the hook must lead into it)
What everyone knows (the big deals and the decision makers with real budgets are on LinkedIn) -> everyone is DMing them and nobody is closing -> what the closers do differently -> Topa.

Joey's own rough direction for the open (do NOT copy it word for word, it is a direction):
"Everyone knows the big $250k to $1m+ deals are on LinkedIn. The decision makers with real budgets. The ones who can hire you and in one deal completely change your life. Yet few know how to get them."

## Hard rules
- One line each. Max 18 words. Spoken-natural. It must read like information, a confession or an observation, never like an ad.
- Statements, not questions. No hook may open on a question.
- Do not open on a testimonial or student result. Do not lead pain-first: lead with curiosity, novelty, a promise, a world-level observation or a contrarian claim. Pain can sit inside the line as the amplifier.
- Give me VARIETY of vehicle across the set. I need at least 3 each of: Curiosity, Call-Out, Contrarian, Proof, Emotion/confession. A couple of Authority is fine. No Urgency (there is no deadline).
- Push harder than feels comfortable. Protect the charged word. Caveman words over clever ones.
- The only numbers you may use: "$250k to $1m+" as the size of deals that exist on LinkedIn (never as a promise to the viewer), "84 connections, 23 replies, 27% reply rate" (Jonathan, who runs Topa, messaging his own existing connections), "350 million contacts", "$79 a month". No other numbers, results, names or quotes.
- NEVER state, quote or hint at the wording of the message Topa sends, and never say how many words it has. Never say anything is "locked" or that you get it "when you join".
- Never say "safe", "guaranteed", "leverage", "solution", "game-changer", "unlock", "supercharge", "hack" or "secret".
- Do not say "know your name".

## SECOND PASS (targeted): the first 20 were mostly one idea
The first batch said "stop DMing strangers, work your connections" about fourteen different ways, mostly as Contrarian or Call-Out. This pass must use DIFFERENT worst truths. Write hooks ONLY from these six angles, at least three from each, and label each hook with its angle letter:
A. THE CHAT BOX. A $250k deal was never going to close inside a chat window. Big deals get done on a call or over a coffee, and all a DM gives you is a chat.
B. THE DECISION MAKER'S CHAIR. Written from what the buyer sees: an inbox that is a graveyard of copy-paste pitches from strangers, deleted in a second. Make the viewer see their own message in that pile.
C. THE YES THEY NEVER CASHED. Every connection request someone accepted was a small yes. They are sitting on hundreds of those and have never messaged them.
D. PROOF. Only these numbers: Jonathan, who runs Topa, messaged 84 of his own existing connections and 23 replied, a 27% reply rate. Do not add any detail that is not in that sentence.
E. THE SPAMMER IN THE MIRROR. The viewer hates pitch DMs and deletes them daily, then sends the same thing to people with bigger inboxes. Identity under threat. Observational or "we" voice only, no invented first-person story.
F. THE MISSING PHONE NUMBER. The gap between a LinkedIn reply and a signed deal is a phone number, an email, an address. Most people on LinkedIn have none of those for the people they are chasing.
Vehicles wanted in this pass: Emotion/confession-style observation, Proof, Curiosity, Authority. No more "stop DMing strangers" Contrarian lines.
Same hard rules as above. Max 18 words. Go for the flinch.

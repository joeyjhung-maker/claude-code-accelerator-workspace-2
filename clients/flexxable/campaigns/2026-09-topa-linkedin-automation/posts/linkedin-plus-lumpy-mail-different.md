## The concept (for Joey)
Everyone fights for the same screen. We're the only ones on the screen AND the desk, working on the SAME prospect, each touch warming the next: LinkedIn connection makes the gift less random → the locked gift makes the DM impossible to ignore ("I'm the one who sent you the bottle") → the DM/gift make the call easy ("did you get it? I hate booze going to waste"). Most people own ONE channel; this stacks two on the same person.

HL: Everyone's fighting for their screen. We show up on their desk.

You're a decision-maker at a decent sized company.

You open LinkedIn… a pile of DMs from people you've never met, all pitching you something.

You check email… same thing.

Delete, delete, delete.

Now… same morning, same desk, same coffee.

But there's a package sitting next to your keyboard.

A bottle with a lock on it… and a letter that says to call a number if you want to open it.

You're not deleting THAT.

Everyone else is fighting for pixels on a screen.

We're on the screen AND on the desk.

And here's why the two together hit different…

LinkedIn warms 'em up first.

You connect, you send something casual, and your name's not a stranger's anymore.

Then the gift lands.

Now you're not "some random who DM'd me"… you're "the one who sent me the locked bottle."

Your next DM?

They actually reply to it.

And the phone call? Dead easy… "Hey, did you get the bottle? I don't like it when good booze goes to waste."

Each touch makes the next one warmer… that compounding is the whole game.

Johnny messaged 84 existing LinkedIn connections… 23 replied.

We've been sending locked bottles for a few months now… roughly 25% of the people we send one to end up on a call.

It all runs from one place… find 'em, warm 'em on LinkedIn, grab their mobile, send the gift.

Most people own ONE channel.

This stacks two on the same person.

If you're ready to stop competing in their inbox and start showing up where nobody else is, comment "DESK" and we'll get the deets to ya.

## The offer, as demoed on the call
- **Price:** $79/mo for 1 LinkedIn account, $99/mo for 2 accounts. Cell-phone lookup costs 1.5
  credits (~7.5¢) per lookup.
- **Positioning line from Jonathan:** about half the price of other LinkedIn automation tools,
  about a third of the price of any phone-enrichment tool, ~70% cell-phone coverage.
- **Mechanism:** connect a LinkedIn account → run automated sequences against either (a) your
  *existing* connections (warm, low-hanging fruit) or (b) *new* connections pulled from Topa's
  350M-record lead database → AI-assisted message writer suggests an opener → replies land in one
  shared inbox → one click ("Get Cell Phone") finds the person's mobile number → you call them.
- **Compliance pitch (the answer to "will my account get banned"):** human-like randomized send
  times, residential IP matched to the account's location, capped sending (15 connections + 20
  messages/day to start, scaling to 25/50 after a few weeks).
- **Demoed proof, real and citable:** Jonathan's own campaign — 84 existing connections messaged,
  23 replies, **27% response rate**.
- **The "hand-raiser" message Dan likes:** "How's business?" — not a pitch, just a conversation
  starter. Dan's read: a 25% reply rate on 500 connections is ~125 conversations; if you're not
  closing several from that, the offer (not the tool) is the problem.
- **Escalation ladder Dan pitched live:** LinkedIn reply → phone call ("this is where the money's
  at") → for the hottest prospects, escalate to a physical B2B Gift bottle. LinkedIn Automation is
  positioned as the *cheap front door*, not a replacement for gifting.
- **Roadmap, not live yet — don't oversell:** AI address-lookup (auto-finds a mailing address from
  a LinkedIn profile), an ad-spend checker. Both will be gated behind the same $79/$99 tier later.
- **Dan's own audience overlay:** he name-checks his own "offer of the century" (reactivating a
  business's *old, dead* leads via AI+SMS, paid on performance) as an example of what someone might
  sell once they've got a warm conversation going. That's Dan's separate flagship offer, not this
  product — don't conflate the two in copy, but see `cddc-worksheet.md` §6 for why the *mechanism*
  parallel (reactivating something dormant) may be worth leaning on.

## CAP campaign architecture — locked 2026-09-28

CAP is both a micro copy sequence and the macro order of this campaign. The same prospect moves
through three mindsets: **Child** wants the big dopamine-rich outcome, **Adult** wants the HOW—the
mechanism, process and steps—and **Parent** becomes skeptical, asking for proof, results and answers
to objections.

- **Micro:** a single post/email can move Child → Adult → Parent. A short T1 can also be deliberately
  pure Child: big outcome, high dopamine, little process or proof, optimised for replies.
- **Macro:** open the campaign with Child-level outcome posts, move into Adult mechanism/process
  content, then finish by addressing the Parent's “sharks”—objections, skepticism and proof.
- **Journey image:** prospects move from Hell Island to Heaven Island across dangerous water. The
  offer is the boat. Make the destination desirable, the crossing understandable and the boat
  safe/proven enough to survive Parent scrutiny.

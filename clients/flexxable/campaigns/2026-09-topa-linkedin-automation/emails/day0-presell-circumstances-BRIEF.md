# Brief: pre-sell email (Mon 28 Sept) — "why it's so hard to land clients right now"

Write ONE email from Dan to his own list. It goes out today (Monday). Tomorrow (Tuesday) Dan releases a YouTube video that reveals the fix. This email only sets up the problem and opens the loop.

## Who's reading
Dan's own list: people selling AI automation / done-for-you services to business owners (mostly his "old leads into sales, paid on performance" offer), plus some selling other B2B stuff. They want BIGGER clients, bigger deals. They already believe LinkedIn is where the bigger business owners and decision-makers hang out. They are NOT beginners to outreach; they've tried stuff and it's getting harder.

## The job of this email
Name the CIRCUMSTANCES that make getting clients hard right now, so the reader nods along ("that's exactly it"). This is pain-led on purpose (Joey asked for it). Then turn to the close:
"The good news is we've built something that fixes this. I'll share all the details with you tomorrow."

Do NOT reveal the mechanism, the tool, the price, or how it works. No link, no CTA to buy. The only ask is: watch for tomorrow's email.

## The circumstances to paint (pick the strongest 3-4, make them concrete scenes, not a list)
1. The bigger deals are on LinkedIn. That's where the business owners and decision-makers with real budgets are, not Instagram or TikTok. Everyone already knows it.
2. Which is exactly why those people are getting hammered. Their LinkedIn inbox is full of connection requests that turn into a pitch, and DMs that all sound the same. You can't just DM the sh*t out of them... everyone's already doing that, and nobody replies.
3. Everyone has the same AI tools now, so everyone's "personalised" message sounds exactly like everyone else's. The prospect can smell it in the first line.
4. Cold email is getting buried. Gmail and Outlook are actively fighting cold outreach, so half of it never even lands.
5. Buying a lead list is a gamble. Data from list brokers is sketchy at the best of times (Dan's own words), so you pay for names that bounce or aren't the decision-maker.
6. Even when someone does reply, most people stall at the next step because they won't pick up the phone.

Tie it together with ONE line that lands the real problem: the best prospects aren't hard to FIND anymore... they're hard to REACH, because everyone else found them too.

## The turn and close
Ladder the pivot across separate lines (setup / objection / reveal), then close on something like:
"The good news is… we've built something that fixes this.
I'll share all the details with you tomorrow."
Optional: one teasing line that it has nothing to do with sending more DMs or more emails. Keep it a tease, not an explanation.

Sign off: Dan

## Facts you may use (verbatim only; don't add numbers)
- Dan spent the last few months doing his due diligence on investing in a new business, and has now gone all in with his co-founder Johnny ("what they showed me blew me away").
- Dan has been practising picking up the phone and dialling himself over the last few months. "Dialling is painful, but it just gets results."
- "Data from list brokers is somewhat sketchy at the best of times" (Dan's line).
Do NOT use any other stats, reply rates, prices, or results. Do not mention bottles, gifts, mail, the lock, Topa, or LinkedIn automation by name. That's all tomorrow.

- Give 3 subject line options and 1 preview line.
- Body 200-300 words. Opens "Yo, it's Dan…" style or straight into the hook (your call, no question in line one).
- Dan's own-list voice: casual, Aussie-inflected, texting a mate who owns a business.

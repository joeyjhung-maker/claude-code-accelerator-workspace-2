## Scoped defaults for Dan's own-list opinion emails

This specimen is for warm readers, especially late in a sequence. Do not impose its structure on cold acquisition, teaching, testimonial or JV partner copy.

- Build one spoken argument: observed oddity → attractive promise → disappointing reality → reader recognition → Dan's buying criterion → recommendation → easy next step.
- Preserve a coherent argument through connective words: “And”, “Then”, “Which means”, “Now”, “If”, “That's why”. Each transition must advance the reasoning; do not turn the list into mandatory wording.
- Use the old offer's features to expose the mismatch. The recommended product needs only enough detail to make the recommendation believable at this campaign stage.
- Let a concrete list unfold across lines when each item earns its place. A three-item feature list is not automatically a manufactured rhythm error.
- Keep familiar speech and meaningful roughness. Do not add slang as decoration, imitate typos deliberately, or sterilise the user's punctuation.
- Read the headline and body as different jobs. Joey restored a direct call-out headline while softening the body into an observation about the offer. Do not infer a universal ban on direct headlines from the earlier opening correction.
- Do not convert a successful choice in one email into a compulsory element of all future emails: member proof, “May I suggest”, a four-bonus P.S., lumpy mail, a price comparison and a keyword CTA all need a reason to be here.

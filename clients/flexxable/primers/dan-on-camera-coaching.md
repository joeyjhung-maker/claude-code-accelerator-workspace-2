# Dan on-camera coaching — first-time talking-head video

Dan has no screen/presenting experience. This is his first in-feed MicroVSL (direct-to-camera
confessional). This doc is general-purpose — applies to every video brief in the Diversity
Decoder gap-fill batch (V3 shipped, V4/V6/V8/V10 still to produce), not just one ad.
Related: [[strategy-map]] [[dan-truth-constraint]]

---

## The one thing that matters most: don't film it in one take

These scripts are already written as a chain of short beats — the `[BEAT]` / `[PAUSE]`
markers in every script mark a natural pause in the thought. **Those are also safe places
to stop the camera, breathe, and start again.** Nobody watching the finished ad can tell a
30-second clip was stitched from three takes recorded five minutes apart — cuts land
invisibly at a pause point.

Practical version: record 2-4 sentences at a time, between markers. 2-3 takes of each
chunk, keep the best one, move to the next chunk. Stop treating it as "perform the whole
3-6 minutes flawlessly" — that's a genuinely hard skill even for trained presenters. Treat
it as "say this one true thing clearly," ten times in a row.

## Read for meaning, not memorization

Nobody needs to recite this word-for-word. A teleprompter app on a phone or tablet, propped
right behind/beside the camera lens, is completely normal for this format — most native-
feeling talking-head ads are read, not memorized. If a line comes out slightly different
from the script but says the same thing, that's fine. Paraphrasing close to the line beats
a stiff, over-rehearsed delivery every time.

## The stumbles are not a bug here

Every `[VISUAL]` note in these scripts says some version of the same thing: **relaxed, not
performing, not dramatic, just honest, speaking like he's telling a friend something true.**
That's not a fallback instruction for when the "real" performance doesn't work out — it IS
the performance. A slicker, more polished delivery would actually work against this ad
style; the whole reason this format converts is that it reads as a real person talking, not
a produced ad. A first take with a small stumble, a genuine pause to think, or an
unscripted "um" is often MORE on-brand than a clean, rehearsed-sounding one. Don't chase
polish.

## Before filming anything

Read the whole script out loud once, off-camera, just to hear it in his own voice and find
where the natural breath points actually are (they might not be exactly where the `[BEAT]`
markers are — that's fine, those are a starting guide, not a rulebook).

## Simple setup, no crew needed

- Phone camera is fine — this account's whole library runs on native-feeling UGC talking-
  head footage, not produced video. A polished studio look would actually stand out as
  off-brand.
- Natural window light, camera at eye level, quiet room. That's the whole brief.
- Look at the lens, not the screen with the script on it, as much as possible — glancing
  down between chunks is fine and normal.

## Reading the `[VISUAL]` notes

Every script has `[VISUAL: ...]` tags throughout — these are the closest thing to a
director's note in the script. They're short tone cues (e.g. "not dramatic, just honest,"
"slightly more direct," "quieter, letting this settle") telling Dan how the NEXT chunk
should feel, not what to physically do on camera. Read the tone note, take a breath, say
the lines. That's the whole method.

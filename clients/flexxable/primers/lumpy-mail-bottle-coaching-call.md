## The system, in order

1. **Data**: use Topa.ai to pull a "dream 100" list — a niche, cell-phone-number-level data.
2. **Send**: a wine/whiskey bottle with a lock on top + a short (~half page) letter, tweaked
   to the niche. Cost per send ~$40.
3. **Follow up relentlessly** — "like a dog with a bone." Never let a dream-100 name go
   until they say no. Channels, in rotation: call, Loom video (proof/results), email,
   WhatsApp, SMS, even calling from a different phone number.
4. **Book the coffee date** (their word for a sales call).
5. **Demo AI live on the call** rather than pitching it — using a build tool ("sales
   wingman" / "Hulk mode") to generate a working demo during the call itself.
6. **Close on a no-risk, performance-based skin-in-the-game test** ($500–750), not a
   standard retainer pitch.

## Priority order Dan gives (his words)

> "The data is the most important thing. The next most important thing, I would say, is
> the follow-up. Then you've got your offer... And the last thing really is the copy in
> this letter... it's not as important."

## The offer of the century (the actual mechanism)

Old/dead leads reactivation, sold as no-risk:

> "How about we take those old leads off you, turn them into sales, and we only get paid
> when you get paid."

Framed as: you're already spending on fresh leads (Dan's example: "$200,000 a month"),
sales team barely chases old leads ("dials them for two days... then thrown into the
bin"), so this is found money with zero downside. Negotiation line: *"Do you want 100% or
nothing... or 50% of an extra $200,000 you never had before?"*

## Proof numbers (real, from this call — do not invent beyond these)

- Matt: 14 bottles sent → 4 coffee dates with $20–80M businesses. Didn't close any of them.
- One inbound call from an $80M CEO at 7am, unprompted, same week as send.
- Cost: ~$40/bottle, ~$150 per qualified booked call all-in.
- 30 total bottles sent over ~6 weeks.
- When Matt rewrote the letter to be "more creative" and pain-led instead of sticking to
  the original: next 18 bottles → near-zero response (down from 3–4 inbound per 12).
- Dan's own team: 75% of coffee dates say yes to the initial test offer.

## What killed Matt's results — the mistakes, named

1. **Rewrote the proven letter to be "creative" / pain-first.** Response collapsed. Dan's
   read: stick close to what's proven, don't improvise on unproven instinct.
2. **Targeted too big.** $50–80M companies route calls through a COO/CMO gatekeeper who
   then has to sell the CEO internally — "which never works out." Sweet spot: **$5–20M**
   businesses, where you reach the actual decision-maker.
3. **Pitched the solution before diagnosing the pain** — sold "AI" instead of doing the
   "doctor's diagnosis" (asking questions, finding the actual bottleneck) before pitching
   anything. Dan's line: *"Don't sell AI, sell the results."*
4. **Didn't follow up hard enough after the initial call** — no Loom, no repeat touches.

## Reusable lines / voice worth mining

- "Dog with a bone" — the follow-up persistence framing.
- "Doctor's diagnosis" — talk 10 minutes, ask questions, find the pain before pitching.
- "Don't sell AI, sell the results."
- "No harm, no foul" — the risk-reversal framing on the test offer.
- Law-of-reciprocity angle: sending something physical first makes the follow-up call feel
  earned, not cold — *"I'll call and say, hey, did you get the wine?"*
- Legal note if this ever informs US-facing copy: alcohol can't technically be shipped in
  the US — take own legal advice; this was flagged live on the call, don't paper over it.

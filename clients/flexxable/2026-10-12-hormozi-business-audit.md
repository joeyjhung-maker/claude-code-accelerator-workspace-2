## 3. What the pivot gets right

**It is a real money model on paper.** The old one was a $14 book and a $5k programme with nothing in between.

**Recurring from the first sale.** The old business got paid two or three times a year, at launches.

**Lead getters are built in.** Affiliates and JVs were the biggest hole in the old model. Now they are the plan.

**Customer first, then affiliate.** Hormozi's rule for affiliates is to make them a customer or an expert first. "Sell Topa with Topa" is that rule.

**Prove it, then teach it.** Nat, then Gavin, then everyone. That is Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate.

**The price matches the JV rule you already paid for.** A free doc and a $9 tier are a safe first yes for a partner's cold list. ROYA off a webinar was not.

**Dan believes in it.** He said he could not sell the AI work with conviction. That matters more than any framework.

**The product is physical and old.** His own line: it worked 20 years ago and will work 20 years from now.

## 4. Where it is exposed

### The cash bridge

Hormozi is blunt on this: continuity is a weak front door because it brings in little cash today.

The arithmetic, from the stated prices only:

- One ROYA sale bills $4,992.
- A Tier 2 member sold by an affiliate at a 50% share leaves $48.50 a month.
- That is about 103 member-months to match one ROYA sale.
- ROYA 6.0 grossed $1.08m. Matching that takes roughly 1,850 affiliate-sold Tier 2 members held for a full year.
- Tier 3 is where the cash is: about 8 member-months matches one ROYA sale at the same 50% share.

None of that says the pivot is wrong. It says the old line has to keep paying until the new one does.

Dan gave Nat six months to prove concept. Nothing in the Loom says what funds those six months.

### The company is betting on products it does not own

Bottles and Topa are Jonathan's.

Dan takes a third of fulfilment. A Topa shareholding was "in discussions" in August. The partnership agreement was due 10 Sept and its status is not in the workspace.

Jonathan and Dean's phone team will sell to the people who opt in for the free leads. Who owns those customers, and who gets paid, is not stated.

What Flexxable owns: the list, the community, the training and the affiliates.

My read: this is the single biggest stability risk. Get it in writing before the whole business points at it.

### Tier 2 and Tier 3 cannot be sold honestly yet

The training does not exist until Nat has done it. Dan said so: "you'll find this out as you move to your 8.33k a month."

So for now, only the lead magnet, the free group, Tier 1 and bottles are real.

Your lane is "NAPs and offers based on Nat's success." For up to six months there may be little success to write about.

That is how the Topa copy ended up with numbers that had to be reconciled three times.

### Tier 2 is a business opportunity

People pay $97 a month to learn to earn commissions, and affiliates earn 50% on bringing in more people who pay $97.

It only stays clean if the money comes from real users of bottles and Topa, not from affiliates recruiting affiliates.

"8.33k a month" is Nat's target. It must never reach copy.

Hormozi pays affiliates in three steps: some for signing up, more for activating, full for sustained results. A flat 50% from day one pays the same for a tyre-kicker as for a real user.

### Three different buyers in one room

Tier 1 is someone with an offer who wants meetings.

Tier 2 is someone who wants an income.

Tier 3 is someone who wants an agency.

The project file already says B2B Leads Lab is for people landing meetings, not people escaping a job.

Dan's traffic plan is "a huge audience that's desperate to make money." That is the AAA Ninjas crowd, and they fit Tier 2, not Tier 1.

Pick who the front door is for. My read: Tier 1 people, with the affiliate tier offered only to members who have used the product.

### The bottle proof was earned selling the old offer

10 sent, 6 calls, 3 clients. 70 sent, 6 clients.

Those clients bought AI work on the coffee date.

Calls booked carries over. Clients closed does not, because the pitch on the call is changing to "we can do this for you."

### 1,200 ROYA members were sold lifetime access

The Loom does not mention them.

They are also the best first buyers of bottles. They already need coffee dates.

### The front end no longer matches the back end

The book ads bring in people who want an AI agency. 75 new book ads were produced on 9 Oct, the day before the Loom.

Nobody has said whether the book keeps running.

### Still Dan

Organic content is "based on Dan's wisdom." The posts are written as Dan.

The good news: Nat and Gavin are about to become proof with faces. Hormozi's line is "how I", not "how to."

### Too many stages at once

Three tiers, affiliates, JVs, a phone team and Rainmakers.

Hormozi: one stage at a time, and each stage pays for the next.

## 5. What is still missing

1. **A first-send offer.** You cannot run the locked gift strategy without a gift. Sell the first batch of sends the moment someone joins Tier 1.
2. **Cash up front.** A founding-member annual deal, sold with a Rainmaker, is how a subscription business gets launch-sized cash.
3. **A guarantee tied to actions.** Send 20 gifts and make the follow-ups, or the next month is free. It also tells a new member what to do first.
4. **An affiliate gate and a stepped payout.** Customers only. Paid more as their referrals stick.
5. **A plan for ROYA members.** Bring them across with early access, never as a paywall on something they feel they already bought.
6. **A weekly proof log from Nat and Gavin.** Sends, calls, closes, commissions. It was already a standing note in the project file and never got a cadence.
7. **Somewhere for a no to go.** Someone who passes on $97 should be offered sends, or Topa alone.
8. **Four-week billing.** Thirteen cycles a year instead of twelve.
9. **A scoreboard for the new line.** Opt-ins, free joins, $9, first send, $97, $1,197.

## 11. Second pass: each Hormozi book run against the new structure

*Added later on 12 Oct at Joey's request. Sections 1 to 10 read the pivot as a whole. This runs the three books one at a time.*

### $100M Offers

**Market: better.** Hormozi ranks market above offer. A B2B seller who wants meetings with big companies has more money and a sharper pain than a beginner buying a $14 book.

**But the traffic plan points at the old audience.** "A huge audience that's desperate to make money" is the book buyers. New market on paper, old market in practice.

**The value equation, tier by tier:**

| | Dream outcome | Believable? | Speed | Effort | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 + sends | Meetings with big companies | Small samples, vivid | Days | Low if the send is done for them | Strong |
| Tier 2 | Affiliate income | No proof yet | Slow, it builds monthly | High: ads, JVs, content | Weakest |
| Tier 3 | Their own appointment-setting agency | One coffee date from 10 bottles | Months | High | Unproven |

**Tier 2 asks people to do the work Dan's best ad tells them they will never do again.** Winning Ad 2 lists ads, funnels, copywriting and email as "serenity sucking." Tier 2 teaches ads, JVs and content.

**Tier 2 arithmetic.** At 50% of $97, "8.33k a month" is about 172 active referred members. That assumes the share is the only income, which is not confirmed.

**Tier 3 is ROYA's shape at nearly three times the price.** $1,197 a month is $14,364 a year against $4,992 once. It needs a reason to be monthly: sends included, seats included, or a weekly deal clinic.

**Unsolved problems in the Tier 1 offer.** Address lookup is not live. US sending was still a wait list in September. The closing script was being rebuilt. Hormozi: every unsolved problem is a reason not to buy.

**No guarantee anywhere.** Hormozi calls it the biggest single lever.

**The tiers have no names.** "Tier 1, 2, 3" is a spreadsheet label, not an offer.

### $100M Leads

**The Core Four, before and after:**

| Channel | Old business | New plan |
|---|---|---|
| Warm outreach | Tappers | Tappers (Joey and Leandro) |
| Content | Four Skool groups, email | Same, plus Alan on organic |
| Cold outreach | None | Bottles and LinkedIn: the product is the channel |
| Paid ads | The book, the engine | "We won't need ads" |

**The audience move is a one-off.** 22,000 Ninjas can be pointed at the new thing once. After that the plan needs affiliates and JVs that do not exist yet.

**No ads is forced, not chosen.** A Tier 1 member is worth $9 plus Flexxable's cut of their sends in month one. That cannot buy traffic.

**Affiliates are paid only after cash arrives.** That part is sound. It is acquisition the customer pays for.

**But gross profit per customer cannot be worked out.** Flexxable's cut on bottles, Topa and phone sales is not written down anywhere here.

**Test size.** Gavin's 10 bottles is a signal, not a test. Capacity was 100 bottles a week. Nat's proof should be counted in hundreds.

**Where it sits on Hormozi's seven levels.** The old business was around level five. The new line restarts at one or two and plans to jump straight to lead getters.

### $100M Money Models

**It is all continuity.** There is no cash offer at the front. Hormozi: continuity is the weakest way to attract and the best thing to stack on top.

**The real product is the send.** The tier is the wrapper. The number that decides everything is how many members buy a first send.

**Plays that fit, in his words:**

- **Decoy.** Tier 1 DIY is the decoy. Done-for-you sends are the premium. Show them side by side.
- **Buy X get Y.** A prepaid pack: buy 20 sends, get 5 free. Cash on day one, and a member who has committed.
- **Win your money back.** Send 20 and make the follow-ups. No calls, credit toward the next batch.
- **Anchor.** Show $1,197 first. $97 then looks small.
- **Rollover.** Credit ROYA members part of what they paid toward Tier 3. It brings them across instead of leaving them behind.
- **Continuity bonus.** Advertise the bonus, not the membership. The 200 free leads code already exists.
- **Waived fee.** A setup fee on Tier 3, waived for a 12-month commitment. Made for offers that take months to show results.
- **Menu upsell for the tappers.** Tell them what they do not need, prescribe what they do, then wine or Yeti, 10 or 25.

**One stage at a time.** Lead magnet, free group, Tier 1 and a first send. Nothing else until that converts.

## 12. Update: the lead magnet is finished, and the $9 is a tier inside the free group

*From Joey, 12 Oct. The free group stays free. The $9/m is a "premium" tier inside it, using Skool's tier function.*

**The path as built today:** ad or Dan video, squeeze page, guide, welcome email, P.S. to the free group, premium tier inside.

**What this settles.** The front door is one free room. The upgrade is one click, with locked content in view. That is Hormozi's decoy, built into the platform. It is also the model Travis described: almost nothing free in the classroom, so the lock does the selling.

**What it exposes.**

- **Everything Flexxable sells sits inside the group, and the group is the P.S.** The first thing a new opt-in is sent toward is the free database, which is Topa.
- **There are no emails after the welcome.** The job of days 2 to 5 is one thing: get them into the group.
- **The squeeze page brief names paid social video as a traffic source.** If ads run to a free guide, something has to pay the ad cost back inside 30 days. $9 will not.
- **The free database is now the lead magnet's bonus.** So the premium tier needs a bonus of its own. (This replaces the "200 free leads" idea in section 11.)

**Sizing the $9 tier.** The group had 254 members in August. Travis's own group converts 13% of free members to premium. That would be 33 people, about $297 a month. Old count, someone else's rate, not a forecast. The point: the $9 tier is a filter that finds buyers. It is not the income.

**Numbers to watch, in order:** opt-in rate, guide opened, joined the group, upgraded to premium, bought a first send.

**Next builds:**

1. Four follow-up emails after the welcome, each pushing the group join.
2. Decide what is free and what is locked in the classroom.
3. The first-send offer, shown at the moment of upgrade.
4. A named bonus for premium.

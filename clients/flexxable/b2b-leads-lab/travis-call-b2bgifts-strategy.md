## Summary

**The mechanism, confirmed working:** locked bottles (~$40-50, priced high enough people won't
skip opening it) get roughly 25% call rate, ~80% of leads eventually respond if you keep
following up. Dan calls it the best client-getting method he's tried.

**Fulfilment partner origin story:** Jonathan (Topa/b2bgifts.ai) is the *student* who originally
sent Dan his first bottle, saw Dan's results, and offered to build the DFY layer himself.
Described as an "AI and data genius" — his backend grades leads **green/yellow/red**, a detail
not previously banked. Dan's own read on this call: "60% of the success is in the list" — does
not quite match the separately-sourced "40% List / 40% Offer / 20% Copy" principle already
banked; both real, from different sources, not reconciled.

**Economics, now with a source:** the "100 people × 20 bottles/week × £20 profit/bottle" figure
already in the project file traces to this call. New: **Dan takes a 1/3 cut of the fulfilment
business.** Real financial detail, not for customer-facing copy.

**The domino offer:** confirms the $99/mo LinkedIn tool is Jonathan's build. New idea: niche-match
the LinkedIn push to whatever niche the bottle campaign targets (e.g. HVAC bottles → HVAC-focused
LinkedIn growth).

**Community/course structure ("the Netflix method"):** freemium hopper group, already created.
Nat's course is **8 weeks** (new specific figure) and functions as "one giant sales letter"
pre-selling the $99/mo DFY offer. Travis's own free tier has one bullet point and zero classroom
content — pure upgrade bait. His numbers: 524 members, 73 premium (13% conversion), 30-35%
free-signup rate from affiliates.

**Growth mechanism — "give away the front end, keep the fulfilment":** never pay affiliates on
the fulfilment business (keep 100%), but for the course/community itself, give other Skool
owners a duplicable "course key" to embed in their own premium tier, keeping 100% of what they
charge — no direct cut for Dan/Jonathan on that transaction, but the licensed course carries
hidden links back to the monetized fulfilment offer. Real example: Travis closed a
1,500-person-community deal like this in 15 minutes.

**Positioning advice — the one most relevant to the parked content-strategy question:** don't
brand narrowly around "B2B lumpy mail" — most people don't know the term. Position around the
broad, expensive, common problem ("leads for B2B businesses") with lumpy mail as the *uncommon
solution* inside it, not the headline. The "enemy" should be **ESPs/spam filters** (Gmail,
Outlook), contrasted against USPS which *wants* the business — sharper than the existing "cold
email gurus could never lol" line.

**Directly relevant to the parked content-strategy tension:** Travis explicitly endorsed
cross-seeding the new group from existing assets ("you've done the hard work, now seed the
community") after Dan described the exact move Joey flagged concern about. Travis didn't address
Joey's specific worry (AAA→ROYA cannibalization, IAA book leads wanting different content) —
this was about seeding generally — but useful context for Thursday's call. Real group sizes from
this call: **AAA Ninjas ~22,000 members, ROYA ~1,200, the third group ~6,000** (presumably MDAD).

**Other details:** flyers planned inside b2bgifts.ai shipping boxes so recipients become leads
too. Course content should release in stages (retention + a fresh "conversion event" each time),
modularized like Travis's own (Leads/Sales/Auctions/Traffic "On Demand"). Remove all execution
friction — hand people the actual letter template, don't make them write it. SEO: set the group
public, "leads"/"direct mail"/"lumpy mail" keywords are wide open on Skool discovery right now.

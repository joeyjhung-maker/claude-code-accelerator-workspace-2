## Core positioning

Wingman 2.0 is being framed as **"The Command Center"**: one cleaner place to manage the full journey from prospect research and pitching through client setup, campaign management, reporting and communication.

The strategic answer behind the update is: **why should a client choose your AI campaign service when their existing software can bolt on AI/SMS for roughly $200/month?** Jamie's answer is a better managed service, with automatic optimisation, a professional client experience, deeper prospect intelligence and less manual technical work.

Jamie's fuller release framing sharpens that into a bigger shift:

> Sales Wingman 2.0 shifts the focus from just providing software to proving your value, streamlining your operations, and creating undeniable “sticky” offers.

This is not just “more software.” It is an agency operating layer built around four themes.

## The four themes

### 1. Simplicity

- Put each tool or answer where the operator actually needs it.
- Consolidate scattered pages and sidebars into company workspaces and one command centre.
- Reduce tab-hopping, settings-hunting and repeated GoHighLevel logins.
- Move routine campaign changes into email so the operator increasingly does not need to log into an app.

### 2. More sales opportunities

- Surface new niches worth targeting.
- Recommend what to pitch first and what to upsell later.
- Turn a current client's result into a case study and a list of similar prospects.
- Use a prospect's real ads and business information to make the demo feel specific and tangible.

### 3. Credibility

- Show live, aggregate results through The Wall.
- Prove an AI agent has survived a rigorous pre-launch test through Gauntlet Accreditation.
- Give clients a polished, white-label portal instead of exposing the messy GoHighLevel backend.
- Create case studies automatically once campaigns begin producing results.

### 4. Results with less work

- Test and improve messaging over time.
- Let email replies update live prompts.
- Let onboarding reminders chase missing client details.
- Let clients or their developers connect API details themselves.
- Let the live-chat knowledge loop learn from help-desk answers.

## Feature map

### The Wall — visible proof of the agency's value

- A live dashboard pulling aggregate results from every campaign the agency has run globally.
- Shows conversations, replies and outcomes together “on the wall.”
- Intended to sit on a TV or appear as a smart Zoom background.
- Sales job: answer “Why you?” with live evidence before the prospect asks.
- Deeper meaning: turns invisible background work into visible proof and makes the agency's experience feel substantial.

### Automated A/B testing — agents improve after launch

- Runs across 30 campaign days.
- Reviews conversational data and tests messaging/prompt changes.
- Checks the result, keeps winners and rolls back changes that do not work.
- Jamie's fuller explanation says suggested prompt tweaks are sent for **simple email approval**.
- Primary payoff: less time manually reading chat logs and less guesswork about which message works.
- Important nuance: this should not be described as uncontrolled, fully autonomous rewriting. The fuller source introduces an email-approval step.

### The Gauntlet Accreditation — proof before an agent meets a client

- A rigorous third-party stress test before an AI agent goes live.
- Speed-runs 100 simulated conversations.
- Tests persuasion, instruction-following, tool usage and error/weakness rates.
- Suggests fixes, lets the operator patch weak spots and test again.
- Passing earns Gauntlet Accreditation plus evidence behind the agent.
- Sales job: separate a tested AI service from unvetted “we built a bot” offers.
- Risk-reversal job: find mistakes before the client does.

### Niche Drops — three researched opportunities every month

- Three under-targeted or “hidden” niches delivered each month.
- Includes research and pitch ideas/playbooks so members know what to sell and how to start.
- Jamie's earlier example: jewellery shops selling engagement rings.
- Jamie's release hint: government policy changes keep creating fresh AI opportunities.
- Sales job: remove the “what niche do I target?” stall and reduce fear that the opportunity has already passed.

### Companies / company hub — the central workspace

- Jamie calls this a main feature and says the release overview still did not cover it fully.
- Brings research, pitch angles, company knowledge, demos, live agents and campaigns into one company workspace.
- Shows website traffic, whether the prospect is running ads and other company insights.
- Recommends the best service to lead with and secondary services worth pitching or upselling.
- Searches LinkedIn for key decision-makers.
- Generates pre-pitch talking points to help build rapport.
- Lets the operator build a demo around the prospect's real business, then manage the client without starting again elsewhere.
- Demos and later campaign work remain attached to the same company record.
- Open loop: this feature needs more source material before we claim to understand its full scope.

### Prospect-specific speed-to-lead demos

- Takes a prospect's actual Facebook ad and turns it into a speed-to-lead demo.
- Uses their advert, their inquiry and a personalised AI response.
- Sales job: let the prospect experience the offer rather than listen to a generic explanation.

### White-label client portal — `agencyportal.ai`

- A brandable client-facing interface: the agency's name, domain and the client's results.
- Syncs GoHighLevel campaigns, messages and conversations into the portal.
- Keeps clients out of the messy GoHighLevel backend.
- Onboarding forms collect the information required to launch a campaign.
- Automated reminders chase missing details.
- Demos can be shared and clients can add feedback.
- After onboarding, the setup section disappears and the client sees the live dashboard.
- Clients can review leads, stats, conversations and campaign updates.
- Smart tags are generated from conversations for quick filtering, such as callbacks and completed conversations.
- Clients can attach a note or issue directly to a campaign and it syncs back to the operator dashboard.
- The same management model also works for voice campaigns.

### Frictionless API bridge

- The client or the client's developer securely enters their own API details in the portal.
- Sales Wingman routes leads and updates the connected CRM on the agency's behalf.
- Removes the operator's need to spend hours searching ChatGPT/YouTube for integration help or hiring a developer for each setup.
- Client payoff: a clean connection to the system they already use.
- Operator payoff: the technical anxiety and integration burden moves out of the agency's daily workload.

### Email-controlled, self-updating prompts

- When a live agent or campaign needs attention, the operator gets an email.
- The operator can reply with the desired change.
- Sales Wingman's AI updates the agent in production.
- No platform login and no hunting through dozens of sub-accounts/settings for a small instruction change.
- Vision: the inbox increasingly becomes the place to keep campaigns sharp.

### Enhanced AI live chat — distinct from GoHighLevel live chat

- A separate Sales Wingman AI live-chat widget, not the GoHighLevel live-chat product.
- Turns underused website traffic into conversations and captured leads.
- Jamie frames it as a possible alternative “offer of the century”: low risk, high reward and tied to new money for the client.
- Can create a support ticket when human help is needed.
- If the chat finds a knowledge gap, it emails the client's help desk.
- The help desk replies with the answer and the chat becomes smarter.
- Sticky-offer logic: the client helps train the asset over time, making the service more embedded and useful.

### Automated case studies

- Once a campaign starts producing results, Sales Wingman creates a case study automatically.
- Credibility job: turn delivery proof into a sales asset without relying on the operator to stop and write it.

### Lookalike clients

- When a client is getting results, Sales Wingman identifies similar businesses the operator should consider pitching.
- Growth job: turn one proven client/niche into the next prospecting list.

## High-value distinctions to preserve

- **The Wall is proof; the portal is delivery.** They solve different credibility problems.
- **The Gauntlet is pre-launch validation; A/B testing is post-launch improvement.** Do not collapse them into one “testing” feature.
- **The Companies hub is the operator workspace; `agencyportal.ai` is the client-facing workspace.**
- **Email-controlled prompt updates are not the same as A/B testing.** One responds to a known FAQ/instruction gap; the other tests performance across campaign data.
- **AI live chat is not GoHighLevel live chat.** Jamie explicitly distinguishes it.
- **Niche Drops finds markets; lookalike clients expand from an existing win.** One is discovery, the other is replication.
- **Automated case studies create proof after delivery; The Wall aggregates proof across the agency.**

## Strategic implications

- Wingman 2.0 attacks the commoditisation objection: it gives the agency an answer when a prospect says, “My software already includes AI.”
- The moat is not one bot or one campaign. It is the system around the bot: research, testing, accreditation, proof, client experience and ongoing learning.
- Several features create a compounding loop: deliver a result → create a case study → find lookalike clients → pitch with stronger proof → win more campaigns → add more data to The Wall.
- The product tries to remove two forms of agency labour: technical admin and credibility-building admin.
- “Sticky” comes from shared operating infrastructure: the portal holds the client relationship, the AI learns from the client's help desk, campaign evidence accumulates and integrations stay connected.

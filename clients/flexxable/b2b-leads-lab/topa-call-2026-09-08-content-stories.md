# Jonathan (Topa) call — content angles and cock-up stories for B2B Gifts

*Monday 2026-09-08 call between Joey and Jonathan Matthews (CEO, Topa.io / b2bgifts.ai fulfilment
partner). Purpose, per Joey: get more stories and content angles for B2B Gifts. Not a strategy or
launch-logistics call like the two before it — this is raw story/anecdote material to spin into
posts. Full transcript below the summary.*

Related: [[b2b-leads-lab-project]]

## Summary

**Cock-up story #1 — sent a bottle to a fake/registered-office address (Tom Wood, owner of Car
and Classic).** Jonathan sent a bottle to Tom Wood — described as a multi-millionaire, owner of
Car & Classic (car auctions) — but the address used was his company's *registered office*, not a
real working address, so the bottle never reached him. Jonathan did get him on a cold call
afterward but wasn't able to "pull him round" without the bottle having landed — no meeting
resulted. **Content angle Jonathan proposed himself:** ties directly into the B2B Gifts /
Topa lead score — a registered-office address should score as a **red flag** on the lead score,
warning the sender before they waste a send. Good "we built this feature because of a real
mistake" story.

**Cock-up story #2 — sent a bottle to a company owner who was way too senior (John Pye
Auctions).** Jonathan sent a bottle to one of the owners of John Pye Auctions (a large auction
company). On the follow-up call he was told the owner was "on his yacht" and was never going to
open it — Jonathan had gone too high up the org chart. His own read: at a company that size he
should have targeted the COO or CMO, not the owner. **Content angle Jonathan proposed himself:**
another lead-score input — flag when a sender is targeting an owner/founder at a company above a
certain staff-count threshold (his own rough rule of thumb: **100+ staff, stop targeting the
owner**) and suggest a more reachable senior title instead.

**Cock-up story #3 — wrong bottle size, didn't fit the lockbox.** Jonathan bought 12 bottles of
Châteauneuf-du-Pape (~£25-30/bottle) for a send, but the bottle's shape was too broad at the top
and didn't fit in the lockbox packaging. Not a total loss — they drank them / gave them to
friends — but it delayed the send. **He fixed this on the next round himself:** before the most
recent fulfilment run he bought a handful of bottles first to taste-test them (confirm they don't
"taste like piss") and check the lockbox fit *before* ordering the full batch of 96. **Content
angle:** this is a "we do the fulfilment testing so you don't have to" trust-builder — a mistake
Topa already ate on the customer's behalf.

**Cock-up story #4 — sent to a contact with no mobile number.** Jonathan sent bottles to targets
where he only had an office landline, not a mobile — total waste, because the office receptionist
fobbed him off ("he doesn't take calls") and there was no way to follow up directly. **This is the
origin of an existing standing rule, not a new one:** don't send to a lead without a verified
mobile number, because you're **3x more likely to get a meeting when you can follow up with a
direct call** instead of routing back through the office. Jonathan noted people do ask "why not
just mail them anyway" when he posts this rule — worth addressing the objection head-on in copy
rather than just stating the rule.

**Multichannel / LinkedIn-first angle (Jonathan's current thinking, not yet built into B2B Gifts
copy).** Jonathan is increasingly thinking about **LinkedIn engagement before the mail send** —
connect, like a few posts, build a flicker of familiarity — so that by the time you send a bottle
(or just pick up the phone), you're not a cold name. His framing for Topa's upcoming LinkedIn
automation product: **it's not "automate your LinkedIn," it's "know who you've already
connected with so you can follow the connection with a bottle or a call."** Planned demo flow:
run a search inside Topa → see everyone you've reached out to on LinkedIn → click a "get cell
phone" button (in progress) → call them referencing the LinkedIn connection. Positioning line
Jonathan wants used: **"we're not cold pitching anymore — they already know who you are before you
reach out,"** whether that familiarity comes from the bottle or the LinkedIn touch. Also planned:
automated engagement (liking posts, endorsing skills, viewing profile) ahead of outreach, so the
prospect may have already half-noticed the sender before contact.

**Content plan mentioned by Jonathan:** he intends to start teasing the LinkedIn automation
feature in posts this week/over the next couple of weeks, ahead of its actual ship date (~2-3
weeks out) — build anticipation early rather than wait for launch. He also wants to film himself
doing cold calls off the back of fresh LinkedIn connections ("hey, we just connected today...") as
proof-of-process content, and plans to keep sending bottles himself (5 or so) to keep his own
agency skills sharp and have real material to show.

**"Why direct mail beats every other outreach channel" — Jonathan's own comparison argument (a
usable content angle, not yet written up).** His core claim: with cold email, cold calling, or
LinkedIn outreach at volume, **you are not the chooser — you have to work with whoever happens to
reply**, and that person may not be a good fit for the offer. With direct mail, **you pick the
exact company you want to speak to** — precision over volume. Comparison numbers he used
illustratively: ~£3k/month on gifts sends roughly 80 pieces; the same £3k on cold email could
"send" something like 100,000 emails, but that volume requires massive inbox infrastructure most
small operators don't have and will burn through fast. His view on **niching down**: in a small,
tightly-defined niche (he cited an old database-reactivation niche — car leasing — with only ~500
addressable UK companies), cold email dies fast because you can only email the list once before
you've "tested" it — whereas direct mail to the same small list can still convert a meaningful
share (his estimate: as many as 30 of 500) because it isn't competing on inbox noise. **His
conclusion, stated as a line worth using: direct mail beats every other outreach method most when
the niche is small — the opposite of the common objection that niching down leaves you with "not
enough people."**

**Cold email volume reality (context/color, not B2B Gifts-specific, useful comparison material).**
Jonathan says people in his network running cold email at real ad-spend scale treat email exactly
like paid ad spend — e.g. ~£5k/month buys roughly 250,000 sent emails at some agencies he's in a
WhatsApp group with. Against that competition, a small operator sending "a few hundred emails a
day" is a rounding error, which is part of why inboxes are flooded and reply rates keep falling.
Illustrative, not a citable client stat.

**Waitlist numbers, as of this call:** ~52 signups, 11 in the UK. Sunday sends haven't performed
as well as other days (Jonathan's impression, not hard data — no real data yet either way).
Another waitlist push was scheduled for Thursday.

**Not yet actioned / unconfirmed:**
- No specific numbers or client names were given for any of the four cock-up stories beyond what's
  above — Tom Wood and John Pye Auctions are named, the wine/lockbox and no-mobile stories are not
  tied to a specific person. Treat all four as real Jonathan-told anecdotes, safe to use as
  "we learned this the hard way" stories, but don't embellish with invented specifics (dates,
  exact figures beyond what's stated) if writing them up.
- The registered-office and seniority-threshold ideas are Jonathan's own proposed lead-score
  inputs — not confirmed as built or scheduled features. Frame in copy as "here's a mistake that's
  shaping what we're building," not as an existing live feature, unless separately confirmed.
- "100+ staff → don't target the owner" is Jonathan's own rough rule of thumb from this one
  incident, not a tested threshold.

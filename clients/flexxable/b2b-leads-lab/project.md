## What it is
Dan Wardrope's second front. A Skool community teaching people to land meetings with 8, 9 and 10 figure companies using **"AI Enriched" Lumpy Mail** — physical gifts (whiskey bottles, letters) mailed to B2B prospects to force a reply.

- URL: skool.com/b2b-gifts
- As of 2026-08-19: **254 members, 18 online, 5 admins**
- Currently FREE. Paid tier planned end of Oct, running alongside the free option.
- Face of it: Dan Wardrope.

**Mechanism to own: "AI Enriched Lumpy Mail."** That phrase is the whole thing — physical gift as the pattern interrupt, AI as the enrichment/personalisation layer. Everything upstream sells that mechanism.

**Offer name, locked 2026-08-20: "The 4-Hour Dream 100 Shortcut."** Modeled on Travis Sago's "30
Year Wealth Shortcut" naming pattern, but flipped — names the NEW fast time-to-execute rather than
the old slow cost being escaped. **Verified fact: 4 hours is Joey's real time-to-execute for one
send** (prospect → package in the post, AI doing the research/personalisation) — confirmed
directly by Joey after I flagged that "2-Hour" wasn't a verified number. Safe to cite "4 hours" as
a real proof point in copy going forward. Ties to the existing "Dream 100" and "coffee date"
vocabulary already live in shipped copy.

**Avatar / TAM (2026-08-20, Joey's correction): anyone whose job is landing meetings with
high-value prospects — NOT just agency owners or people trying to quit a job.** This includes
GTM roles inside big tech/SaaS companies, and sales teams in banking, recruitment, etc. — happily
employed 9-5ers who'd use this to hit their own numbers, not escape anything. Very big TAM.
**Do not use "escape the 9-to-5" / "quit your job" framing in B2B Leads Lab copy or villain-naming —
that's the ROYA/AAA-Ninjas audience (which genuinely is chasing an exit), not this one.** Easy
mistake to make since both are Dan Wardrope Skool groups in the same file tree — check which
group's copy you're writing before reaching for identity/escape angles.

### What the group actually looks like inside
Content is tactical and show-your-work. Two live examples:
- Dan's "Three Pronged Attack Part 2" webby replay — the real-life "coffee date" close on tape.
- A member's "My Whiskey Letter — Group Feedback" post: he tracks businesses on radio and billboards, opens on a lost-calls number that changes per city.

That's the tone: whiskey, letters, coffee dates, tracked numbers. Not agency-speak. Useful when writing anything that points at the group — the promise is *land the meeting*, not *build the agency*.

## Platform notes

**2026-08-19 — Skool text fields cap at ~1000 characters, and the in-app counter isn't a plain character count.** Wrote the About-page description at 1148 chars by a straight Python `len()` count; Skool's own counter read 1148 too on the *original swipe* length but landed differently once emoji/dashes were swapped — budget roughly 2% under whatever your own count says, or just paste into the field and read the live counter before calling it done. Bit us once on the About-page description draft — check before writing long-form for any Skool text field (About page, post body, bio).

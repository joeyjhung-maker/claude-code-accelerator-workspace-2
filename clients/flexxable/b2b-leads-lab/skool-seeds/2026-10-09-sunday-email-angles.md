# B2B Leads Lab — Sunday email angles — 2026-10-09

Mode: Pointed. 20 angles for the Sunday 11 Oct email to the B2B group. Pure value after the Topa
LinkedIn campaign. Seeds only, no copy. Checked against everything shipped to this room up to
2026-10-09 (including Friday's signals post).

Format note: email, so body only and it ends with the `Dan` sign-off. A homework close in an email
becomes "hit reply".

"Check first" = the outside fact needs verifying before it is briefed. "In the files" = the proof
is already verified in the project or seed bank.

## Safer

**E1 — You found the signal. Now what do you SAY?** Follow-on to Friday's post: the three parts of
a follow-up DM that names the signal without sounding like a stalker. Source: Friday's signals
post; Travis's "posts lead on from the one before" (SK20, 2026-09-02).

**E2 — The Monday morning test.** If a prospect replied tomorrow, do you already know the small,
safe first offer you'd put in front of them? Source: SK22, 2026-09-24. In the files.

**E3 — The internal-forward test.** If they can't explain your idea to their COO in one sentence,
it dies when it gets passed up. Source: SK5, 2026-09-25 (Mateusz interview).

**E4 — Your first 10 gifts are a test batch.** Send 10, learn whether the list, the letter or the
offer is broken while the mistake is still cheap. Source: SK1, 2026-09-25. In the files.

**E5 — The no-reply postmortem.** Five things to check before you blame the channel: delivered,
right person, right address, follow-up route, reason to reply. Source: SK8, 2026-09-25.

**E6 — "Did you get the wine?"** The gift doesn't replace the follow-up call. It gives you the
easiest opening line you'll ever have. Source: SKM6, Matt interview. In the files.

**E7 — Sent it to the wrong person? Good.** How to turn a gift that landed on the wrong desk into
an introduction to the right one. Source: SK9, 2026-09-25.

## Big swings

**E8 — Creativity is dangerous before you have data.** Matt's proven letter got replies. His
clever rewrite took the next 18 sends to near zero. Source: SKM3. Verified in the files.

**E9 — The campaign that worked and still landed nobody.** 14 bottles, 4 coffee dates, 0 clients.
The outreach won, so the leak was somewhere else. Source: SKM1 / SKM14. Verified in the files.

**E10 — A call with an eight-figure decision-maker for about $150.** Stop pricing the postage.
Price the conversation. Source: SKM15. Verified in the files.

**E11 — Stop personalising. Start being relevant.** A fact off their website proves you looked.
Only a commercial problem gives them a reason to reply. Source: SK4, 2026-09-25.

**E12 — Chase businesses whose stock goes off at midnight.** Empty appointment slots, unbooked
rooms, unsold seats. Yesterday's gap can never be sold tomorrow, so they feel the pain daily.
Source: SK2, 2026-09-25 (adventure-park experience).

**E13 — Your offer is why they're not replying.** 40% list, 40% offer, 20% copy. Most people
rewrite the 20%. Source: verified 40/40/20 principle in project.md.

**E14 — The best lead on your list already said no.** A "no" from six months ago is a known
contact with a known objection. Go back when a signal shows something has changed. Source:
Friday's signals post plus the reactivation doctrine. No stats.

## Wild

**E15 — Where "Dream 100" actually came from.** Chet Holmes, working for Charlie Munger, went
after a short list of the biggest advertisers and kept going through months of nothing. Check
first: the exact numbers (167 advertisers, time to first deal, sales doubling).

**E16 — The A-pile and the B-pile.** Gary Halbert's idea that everyone sorts their mail over a bin
into two piles, and only one gets opened. A package is never B-pile. Check first: Halbert's
wording.

**E17 — What casinos know about gifts.** They don't hand out free rooms to be nice. They work out
what a guest is worth first, then decide the gift. Do the same sum before you choose what goes in
the box. Check first: how comps are calculated.

**E18 — The drug rep and the sandwich platter.** Pharma reps couldn't get five minutes with a
doctor, so they brought lunch for the whole office. The gift got them past the front desk. Check
first: the specifics, and that the tone doesn't read as bribery (see SK15 on gift size).

**E19 — Ben Franklin borrowed a book from a man who hated him.** Asking a rival for a small favour
turned him into a friend. Why a tiny ask can warm a prospect faster than a big gift. Check first:
the story as told in his autobiography.

**E20 — The Trojan Horse was the first lumpy mail.** Nobody wheels an email inside the walls. A
playful one: obviously a myth, used for the laugh and the point that curiosity opens the gate.
No fact check needed, but the "trick" overtone needs a light hand.

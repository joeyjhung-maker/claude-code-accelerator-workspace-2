## Ideas

**V1. Test the lock before you buy a batch.** The team's own mistake: some wine bottles didn't fit the lock. Buy one gift and one lock first, test them together, then buy at volume. Short, funny, practical.
Reply prompt: "What are you sending? Drop the gift below."
Source: course video L6/L7 transcripts, SK30.

**V2. Your screen goes to sleep and Claude stops.** Setting up a Dream 100 with Claude in Chrome: screen slept after 5 minutes and it stopped every time. Set sleep to an hour. Plus the second gotcha: keep lists to about 100 companies per chat.
Reply prompt: "How many companies are on your Dream 100 so far? Number below."
Source: course notes section 2, video transcript line 58, SK27. Tool-forward but a pure tip. The presenter is a woman, so this is "we/the team" learnings, not Dan's first person.

**V3. Two sales a month vs a cat hotel (niche sanity check).** Annuity brokers at 5k a month: that's only two sales and they've covered it. Cat hotels: low first order. Will this niche pay you?
Reply prompt: "What would ONE sale be worth in your niche? Number below."
Source: L1 transcript, SK41.

**V4. Why the letter begs them to tell you to go away.** The lock turns a no into a phone call, and a phone call is all you needed. "Call me even if it's just to tell me to go away."
Reply prompt: guess game: "What do you think happens to most of the calls that start with 'go away'?" (answer in comments, Travis shape). Needs Dan/Nat's real answer, not invented.
Source: every letter template, SK26.

**V5. Send the imperfect letter.** The letter is a working document, rewritten from real objections. Waiting for perfect means zero bottles out.
Reply prompt: "What's the one thing stopping your first send? One line."
Source: L7, SK37.

**V6. Track every send like a test.** Log letter length, lock code, gift cost, postage. After 10 sends you know which niche, letter and gift win.
Reply prompt: set up the sheet, comment "SHEET". (Domino-assignment.) Check the tracking sheet is in the classroom.
Source: L7, SK40.

**V7. Three checks nobody thinks to make before you post it.** Restaurants and salons often closed Mon/Tue. LinkedIn location vs office (London office, Scotland profile). Never send to the same decision maker twice (different ones in a big business is fine).
Reply prompt: "Which one would have caught you out? 1, 2 or 3."
Source: verify lesson + L6 transcript. Overlaps the earlier address/proximity posts, so lead with the open-days and same-person-twice tips.

**V8. 200 free decision-maker mobiles and emails a month.** Redeem the code under Billing, then Manage Plan, and the free plan gives 200 lead downloads a month.
Reply prompt: "Done it? Comment YES."
Source: course notes section 2. Check first: the 19 Oct Topa click-driver left B2B out on purpose. Joey to say if the code tip goes in the B2B room.

**V9. "Are they hiring salespeople?" is a buying signal.** A firm hiring reps is telling you it wants more deals. Partly covered by the 9 Oct signals post, so use only if it's a different angle (hiring as a DREAM 100 filter, not a follow-up trigger).
Reply prompt: "Name one company hiring reps in your niche right now."
Source: verify deck, transcript line 86, SK29.

## Claude's pick
1. **V1 (test the lock):** the only one with a real "we" mistake. Funny, short, and the reply prompt is easy.
2. **V2 (screen sleep):** a gotcha people hit on day one and a single sentence of advice.
3. **V3 (cat hotel):** vivid, and the reply prompt asks for a number they already know.

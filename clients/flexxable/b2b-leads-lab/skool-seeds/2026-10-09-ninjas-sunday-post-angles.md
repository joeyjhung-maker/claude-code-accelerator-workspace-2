## Safer

**N1 — The 48-hour rule.** Let the client's sales team have first crack at a lead. After their
window closes, the AI picks up everything they didn't close. Dan's own tip under Jack's post.
Source: `clients/flexxable/member-proof-2026-09-27-jack-brandt.md`, "Dan's replies". In the files.

**N2 — The Found-Money hunt.** Where money is already trapped in a business: old leads, dead
quotes, lapsed customers, missed calls. Pick one pool and pitch that, not "AI automation". Fits a
10-minute homework close. Source: SKN4, 2026-09-11.

**N3 — How to price an AI service without guessing.** Work back from what one call, appointment or
recovered sale is worth to the client. Source: SKN6, 2026-09-11.

**N4 — The campaign autopsy.** Don't call a campaign dead. Find the first broken stage and fix
only that. Source: SKN1, 2026-09-11.

**N5 — The Green M&M scorecard.** Six things that tell you a prospect is worth the chase, and
permission to bin the rest. Source: SKN3, 2026-09-11 (coaching-call bank).

**N6 — Diagnose before you prescribe.** Run the coffee date like a doctor: symptoms first, then
the expensive leak, then the painkiller. Source: SKN5, 2026-09-11.

**N7 — How a non-techie vets a tech partner.** One small job with a deadline before you trust
them with anything big. Source: SKN15. The answer was Leandro's, so credit him.

## Big swings

**N8 — Performance isn't the risky deal. It's the EASY sell.** Jack's own reason for switching:
"primarily to reduce the friction." A client who pays only on results has nothing to argue about.
Source: Jack's comment to Fredrik, member-proof file. In the files.

**N9 — The slowest yes pays the most.** It took Jack 5 to 6 months to convince one client "they
had gold in their database." What to do in the months before they believe you. Source:
member-proof file. In the files.

**N10 — He's only touched a ninth of the list.** 50K of 450K. What that says about how much is
sitting in any decent database. Source: member-proof file. In the files.

**N11 — Why most agencies stall at the same client count.** Dan's read on the pattern. Source:
SK23, 2026-09-05. Needs Dan's actual take from Joey before it can be briefed.

**N12 — Sell it first. Build the parachute on the way down.** Stop building a delivery machine
nobody has bought yet. Source: the book (Sell First, Build Later), SK35.

**N13 — More bots, fewer sales.** When three automations chase the same lead, conversion drops.
Draw who owns each lead before you stack anything. Source: SKN14 (Gareth's case). Check the
numbers and attribution before use.

**N14 — "Our sales team already follows up" is the best objection you'll hear.** It tells you
exactly where the leak is: what happens to a lead after day three. Source: SKN8 (Nat's Lead
Rescue).

## Wild

**N15 — Acres of Diamonds.** The old story of the farmer who sold his land to go hunting for
diamonds, which turned up on the farm he'd sold. Every client's database is that farm. Check
first: the story as Russell Conwell told it.

**N16 — Gold in the waste pile.** Mining companies go back through their own old waste heaps
because the first pass missed gold. A dead lead list is the same heap. Check first: how tailings
reprocessing works.

**N17 — Be the no-win-no-fee lawyer of your niche.** They take a big slice and clients thank them
for it, because nobody pays unless they win. Check first: typical contingency terms, and keep it
as a picture, not a stat.

**N18 — Alec Guinness and the Star Wars points.** He reportedly thought the script was rubbish and
still took a share of the film over a bigger fee. Fee versus slice, told through an actor. Check
first: the actual deal.

**N19 — Storage unit auctions.** People bid real money on a locked unit full of someone else's
forgotten stuff. Your client owns the unit and has never opened the door. Playful picture, no
fact check needed.

**N20 — The "you up?" text.** A two-line message to someone who already knows you beats a perfect
pitch to a stranger. Cheeky lens on why the 2-line SMS works. Needs a light hand.

- Their own appointment-setting agency website, landing page, VSL, affiliate links and emails, as one "ecosystem" that gets them bought in.

- **Students get it before the upper tier,** on purpose. When they show prospects the bottle lead magnet, Dan expects prospects to ask "can you do this for us?" That lands bigger clients and moves the students up a tier.
- **Branding:** for now, mid-tier students present as a "partner of Flexxable". Using Dan's name stays **upper tier only**, because those people are vetted. The risk is affiliates you don't control. Dan: "a challenge that I haven't thought too much about yet."

## Two avatars (new)

- **Mid tier:** make-money-online / BizOp people who **have tried to land B2B clients before** and know how hard it is. That's the only criterion. Not ecom. Coaches fit, especially the ones who can't get clients and the unhappy $5K coaches, like therapists and mindset coaches. "Give them something easier to sell."
- **Upper tier:** established businesses that want appointments. They build a Dream 100 and send bottles. They aren't interested in being affiliates.
- **The LinkedIn profile stays generic,** about selling the bottle concept. The signals campaign (Travis calls them "indicators of interest") targets keywords like "health coaching". **The niching happens in the DMs.**
- Dan's hunch: plenty of people who comment on taps will want the bottles sent for them. That makes the upper tier ("the 1497") an easy upsell.

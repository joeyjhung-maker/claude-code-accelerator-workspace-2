# Brief: NAP, the mystery shop. Prove their follow-up is broken, don't claim it.

**Job:** pure value for Wed 21 Oct. Mon and Tue were asks, so NO carrot, NO keyword, NO link, NO P.S. into a group. Give the tactic away so the reader can use it this weekend. Quietly sets up why follow-up matters (Wingman Lifetime runs 26 to 29 Oct). Do not mention Wingman.

**Avatar:** AI-agency builders and AI-deal people (AAA Ninjas, MDAD, the book list). They want to land clients with an AI follow-up offer and struggle to prove the prospect has a problem.

**The idea:** telling a prospect "your follow-up is broken" is a claim they can argue with. Showing them with THEIR OWN website is proof they can't. Proof before pitch.

**Source (Nat, coaching call, 10 Aug, verbatim):**
"Mystery shop under a fake name... put in a lead on the website, pick a really sociable time, like 9 o'clock on a Saturday night. Then say, hey, I just saved one of these for you, and it's worth 10 to 30 grand. All I'm asking for is X per month."

**Facts allowed (nothing else):**
- The tactic is Nat's. He shared it on one of our coaching calls. Dan is passing it on. Dan has NOT said he did it himself. Use "Nat" / "we", never "I did this" and never "I tried this".
- Step 1: put an enquiry in on the prospect's website under a fake name.
- Step 2: pick a really sociable time. Example: 9 o'clock on a Saturday night.
- Step 3: see how long it takes them to get back to you, if they do at all.
- Step 4: go back to them with "I just saved one of these for you, it's worth 10 to 30 grand" and ask for X per month. (The 10 to 30 grand is Nat's example of what one lead can be worth. Present it as an example.)
- The contrast: telling them their follow-up is bad vs showing them with their own data.
- It's a bit cheeky.

**Do NOT:** invent a prospect, a business type, a reply time, an outcome, a client who tried it, or what Dan or Nat saw. No "last week we...". No stats. Keep the action hypothetical ("picture it") or as instructions ("do this"), never as a real result. No fabricated reaction from a prospect.

**Reluctance to disarm (rubric rule):** it feels a bit sneaky. Name that, then flip it: you only asked the question a real customer would ask. The only thing you're testing is whether anyone answers.

**Close:** soft. A plain spoken instruction on what to do with it, e.g. try it on one prospect this weekend. No question bolted on, no keyword, no "comment below".

**Hard rules (Joey):**

- Headline opens a loop. NO stat stack. Numbers live in the body.

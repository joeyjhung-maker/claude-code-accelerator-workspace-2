# Brief: welcome email for Locked Gift guide opt-ins

Related: [[client]] [[dan-skool-voice]]

## The job

Write the welcome email Dan sends the moment someone opts in for the free guide.

Write it as Dan, first person, the same Dan as the samples you absorbed.

Give me **2 versions**, labelled VERSION 1 and VERSION 2. Each is 110 to 180 words.

Also give me **5 subject lines** at the top.

## What they just did

They were on this page and typed in their email:

> FREE TACTICAL GUIDE FOR AGENCY OWNERS, CONSULTANTS, B2B SALES PEEPS:
> How We're Getting CEOs Of $20M-$80M Companies To Call US… For Just $40 Bucks… Using "LOCKED GIFTS"!
> Get the EXACT 6-step process to booking more calls with your Dream 100 (PLUS: get access to our 350M+ lead database for FREE)

So they are expecting: the guide, the 6-step process, and the free lead database.

## What the email must do, in this order (Joey's correction, 2026-10-12)

This is a WELCOME email. Welcome first, guide second.

1. **Welcome them.** Open warm. Dan is glad they're here. Say who he is in a line or two, so a stranger knows whose email this is.
2. **Then give them the guide.** Write the link as: [GUIDE LINK]
3. **Tell them where the good stuff is.** The guide is in 5 short parts. The 6-step process and the free database access are in Part 5. The bit most people miss is in Part 3.
4. **One P.S.** that points to the free community, B2B Leads Lab: https://www.skool.com/b2b-leads-lab. Inside, Dan shares what we send, what's working and what's NOT.

The guide link is the main thing to click. The community is only in the P.S.

## Keep the open question open

The guide's big reveal is what goes inside every package besides the gift. Do NOT say what it is. Do not mention a letter in the body. 

You can tease it: the gift is not the clever bit, and Part 3 shows what is.

Do not mention letters anywhere, including the P.S.

## The only facts you may use

- 10 gifts sent. 6 calls booked. 3 clients closed. 60% response rate.
- 70 gifts sent. 6 new clients.
- A mate of Dan's in the US sent 14 and booked 4 calls with businesses doing $20M to $80M a year.
- A whole package costs around $40.
- The gift arrives locked. They have to call to get it unlocked.
- The guide is free and in 5 short parts.
- Dan Wardrope has spent 7 figures+ on advertising directly to business owners over the last couple of years.
- He is actively coaching 1288+ students on how to get in front of B2B businesses.
- B2B Leads Lab is a free community.

## Hard rules

- No made-up numbers, quotes, deadlines or scarcity.
- Do not promise that Dan reads or answers replies.
- Do not say how long the guide takes to read.
- No three abstract benefits in a row.
- No corporate words: leverage, solution, deliverable, onboarding, streamline.
- The welcome must sound like Dan, not like a company. No "Thank you for subscribing", no "We're thrilled to have you".
- Do not promise future emails, a schedule, or what he will send next.
- Never insult the reader.
- No headline. Emails are body only.
- End with the sign-off: Dan

## Voice

- Casual, cheeky, direct. Like he is texting a mate who owns a business.
- One sentence per line, blank line between. But not machine-gun fragments: mix short lines with fuller spoken sentences.
- CAPS on the one word per line he would stress out loud.
- Ellipses for pauses…
- He opens emails with "Dan here…" or "Yo yo!!"
- "ya" at most twice.

## Make the 2 versions different

- VERSION 1: short. Warm welcome, who Dan is, here's the guide, where to start.
- VERSION 2: a touch more personality in the welcome, and uses the result (10 sent, 6 calls, 3 clients) as the reason to open the guide today.

Output only the subject lines and the two versions. No notes.

# Skool brief — The real cost of 1,000 generic messages vs 20 deeply personal ones

**Room:** B2B Leads Lab.

**Job:** Pure-value teaching post. Make the reader rethink "scale" in outbound by comparing the
hidden cost of 1,000 generic messages with the upfront work and strategic value of 20 genuinely
relevant ones.

**Tap position:** T1. Hook-forward and useful, with no pitch, link, handraiser, or public
engagement question.

**Mechanic:** NAP / philosophy stance with one practical test.

**The Ask:** Private action only: before the next campaign, run the message through the
"swap-the-name test." If the company name can be replaced and the message still works unchanged,
it is generic.

**Avatar:** B2B sellers, agency owners, GTM people, recruiters and sales teams whose job is landing
meetings with high-value prospects. Do not use quit-the-job or agency-only framing.

**Core argument:**

- Sending is cheap; wasting a first impression with the right prospect is expensive.
- 1,000 generic messages may consume data, tools, inbox/domain reputation, sorting time and 1,000
  chances to be dismissed. These are conceptual costs, not measured campaign results.
- Twenty deeply personal messages demand more thinking upfront, but the research improves the
  target, the offer, the message and the follow-up.
- "Deeply personal" does NOT mean a fake AI compliment, a scraped podcast reference, or "I noticed
  your company does X." It means the message is specific to that company's situation, problem and
  useful outcome.
- A message should make less sense when the prospect's name is swapped. If it fits everyone, it
  feels written for no one.

**Truth constraints:** The 1,000-versus-20 numbers are a hypothetical contrast supplied by Joey,
not a result. Do not claim that 20 messages outperform 1,000, invent reply rates, calculate money
lost, or imply Dan ran a controlled test. No unverified numbers or outcomes.

**Approved related doctrine:** The established direct-mail principle "40% List / 40% Offer / 20%
Copy" is verified in the project file and may be used only if it sharpens the argument. Do not
overload the post with a second framework.

**Format:** Dan's spoken Skool voice. Headline, short paragraphs, one sentence per line in the
final, Dan sign-off. Around 250–330 words. No P.S. unless it adds a genuinely new beat. No CTA and
no generic question bolted onto the end.

**Source route:** Internal doctrine + the approved B2B Leads Lab stance against fake AI
personalisation. Write fresh.

**Cross-seed check:** This develops the existing narrow-pitch lesson into a cost-of-volume
argument. Do not retell the trampoline-park scene or duplicate the earlier post's wording.

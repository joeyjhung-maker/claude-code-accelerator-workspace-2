# Skool brief — The outreach priority order

Room: B2B Gifts / B2B Leads Lab.

Job: Correct what members prioritise when building and improving lumpy-mail campaigns.

Tap position: T2. Tactical philosophy post with no promotional CTA.

Mechanic: Reveal a counterintuitive hierarchy and explain why beginners usually work in the reverse order.

The Ask: None. End with a practical instruction for diagnosing or improving the next campaign.

Avatar: Anyone responsible for landing high-value B2B prospects. No escape-the-9-to-5 framing.

Proof: Dan’s stated priority order in Matt’s interview was: “The data is the most important thing. The next most important thing… is the follow-up. Then you’ve got your offer… And the last thing really is the copy in this letter.” Source: `clients/flexxable/primers/lumpy-mail-bottle-coaching-call.md`, section “Priority order Dan gives.”

Core idea: Members often obsess over wording because it feels controllable and creative. But the campaign is decided more heavily by whether the right person receives it, whether the sender follows up persistently and whether the offer is worth discussing. Copy matters, but it cannot rescue a poor list, weak follow-up or irrelevant offer.

Format: Skool community post from Dan. Roughly 150–200 words. Headline, body and Dan sign-off. One sentence per line. Plain text only with no Markdown bold formatting.

Guidelines:
- Make the reverse-priority mistake vivid and slightly cheeky.
- Preserve the order exactly: data, follow-up, offer, copy.
- Do not invent percentages or results.
- Do not mention or link to Matt’s interview.
- No promotional CTA or generic engagement question.

Cross-seed check: No conflict. Pure-value teaching inside B2B Gifts.

Seed verbatim: Rank what matters in outreach: data first, follow-up second, offer third, letter copy last—most beginners obsess over the reverse order.

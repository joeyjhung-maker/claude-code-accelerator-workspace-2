# Brief: new lead for "The Locked Gift Strategy" lead magnet

Related: [[client]] [[cap-equals-cdcdcd]]

## The job

Write the NEW OPENING (the "lead") of Dan Wardrope's free lead magnet, a long Google Doc.

Write it as Dan, first person, the same Dan as the samples you absorbed.

Give me **3 different versions**. Each one is 120 to 200 words. Label them VERSION 1, VERSION 2, VERSION 3.

## Where it sits

The headline above it is already written and stays as it is:

> How We're Getting CEOs Of $20M-$80M Companies To Call Us For $40… Using "LOCKED GIFTS"!

Your lead goes straight under that headline.

The line that comes straight AFTER your lead is already written too. Your last line has to flow into it:

> And I've spent 7 figures + on advertising directly to business owners over the last couple of years.

After that, the doc goes into the story of how Dan found the idea, the proof, and the how-to. You do not write any of that.

## Who is reading

Agency owners, consultants and B2B sales people.

They just watched a short video of Dan holding a bottle of whisky with a lock on the cap.

So they already know there is a locked gift. They do NOT know why it gets people to call, or what the results are.

## The one thing this lead must do (feedback from Dan's mentor)

The old opening went straight into explaining the idea. That is logic first.

The new opening must hit the BENEFIT first. Make the reader want the result before you explain anything.

The mentor's pattern for it: a few "In a world where…" contrasts.

- What everyone else does and gets (lots of cold outreach, very little back).
- Then what we are getting from a tiny number of sends.

His best line, use it or something as good:

> We're getting more clients than most people get replies.

Then leave ONE open question so they keep reading. The open question is: the gift is not the clever bit, so what is? (It is the letter that goes with it, but do NOT say that. Do not mention the letter at all.)

## The only facts you may use

These are real. Use only these numbers. Do not change them and do not add any.

- 10 gifts sent. 6 calls booked. 3 clients closed. 60% response rate.
- 20 gifts sent. 7 replies. 35% response rate.
- 70 gifts sent. 25% response rate. 6 new clients.
- A mate of Dan's in the US sent 14 and booked 4 calls, with businesses doing $20M to $80M a year.
- A whole package costs around $40.
- The gift arrives locked. They have to call to get it unlocked.

You do not have to use every one. Pick the ones that hit hardest. The 10 / 6 / 3 result is the strongest.

## Hard rules

- **No made-up numbers.** No cold email reply rates, no cost-per-call figures, no "studies show".
- The "what everyone else does" side must be clearly a picture, not a statistic. For example "you could fire off hundreds of cold emails and hear nothing back". Never state it as measured data.
- Do not explain HOW it works. No steps. No letter. No list building. No tools.
- Do not mention any software, database, coupon code, or the community. That all comes later in the doc.
- Do not open with a question.
- Do not open on pain. Open on the result. The pain is only there to make the result look bigger.
- No three abstract benefits in a row (like "more leads, more calls, more clients").
- No corporate words: leverage, solution, deliverable, onboarding, streamline, unlock your potential.
- Do not invent any backstory for Dan. No scenes he did not describe above.

## Voice

- Dan is an Aussie living in the UK. Casual, cheeky, direct. Like he is texting a mate who owns a business.
- Short lines. One sentence per line. Blank line between each.
- But NOT machine-gun fragments. Mix short lines with fuller spoken sentences.
- CAPS on the words he would stress out loud.
- Ellipses for pauses…
- He uses :-) and "bloody" and "Yo yo!!" and "Dan here :-)".
- "ya" at most twice.
- His greeting "Yo yo!! Dan here :-)" can come AFTER the first hook lines. It does not have to be line one.

## Make the 3 versions properly different

- VERSION 1: lead with the 10 / 6 / 3 result and the "more clients than most people get replies" contrast.
- VERSION 2: lead with the picture of a big-company CEO picking up the phone to call YOU, for the price of a $40 package.
- VERSION 3: your own best idea. Surprise me.

Output only the three versions. No notes, no explanations.

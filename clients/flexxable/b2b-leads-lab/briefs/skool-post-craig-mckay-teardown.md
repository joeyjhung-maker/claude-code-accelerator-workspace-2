# Brief: Dan Skool post — Craig McKay whiskey-letter teardown

**Where this posts:** B2B Leads Lab Skool group, as Dan, community post (not an email, not an ad).

**Offer/mechanism:** AI Enriched Lumpy Mail — "The 4-Hour Dream 100 Shortcut." Physical gift (whiskey bottle, letter) as pattern interrupt, AI as the enrichment/personalisation layer. This post is not selling the offer directly — it's a teaching/teardown post that reinforces the mechanism by showing what makes a lumpy-mail letter actually work.

**Avatar:** Broad TAM — NOT just agency owners or people escaping a job. Includes GTM roles at big tech/SaaS companies, sales teams in banking/recruitment — happily employed people who'd use this to hit their own numbers. **Do not use "escape the 9-to-5" / "quit your job" framing.** That's the ROYA/AAA-Ninjas audience, not this one.

**The angle:** A real member, Craig McKay, posted his own whiskey-letter draft (HVAC angle — a locked whiskey bottle, the code comes via a phone call, he tracks target businesses off radio/billboards) into the group asking for feedback. Dan gave him real, live, numbered feedback. This post turns that real feedback into a public teaching post for the whole group — a "teardown" showing HOW Dan thinks about fixing a letter, using the actual critique already given.

**The 6 real points to build the post around — use these verbatim as the teaching content, do not invent additional critique points or soften/change what Dan actually said:**

1. Craig claimed "12+ calls lost a month" with nothing backing it up. Dan's note: "Can you add a line of proof/evidence of where this comes from? That would beef it up." — Fix: back up specific numbers with a proof line, don't just assert them.

2. Craig wrote "Based on your call volume and the response windows I found, your number sits in that range" — an overclaim if he can't actually prove it. Dan's note: the wording "I found" is strong unless backed by definitive proof; if the prospect reads it and thinks "that's not me," the deal's lost right there. Dan's suggested fix: hedge it — "based on XYZ, I believe you might be losing X" — to soften without losing the point.

3. Craig asked for the 10-minute call too early (paragraph 3), before the prospect was sold on why they should work with him. Dan's note: move it to the end, or remove it from that spot.

4. Dan rewrote the whole "we're looking to partner with one HVAC business per city" paragraph into: "I've done my research on [Company]. And based on my findings on your potential call volume and the response windows for a company like yours… your missed calls number probably sits in that range? With a few specific tweaks it's possible to plug this gap so that you BENEFIT, BENEFIT, BENEFIT." — ends on a softer question, not a hard claim, then bridges into stacked benefits.

5. Craig's "here's how we're different from every other offer on your desk today" line had nothing backing it. Dan's fix: "One business using our systems was able to (insert client result or use white-label case studies number). We like to put our money where our mouth is, so here's how we do things differently:" then the real differentiator — "You pay only when a job lands in your calendar."

6. Craig's close ("every day your calls go to voicemail... that's real money... gone... every single month you don't act") builds the reader up then pulls the rug out with a shame-based close right before the ask. Dan's note: "This feels a bit off as a close. You've got them all excited and then pulled the rug out and pushed the shame dial a bit. I would move higher and soften. Or remove and keep the energy high here so they're excited to call you."

**Proof (verbatim, do not invent or round):** Craig's own draft claimed 12+ lost service calls a month for an average HVAC company (his working number, not independently verified — present it as Craig's number, not a fact Dan is vouching for). No other numeric proof for THIS post — the post is about the craft of writing the letter, not a new campaign-result claim.

# Brief: AAA Ninjas teaser -> Dream 100 Scorecard pillar post

**Where this posts:** AAA Ninjas Skool group, as Dan. This is a cross-group handraiser: people
comment, then the team DMs them the B2B Leads Lab pillar-post link so they can join that group and
read it.

**Asset being gated:** "The Dream 100 Scorecard (Don't Mail Until They Pass This!)". It is a
practical five-check filter for deciding which companies on a Dream 100 shortlist are actually
worth the money and time involved in sending lumpy mail.

**Job of this post:** Make the scorecard feel valuable enough that AAA members raise their hands.
Teach WHY the filtering step matters, but do not reveal the checklist labels or explain how to run
the test. The five checks are the gated payload. Sell access to the pillar post, not the group
mechanics.

**Structural swipe:** Mirror the persuasive sequence of Joey's shipped AAA Dream 100 teaser at
`copy/2026-09-02-aaa-ninjas-skool-post-dream100-teaser.md`:

1. Named free asset in the headline.
2. Establish that WHO they target determines whether the campaign has a chance.
3. Explain why guessing gets expensive, especially with lumpy mail.
4. Introduce the asset without revealing its five checks.
5. State the practical payoff: stop wasting packages/time on companies or contacts that cannot
   buy, cannot be reached, or are a poor budget fit.
6. One comment-keyword CTA.
7. Dan sign-off and a useful P.S.

Mirror the argument and information density closely, not every sentence. This should feel like the
next post in the same proven series.

**Approved source facts — do not add others:**

- Lumpy mail costs real money and time to put together.
- The scorecard contains five checks, worth one point each.
- The checks are: right person with buying authority; current physical address that reaches them;
  verified mobile and email before mailing; a real buying signal; and budget fit.
- Dan would only mail to people with all five points checked.
- Examples of bad targets from the pillar: a generic inbox or reception; a PO box, co-working front
  desk, or stale directory address; a company whose site, reviews, or team size indicate a size
  mismatch.
- Do not invent a campaign result, quote, or numerical payoff.

**Avatar:** AAA Ninjas agency owners trying to land worthwhile B2B clients. Keep it useful beyond
lumpy mail where natural, but do not dilute the asset: it exists to stop people mailing the wrong
prospect.

**The ask:** Comment **"SCORECARD"** and the team will get them access. Do not name B2B Leads Lab
or explain that they need to join another group in the public post.

**Voice and format:**

- Dan's fast, conversational Skool voice.
- Short punchy paragraphs, one sentence per line in the final.
- Concrete, not abstract; playful only where it helps.
- Headline, Dan sign-off, and a P.S. that reinforces the decision rule.
- Around 180-240 words.
- No corporate language and no invented proof.

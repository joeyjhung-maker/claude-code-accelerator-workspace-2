# Skool brief — Copywriters first, bot builders second

Room: AAA Ninjas.

Job: Teach members not to accept “the leads are dead” as the automatic diagnosis after a failed AI reactivation campaign. The message, offer, channel, timing and conversation may have failed before the database did.

Tap position: T2. Tactical teaching with no promotional CTA.

Mechanic: NAP.

The Ask: Before walking away from a previously worked database, separate the list from the campaign and propose a small, risk-reversed test using a different conversation. This is a private action, not a comment ask.

CTA type: None — pure-value teaching post.

Avatar: AAA Ninjas members selling DBR and other AI services who may be discouraged when a prospect says another provider already tried AI on the database and failed.

Proof and source facts supplied by Joey from Richard Challenor's Skool post and the replies:
- Richard spoke through LinkedIn with the managing director of a large claims company.
- The company had previously given another provider 10,000 old leads.
- The provider used AI voice calls, WhatsApp and messaging and achieved virtually no uptake.
- That provider said the leads had been worked very hard and there was nothing left.
- The leads were all from within the previous year.
- The company generated them through Facebook ads, Google ads and door-to-door activity, and it continues receiving new enquiries.
- Dan first asked how the leads were generated and whether the company generates new leads every day.
- Dan's proposed positioning was: “It sounds like your data has been quite well worked. However we are copywriters and direct response marketers FIRST, and bot builders SECOND. It doesn't matter how good the AI is if the lead doesn't respond. How about we take a few thousand of these leads, and run them through our system. After all, we take all the risk?”
- The Done For You Build reply added that often the issue is how leads were reactivated; their system uses simple SMS conversations rather than blasting, and recommends testing a small batch instead of all 10,000.

Core argument: A failed bot campaign does not isolate the cause of failure. The list may be exhausted, but the copy, offer, channel, timing or conversational approach may have killed response first. AI cannot rescue a message nobody answers. Diagnose the source and ongoing lead flow, then test a small batch with a different direct-response conversation and remove the prospect's risk. If the test fails, the conclusion is earned rather than inherited from the previous provider.

Format: Skool community post from Dan. Roughly 250–350 words. Headline, body, practical close and Dan sign-off. Plain text only with no Markdown bold formatting. Use a non-breaking space on every visually blank spacer line in the user-facing version so Google Docs preserves the gaps. One sentence per line with varied length; avoid machine-gun fragments.

Guidelines:
- Open with the concrete 10,000-lead failure or a sharp diagnosis, not a first-line question.
- Do not promise that the old leads will work or insult the previous provider without evidence.
- Preserve Dan's central line: copywriters and direct-response marketers FIRST, bot builders SECOND.
- Make the distinction between “bad list” and “bad campaign” easy to understand.
- Explain that simple SMS conversations can outperform blasting because response depends on the message and approach, but do not invent performance numbers.
- De-risk the next move: test a small batch, not all 10,000, and take the performance risk.
- If mentioned, ongoing fresh enquiries create another possible route such as speed-to-lead, but keep the main spine on diagnosis and testing rather than listing offers.
- Use “ya” no more than twice.

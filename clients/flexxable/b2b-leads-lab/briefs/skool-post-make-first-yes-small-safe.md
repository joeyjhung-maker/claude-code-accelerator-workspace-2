# Skool brief — Make the first yes small and safe

Room: B2B Gifts / B2B Leads Lab.

Job: Teach members how to reduce resistance on the first coffee date.

Tap position: T2. Practical sales insight with no promotional CTA.

Mechanic: Philosophy stance using a simple dating or marriage metaphor if it fits naturally.

The Ask: None. Finish with a concrete instruction members can use on their next coffee date.

Avatar: Anyone responsible for landing high-value B2B clients. No escape-the-9-to-5 framing.

Proof and mechanism: In Matt’s interview, Dan recommends closing on a small paid test with $500–$750 of skin in the game rather than pushing immediately for a standard retainer. The service is positioned on a performance basis so the provider wins when the client wins. The small test lowers risk but avoids a completely free trial. Source: `clients/flexxable/primers/lumpy-mail-bottle-coaching-call.md`, sections “The system, in order” and “What killed Matt’s results.”

Core idea: The first sale should not require the prospect to make a giant leap of faith. Ask for a small paid commitment, prove the service works, then allow the result to justify the larger relationship. Small and safe does not mean free; some skin in the game keeps the prospect committed.

Format: Skool community post from Dan. Roughly 150–200 words. Headline plus body and Dan sign-off. One sentence per line. Plain text only, with no Markdown bold formatting.

Guidelines:
- Make the mistake and alternative easy to picture.
- Do not imply $500–$750 is mandatory for every offer; it is Dan’s example structure.
- Do not mention or link to Matt’s interview.
- Do not promote a Flexxable product.
- Keep one clean sales spine and Dan’s playful personality.

Cross-seed check: No conflict. Pure-value sales teaching inside B2B Gifts.

Seed verbatim: Make the first yes tiny and safe: a paid performance-based test reduces commitment while ensuring the prospect has skin in the game.

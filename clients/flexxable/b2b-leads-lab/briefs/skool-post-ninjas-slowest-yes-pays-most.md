# Skool brief — The slowest yes pays the most (AAA Ninjas)

**Room:** AAA Ninjas (AI Automation Agency Ninjas, Skool).

**Job:** Nurture with wisdom. Joey, 2026-10-16: "solves a problem/recurring issue/shines a light
and offers wisdom as Travis puts it to help them move forward."

**The recurring problem:** Ninjas pitch a business owner on reactivating their old leads (DBR).
The owner doesn't say yes straight away. They say "maybe", "not right now", "let me think", or go
quiet. Most people take that as a no and move on to the next prospect. They give up on the very
deals that end up paying the most.

**The light to shine:** A slow yes is not a no. The owner isn't saying "no", they're saying "I
don't believe you yet". Belief takes time when you're telling someone there's money sitting in a
list they've written off. The ones that take longest to believe often turn into the biggest
clients, because once they DO believe it, they back it properly.

**Proof (real, from Jack Brandt's own post in this group, 27 Sept; Ninjas already saw a post
about him on 9 Oct, so keep it short and don't re-tell the whole story):**
- It took Jack 5 to 6 months to convince one client "they had gold in their database."
- That client had 450K people in their database.
- 7 weeks after going live: $432,252 from 372 sales (his dashboard).
- Use only these. Do not invent what Jack said or did during those 5 to 6 months.

**What to do while they make up their mind (Dan's own teaching, from the Matt interview and the
offer doc; write it as Dan's advice, not as what Jack did):**
- Follow up "like a dog with a bone". Don't let a good prospect go until they actually say no.
- Mix the channels: a call, a short Loom showing a result, an email, a WhatsApp or SMS.
- Lower the bar to yes: offer a small, no-risk test on a slice of their old leads, paid on
  results. Make it easy to say "go on then."
- Keep it about THEIR money sitting in THEIR list, not about AI.

**The Ask:** Soft close, no keyword (Dan rubric default for NAPs). Something that sends them back
to a prospect they gave up on, e.g. "Who's your slow yes? Go back to 'em this week."

**Avatar:** AAA Ninjas members selling AI services (mostly DBR) to business owners. Some have had
a few "maybes" and are tempted to quit on them.

**Do NOT say:** any revenue or close-rate claim beyond Jack's numbers above; that Jack's
performance deal is signed (it was "in the works"); Jack's $22K forecast; anything about Topa.
Dan doesn't claim his own slow-yes story unless it's in the files (it isn't).

**Format:** Skool community post as Dan. Headline, short spoken lines, about 180 to 280 words.
No sign-off on the Skool version. Aussie, warm, wise rather than hypey: the Travis
"shine a light" register.

**Source route:** Seed N9 plus Dan's doctrine. Real Jack facts kept exact.

# Registered office ≠ real office

**Avatar (room-locked):** broad TAM — anyone whose job is landing meetings with high-value
prospects, not just agency owners escaping a job. No "escape the 9-5" framing.

**The angle:** A gift sent to the wrong kind of address never lands — and it's an easy mistake to
make, because a registered office LOOKS like a real address on paper (it's what's filed publicly),
but it's often just an accountant's office or a mail-forwarding service, nobody there to receive
anything. The lesson: your list is only as good as the address behind it. Address-type is exactly
the kind of thing worth checking before a send, not after.

**The true story to open on** (verified, `topa-call-2026-09-08-content-stories.md` lines 12-24):
one of our fulfilment partners sent a bottle to the owner of a well-known car auction platform —
a big-money name, multi-millionaire. The address on file was his company's *registered office*,
not a real working office. The bottle never arrived. He got the guy on a cold call afterward
anyway, but without the bottle having landed, couldn't pull him round — no meeting.

**The transition:** This is why address-type belongs in the score before a send goes out, not
just company size or title — a registered office should flag red, same as any other "looks right,
isn't" trap in a Dream 100 list.

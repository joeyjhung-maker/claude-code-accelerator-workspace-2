# Skool brief — Don’t let AI write your first impression

Room: B2B Leads Lab.

Job: Deepen engagement by warning members against mailing untouched AI-generated letters that make them look generic or lazy.

Tap position: T2. Timely current-event teaching with a practical quality-control instruction, but no promotional CTA or generic engagement question.

Mechanic: NAP. Use LinkedIn’s new “Seems Like AI Slop” button as the opening current-event hook, then transfer the lesson to physical prospecting letters.

The Ask: None. End with a concrete instruction to read the letter aloud, remove generic AI language and add enough genuine personality that it sounds like the sender.

Avatar: Anyone responsible for landing meetings with high-value B2B prospects. Do not use escape-the-9-to-5 framing.

Proof: LinkedIn reported on 15 September 2026 that more than one million members had used its new “Seems Like AI Slop” feedback button, while its recent efforts had reduced views of content classified as AI slop by 40%. Source: https://news.linkedin.com/2026/how-linkedin-is-tackling-ai-slop

Core idea: Members will naturally want AI to write their lumpy-mail letter because it is fast and convenient. The danger is sending the untouched output. The letter is often the prospect’s first meaningful impression of the sender; generic phrases, fake enthusiasm and obvious machine-personalisation can immediately place the sender in the same mental box as every low-effort cold email. AI can perform research, surface details and help create a rough draft, but a human must own the final voice.

Dan’s stance: Dan puts personality into everything, including his community posts and letters. Members do not need to imitate his exact level of slang, jokes or exaggeration. They do need to sound recognisably human and like themselves.

Format: Skool community post from Dan. Roughly 180–240 words. Headline, body and Dan sign-off. One sentence per line, but vary sentence length and avoid extended runs of tiny fragments. Plain text only with no Markdown bold formatting.

Guidelines:
- Make clear that LinkedIn’s button applies to LinkedIn content; the prospect is the equivalent judge of a physical letter.
- Do not claim AI-written letters never work or tell members to avoid AI entirely.
- Draw a firm distinction between AI-assisted research/drafting and mailing untouched output.
- Make the reputational cost vivid: the first impression feels generic, lazy or fake before a conversation begins.
- Include one or two recognisable examples of AI-slop language only if they sharpen the point.
- Do not pitch a product.
- Dan voice: direct, cheeky, practical and personally opinionated.

Cross-seed check: No conflict. Pure-value teaching inside B2B Leads Lab that reinforces responsible use of AI within the lumpy-mail mechanism.

Seed verbatim: LinkedIn’s “Seems Like AI Slop” button — could your latest post or outreach message survive the button?

User steering verbatim: Warn people against using AI to write their letters because they will want to do it. Be careful using AI to write a generic AI-slop letter: it is your first impression, and being placed in that box on the first outreach message will make you look bad. Dan always adds personality to everything, as seen in his posts, but members do not have to go as far as he does.

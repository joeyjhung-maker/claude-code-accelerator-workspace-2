# Skool brief — Give the prospect what they ask for

Room: AAA Ninjas.

Job: Teach members to listen for additional pain points after leading with DBR, then tailor an existing Android when the requested solution is simple and commercially sensible.

Tap position: T2. Tactical teaching with no promotional CTA.

Mechanic: NAP.

The Ask: On the next prospect call, listen for one additional pain the prospect names and decide whether an existing Android can solve it with a simple prompt or knowledge-base change. This is a private action, not a comment ask.

CTA type: None — pure-value teaching post.

Avatar: AAA Ninjas members selling AI services who know DBR is the reliable revenue-first lead-in but may overlook easy adjacent problems that can deepen and lengthen the client relationship.

Proof and source facts supplied by Joey from Eric Irvine's Skool post and Dan Wardrope's reply:
- Eric spoke with an ecommerce owner doing 20–40 sales per day with a customer database of more than 20,000 people.
- Eric will offer DBR because it is the bread-and-butter revenue solution and can make the client more money.
- The owner also identified a separate pain: he manually holds one-to-one customer conversations to learn what customers love, hate and want changed.
- The owner values the real connection and honest feedback, so the solution should scale the process without taking that away completely.
- Dan advised Eric to ask for the owner's feedback SOP, use it as the knowledge base for the Android, tailor the Social Replies Android to ask for feedback at scale, and send the owner the transcripts.
- Dan suggested a natural permission-based conversation that confirms the buyer and offers a 50% discount code in exchange for answering a few questions.
- This is a simple tailoring of an existing bot, not a fancy custom build.

Core argument: Lead with DBR because revenue is the most important need for nearly every business, but keep listening after the obvious sale. When a prospect plainly tells you another painful process they want fixed, give them what they want if an existing system can be adapted without a complicated custom build. The client feels heard, receives more value, the agency sells another useful solution, and the relationship becomes more likely to last.

Format: Skool community post from Dan. Roughly 250–350 words. Headline, body, practical close and Dan sign-off. Plain text only with no Markdown bold formatting. One sentence per line with varied sentence length; avoid machine-gun fragments.

Guidelines:
- Do not frame this around preparing or delivering a demo.
- Make DBR the first solution because it creates sales and money; do not imply customer feedback replaces it.
- Pivot into the second opportunity through the pain the prospect volunteered.
- State the rule plainly: if a prospect tells you what they want, give them what they want, provided it does not require an elaborate custom build that destroys fulfilment economics.
- Explain the exact solution Dan advised: get the existing SOP, use it as the knowledge base, tweak Social Replies Android to conduct feedback conversations, and return the transcripts.
- Include one concise example of the permission-based feedback opener if it helps make the solution tangible.
- Emphasise the win-win: more useful solutions and revenue for the agency; a heard, helped client who has more reasons to stay.
- Close with Joey's desired philosophy: keep your ears open, be helpful, solve problems, get paid.

Cross-seed check: No conflict. This is native AAA Ninjas teaching about selling and retaining AI-service clients and does not redirect members into B2B Leads Lab.

Cadence check: No public ask. This is another pure-value teaching post.

## One-breath lock

This is a pure-value T2 NAP for AAA Ninjas that keeps DBR as the universal revenue-first offer but teaches members to hear and solve the additional pains a prospect volunteers. Eric's ecommerce prospect needs customer feedback at scale, so Dan's advice is to adapt the existing Social Replies Android using the owner's SOP and return the transcripts rather than custom-building something elaborate. The reader leaves with a simple long-term-client philosophy: listen, adapt what already exists, solve more useful problems and get paid.

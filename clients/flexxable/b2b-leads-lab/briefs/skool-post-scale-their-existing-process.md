# Skool brief — Scale the process they already value

Room: AAA Ninjas.

Job: Teach members how to turn a prospect's manual process into a valuable AI demo instead of forcing the prospect into a prebuilt bot or feature menu.

Tap position: T2. Tactical teaching for members preparing a demo or diagnosis call, with no promotional CTA.

Mechanic: NAP.

The Ask: Before the next demo, ask for the prospect's current process or SOP, identify what they refuse to lose, then build one demo that preserves that value while doing the process at scale. This is a private action, not a comment ask.

CTA type: None — pure-value teaching post.

Avatar: AAA Ninjas members building an AI agency who can demo the available tools but may struggle to connect those tools to the prospect's real commercial need.

Proof and source facts supplied by Joey from Eric Irvine's Skool post and Dan Wardrope's reply:
- Eric spoke with an ecommerce owner doing 20–40 sales per day with a customer database of more than 20,000 people.
- The owner's real need was customer feedback. He was manually holding one-to-one conversations to learn what customers loved, hated and wanted changed.
- He enjoyed the human connection and honest feedback, so the answer could not simply remove that part of the process.
- Dan advised Eric to ask for the owner's feedback SOP, use it as the knowledge base for the AI Android, run the feedback conversations at scale and send the owner the transcripts.
- Dan also suggested using a natural permission-based opener and offering a discount code in exchange for answering a few questions.
- DBR and repeat-buyer campaigns were additional opportunities, but they are not the main lesson of this post.

Core argument: The best AI demo does not begin with the bot you want to sell. It begins with the job the prospect already values enough to do manually. Ask for the existing process, preserve the human outcome they care about, and automate the repetition or scale around it. The diagnosis determines the demo.

Format: Skool community post from Dan. Roughly 250–350 words. Headline, body, practical close and Dan sign-off. Plain text only with no Markdown bold formatting. One sentence per line with varied sentence length; do not machine-gun short fragments.

Guidelines:
- Open with a concrete contrast or diagnosis, not a first-line question.
- The prospect has effectively handed over the product brief: he wants more honest customer conversations without personally conducting every one.
- Explain why pitching a generic reviews bot or feature menu misses the point.
- Teach the Doctor's Diagnosis principle: understand the current process, desired outcome and non-negotiable human value before prescribing the automation.
- Include the practical move: ask for the SOP, train the Android on it, scale the conversations and return the transcripts/insights.
- Use one short example of a natural feedback opener, but do not let the script dominate the post.
- Keep the post focused on creative problem solving, not Wingman feature promotion.
- End with the transferable rule: do not demo what you built; demo a faster, larger version of what the prospect already wants.

Cross-seed check: No conflict. This is native AAA Ninjas tactical teaching and strengthens members' ability to close and deliver AI services without redirecting them into B2B Leads Lab.

Cadence check: No public ask. The recent tracked AAA Ninjas cadence includes both asks and pure-value posts; this adds another substantial teaching post without heating the ask ratio.

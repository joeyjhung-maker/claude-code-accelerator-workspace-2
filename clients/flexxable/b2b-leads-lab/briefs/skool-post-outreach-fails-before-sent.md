**The angle:** Most people think outreach fails because the offer's weak or the copy's bad. It usually fails earlier than that — the LIST is wrong. Wrong person, wrong company, wrong timing. No amount of a great offer or great copy survives being aimed at the wrong target. This is the case for why the "AI Enriched" research/targeting layer matters as much as the gift itself.

**The true story to open on — use these facts verbatim, do not invent or embellish beyond what's given:**

The 190th Fighter Squadron / Blues and Royals friendly-fire incident, 28 March 2003, near Ad Dayr, Iraq (about 25 miles north of Basra).

- Two US A-10 Thunderbolt pilots (Idaho Air National Guard) were flying close air support. NOT a rifle sniper — correct this if the draft calls them a "sniper"; they're pilots doing a strafing run. Use "pilots" or similar, not "sniper."
- Below them: a British Household Cavalry (the Blues and Royals) reconnaissance patrol, correctly marked with orange ID panels, thermal reflectors and Union flags, sitting inside a declared no-engagement zone.
- One pilot spotted the orange panels and did the responsible thing — asked ground control: "Do we have any friendlies up in this area?"
- Ground control answered: "That is an affirm. You are well clear of friendlies." Wrong answer.
- Pilots were still uneasy — described the panels as "orange rockets" — but engaged anyway on the strength of the clearance they'd been given. First pass, then a second pass.
- Lance Corporal of Horse Matty Hull was killed inside his Scimitar armoured vehicle. Five other British soldiers were wounded.
- Cockpit audio caught the moment they realised: "Ahh shit... looks we might have a blue on blue situation," and after learning there was a body: "We're in jail, dude."
- The 2007 coroner's inquest ruled it an unlawful killing. The coroner's own words: "the coordinates were not checked, identification of the British armoured convoy was not checked... this could have been entirely avoidable."
- No pilot or board of inquiry ever faulted HOW they flew or fired — the strike itself was executed exactly as briefed. The entire failure was upstream of the trigger: the intel they trusted was wrong.

**Do not name the pilots** — their identities were never officially released and sources conflict on this; only Matty Hull's name is solidly documented and safe to use.

**The transition (Dan's logic, build this out, don't just state it flat):** That's outreach. You can write the best letter in the world. You can send the most expensive gift in the world. If it lands on the wrong desk — wrong company, wrong title, wrong moment — none of it matters. The pilots didn't lose because they couldn't fly. They lost because someone told them the coordinates were clear when they weren't. Most outreach fails the same way — not in the pitch, before it. In the list.

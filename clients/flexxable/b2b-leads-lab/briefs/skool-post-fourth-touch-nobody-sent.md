# Skool brief — The fourth touch nobody sent

Room: B2B Gifts / B2B Leads Lab.

Job: Teach members that the first pitch rarely carries the whole sale and show why Dream 100 list-building creates a creative follow-up advantage.

Tap position: T2. Tactical philosophy post with a practical takeaway, not a promotional CTA.

Mechanic: Reframe lost deals as follow-up failures rather than pitch failures, then show how a fully enriched Dream 100 enables relevant multi-channel touches.

The Ask: None. End with a concrete instruction to plan the next several value-adding touches before the first send.

Avatar: Anyone responsible for landing meetings and clients from high-value B2B prospects. No escape-the-9-to-5 framing.

Current research:
- Do not repeat the unsupported universal claim that every sale requires exactly 11 touches.
- 6sense’s 2025 B2B Buyer Experience Report studied nearly 4,000 B2B buyers and found an average of 16 interactions per person with the winning vendor. These included content and human interactions; they were not necessarily 16 seller follow-ups.
- The same research found 95% of winning vendors were already on the buyer’s Day One shortlist, supporting the importance of becoming familiar and credible before the sales conversation.
- Gartner’s 2025 survey of 632 B2B buyers found 73% actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach. Therefore, the lesson is repeated relevant exposure, not repeated “just checking in” messages.

Core idea: Dream 100 work is not merely a mailing list. By collecting verified email, mobile, physical address and LinkedIn details before outreach, members can continue the relationship across channels with different useful touches: a locked gift, phone call, personal email, LinkedIn engagement, case study, Loom, relevant win, useful information or a different offer. Modern tools make this manageable. The prospect may not reject the pitch; the sender may simply disappear before familiarity and trust have accumulated.

Format: Skool community post from Dan. Roughly 200–280 words. Headline, body and Dan sign-off. One sentence per line, but vary sentence length so the rhythm does not become a run of tiny machine-gun fragments. Plain text only with no Markdown bold formatting.

Guidelines:
- Headline angle: “Why most deals are lost on the fourth touch nobody sent.”
- Explain that 16 interactions is a buying-journey average, not a command to send 16 cold messages.
- Make the alternative concrete and creative.
- Do not advocate spam, channel flooding or repetitive nudges.
- Do not mention a product or add a generic engagement question.
- Dan voice: cheeky, direct, energetic and practical.

Cross-seed check: No conflict. Pure-value B2B Leads Lab teaching.

User angle: What is great about starting with list-building and the Dream 100 is that once we have emails, mobiles, addresses and other contact details, we can get creative with follow-up across channels, methods, proof, information and offers.

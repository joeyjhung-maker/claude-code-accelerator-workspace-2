## Summary

**Beta-tester framing corrected, in favour of Dallas event attendees:** Jonathan pushed back on
calling event buyers "beta testers" — since floodgates could open UK-wide within ~2 weeks once
Yeti fulfilment is sorted, gatekeeping to a small beta group doesn't make sense. Reframed as: they
get 1:1 help planning/building their mail campaign, and they're guaranteed to be the **first 10**
regardless of how many sign up after them. Nat's invite copy promises pre-event goodies so buyers
"make their money back before they even leave the tarmac."

**US positioning at this point (28 Aug, before the 4 Sept call's dates existed): "aim to be ready
in the US by November."** If delayed, US Dallas buyers are still promised first-in-line status
once ready. **Note: this Nov target is from a call three weeks before the 09-04 call already on
file, which didn't restate a US date** — treat November as the last-known US target unless a
newer call says otherwise, not a confirmed current date.

**Landing page changes agreed this call (predates and is likely superseded by the finished page
seen on 09-04):** UK flag on the wine, UK+US flags on the Yeti. Adding more gift options beyond
the binary wine/Yeti choice — AirTag, Stanley tumbler — partly as a "what's your favourite gift?"
soft-poll mechanic rather than a hard commitment, worded as "other options we're sourcing at
scale" so nothing's promised that can't be delivered. Prices to show in both £ and $. Wait-list
counter planned but deliberately held back until there are "a fair few" signups — a counter
showing 5 people reads as dead, not urgent.

**Pricing discussion at this point (superseded by the locked £39.99/£54.99 from 09-04, kept for
the reasoning behind the final numbers):** target margin ~£15–20 (~$20) profit per send.
Deliberately chose one all-in price shown at checkout rather than a base price + shipping
surcharge — Jonathan and Nat both cited the "flannels.com tax at checkout" feeling as something to
avoid. At-cost pricing for the first 10 Dallas guinea pigs (later loosened to a cap — "first 100
sends at cost," Jonathan not worried about margin on a first cohort) — Joey to get a discount code
for at-cost ordering on Nat's behalf.

**Whiskey is being dropped in favour of wine.** Jonathan explicitly: "I definitely think whiskey
is the wrong play" — people are already uneasy about alcohol generally, and whiskey reads as
"hardcore alcohol" versus wine. Going forward, positioning splits roughly half wine / half
Stanley-or-Yeti tumblers rather than leading with spirits. (Consistent with the wine + Yeti
locked pricing that shows up on the later 09-04 call — this call is where that shift was decided.)
No age-verification step planned for the wine — reasoning: recipients are business addresses,
assumed 18+.

**New internal fact: Dan is in discussions to take a shareholding in Topa itself** (distinct from
the already-banked "Dan takes a 1/3 cut of the fulfilment business" fact from the Travis call —
this is equity in Jonathan's company, not a revenue share of the fulfilment side). Jonathan says
Dan isn't yet sold on LinkedIn automation as a product and is actively trying to convince him,
citing **Hey Reach** (~$50M valuation, 10,000 users, pure LinkedIn-outreach SaaS) and **SalesFlow**
(20,000 users) as proof the category works. Internal financial/cap-table fact — not for
customer-facing copy, flag before repeating externally.

**LinkedIn automation product walkthrough (Jonathan demoed live):**
- Flow: connect LinkedIn account → create campaign → target existing or new connections → name
  campaign → send connection request → wait ~24h → first message if accepted → further follow-ups
  if no reply. A website-scanner feature drafts an opening line by scanning the prospect's site.
- Lead sourcing: CSV upload (for Apollo/Clay-style users) or Topa's own lead database with an
  AI-driven search — keyword-based against LinkedIn company data, supports some Boolean, but not
  true negative-keyword filtering yet (Nat specifically asked for "adventure park, NOT insurance"
  style exclusion — noted as a real feature gap, same one flagged again on 09-04).
- **No duplicate-exclusion yet** — re-pulling the same keyword search later will re-include
  people already contacted. Logged as a plausible near-term feature request, not built.
- Rate limits, framed as a selling point for legitimacy: **max 25 connection requests/day, ~500 a
  month per LinkedIn account**, timing randomized between actions, and outreach is routed through
  a licensed automation-detection-avoidance layer (Jonathan names it as "Engine" on the call —
  transcription uncertain, could be a mis-hearing of the actual vendor name) that geo-matches IP
  addresses to the account holder's real location. Jonathan's own numbers reaching active LinkedIn
  posters specifically: **60–70% connection-acceptance rate, ~50% reply rate.**
- Planned fast-follow: a Sales-Navigator-style filter limited to LinkedIn users who are actually
  active/posting (not just anyone with a profile) — framed explicitly as "cancel your Sales
  Navigator subscription, it's built into Topa," since regular Sales Nav can only filter by
  "posted in the last 30 days," not true activity.
- Ad-spend checker: Jonathan calls this "easy," a quick build once he has bandwidth — consistent
  with it being rushed after the 09-04 call.
- Lead ranking (red/amber/green) — reconfirmed as **gated behind the paid LinkedIn automation
  tier**, not the free lead database, same as the 09-04 call.
- Inbox: currently shows outbound messages only; reply-visibility plus a "click to reveal cell
  phone number" button is planned, explicitly to nudge users off LinkedIn DMs and onto a real
  phone call once a prospect is engaged — Jonathan's stated best practice, repeated from other
  B2B-outreach communities he's in ("every single go-to-market guru... gets the cell phone and
  rings them").
- **Jonathan is deliberately NOT building a fully AI-personalized outreach or an AI auto-responder**
  — his stated view: over-personalized "I noticed your company does X" AI messages read as
  obviously AI and are losing effectiveness ("AI slop" backlash visible on LinkedIn right now); a
  plain, honest DBR-style pitch ("open to more sales/leads, no risk, commission only") outperforms
  fake personalization. Replies should go to a human, not an AI responder. **This is a real
  positioning angle for copy: Topa's outreach is "undetectable automation," not "AI writes and
  replies for you."**

**Dallas event bonus, as specified on this call:** free Topa license (unlimited/10,000 lead
downloads a month) + free LinkedIn automation seat (list price stated inconsistently on the call
as both "$99/mo" and, when Jonathan pulled up actual pricing, "$79 for one account" — flag the
discrepancy, confirm the real current price before quoting it in copy).

**Copy/positioning tension flagged by Nat, unresolved — worth carrying into any LinkedIn-
automation content:** B2B Leads Lab's core pitch is "we don't do cold DMs, cold email, or paid
ads" — so how do you then sell LinkedIn automation without contradicting the whole positioning?
Jonathan's answer: keep bashing cold *email*, but frame LinkedIn growth as building authority and
presence (his sister's agency example: 10 years of automated LinkedIn outreach → 10,000+ followers
off ~500 sends/month), which is a different claim to "outreach that replaces lumpy mail." Not
fully resolved as a single line of copy — needs a deliberate positioning decision before this gets
pushed hard.

**Differentiation question also flagged, unresolved:** the audience has already bought Jack's
LinkedIn sprint / a white-labeled automation tool, and separately did "Rob Brown training" (fully
manual LinkedIn outreach) — so "how is Topa different from what I already have" is a live
objection. Also: a member mentioned in a support call that LinkedIn has "cracked down" on
automated connection/message tools recently — Jonathan had not heard this and pushed back citing
Hey Reach/SalesFlow's scale as evidence the category is fine; **treat the crackdown claim as
unverified, sourced third-hand from a member via Jack, not confirmed by Jonathan.**

**New tool mentioned by Joey, not yet cross-checked against Topa: Endole** ("endol"/"end all
kariko" in the transcript — likely Endole.co.uk, a UK company-data lookup). Has director names,
sometimes links to director LinkedIn profiles, and confirms registered office address — useful
alongside Topa for double-checking addresses before a physical send. **UK-only**, so doesn't help
with US targeting.

**Nat's coffee-date/demo call script — planned overhaul, her own call, not yet built:** current
call structure is "a feature dump" (demo DBR, voice, reputation, live chat back to back) with no
real space to dig into the prospect's actual pain first. Nat, prompted partly by a conversation
with a member ("Johnny says he's not a salesperson"), is taking creative license to redesign it —
lead with pain, demo second. Also considering a **higher-ticket retainer tier for bigger
companies**: ~£1,500/month retainer + £500 setup fee, "tripling monthly" (exact mechanic
unclear from the call — worth confirming with Nat before citing). This is a separate, bigger-
ticket track from the core £39.99/£54.99 gift product — internal detail, not yet a public offer.

**Operational note:** Nat flagged she may need to borrow some of Joey's email-scheduling slots the
week of 1 Sept (August bank holiday, short-staffed) and won't be back to full sending capacity
until roughly 7–14 Sept — relevant if any B2B Leads Lab email cadence looked light that week.

**Coordination gap surfaced:** Alan (a designer) had independently built and shared his own
version of the wait-list landing page/copy in the group, duplicating work already done by
Jonathan off Joey's copy. Jonathan's fix: catch up with Joey/Nat twice a week rather than once, to
stop this kind of duplicate work happening again.

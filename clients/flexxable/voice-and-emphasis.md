# Flexxable — Voice, Emphasis & Output Format

*The fingerprint to mirror on every Flexxable email and piece of copy. Pulled from Dan's hand-formatted Hulk squeeze page (2026-06-10). This is per-client — these rules are Flexxable's, do not leak them into another brand.*

## OUTPUT FORMAT (the formatter spec — apply to every output)

- **Font: Arial.** Always.
- **Body: size 12.**
- **Headline: size 35** (when the piece has one).
- **Subheadline: size 30** (when the piece has one).
- **Emails:** no headline, no subheadline. Body only.
- Squeeze/landing pages: headline + subhead + body.
- Save finished outputs to Google Drive: `My Drive/Claude Code File Outputs/` (or the named client folder).

## THE EMPHASIS SYSTEM (how Dan punches up copy)

**1. Bold carries the skim-path — the spine.**
Read ONLY the bolded words top to bottom and the whole pitch must still sell.
Bold lands on: the headline, the CTAs, the **label-opener** of a parallel line, and the **benefit verb-phrase** of a line — not the whole sentence.
> Test every draft: lift the bold-only, line it up, does it sell the offer alone? If not, the emphasis is on the wrong words.

**2. CAPS = one punch-word per line.**
Caps the single emotional/contrast word that carries the beat — MANY, TWO, YES, YOU, ONLY, PAID, ALSO, REVENUE. Never a whole clause. Roughly one per line, max.

**3. `!` is rationed — energy only.**
~4–6 per page, only on command / direct-address / enthusiasm lines ("hold ya back!", "sell it!", "…as you want!"). Never on a calm or proof line.

**4. Ellipsis `…` is the core rhythm device.**
Lines trail off; the next line catches the thread. Creates forward pull. Used heavily and deliberately.

**5. Blue (`#1155cc`) on links/CTAs only.** The subhead and the button lines read blue.

## VOICE TELLS (the texture — never scrub these)

- **Slang register:** peeps, ya, biz, lil', burnin', darn, sales bro, chunky cut, coffee date, cash the cheques. Apostrophe-dropped, casual, mid-Atlantic. Scrubbing this kills the voice.
- **Coined terms in 'quotes':** 'Sales Wingman', 'Money Bags' campaign, "stack", "manage", "Landlord". Quote a proprietary/winking term to flag "this is my thing."
- **Sustained mind-movie metaphor:** cast the mechanism as a vivid role ("collecting rent," "your very own Landlord," "whales," "TECH hell"). Not a one-off — carry it.
- **Parallel pain pairs:** symmetrical, bolded labels ("stuck with SALES…" / "drowning in TECH hell…").
- **Number specificity:** 5 androids, 40%+, 7 and 8-figure, ONE click. Never "several / lots."
- **Direct-address questions pull the yes:** "Would you be down for it?", "If you say YES…".
- **Winking aside:** the occasional `;)` at a payoff beat ("While you cash the cheques ;)").
- **One idea per line.** Fragments over full sentences. (Matches the global Script.)

## Corrected from a miss

- Automatic correction loop: every time Joey sends edited Flexxable copy, save his version as the approved source of truth, compare it with the draft he received, and immediately bank any consequential or repeated lesson here so future drafts improve; do not wait for him to ask.
- For student-win emails, celebrate the student first and let the proof breathe. If there is a conversion move, put it in the P.S. as a simple explanation of why they moved fast, followed by a low-pressure comment-keyword CTA.
- One sentence per line does not mean every sentence should be tiny. Dan varies fuller conversational sentences with short punch lines; avoid runs of 2–3-word sentences that create a robotic machine-gun rhythm.
- For YouTube-long teaser posts, sell the dramatic result and possibility but hide the detailed technical implementation behind the click. Name the tools and build steps inside the video, not in the teaser.
- Make the video format obvious in the headline when useful, then add any downloadable template or workflow as a second concrete reason to watch and comment.
- When presenting finished copy to Joey, use plain text without Markdown bold formatting so it can be pasted cleanly into Google Docs. Preserve emphasis only when banking Joey’s already-formatted final version.
- When warning a broad audience about AI-written copy, name the familiar category tool “ChatGPT” rather than “Claude,” use Dan’s everyday shorthand (“peeps”), and prefer the blunt spoken reaction (“No human would ever say this lol”) over a writerly aphorism. A P.S. can widen one specific lesson across adjacent channels and restate the practical payoff.
- For action posts, strip repeated urgency once the instruction is clear, put small caveats in a quick parenthetical, and ask members to report back with the work. In a gift-led sequence, call while the gift and sender’s name are still fresh, then continue through LinkedIn and email. Dan can close with a strong personal prediction (“I’d be shocked if…”) when it is explicitly qualified by correct targeting; never rewrite that conviction as a guaranteed result or universal statistic.
- Enforce sentence-length variation structurally, not by intention: one sentence per line does not mean one tiny beat per line. Before showing Dan copy, scan for three or more consecutive lines under roughly six words and combine related clauses into a fuller spoken sentence unless the short run is an intentional final crescendo.
- For serious mindset or rally posts, lead the headline with the desired identity/outcome rather than merely describing the threat, and do not force playful Dan-isms into every beat. Plain CAPS (“HUGE”) can carry more conviction than a coined spelling (“YUGE”) when the subject needs gravitas.
- For pre-sell emails that open on pain, confirm the pain rather than reassure the reader it's imagined, and scope it with a quick parenthetical to the real cause: "it's because it has (if you're going after ice-cold leads)", not "it's not in your head." The scope stops warm readers feeling accused and sets up the fix. (Joey, Topa LinkedIn day-0 email, 2026-09-28.)
- A pre-sell "details tomorrow" close should segment the reader by situation and promise each segment a concrete outcome, not end on a cryptic mystery line: "If you… 1) Have a LinkedIn network already… this will help you extract coffee dates & sales from it. 2) DONT have one yet… this will help you create one full of your perfect ICP." Keep the mechanism hidden; show the outcome for each reader. Then "deets" plus a winking ;-) on the tomorrow line. (Joey replaced "it reaches them somewhere their inbox can't touch", 2026-09-28.)
- STANDING CTA for every Dan Skool post (Joey, 2026-09-28): transition into "If you're ready to [outcome matching this post's theme], comment "[KEYWORD]" and we'll get the deets to ya." The keyword is one themed word in caps, personalised to the post (DESK, GENERIC, OLDSCHOOL), never a generic "YES". No YouTube or link pushes unless Joey asks for a video click-driver.
- Named-system posts (Joey, "Supercharged Cold Outreach", 2026-09-28): Dan owns the system name in first person ("I call our new outreach system…") and states its core promise with full conviction ("becomes inevitable", not "almost books itself"). Before a "the real beauty happens AFTER" pivot, credit step one as big on its own ("That by itself is a huge gamechanger") so the pivot escalates. After stacking channels, cash it out as UNLIMITED follow-up options and land a cheeky line before the CTA ("Until they book a coffee date or tell us to f off lol").
- Whenever a step asks the reader to do something they fear (usually picking up the phone), add the disarm line straight after it: "It takes balls but if you're hungry, this is the FASTEST way to book calls." (Joey added it by hand when the judge missed it, 2026-09-28.)
- First mention of a product name gets its category in brackets: "Topa (our new LinkedIn Automation tool)". Don't assume the room knows the brand. (Joey, 2026-09-29.)
- Cash a long-term benefit out with a "which means…" ladder that ends at booked coffee dates ("Which increases reply rates… / Which means more warm convos… / Which means more opportunity to send lumpy mail to peeps who already know you… / Which means more coffee dates booked…"). In Topa/LinkedIn copy, the ladder should thread lumpy mail back in. (Joey, 2026-09-29.)
- REPEATED (2x, promote): the payoff of LinkedIn + enrichment is endless follow-up. Say what you end up owning (a LinkedIn network PLUS a database of enriched contact info) and that it lets you follow up across channels, again and again, until they book ("UNLIMITED options to follow up…", "follow up… staying top of mind… over and over"). (Joey, 2026-09-28 and 2026-09-29.)
- Quote a known saying in its familiar form and frame it plainly ("The ol' saying: … …is still true."). Don't bend it into a custom version ("your network tree… 5 years ago"). (Joey, 2026-09-29.)
- Name what the tool does NOT do in a quick parenthetical ("(You still gotta do the replies, we don't automate that part and likely never will)"). Honest limits set expectations, build trust, and head off the "it's a spam bot" fear. Hedge automation claims ("a tractor that PRACTICALLY drives itself"). (Joey, 2026-09-29.)
- Analogy posts: carry ONE analogy through the whole post as named stages (PLANTING / GROWING / HARVEST), give the old way a physical cost ("I broke my back farming by HAND"), and never explain the analogy after it lands (cut "That's this tool…"). (Joey, hunters vs farmers post, 2026-09-29.)
- BANNED openers/fillers, never write them (Joey, 2026-09-29): "Fair question…" and "So yeah…". They're throat-clearing that validates or recaps instead of saying something. Open on the point, or on a plain honest line ("I'm not gonna BS ya on this one").

# MAKEMONEY_ActiveAgencyOwner_Experiment_OneVsTen_VIDEO_v01

Faithful video swipe of Zack Kravits's controlled-comparison explainer, rebuilt for
Flexxable's active agency-owner segment. The account's existing one-client-versus-ten
winner supplies the comparison; the new ad earns that result by explaining the deal-model
mechanism behind it.

## DNA

- **Segment:** Active lead-gen or marketing agency owner currently selling retainers and
  looking for a higher-value AI offer for existing clients. This is the Decoder's
  under-served active-agency-owner segment (`strategy-map.md:203-212`).
- **Awareness:** Solution-Aware. They understand agencies and recognise the AI opportunity;
  the ad's job is to show why this business model is different.
- **Concept type:** **Side-by-Side** — from the Solution-Aware concept library in
  [[creative-coverage]].
- **Concept (flat):** Client count stops predicting agency income when the deal model
  changes. Dan made more from one performance-based AI client than he used to make from
  ten retainer clients.
- **Angle:** **Experiment** — from the controlled angle vocabulary in
  [[creative-coverage]]. Dan tried a new offer with his existing lead-gen clients and
  compares it directly with his old agency model.
- **Hypothesis:** The account has already validated the one-client-versus-ten claim in a
  winning ad, but the active-agency-owner segment remains under-served at 6 of 75 ads.
  Rebuilding that winner inside a controlled-comparison explainer should make the result
  more believable by showing why the model caused it, while opening a comparison-led lane
  the account barely uses.
- **Existing belief preserved:** Getting clients matters.
- **Belief change:** Client volume is only one side of the equation. Deal economics
  determine whether growth creates freedom or ten new bosses.
- **Problem mechanism (UMP):** Fixed retainers cap the upside while delivery and client
  management multiply with every new account.
- **Solution mechanism (UMS):** Take a business's old leads, reactivate them with AI and
  SMS, generate sales, then earn through a performance split.
- **Problem name:** **The Vendor Leash** — the existing Flexxable handle for the retainer
  trap, not a new label invented for this brief.
- **Binary:** **Ten Bosses vs One Partner.** Retainer clients pull the operator in ten
  directions; a performance partner shares risk and upside.
- **The Image:** Split screen. On the left, Dan is being pulled in ten directions by ten
  red client leashes. On the right, he shakes hands with one partner while an Android
  works through a pile of old leads, and the one-client money bag outweighs the ten
  retainers. A ten-year-old could draw the core picture: ten leashes versus one handshake.
- **Spokesperson:** Dan as **the reformed agency insider who tested the new model himself**.
  His old-agency experience stays in the past tense. First-person claims cannot extend
  beyond documented Dan experience; see [[dan-truth-constraint]].
- **CTA:** Get The Instant AI Agency book for $14.40, including the training and tools,
  backed by the 30-day keep-everything guarantee. Route to `book.flexxable.com/optin`, the
  account's most-tested destination (`strategy-map.md:214-216`).
- **Format:** Approximately 90-second vertical presenter video. Dan talks directly to
  camera with a simple slide track carrying the comparison, mechanism diagram and CTA.
- **Source route:** **Swipe.** Joey explicitly selected the organic reel as a structure to
  swipe. Preserve its beat order, rhythm and approximate length while replacing every
  creator-specific claim with Flexxable material. Carry the original link throughout
  production.

## Frozen beat order

1. Conditional agency-owner callout.
2. Agree that getting clients matters.
3. Reveal that client count is only half the equation.
4. Promise first-party proof.
5. Compare ten retainer clients with one performance-based client.
6. Explain how retainers create the Vendor Leash.
7. Install the old-leads + AI/SMS + performance-split mechanism.
8. Position the book as the bridge.
9. Restate the one-partner payoff.
10. Close on the $14.40 offer and guarantee.

## Guidelines

- Additive flip, no villain. Do not claim clients, retainers or client acquisition are
  worthless. The new model is the missing half, not a negation of everything the viewer
  already knows.
- Keep the comparison understated. The result carries the voltage; the copy does not need
  to shout about it.
- The ten-versus-one comparison remains the central proof beat. Student results may support
  it later but cannot replace or interrupt it.
- The hidden cause must be the deal model, not AI itself. AI + SMS is what fulfils the
  performance offer after the belief flip lands.
- The book must feel like the bridge to installing this model, not a product bolted onto
  the ending.
- Do not open with student proof or testimonials.
- Preserve Dan's casual, direct, British-inflected register and short one-idea-per-line
  rhythm in /produce.
- No hooks or body copy are locked in this brief. /produce starts at the hook gate.

# Brief — "Don't Build A Biz, Acquire Old Leads" video script

## Offer
The Instant AI Agency — $14.40 book (was $47, "Special Deal"). Promise: start a
performance-based AI automation business, earn 30-50% of client profits, AI does the
work.

## Mechanism (get this mechanically right — do not drift into "lead generation")
This is OLD/DEAD LEAD REACTIVATION, not new-lead generation. Businesses are sitting on
databases of old leads they already paid for — enquiries that went cold, past
customers, people who showed interest and never bought. You get control of that
existing database (not a new one), point an AI system at it, it sends a two-line SMS
that reactivates those old contacts and turns them into sales. You take 30-50% of what
it generates. No cold calls, no ad spend, no code required from you.

## Avatar
Someone weighing up starting an online business/agency the traditional way — build a
product, a brand, a following, grind for years — versus a lower-risk, faster-cash
alternative. Not tied to a specific persona name; broad biz-opp audience considering
"should I build something from scratch."

## The angle (locked — this is a live static already running, script for video off it)
Contrarian positioning against "build a business" as the default path to wealth.
Building has real, known downside (time, risk, most new businesses don't survive long
term) — this offer routes around that by having you acquire an existing asset
(a business's old lead database) instead of building anything from zero.

## Hook — REWRITE, do not flat-read the static's headline as the cold open
Previous draft opened by reciting the static's tagline verbatim as three declarative
lines ("Don't build a biz. / Acquire old leads. / Less risk, faster cash.") — Joey
rejected it as a weak opener. Don't do that again.

The "don't build, acquire" positioning is still the SPINE of the script — it can still
appear as a line, ideally as a turn/reveal partway through rather than the literal
first three words. But the actual cold open (first 3-5 seconds) needs to EARN
attention on its own — curiosity, a concrete claim, an objection pre-empt, or a
contrarian statement that isn't just reading the ad headline out loud. Pull from the
primer's hook DNA (cold-open income claim / objection-kill / overwhelm-to-simple) for
what a strong spoken opener sounds like here, then land "don't build a business,
acquire an existing asset instead" as the reframe once attention is earned — not as
line one.

- Price: $14.40, was $47 ("Special Deal")

## Format
Talking-head video script for Dan to record. Spoken word, first-person, one idea per
line, short punchy lines — no walls of text. Casual, direct. Plain spoken register per
the primer (this is biz-opp/mass-market voice, not the agency-insider register).

## Length
Target 190-210 words of spoken script for a 2-minute video. Dan's actual delivery
pace is ~100 words per minute (measured from his real recordings, not a generic
spoken-word estimate) — do not use a 140-150 wpm assumption, it runs long on camera.
This is still a full mechanism walkthrough, not a quick clip, so be economical: earn
the hook, name the problem with "building," show the mechanism, stack the proof,
close — but every line has to earn its word count at this length. Cut supporting
color (extra examples, rhetorical asides) before cutting mechanism or proof.

# Strengthen one hook — 12 versions

The operator is testing three hooks on one locked body. Hook 2 is the weakest. Write **12 stronger versions of Hook 2**. Hooks only.

## Hook 2 as it stands (the operator's line)

> Your best retainer client is probably figuring out whether AI can do your job for less.

## Why it is weak

1. **It sells the wrong fear.** "Do your job" is the replacement fear. This ad's whole idea is that the client KEEPS you and pays you less. The price drops, the work stays.
2. **It answers itself.** The body's reveal, five lines later, is: clients now think "Can AI do this cheaper?" Hook 2 says that out loud in line one, so there is nothing left to find out.
3. **Nothing to see.** "Probably figuring out whether" is a soft, foggy verb. There is no moment, no picture.
4. **No turn.** The other two hooks land a second beat. This one is a single flat line.

## What to keep

The type is **Secret Terror Under Your Nose**: the thing they think is SAFE is the thing that's dangerous.

The safe thing is **their best retainer client**. The loyal one. The one who loves the work, pays on time, sends referrals. Keep that client as the subject. That is the trust token.

## What a stronger version does

- Opens on the best client, the safe one, the one they would never worry about.
- Puts that client in a MOMENT you can see: the renewal call, the monthly invoice landing, a tab open on their laptop, a quiet chat with their business partner, the number they scribble before the call.
- Makes it about the PRICE, not about being replaced. They are staying. They are just about to pay less.
- Leaves ONE question open: why would the client who loves me do that? The body answers it.
- Does NOT say "AI can do it cheaper" or any version of that sentence. You may show AI as an object in the scene (a tab, a tool they've been playing with) but do not state the conclusion.
- Two short spoken lines, with the turn on line two. 26 words at most across both.
- Hands straight into the body's first line: "The same work you used to charge $5K for… / Is now “worth” $2K."

## The other two hooks in the test (do not copy their shape)

Hook 1:
> AI doesn’t have to replace you to take your money.
> It just has to make your work look like a five-minute job.

Hook 3:
> Highly paid service providers are being decimated in the new AI economy.
> They sold you the dream of AI speeding up your work so you could take more time off.
> Instead you’re doing MORE work for less pay.

## Rules

- A statement. Never open on a question.
- Tradie words, blunt and physical. Sounds like Dan saying it to a mate.
- No numbers, no timeframes, no invented client quotes attributed to a real person, no Dan story.
- Keep one small hedge ("probably", "right now, somewhere") so it does not claim every client.
- Do not blame a cheaper rival agency or freelancer.
- No crime-scene, knife, blood, guts or clearance-rack imagery. Banned: leverage, solution, deliverable, onboarding.
- Do not repeat "not because your skills got worse".

## Output

Numbered 1 to 12, two lines each with " / " between them. No commentary.

## The body they bolt onto

The same work you used to charge $5K for…

Is now “worth” $2K.

Not because your skills got worse…

But because clients now think:

“Can AI do this cheaper?”

So here’s how to flip that:

The people crushing it in the new AI economy aren’t trying to COMPETE with AI…

They’re simply charging for RESULTS, not output.

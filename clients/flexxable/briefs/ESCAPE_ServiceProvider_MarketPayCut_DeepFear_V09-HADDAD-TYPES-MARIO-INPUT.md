# Hooks by emotional entry point — two per type, eleven types

The ad at the bottom is finished and locked. Dan Wardrope, spoken to camera, Facebook video, selling The Instant AI Agency book to agency owners, freelancers, copywriters, funnel builders, SMMA managers and SEO people.

The operator wants to see the SAME ad opened through each of Chris Haddad's hook types, one door at a time.

Write **two hooks for each of the eleven types below. 22 hooks.** Hooks only. Do not rewrite any body line.

Each hook replaces the two control lines and should hand into the first body line:

> The same work you used to charge $5K for…
> Is now “worth” $2K.
> Not because your skills got worse…
> But because clients now think:
> “Can AI do this cheaper?”

## The control (do not rewrite or paraphrase)

> AI doesn’t have to replace you to take your money.
> It just has to make your work look like a five-minute job.

## The eleven types

A hook is the emotional way in, not the product's feature. Same ad, different door.

1. **Magic Mechanism** — the "how" is so easy and hands-off it removes effort and self-doubt. The mechanism itself is the appeal. For this type ONLY you may tease the mechanism (a text message, old leads sitting in a CRM, AI doing the talking) but tease it, do not explain it.
2. **STUYN (Secret Terror Under Your Nose)** — something they assume is safe is secretly dangerous. Attack a thing they trust: their portfolio, their retainer, their "premium" positioning, their best client, their proposal.
3. **I Knew It!** — confirms what they already suspected and were scared to say. Closes the anxiety loop. "You're not imagining it."
4. **I Didn't Know It** — takes a belief they hold firmly and flips it on its head. Their firm belief: "If my work is good and I explain the value well enough, I keep my old rate." Also fair game: "the fix is to get better / niche down / raise my prices".
5. **Outrage Society** — names a villain the tribe should be angry at. Us against them. Pick a villain that is TRUE and that Dan could say on camera without being sued: the gurus telling them to "just raise your rates", the AI tool ads telling business owners they can fire their agency, the "anyone can do this in five minutes" crowd.
6. **Ancient Secrets Revealed** — recovered old wisdom. Something business did long before retainers and hourly rates existed. Only use things that are commonly known and true (a cut of the sale, commission, no win no fee, the handshake deal).
7. **Foreign Wisdom** — another culture or another trade already knows better. "Another trade" counts: real estate agents, commission salesmen, no-win-no-fee lawyers, recruiters. They have never had to defend an hourly rate.
8. **New Science** — a recent discovery makes a hard problem easy, and explains why it wasn't possible before. Do NOT invent a study, a lab or a statistic. The true "new thing" here is the technology itself: AI can now hold a real back-and-forth text conversation, which it could not do well before.
9. **Yoda of the Niche** — the category's master reveals what is normally held back. The master is Dan. Use ONLY the verified Dan facts below.
10. **Unknown But Fascinating Guru** — someone they have never heard of who turns out to be the best at this, and has a reason to share it. Use ONLY verified facts. If you cannot do it truthfully, write the hook with a clearly marked [FACT NEEDED: …] slot instead of inventing.
11. **I'm Just Like You But Worse** — an ordinary person who beat bigger obstacles than the viewer has. Use ONLY the verified Lachie facts below.

## Verified facts you may use (nothing else)

- Dan has taught over 1,000 everyday people how to charge for results and use AI to handle the work.
- Dan had an engineering salary before this. The approved framing is: "Made more money from one deal than my entire engineering salary." Do not say he quit, was laid off, or built it on the side.
- Lachie was a finance guy with zero business, sales or tech experience. He tried this, hit $20k/mo, then quit his job.
- The model: find a business with old leads, use AI and SMS to turn them into sales, take a cut. No upfront payment from the business.
- Any other name, number or result that appears word for word in the sample ads you absorbed.

If a type cannot be written truthfully from these, use a [FACT NEEDED: …] slot. Never invent a person, a study, a country, a number, a timeframe or a Dan story.

## What every hook must still do

- Make them think "wait… WHAT?" and need the next line. Too polite is a FAIL.
- Leave ONE question open. Give A and Z, hide B through Y.
- Do not give away the body's line "Can AI do this cheaper?" and do not repeat "not because your skills got worse".
- Do not blame a cheaper rival agency. The problem is in the client's head.
- One or two short spoken lines, 28 words at most across both.
- A statement. Never open on a question.
- Tradie words. Blunt and physical. Sounds like Dan saying something true to a mate.
- No crime-scene, knife, blood, guts or clearance-rack imagery. Banned words: leverage, solution, deliverable, onboarding.

## Output

For each type: the type name as a heading, then hooks A and B. A two-line hook uses " / " between the lines.

After each hook add one tag: [BOLTS] if it runs cleanly into "The same work you used to charge $5K for…", or [NEEDS BRIDGE] if it would need a line in between.

No other commentary.

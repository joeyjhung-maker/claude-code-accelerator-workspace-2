# Bolt-on hooks — two alternates for a finished ad

The ad below is finished and locked. It is a spoken Facebook video, Dan Wardrope to camera, selling The Instant AI Agency book.

The current hook (the control) is the first two lines. I need ALTERNATE hooks to test against it.

Each alternate replaces the two control lines and must hand straight into the first body line, which does not change:

> The same work you used to charge $5K for…
> Is now “worth” $2K.
> Not because your skills got worse…
> But because clients now think:
> “Can AI do this cheaper?”

Read your hook, then read those five lines out loud. If there is a bump, the hook fails.

## The control (do NOT rewrite it, do NOT hand back near-copies of it)

> AI doesn’t have to replace you to take your money.
> It just has to make your work look like a five-minute job.

Vehicle: Contrarian. Every alternate must come in through a DIFFERENT door. I want a spread across Call-Out, Emotion, Curiosity, Urgency and Authority. No more Contrarian "AI doesn't have to X to Y" shapes.

## Who is watching

Agency owners, freelancers, copywriters, funnel builders, SMMA managers, SEO people. People who sell skilled work online.

They are scared of AI replacing them. The thing that is actually happening is quieter: the same job now gets a smaller budget, because the buyer has watched AI spit out words, pages and campaigns fast and assumes the hard part is gone.

Most of them still blame the individual cheap client. They think "if my work is good and I explain the value well enough, I can keep my old rate."

## The hard gate — grab them by the throat, then grease the slide

The hook must make this person think "wait… WHAT?" and need the next line.

Show the shocking contradiction or the consequence. HIDE the explanation. Give them A and Z, withhold B through Y.

The body is what explains WHY the price dropped (clients think AI can do it cheaper). So the hook must NOT say that. If the hook explains its own twist, it fails.

Too polite is a fail. Read it aloud: if a freelancer would not wince, laugh or say "damn", it is dead.

## Shape

- One or two short spoken lines. 25 words at the very most across both.
- Statement, not a question. Never open on a question mark.
- Words a tradie would use. Blunt, physical, Germanic: pay, cut, broke, cheap, sweat, haggle, quote, invoice. Not "commoditised", "margin", "value perception".
- Put a real moment in it where you can: the quote going out, the reply coming back, the invoice, the renewal call.
- It has to sound like Dan saying something true to a mate, not like an ad.

## Truth rules

- No invented numbers, stats, studies, timeframes or client quotes. The $5K and $2K live in the body, do not repeat them in the hook.
- No invented Dan backstory. No "I used to", no "last week a client told me".
- No student proof or testimonial in the hook.
- Do not claim every client or every freelancer.
- Do not mention old leads, SMS, the book or the offer. Consequence before mechanism.
- No crime-scene, knife, blood, guts or clearance-rack imagery.
- Banned words: leverage, solution, deliverable, onboarding.

## Output

Numbered list, one hook per number (a two-line hook stays on one number with a " / " between the lines). Tag each with its vehicle in square brackets at the end. No commentary.

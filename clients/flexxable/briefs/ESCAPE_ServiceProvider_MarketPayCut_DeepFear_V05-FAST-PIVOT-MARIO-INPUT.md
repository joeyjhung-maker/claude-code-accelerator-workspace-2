# Rewrite — the problem section is far too long. Get to the way out fast.

The operator read the current draft (pasted at the bottom) and rejected the shape:

> "It takes far too long to get into the transition to the solution. We poke too hard. Make the poking maybe 20% of the opening."

In the current draft the problem runs seven lines and about half the ad before the pivot. That is the failure.

This instruction OVERRIDES the argument spine in the original brief wherever they clash.

## The new shape

**The poke: about 20% of the ad. Three lines, 45 words at the very most.**

1. The opening line. Keep the current one or beat it: one blunt market truth, said flat, that does not explain itself.
2. The brief lands in the inbox, full-sized, with the budget already thinner before you've quoted.
3. One line of why: plenty of buyers have watched AI crank out the output fast, so they assume the hard part is gone, while you still carry the revisions and the blame.

Then stop poking. No second or third line on how it feels. No separate beat about padding proposals and justifying the old rate. If the failed response appears at all, it is one clause inside the pivot line ("Instead of padding the proposal to defend the old rate…").

**The pivot arrives on line 4.** Ladder it across two lines with a trailing ellipsis on the first.

**The way out: everything after that, about 80% of the ad.** Spend the freed-up words here, slowly and concretely, so the reader can picture the deal:

- What you actually do: take a business's old leads and use AI and SMS to reactivate them and turn them into measurable sales.
- Why a business owner says yes: they already paid to acquire those leads and have largely written them off, there is no setup fee, and they pay you from the sales generated.
- What you take: 30-50% of the revenue generated.
- Why this gets you out of the squeeze: nobody compares a sale against what AI can produce cheaply. You use AI to create the result the client wants, then get paid from that result.
- The book: a step-by-step blueprint for finding clients, reactivating old leads with AI and SMS, and structuring performance-based deals. $14.40. 30-day money-back guarantee and you keep the book either way.
- CTA and URL.

The tone of the back 80% is relief. A mate showing you the door, calmly.

## Hard rules, unchanged

- Return primary ad copy only. No notes, no headline, no word count.
- Hard maximum **250 words** including the CTA and URL. Aim for **200-230**. Count before you answer and cut if you are over 235.
- One sentence per line.
- No numbers except 30-50%, $14.40 and 30-day. No invented results, timeframes, client stories or Dan backstory.
- Do not pad the back 80% with invented detail to fill space. If you run out of verified things to say, the ad is shorter.
- Do not say the client "risks nothing". State the fact: no setup fee, paid from sales generated.
- Do not promise AI can't touch you. Do not say performance deals are never negotiated.
- Banned words: leverage, solution, deliverable, onboarding. No "here's the thing" run-ups.
- No fragment stacks, no tidy "not X, it's Y" flips, no three abstract benefits in a row.
- Do not start a line with an ellipsis. No emoji. No dashes.

## Audience

Existing agency owners, freelancers and online service providers, especially:

- Copywriters
- Funnel builders
- SMMA managers
- SEO providers
- Other people selling skilled online services

They fear being replaced by AI.

But even when AI doesn’t replace them, new projects are becoming harder to win at the rates their work used to command.

Clients expect more output while wanting to pay less.

## Awareness

The reader is problem-aware.

They’ve seen thinner budgets and lower offers, but may still blame individual cheap clients rather than recognise that AI has reset what buyers expect skilled output to cost.

Challenge this belief:

“If my work is still good and I explain its value clearly enough, I can keep charging the old rate.”

## Core concept

AI doesn’t need to replace a service provider to hurt their income.

It can make buyers value their output less before they’ve even quoted.

The provider still carries the thinking, revisions, responsibility and blame when the finished work fails.

But the buyer has seen AI produce words, pages, images and campaigns quickly, so they assume the hard part has vanished.

Internal problem name: The Pay Cut Before the Pitch.

Do not force that phrase into the script unless it sounds natural.

## Angle

Deep Fear.

The feared pay cut isn’t waiting somewhere in the future.

It’s already appearing inside new briefs and smaller buyer budgets.

The emotional movement should be:

Recognition → threat → frustration → relief.

Avoid melodrama.

## Concrete image

A familiar, full-sized project brief lands in the provider’s inbox.

The client expects the same calibre of work and the same commercial outcome.

But the budget is thinner before the provider has even quoted.

## Central contrast

Marked-Down Output vs. Paid-On-Results Revenue.

Output is compared against what AI can produce quickly and cheaply.

Measurable sales still carry obvious commercial value.

## Argument spine

1. Open with curiosity or a blunt market truth.

2. Don’t explain the entire argument in the first line.

3. Show the familiar brief arriving with the same expectations and less money attached.

4. Explain that buyers have seen AI produce output quickly, so they assume the hard part has disappeared.

5. Show that the provider still carries the judgement, revisions, responsibility and blame.

6. Name the squeeze: clients expect more while paying less.

7. Show the usual failed response: longer proposals, fatter retainers, more value justification and another large upfront fee.

8. Pivot: stop fighting over what output is worth and change what the client pays for.

9. Introduce the Instant AI Agency model: use AI and SMS to reactivate a business’s old leads and turn them into measurable sales.

10. The business has already paid to acquire those leads and largely written them off.

11. Offer to work without a setup fee and get paid from the sales generated.

12. The provider takes 30–50% of the revenue generated.

13. Make the “AI-proof” point carefully: use AI to create the result the client wants, then get paid from that result.

14. Transition into the book, price, guarantee and CTA.

- Don’t use testimonials or student proof.
- Don’t invent first-person history for Dan.
- Don’t claim every buyer, provider or brief behaves this way.
- Don’t quantify the decline in service rates.
- Don’t claim performance deals can never be negotiated or rejected.
- Don’t call the offer completely risk-free.
- State the exact fact: no upfront fee, with payment coming from sales generated.
- Don’t promise literal immunity from AI.

## Voice

- Dan speaking slowly to camera.
- Casual, blunt and Australian-inflected.
- Sound like he’s talking to a mate who owns a business.
- Short paragraphs with natural spoken flow.
- Use ellipses sparingly.
- Use simple, concrete words rather than marketing jargon.
- Pain should amplify after the opening, not become a generic suffering hook.

## Avoid

- Crime-scene, knife, blood, biting, guts or clearance-rack metaphors
- Corporate words such as “leverage,” “solution,” “deliverable” or “onboarding”
- Tidy “not X, but Y” reversals
- Stacked abstract triplets
- Fake one-word punch lines
- Proof stacks
- More than one curiosity thread

The finished script should feel like one inevitable argument:

AI has marked down skilled output, so stop defending the old price of output and get paid from measurable sales instead.

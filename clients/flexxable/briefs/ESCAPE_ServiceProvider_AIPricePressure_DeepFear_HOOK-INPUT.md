# Genesis New Hook Bot input — The Service Provider Squeeze

## Parameters

- **output_type:** hooks
- **generation_style:** full_spectrum
- **quantity:** 15
- **include_strategy_analysis:** true
- **include_scoring:** true
- **platform:** Facebook long-form text ad opening line
- **strategic_goal:** Stop an agency owner/freelancer in the feed and make them read the
  body. Do not sell the book or explain the solution in the hook.
- **audience_sophistication:** problem_aware

## Content details

This ad sells Dan's $14.40 book, The Instant AI Agency, to existing agency owners,
freelancers, copywriters, SMMA managers, SEO providers and other online service
providers.

The body will eventually show how to stop selling output on an upfront retainer and
instead make a risk-free offer: use AI and SMS to reactivate a client's old leads,
create measurable sales, and get paid a share of the results.

The hook must NOT explain that mechanism yet.

## Locked unique angle — do not change or replace it

**The Service Provider Squeeze.**

AI is attacking output-based service providers from two sides:

1. Some are being replaced outright.
2. Those who keep their clients can still have their rates crushed because AI makes
   output feel cheap. Clients expect more work for less money and resist the old
   upfront-retainer deal.

The provider is squeezed between AI commoditisation and buyer pressure.

This is a macro market-change ad. It is NOT primarily about scope creep, finishing work
faster, cleaning up ChatGPT drafts, late-night revisions or generic burnout. Those may
appear as supporting texture later, but they cannot become the hook's main idea.

## Key transformation

Stop trying to survive by defending the price of output. Change what the client pays
for: move from upfront retainers/deliverables to a risk-free offer paid from measurable
sales results. That is the body's resolution, not the hook's reveal.

## Audience beliefs and fear

- “AI could replace me.”
- “If it doesn't replace me, clients will use it to grind my rates down.”
- “Clients expect more output but want to pay less.”
- “I need to prove I'm better than AI and lock clients into retainers.”
- “Performance deals sound risky, but retainers are getting harder to sell.”

## Failed response

Defending rates, adding more output, proving quality and fighting to preserve the old
upfront-retainer model.

## Brand voice

Dan: casual, direct, Australian-inflected, spoken-natural, blunt but not corporate.
Short punchy sentences. The opening should feel like an observation, warning or piece of
news—not polished agency copy.

## Hard constraints

- Generate 15 genuinely different hooks, not 15 phrasings of one line.
- Every hook must preserve the two-sided macro idea: outright replacement OR retained
  but commoditised/underpaid. A hook may foreground one jaw only if it clearly leaves
  the second jaw as the open loop.
- Include varied lanes/archetypes/patterns. Do not let more than four hooks use the same
  underlying structure.
- Use **The Service Provider Squeeze** in no more than five hooks.
- No invented first-person Dan stories, client stories, statistics, percentages,
  timeframes, quotes, results or market data.
- Do not open with student proof or a testimonial.
- Do not begin with a generic “AI is changing everything” or “AI will replace you.”
- Do not shrink the idea to revisions, Reels, TikTok, ChatGPT cleanup or scope creep.
- Do not reveal old-lead reactivation, SMS, revenue share, the book, price or guarantee.
- Avoid agency jargon and these words: leverage, solution, deliverable, onboarding,
  unlock, game-changer, hack, secret.
- One or two spoken-natural lines per hook, ideally 12–30 words.
- Questions are allowed only when the question itself carries the full tension.
- Push to the edge of reality without claiming universals we cannot prove.

## Quality bar

The bare idea under each line must still be interesting without charged wording.
The reader should instantly understand the threat without completing the argument.
The strongest hooks should make a service provider think: “Fuck—that is exactly what is
happening. Keeping the client doesn't mean I'm safe.”

# Claude comparison brief — Instant AI Agency

Write one spoken Facebook video ad for Dan Wardrope's **Instant AI Agency** book.

Treat this as a fresh write. Do not ask for or imitate any existing draft.

## Output

- Return **primary ad copy only**.
- Hard maximum: **250 words including the CTA and URL**.
- Aim for **210-235 words** because Dan speaks slowly.
- One sentence per line.
- Do not include headlines, strategy notes, explanations or a word count.

## Audience

Existing agency owners, freelancers and online service providers, especially:

- Copywriters
- Funnel builders
- SMMA managers
- SEO providers
- Other people selling skilled online services

They fear being replaced by AI.

Even when AI does not replace them, they feel another threat: new projects are harder to win at the rates their work used to command.

Clients expect more output while wanting to pay less.

## Awareness and belief

The reader is **problem-aware**.

They have seen thinner budgets and lower offers, but may still blame cheap individual clients rather than recognise that AI has reset what buyers expect skilled output to cost.

Challenge this belief:

> “If my work is still good and I explain its value clearly enough, I can keep charging the old rate.”

## Concept

**Concept type: The Real Cause.**

AI does not need to replace a service provider to hurt their income.

AI can make buyers value service output less before the provider ever quotes, even though the provider still carries the judgement, revisions, responsibility and blame when the finished work fails.

Internal problem name: **The Pay Cut Before the Pitch.**

Do not force that phrase into the script unless it sounds natural.

## Angle

**Deep Fear.**

The feared pay cut is not waiting in the future.

It is already appearing inside new briefs and smaller buyer budgets.

The emotional effect should be recognition followed by relief, not melodrama.

## Concrete image

A familiar, full-sized project brief lands in the provider's inbox.

The client expects the same calibre of work and the same commercial outcome, but the budget is thinner before the provider has even quoted.

## Cognitive binary

**Marked-Down Output vs. Paid-On-Results Revenue.**

Output is compared against what AI can produce quickly and cheaply.

Measurable sales still carry obvious commercial value.

## Argument spine

1. Open with curiosity or a blunt market truth. Do not explain the entire argument in the first line.
2. Show the familiar brief arriving with the same expectations and less money attached.
3. Explain that buyers have seen AI produce service output quickly, so they assume the hard part has vanished.
4. Show that the provider still carries the thinking, revisions, responsibility and blame.
5. Name the squeeze: clients expect more while paying less.
6. Show the failed response: longer proposals, fatter retainers, more value justification and another large upfront fee.
7. Pivot: stop fighting over what output is worth and change what the client pays for.
8. Introduce the Instant AI Agency model: use AI + SMS to reactivate a business's old leads and turn them into measurable sales.
9. The business has already paid to acquire those leads and largely written them off.
10. Offer to work without a setup fee and get paid from the sales generated.
11. The provider takes **30-50% of the revenue generated**.
12. Make the “AI-proof” point carefully: use AI to create the result the client wants, then get paid from that result.
13. Transition into the book, price, guarantee and CTA.

## Product and offer

- Product: **The Instant AI Agency** book
- Price: **$14.40**
- Promise: a step-by-step blueprint for finding clients, reactivating old leads with AI + SMS and structuring performance-based deals
- Guarantee: **30-day money-back guarantee where the buyer keeps the book either way**
- CTA URL: `book.flexxable.com/optin`

## Verified claims allowed

- The model uses AI + SMS to reactivate a business's old or inactive leads.
- The model is performance-based rather than an upfront retainer.
- There is no setup fee in the offer.
- The client pays from sales generated.
- The provider can take 30-50% of the revenue generated.
- The book costs $14.40.
- The 30-day guarantee allows the buyer to keep the book.

## Proof and truth constraints

- Do not invent a result, statistic, timeframe, study, client quote or personal story.
- Do not use testimonials or student proof in this ad.
- Do not invent first-person history for Dan.
- Do not claim every buyer, provider or brief behaves this way.
- Do not quantify the decline in service rates.
- Do not claim a performance deal can never be negotiated or rejected.
- Do not call the offer completely risk-free. State the exact fact: no upfront fee and payment comes from sales generated.
- Do not promise literal immunity from AI. “AI-proof” is positioning for changing what the provider gets paid for.

## Voice

- Dan speaking slowly to camera.
- Casual, blunt and Australian-inflected.
- Sound like he is talking to a mate who owns a business.
- Short paragraphs with natural spoken flow.
- Use ellipses sparingly where a thought genuinely trails.
- Use simple, concrete words rather than marketing jargon.
- Pain should amplify after the opening, not become a generic suffering hook.

## Avoid

- Crime-scene, knife, blood, biting, guts or clearance-rack metaphors
- Corporate words such as `leverage`, `solution`, `deliverable` or `onboarding`
- Tidy `not X, but Y` reversals
- Stacked abstract triplets
- Fake one-word punch lines
- A proof stack
- More than one central curiosity thread

The finished script should feel like one inevitable argument:

> AI has marked down skilled output, so stop defending the old price of output and get paid from measurable sales instead.

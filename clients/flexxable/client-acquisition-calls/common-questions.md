# Common Questions — Ranked (25 calls, Mar–Aug 2026)

What ROYA students actually ask on these calls, ranked by how often the theme
came up across the full 25-call corpus (5-call Aug pilot + 20 calls pulled
from Mar–Jul 2026, back to the start of Zoom's retention window — see
[README.md](README.md) for the retention-boundary note). This is the "what
problems are people having" signal — useful for picking daily-post topics
that answer a real, recurring question rather than guessing.

Ranking is by number of distinct students who raised the theme (not raw
mention count), since one talkative student can otherwise skew a smaller
sample. The 20 new calls didn't just add more instances of the pilot's 9
themes — they surfaced one theme the pilot barely touched (item #2b below),
which shows up constantly once you look at first-timers specifically.

## 1. Pricing — how much do I charge, and how (retainer vs. performance vs. setup fee)?
**Raised by:** Abhi, Patrick, Gary, Pierre, Greg, Phil (all pilot batch —
08-10/08-12/08-19/08-24), plus, in the new batch: Alex and Dorene (03-30,
super-connector referral-fee framing), Charles Koonce (05-04, sales-cycle
length vs. commission structure), Rob (06-24, referral-fee percentage vs.
cap), Gareth (07-08, £18K retainer vs. per-sale for a heating company that's
"not short of cash"), Philip Jepson (07-01, pushback on keeping "payment by
results" deliberately vague), Jenisse Lim (06-08, first-ever deal, unsure
what to charge). Still, by a wide margin, the single most common theme —
nearly every call has at least one extended pricing conversation.

Sub-questions that recur inside this theme, now with more resolved answers
from the new batch:
- Retainer vs. pure performance vs. per-appointment vs. a hybrid — which,
  and when? (new answer pattern: ask "what's going to make this feel like a
  relief rather than a risk for you?" and let the client pick the shape —
  Nat, 07-01)
- How do I figure out what a specific client can bear? (size the price to
  their pain, not a fixed rate card — gold-nuggets.md #7; reverse-engineer
  it from what they already pay per call/lead — gold-nuggets.md #31)
- Anchor a referral fee as a fixed "up to £X" cap rather than a percentage —
  it reads as a stronger, easier-to-say-yes-to number (gold-nuggets.md #30)
- How do I verify a performance deal without full access to their CRM?
  (campaign-level, view-only access — gold-nuggets.md #42)
- Client is pushing back / lowballing — how do I hold the line without
  losing the deal?

## 2a. "Where do I even start?" — niche selection and general overwhelm
**Raised by:** Greg, Patrick, and Gary (08-12, pilot batch), Tallinn and
Marc Krull (pilot batch), plus, in the new batch: David Easton (07-13, big
warm-network list but no idea how to sequence it), Howard Whiteson (05-18 —
the *inverse* problem, paralyzed by having *too much* warm network across
5,000 LinkedIn contacts and multiple countries, not too little), Gabriel
(05-25, unsure which of several client opportunities to focus on).

Still an emotional/orientation question more than a technical one — but the
new batch shows it isn't only "I don't know anyone," it's also "I know too
many people and don't know where to start," and "I have leads coming from
three directions and don't know which to prioritize."

## 2b. Confused about ROYA's own onboarding structure — NEW, and very common among first-timers
**Raised by:** an unnamed member posting as "GHD Agency" (03-30), Cody Dale
(05-11), Kip Niswonger and Joe Garrity (06-29), Joe Garrity again across at
least three more calls (07-01, 07-06, 07-13, 07-20), Eric Gousheh (06-22,
re: the Hulk update specifically), Paul Brenkman (07-13, re: Hulk + Twilio
voice setup), Wade Collins (05-25, a returning member locked out of content
he used to have access to).

This is distinct enough from 2a that it deserves its own line: it's not
emotional overwhelm about *sales strategy*, it's literal navigational
confusion inside ROYA's own product surface — which of the "flexible
members area" (the original book-buyer area, still showing the old Prince
Charming Challenge) vs. the Roya 3.0 course vs. the Roya School group vs.
the Sales Wingman platform vs. a paid "done-for-you" build is the right
place to be, and in what order. Joe Garrity's case across four separate
calls is the clearest single illustration: he kept defaulting back to the
Prince Charming Challenge (the book buyer's original area) because ROYA's
welcome email doesn't get resent to people who already have a login from
buying the book, so he never saw the "here's what's actually new" email
pointing him to the real course. Several first-timers also don't realize
you can demo everything (Sales Wingman) with zero tech setup, before you've
touched GoHighLevel, A2P, or a phone number at all — Cody Dale and Kip
Niswonger both got stuck feeling like they had to fully build the tech
before they were "allowed" to start selling.

**Why this matters for content:** it's a real, repeated pain point, but it's
about ROYA's product experience, not about client-acquisition strategy —
so it's not directly usable as a Flexxable content angle the way the other
items are. Flagging it here mainly so Joey isn't surprised by how much of
the raw call time in the new batch is "where do I click," not sales
coaching.

## 3. Which outreach channel actually works — LinkedIn vs. Facebook groups vs. cold calling vs. events vs. lumpy mail?
**Raised by:** the whole pilot batch (effectively every call), plus, in the
new batch: Philip Jepson (06-24/07-01, LinkedIn crickets → cold email →
direct mail → billboards/local ad booklets as conversation-openers), Mark
"yoben" (06-24/06-29, Meta ad ban forcing a pivot to trade shows and local
print), Joe Garrity (05-25 onward, cold-calling roofers and fighting past
gatekeepers), David Easton and Michael Oliver (07-13, warm-network-first
sequencing).

New answer patterns from this batch: check *offline* ad spend too (local ad
booklets, billboards) the same way you'd check Facebook Ad Library online —
"I could imagine what they're paying for that" is as valid an opener as a
digital ads check; and cold-calling gatekeepers specifically benefits from
targeting the sales manager rather than the receptionist, and appealing to
the gatekeeper's own self-interest (does this make them look good to their
boss?) rather than the end business owner's.

## 4. A2P / SMS compliance and international rules
**Raised by:** the whole pilot batch, plus, in the new batch: Mike Dudley
(07-01, Australia's brand-new SMS sender-registration rule, effective 1
July 2026 — essentially an Australian A2P equivalent), Jenisse Lim and Ethan
(06-08, Malaysia — SMS isn't culturally used there, so DBR runs over
WhatsApp instead), Brian (03-16/03-30, US financial-services and healthcare
A2P nuances, HIPAA edge cases), Marc (05-27, Canada's stricter list-cleaning
rules).

Confirmed pattern across an even wider set of countries now: every market
has its own version of "prove you're a real business before you can text
people at scale," and the smart move on a coffee date is to reframe the
delay as a feature ("this is what keeps us separate from scammers"), not
apologize for it.

## 5. Tech architecture — how does Sales Wingman actually connect to GoHighLevel?
**Raised by:** the whole pilot batch, plus, in the new batch: Franky
(05-11/06-08, A2P number-per-client rules, porting numbers, whether
speed-to-lead and DBR can run simultaneously), Eric Gousheh (06-15/06-22,
what the "Hulk" mode actually is and when to use it vs. the original
one-bot-at-a-time build), Paul Brenkman (07-13, Twilio credentials for
non-English voice AI), and a general wave of confusion once the "Hulk"
feature launched (multiple students across 06-15 through 07-13).

Sub-questions, updated for the Hulk era: the Hulk builds all six bot types
off one web-scrape so you're never caught without a demo mid-coffee-date,
but it isn't required if a client only wants one specific bot; you still
need a real phone number and A2P only once a client says yes, never to
demo; e-commerce/Shopify builds are handled separately from the Hulk because
they need special inventory-scraping tech.

## 6. Positioning and objection handling
**Raised by:** the whole pilot batch, plus a much wider set in the new
batch: Franky (05-11, "is this AI?" coming up mid-demo), Michael Oliver and
Alexander Hamilton (06-01/07-13, "why not just use GoHighLevel's built-in
AI / ChatGPT myself"), Mark Avila (06-01, "we don't work with recruitment
agencies" as a knee-jerk category objection), JSpeck (05-25, "what's the
price?" as the very first thing a cold prospect asks).

New, more developed answer patterns: don't deny it's AI, give it a name and
a personality instead (gold-nuggets.md #26); reframe a flat categorical "no"
as agreement, then ask an assumptive question instead of an open one
(gold-nuggets.md #39); the native-tool objection is best met with an
analogy (a personal trainer doesn't teach you anything you couldn't Google,
but you still hire one to consolidate it — gold-nuggets.md #22).

## 7. DBR mechanics — how old is too old, what's the ideal lead age, and what counts as a usable list?
**Raised by:** the whole pilot batch, plus, in the new batch: Franky
(05-11, realistic response/conversion-rate benchmarks — 30-50% response,
5-20% conversion), Dorene Wharton (07-06, a list with no first names at
all), Joe Garrity (07-06/07-08, the difference between leads bought from a
lead-gen brand vs. a scraped data list), Rob (06-24, whether DBR works for
lapsed golf-club memberships, not just sales leads).

The new batch broadens this beyond "how old" into "what even counts as a
workable list": DBR now confirmed to work on cancelled memberships and
subscriptions (gold-nuggets.md #40), and the "bought leads" question needs
a follow-up clarifying question every time — opted-in leads from a lead-gen
brand are fine, a scraped data list is a hard no (gold-nuggets.md #41).

## 10. Finding and vetting a technical partner — NEW
**Raised by:** Joe Garrity (07-13/07-20, posted in every "Tech Terminator"
thread he could find and got ghosted by all but one), Cal Costea (07-13,
vetting a website-and-SEO partner he wants to bring in on a bigger deal),
Eric Gousheh (06-22, whether to use the paid "done-for-you" build or learn
the Sales Wingman himself).

This is a genuinely new theme in the 20-call batch: non-technical members
(a large chunk of ROYA's membership, by their own description) hitting a
wall trying to find someone to do the technical build for them, either
because Tech Terminators aren't responding or because it's unclear when
you actually need one versus when Sales Wingman already covers it. Leandro's
answer pattern: vet a prospective partner the same low-risk way you'd vet a
client — give them one small, deadline-bound task (like "send me that CSV
by Thursday morning") before trusting them with a real deal, and favour
people who already have a reputation to protect inside the ROYA community
itself.

## Read on this data
Pricing anxiety and orientation/overwhelm still dwarf everything else across
the full 25-call corpus — that hasn't changed since the pilot. What *has*
changed with 20 more calls in view is that "where do I even start" is now
clearly two separate problems, not one: a strategic/emotional one (2a, which
was already visible in the pilot) and a purely navigational one specific to
ROYA's own product surface (2b, which barely showed up in five calls but is
everywhere once you look at 20 first-timers). If Joey wants the
highest-leverage daily-post topics, pricing and 2a are still the safest
bets — they're not niche-specific, they hit almost every student regardless
of experience level, and they map directly onto content Flexxable can
produce without needing a specific student's story (avoids the
naming/attribution caution in [README.md](README.md)). Item 2b is real and
worth knowing about, but it's ROYA-product feedback more than a
client-acquisition insight, so it doesn't translate into a Flexxable content
angle the way the others do.

# Pull out the message — paste this, then paste the ad

You are decoding a winning ad into the parts that must survive when it's re-told in a new format or a new setting. Do not rewrite it, do not improve it, do not recommend anything. Quote exactly. Never turn a charged line into a softer one. If something can't be told from the text, say so.

Answer in this order:

1. WHO IT'S FOR: what they want, who they are, why they're here, and what they already believe when the first line lands (unaware / problem-aware / solution-aware / product-aware).
2. THE CLAIM: the hidden cause behind their symptom and how the product reaches it, in the ad's own words. Is the claim causal (X causes Y), a relabel (it's not X, it's Y), a magnitude (N times), or a reveal (you can now see)?
3. WHY THEY CARE NOW: who is telling them and why them, where this came from, why now, what's at stake.
4. THE BEATS, in order. Tag each with the question it answers: my problem is really caused by Y · what I tried never touched Y · Z fixes Y · this applies to me · I can't lose · now, not later. Mark which beat is the hook and which carries the whole ad.
5. THE HOOK: the first line verbatim, and what it flips (what the reader assumed, what the line inverts).
6. THE LOADED WORDS: every phrase doing work, as exact quotes: coined names, crime verbs (the villain doing something physical), lines lifted from the market. These travel unchanged.
7. NOT THE MESSAGE: who's on camera, where, how it looks, how it's delivered, how the argument moves through time, how long it is. This is what can change.

Finish with two lists: FROZEN (survives verbatim) and CHANGEABLE (everything in 7). Then name any of the six questions the ad never answers.

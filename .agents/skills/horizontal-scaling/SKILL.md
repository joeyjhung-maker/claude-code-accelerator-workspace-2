# Horizontal Scaling

There are two ways an ad account grows with creative. **Vertical** is same people, more to say to them. **Horizontal** is new people. This is horizontal: stop asking "what else can I say to these people?" and ask "who else is out there?"

One warning first: **don't go horizontal too early.** If two segments are printing, squeeze them. Horizontal is for when vertical is running dry.

The sentence to keep: *ads are made of parts, some parts are proven and some are cheap to swap, so every launch should be one cheap swap away from something proven.* A new segment is the expensive swap. This method exists to make sure the segment is real before anyone pays for it.

## 1. The grammar: ODF

A segment is not a vibe. It has three parts.

- **O — Outcome.** What they want. Every outcome breaks into **sub-outcomes** through five lenses: type (which kind), location (where it shows), function (what stops working), moment (when it hits), severity (how bad).
- **D — Demographic.** Who they are in a way you could tick from across the room: gender, age band, buying for self or someone else, occupation, ethnicity.
- **F — Facet.** Why they're here. Invisible from across the room, and the most underrated axis. Five families: **Belief** (what they think causes it), **Identity** (who they say they are), **State** (a condition, what they're on, what they tried), **Value** (what they prefer), **Occasion** (something that *happened*; it has a date).

A segment is one outcome, one demographic, a facet or two: "belly fat · women 45+ · just hit menopause." Change any part and it's a different human, usually a different ad. Most accounts slice on O and D. **The gold is in F and the sub-outcomes.**

Two laws of a good facet: it's **self-verifiable** (they settle "is this me?" in one second: "do you sit six hours a day?") and it usually **carries its mechanism** (sitting → blood flow), which half-explains the problem before the ad does. The facet also sets the awareness: "just hit menopause" is problem-aware; "tried three cleanses that failed" is solution-aware and needs a failure-explainer, not an intro.

The trap: going wider on the outcome ("maybe we hit the cholesterol market") is a new market, with new proof and a new story. Keep the outcome, add a facet. Same product, same proof, new door.

## 2. The flow

1. **Load what they have.** Product facts, the outcome they sell today, who buys now, what's winning, what's fatiguing. Say it back in a paragraph.
2. **Gather the market's words.** Two sources, both cheap: their own top ads and their competitors' longest-running ads (an export from any ad-intel tool, or pasted), and real humans talking: comments under those ads, Amazon reviews of competitors (1–3★ and 5★), Reddit, YouTube comments. Paste it all in. Never edit a quote.
3. **Draft the map.** Outcomes with their sub-outcomes through the five lenses; demographics; facets by family, each with its one-second self-ID line and the mechanism it carries. Then tag every ad (theirs and competitors') to ONE cell, so you can see which cells are taken and which are empty.
4. **Review it together, numbered.** One list, existing items first, then proposed. Each line: the name, which axis, the proof (quote the line: an own ad, a competitor ad with its run-days, a comment, or HYPOTHESIS if you invented it), one-line take (good / meh / bad and why), and the ask (keep / add / merge / drop / hold). The user answers by number in one message.
5. **One more loose pass.** What else is possible? Walk each outcome through the five lenses again; propose the missing siblings (one ethnicity → the others; an age band that stops at 45 → the one below; the conditions the category obviously has: vegetarian, smoker, diabetic, on a GLP-1, on statins, shift worker). Mark them HYPOTHESIS.
6. **The tiered persona list.** Every segment worth a persona in three tiers: **Tier 1 · you should have these** (proven on two sides, or the obvious missing column), **Tier 2 · recommended** (real signal on one side), **Tier 3 · could do** (hypotheses; cheap to test small). **Name the human, not the outcome:** *Booster-Burned Bill (testosterone · tried boosters, failed · wants lab proof)*. Each entry: name, coordinate, proof in a phrase, what changes in the ad. They reply with numbers.
7. **Write the personas they picked.** One page each: the coordinate, the awareness, the one-second self-ID line, what they've tried that failed, what they won't hear, the **verbatim quote bank** (their exact words, with the source), and the first transplant: which existing winner to aim at them and the only things that change.
8. **Swipe the winners by segment. Mad Libs, not rewrites.** Aim every existing winner at every new persona. The winner's skeleton transfers word for word: sentence count, line breaks, rhythm, beat order, mechanism, proof, offer, CTA, certainty. Only the segment payload moves: the self-ID line, the symptoms and scenes, what they tried, the words from their quote bank. Most sentences of a good pass are untouched. Never add a beat, an explainer, a risk line or an urgency line the winner doesn't have; never soften a line. Then ten extra hooks per swipe in the persona's own words. A swipe that wins validates the segment.

## 3. Rules that keep the map honest

- **Proof is quoted, not summarized.** Strength: own winner > long-running competitor ad > comment under a running ad > cold comment > hypothesis. Never hide your own inventions.
- **The AI-artifact test.** A phrase that appears in many ads and zero human mouths ("the 2 p.m. crash", "brain fog") is a universal symptom line every buyer nods at. That's copy, not a segment. Ask: does this split the market into a different human who needs a different ad? Count across humans in their own words, not across ads.
- **Function and severity cuts make different humans** (dead bedroom vs. weak erections); **moment cuts usually make symptom lines.**
- **Competitor coinage** ("stress bloat") is not the market's word. Map the plain word.
- **Refuse new markets in disguise.** A sub-outcome that needs new proof is a different account.

## 4. Quality bar

Every facet passes the one-second test · every quote is exactly as the human wrote it · every persona has a self-ID line built from their words and a first transplant · a swipe keeps most of the winner's sentences verbatim and adds none · outputs are complete and ready to paste.

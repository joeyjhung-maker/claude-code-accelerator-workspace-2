## The genome

*The five diagrams from the deck are saved in `concepts/_assets/` and shown in `concepts/Ad Genome.md`.*

Every ad carries a stack of parts. From most fundamental to most subtle:

1. Segments and sub-segments
2. The five awareness levels
3. Concepts, angles, formats
4. Conceits
5. Actors, styles, looks
6. Copy beats

A winner is one lit-up path through that stack: one segment, one message, one
creative treatment, proven with spend. The work is finding more lit-up paths.

- **Horizontal** = a new spot on the beach. New segments and personas.
- **Vertical** = the same hole, deeper. Winning message × new styles, formats
  and conceits.

## Segments: outcome, demographic, facet

Joey's `/segments` skill already runs this model (what / who / why). The deck
adds detail.

- **Outcome** = what they want. It splits into sub-outcomes through five
  lenses: the type, where it shows, what stops working, when it hits, how bad.
- **Demographic** = who they are, visible from across the room.
- **Facet** = why they're here. Invisible from across the room, and the most
  underrated axis. Five families: a belief, an identity, a state, a value, an
  occasion (something that happened, with a date).

**Two tests for a good facet.** They can check "is this me?" in one second.
And it usually carries its own mechanism, so it half-explains the problem
before the ad does.

**The trap.** Going wider on the outcome is a new market, with new proof and a
new story. Keep the outcome and add a facet: same product, same proof, new
door.

**Keeping the map honest.**
- Proof is quoted. Strength order: own winner, long-running competitor ad,
  comment under a running ad, cold comment, hypothesis.
- A phrase that appears in many ads and no human mouths is copy, not a segment.
- A competitor's coined term isn't the market's word. Map the plain word.

**Tiering personas.** Tier 1: proven on two sides, or the obvious missing
column. Tier 2: real signal on one side. Tier 3: hypotheses, cheap to test.
Name the human, not the outcome.

## Styles

A style is the filter the ad is rendered through. Same script, same claims,
same offer, different picture. Test: could the exact script be re-rendered in
it with nothing changed but the picture? If the script has to change shape,
it's a conceit.

The Genesis list has 183 entries in seven groups: live-action captured looks;
3D and rendered; 2D and drawn; screen and interface; broadcast, print and
document; motion graphics; composite and mixed reality. It lives in their Drive
folder (`2-styles/winning-ad-styles.md`), with public examples in
`style-references.md`.

Three rules: match the skin to the awareness; a cut from a drawn style to real
footage at the product reveal signals "this part is real"; some styles pick the
audience before a word lands.

## Formats and conceits

- **Format** fixes the beat order: timeline, listicle, story, comparison,
  confession, testimonial.
- **Conceit** fixes who speaks, where, and the genre's conventions: podcast
  clip, street interview, doctor consult, the villain speaking.

**The message** is what must survive a re-staging: who it's for and what they
already believe; the claim; why they care now; the beats in order; the hook
verbatim; the loaded words.

See also the wiki page `ad-grammar-structure-conceit-actor` and, for one format
worked out in full, `/timeline-ad`.

## Comments

Sort every comment into lenses. Output is lists of quotes.

Who's here · what's blocking the sale (trust, offer, competition, efficacy,
frame) · what the likes hide (rebuttals of the premise, outcomes nobody
promised) · objects · where they are in the story · body states · symbols ·
settings · identity · language · trust and transaction · comments that are
already hooks · flat facts about the audience.

Then: raw ideas (what do they blame that isn't responsible? what are they doing
that makes it worse?) → the uninhibited version of each → hooks. Soften only
the one piece the product can't back, and only as far as needed.

One page type per run. Don't pool a guru page's comments with a
direct-response page's.

## Organic hooks

Organic proves people stop. It proves nothing about whether they buy. So it
only ever supplies the top of an ad.

Burner account → prime the feed on the market's own search terms → spot
outliers against each creator's own normal → pull the three hooks (spoken,
on-screen, caption) word for word → weld onto a frozen proven body.

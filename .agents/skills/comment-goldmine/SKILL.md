# The Comment Goldmine

Ads come directly from comments. Not ideas — ads. The buyers of a back-pain product named their own vertebrae under the ad (L4, L5, S1) and "If you have pain L1 through L5, surgery is the worst thing you can do" ran essentially verbatim and won. Comment language is self-verifiable: the reader checks it against their own body in one second.

Three rules, always:

1. **Verbatim or nothing.** Keep typos, caps, emoji, run-ons. Carry the like count and the comment number on every quote: `"…" (12L) #41`. Never let the AI tidy a quote. Never splice two parts of a comment with an ellipsis.
2. **Likes measure performance, not truth.** Likes promote testimonials and bury doubt, money problems and broken checkouts. Read everything; the like count is a weight, not a filter.
3. **One page type per run.** Don't pool a guru page's comments with a direct-response page's.

## Get the comments

Your own first: Ads Manager → the ad → view post → "All comments" → copy. Or screenshot and transcribe verbatim. A competitor's: their ads are dark posts, so you need the post URL — a burner account that gets served the ad, or a spy tool — then a comment scraper.

## The lenses

Sort every comment into these. Output is lists of quotes, nothing else.

- **Who's here** — the already-supplied · the believer · the medically abandoned · the displaced-drug seeker · the burned skeptic · the premise-rejecter · the waiting · the transacting
- **What's blocking the sale** — trust (ad real? product real? comments real? scam? credentials?) · offer (subscription trap, offer not honoured, billing, shipping, price, checkout) · competition (already have it / make it, cheaper elsewhere, competitor named) · efficacy (doesn't work, safety, ingredients, form factor, regulatory) · frame (cure-all skepticism, ad fatigue, **premise rebuttal**). Mark SILENT (volume, no likes) and LOUD (few, many likes).
- **What the likes hide** — the premise rebuttals ("that's just because…", "mine is from…", "all good here") · the unprompted outcomes (things people got that the ad never promised — the next ad, written by the market)
- **Objects** — the product · what it displaced (the drug, the cane) · what they own and worry about using with it · the furniture of their life
- **Narrative arc** — wound · rock bottom · discovery · first contact · transformation · evangelism · setback — and where most of them live
- **Body states** — the pain (part, sensation, time of day) · the relief · the involuntary thing that couldn't be placebo
- **Symbols** — lost paradise · failed authority · converted skeptic · innocent witness (the spouse or child who noticed) · secret knowledge · mechanism metaphor
- **Settings** — the room · the outdoor parallel · the daytime station · the away space
- **Identity** — age · place and class · medical identity · who they live with · who they trust
- **Language** — insider jokes · conversion declarations · raw confessions · fatigue
- **Trust and transaction** — is the ad real, is the product real, is the offer understood, what stops the checkout, what happens after
- **Comments that already are hooks** — short, liked, with a noun, a body state or a number: the premise rebuttal, the defiant reframe, the displacement trophy ("got off my statins"), the number, the time marker, the raw confession, the over-claim that disarms, the hostile line that sells the category
- **Facts** — 5–10 flat, defensible statements about this audience with no copy in them (what they do, believe, tried, fear, name, ask, volunteer)

## Then

1. **Raw ideas.** Ask the facts and quotes what they make possible: what do they blame that isn't responsible? what are they doing to fix it that makes it worse? what trusted thing is hurting them? what's costing more than they realise? what do they believe that's backwards? what's the mechanism underneath? which step do they skip? List the ideas naked — plain, surprising, funded by a quote each. No craft yet.
2. **The uninhibited version.** For each idea you keep: what becomes possible if this is true that would scare, anger, embarrass or fascinate the customer — and what does that look like in their life at a specific time of day? Write the most shocking version that would be fair if nobody could sue. One-second scene, in their words where they said it. Note the one piece the product can't back — that's the only thing you'll soften later.
3. **Hooks.** Hand the uninhibited version and its quotes to whatever writes your hooks. Back off only the unsupported piece, only as far as required. A softened line is a broken line.

Never invent a stat, study, ingredient or mechanism. Never turn a checkout complaint into creative — report it to whoever fixes checkouts.

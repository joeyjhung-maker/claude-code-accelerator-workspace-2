# Hook Quality — the bar, the elements, the techniques

The hook is the highest-leverage line in the whole ad. This is the "ad book" — the rules a hook must
hit and the moves to generate them. Used by `bots/hook-bot.md` and the first-win flow. **Spend ~80% of
your writing time on the hook** (and what immediately follows) — the rest is structure AI is good at.

## Hooks must be EMOTIONALLY VICIOUS
Not mean — **vicious.** A vicious hook grabs something the reader already feels (a fear, a shame, a
dread, a suspicion) and **squeezes it so hard they physically cannot scroll past.** It goes right for
the throat in the *very first line* — no throat-clearing, no warm-up. A polite, comfortable, hedged, or
vague hook is a **dead hook.** If it doesn't make someone flinch, gasp, or feel personally called out,
it's not vicious enough — go find the version that flinches. **There is always a more vicious version.**

**The 8 principles:**
1. **Relevance in the first line** (the first 10–20 words). Three ways in: **pain/condition call-out**
   ("the water you're drinking is feeding the bacteria slowly killing you") · **belief call-out** ("they
   told you red meat would clog your arteries") · **general openness** (broad intrigue — "my husband
   came out as gay after 26 years, and it nearly killed me"; hardest, biggest bets).
2. **Emotionally charged language** — visceral, embodied: *banned, sexless, stink, lying, die, naked,
   killing, terrifying, beg.*
3. **Natural intrigue / open loops** — promise a story or a reveal the reader *has* to resolve.
4. **Stakes / rubbernecking** — real, present consequences they can't look away from.
5. **Specificity & tangibility** — "a 300-page report from 1910," not "an old report." Specific = reads
   like a fact; vague = reads like an ad.
6. **Doesn't read like an ad** — it reads like news, a story, a confession, or an insight; the payoff is
   catharsis/intrigue, not a pitch.
7. **Caveman / Germanic language** — short, blunt, 5-year-old-simple. *fabrics→cloth · excessive→too
   much · insomnia→sleeplessness · severe→bad · perspire→sweat · inflammatory response→swelling.* The
   French perspire, the Germans sweat — **use the German word.**
8. **Consequence first, mechanism later** — lead with the wound, not the scalpel. ("THIS habit spikes
   cortisol" is mechanism — invisible to the reader. Lead with what happens to *them*.)

**Every word must earn its place — protect the charged word.** "Sexless" can't become "unfulfilling";
"killing" can't become "hurting." If you reach for a softer, safer, clinical synonym, STOP — that
instinct is the enemy. **Push to the edge of reality** (sounds almost unbelievable, without crossing it).
This is more art than science — built by saturating your brain on ads.

## The checklist — every hook hits ALL of these
- ☐ **10–20 words before the fold** (the text before "See more")
- ☐ **Tangible & specific** — symptoms, numbers, concrete details (not abstractions)
- ☐ **Unresolved curiosity** — the reader MUST know what comes next
- ☐ **Relevant to their pain + things they already know** — speaks to their world
- ☐ **Promises value** — insight, reframe, new information
- ☐ **Subtle sensationalism, not clickbait** — bold but credible
- ☐ **Emotionally charged / visceral / visual** — physical, embodied words
- ☐ **Widely relevant** — doesn't disqualify too early with niche specifics
- ☐ **Simple enough to grasp immediately**
- ☐ **Reads like information/news, NOT like an ad** — editorial tone
- ☐ **Level 3 emotional viciousness** — an emotional bomb, not vague or hinting

## The 10 power elements (draw on as many as possible)
Novel · Shocking · Engaging · Controversial · Specific · Unpredictable · Bold · Emotional · Urgent ·
Topic-framed (clearly about their world).

## Pre-hook ideation — mine before you generate
1. **Mine the material** — read the brief/winning ads, list EVERY interesting idea, line, sentiment,
   mechanism. Look at what's buried, not just the obvious angle.
2. **Promotion check** — for each seed, ask "how do I promote this into the opening line?" A hook can
   come from anywhere: body copy, mechanism, a proof point, a comment phrase.
3. **Apply the three techniques:**
   - **Transfer** — find the category a concept belongs to, then substitute. (coffee → ritual → gym
     routine, prayer, bedtime routine.) Preserves the emotional structure, fresh surface.
   - **Reframe** — "It's not X, it's Y." Flip their belief about the cause. ("It's not low T, it's
     high cortisol." "It's not willpower, it's your gut bacteria.") Installs a new mechanism belief.
   - **Promote** — take a line buried in paragraph 3–4 that makes you flinch and make it the opening.

## Relevance — two dimensions
1. **Pain/desire** — signal "this is about your [health/…]" within the first few words.
2. **Things they know** — reference symptoms, people, cultural touchstones already in their awareness.
   (Explicit: "Are you not hungry for breakfast? That's high cortisol." Implicit: "A single 300-page
   report published in 1910…") Both work — they must recognize it's FOR THEM within seconds.

## Example hooks (style reference — note the charged words doing the work)
- "Are you not hungry for breakfast in the morning? That's high cortisol."
- "They told you red meat and butter would clog your arteries."
- "Most people who die in their sleep never realize their body gives three quiet warnings first."
- "This is the body of an average senior in 1940… and this is the body of an average senior today."
- "A single 300-page report published in 1910 that wiped out an entire branch of medicine in a year."
- "Can I show you something terrifying?" · "Are your shoes killing you?"
- "Why did heart disease rates suddenly explode in the 1940s?"

Charged words: *farts, baffled, terrifying, killing, explode, banned, medical apocalypse.* They do
the heavy lifting.

## The generation loop
Prime on `knowledge/primers/hooks.md` → mine seeds → generate 10 hooks against the bar → **double-pass**
(take the best, apply **Transfer / Reframe / Promote**, push to Level 3, generate 10 more) = 20 total →
human selects → winners go back into the hook primer.

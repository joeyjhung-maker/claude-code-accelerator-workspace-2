# Primer Template (canonical structure)

A primer = **raw winning ads/hooks + a light wrapper.** It is the priming message you send first (the
"process this" call of the 2-step protocol). The customer fills the `###IMPORTANT` slot with their
always-use / don't-use steering. Copy one of these per primer type into `knowledge/primers/`.

---

## BODY primer (`knowledge/primers/payload/long-unaware-problem.md`, etc.)

```
Please process this and tell me you understand.

These are all of the winning ads for [BRAND] that we'll be writing for.

You'll be using these as models to create NEW ads based on their style, tone, length, details and more.

For now just use these as baseline, and we'll work through our process together step by step.

Do NOT write or make any ads TOO derivative from these, but just capture the essence, feel and
important details of the ads.

###IMPORTANT:
For most ads when writing or processing, please pay attention to these important details.

[Leave blank — customer fills in via dashboard with "always use" / "don't use" instructions]

###EXAMPLES:
[Paste 8–12 full winning ads of this type — complete, nothing truncated.]
```

## HOOK primer (`knowledge/primers/hooks.md`)

```
Please process this and tell me you understand.

These are all of the winning hooks for [BRAND].

You'll be using these as models for style and structure when generating new hooks.

Do NOT copy these directly — capture the essence, feel, and structure.

###IMPORTANT:
[Leave blank — customer fills in via dashboard]

###HOOKS:
[Paste the winning hooks.]
```

## HEADLINE primer (`knowledge/primers/headlines.md`)
Same shape, swap "hooks" → "headlines" and `###HOOKS:` → `###HEADLINES:`.

---

**Rules:** the winning ads ARE the truth — don't add product fact-sheets. Hand-write entries when you
can (most human). Cut by **awareness × length** for payload primers, by **format** for conceit primers.
Every winner you earn gets appended → the primer tightens over time (the flywheel). The `###IMPORTANT`
block is where steering lives (e.g. "always use the factory/blackout metaphor," "never name a year").

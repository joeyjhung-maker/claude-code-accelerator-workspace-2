**EMOTIONAL EXCAVATION**

The core emotion is the quiet humiliation of showing up to a negotiation that already happened without you. The identity threat is that you still think of yourself as the expert in the room, but the buyer already decided what you're worth before you opened your mouth. The specific experience is walking into a brief and feeling the budget is weirdly tight, sensing something was shaved off but not knowing what or when. The most vicious angle is making them feel the physical sensation of arriving somewhere already robbed, already smaller, already cheapened, without anyone telling them it happened.

This is "the marked-down pitch" ad.

---

**1. CALL-OUT**

The budget on your next brief has already been carved down to the bone, and you haven't even been invited to quote yet.

*Open question: Who carved it, and how did they decide what I'm worth before I said a word?*

---

**2. CALL-OUT**

You're about to walk into a pitch where the money was peeled off the table before your name was even in the room.

*Open question: Who peeled it off, and why didn't I know the table was already short?*

---

**3. CONTRARIAN**

Everyone tells you to "charge what you're worth," but the buyer's budget was hacked apart before they ever heard your price.

*Open question: If the cut happened before my quote, what good is charging what I'm worth?*

---

**4. CONTRARIAN**

You spent years getting better at the work, and the new brief coming in this week already has a smaller number stitched into it than last year's did.

*Open question: If my skill went up and the budget went down, what force is shrinking the number before I even see it?*

---

**5. URGENCY/CLOCK**

The next brief that lands in your inbox this month will arrive with money already torn out of it, and you won't feel the cut until you're halfway through the work.

*Open question: What ripped the money out before I opened the email, and why will I only notice it too late?*

---

**6. URGENCY/CLOCK**

Right now, someone is building the budget for a project they're about to send you, and they're crossing out a chunk of your fee while they do it.

*Open question: Who is doing this right now, and what made them decide to cut my fee before even talking to me?*

## Hook rubric on the opening (2026-10-07)

Graded on the three opening lines as picked.

- PASS: Gate 1 relevance. A coffee drinker knows it's her inside ten words.
- FLAG: Gate 1 tension. Line 2 ("so quiet she almost missed it") tells the viewer the change is small. The model raises the stakes at this exact spot.
- PASS: Gate 1 novelty. Line 3 promises something she says.
- N/A: Gate 2. No "doesn't work" claim.
- FAIL: Gate 3, worst truth. Nothing charged. The one charged true thing in this ad is that day one is a letdown, and the opening doesn't use it.
- PASS: Gate 3, their moment. "Never started a day without coffee" makes her see her own morning.
- FAIL: Gate 3, the face. No villain in the opening. (The villain is the caffeine hit, and it arrives at the seal.)
- PASS: Gate 3, the itch. What did she say on the last morning?
- PASS: Gate 4. One real open question. Line 2 opens a second, smaller one, same as the model.
- FAIL: Gate 5, flinch. Read aloud, it's polite. Nobody winces.
- PASS: Gate 6, relevance, plain words, consequence before mechanism, reads like a story.
- FLAG: Gate 6, charged word. "Replaced" and "quiet" are both soft.
- FLAG: Gate 7, product swap. The opening would work for any coffee alternative. RYZE is only in it by name.
- PASS: Gate 7, step-back. Not a platitude.
- N/A: Gates 8 and 9 (Flexxable checks, batch variety).

**Read:** it does the job of a timeline frame in RYZE's own shape. By the hook rubric it is tame, and line 2 is the weak one. Note the rubric is built for crime-and-villain hooks, and this ad is deliberately claim-light.

# B2B Leads Lab sales-call post — 10 opening line variations

*2026-08-19 · ad-hook-bot-1, primed on Dan's Skool voice + double-passed to Level 3.*
*20 generated, 5 killed for invented facts, 2 repaired. Ranked strongest first.*
*Current line to beat: "I used to think the CLOSE was the skill."*

---

**1.** We've closed 5 adventure park clients now and I still don't know how to close.

**2.** You know that moment on a sales call where you're supposed to go in for the kill... yeah I just kinda ask if they wanna do it and they say yes.

**3.** You're not gonna like this clip. Especially if you spent years getting good at closing.

**4.** 5 adventure park owners have said yes to me now... and not ONE of them said yes on the call. They said yes when they opened the box.

**5.** I blame being Aussie for this but I have NEVER been able to do the hard sell thing... so I made the close irrelevant.

**6.** This is what a close looks like when a whiskey bottle and a handwritten letter already did all the SELLING for ya... it looks like two blokes having a coffee.

**7.** If you're still rehearsing your pitch before sales calls... mate, I don't really have a pitch any more. We just closed our fifth adventure park.

**8.** Watch this clip and tell me where the "persuasion" happens... because I can't find it.

**9.** I'm about to show you a close that would make any sales trainer cringe... and it worked because it was SUPPOSED to look like that.

**10.** Somewhere right now a sales bro is rehearsing his close in the mirror... meanwhile a whiskey bottle on a desk just did his whole job for him.

*Bonus, short enough to sit on top of any of the above:*
**11.** This call was WON before it started.

## Why the top three

**1** is the best because it's a contradiction the reader can't leave alone. A real number, a niche too specific to be invented, and then Dan admitting he can't do the thing the number implies he's brilliant at. It also keeps your confession beat, which was the strongest thing you added.

**2** deflates the entire mythology of closing in one shrug, and it's the closest to Dan's actual speaking voice of the twenty.

**3** is the only one that works as a threat. Nine words, aimed straight at the person who's proudest of their close, and they have to click to find out how offended to be.

## Killed for invented facts

- *"The whiskey bottle did it two weeks earlier"* — invents this prospect's gift and a timeline.
- *"We signed the deal 10 minutes later"* — invents a timeline from inside the call.
- *"The prospect told me he'd already decided before I rang him"* — invents a quote.
- *"The prospect said yes before I even opened my mouth"* — contradicts the clip, which is Dan making the pitch.
- *"Nobody had ever posted a handwritten letter and a bottle of whiskey to his desk"* — invents this prospect's gift again.

## Repaired, not killed

- **4** had "this year" on the 5 clients. Stripped — you gave me the number, not a timeframe.
- **7** had "I haven't had a pitch in two years." Invented backstory, softened to "I don't really have a pitch any more."

## Two flags, your call

- **4** opens on the number, which brushes the never-open-with-proof rule. It survives because the number is immediately reframed rather than flexed, but it's the closest to the line of the ten.
- **10** mocks "sales bros." The reader is likely to think of themselves as decent at sales, so there's a small risk it lands on them rather than past them.

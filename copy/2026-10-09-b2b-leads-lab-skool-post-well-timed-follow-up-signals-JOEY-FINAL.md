HL: ONE well-timed follow-up beats ten generic ones

Most follow-ups fail before you even hit send.

And it’s not usually (altho sometimes it can be) because the message sucks.

But because the TIMING is off.

“Just bumping this up” is code for “I have nothing useful to say but I want something from you.”

Your prospect can smell that a mile away.

And ten of those in a row doesn’t make you persistent… it makes you annoying.

Here’s what actually works.

ONE follow-up that lands while the prospect is already IN MOTION.

Meaning they’re actively thinking about the problem you solve… right now… today.

So before you type a single word, look for signals.

Are they hiring for a role that hints they need help?
Are they actively growing?
Have they just opened a new site?
Are they openly looking for help?
Are they commenting on posts about a topic YOU can help with?

That last one is the juiciest.

Because LinkedIn is FULL of people posting lead magnets and free guides… “here’s how to fix X - comment XYZ if you want it” type stuff.

If your prospect commented on one of those posts…

They just raised their hand and told the entire internet they want help with that exact thing.

Now imagine your follow-up says something like…

“Saw your comment on [name]’s post about [topic]… we actually help companies sort that out. Worth a quick chat?”

You’re not comin’ in cold anymore.

But with a RELEVANT message landing at the exact right moment.

Totally different energy.

Which means the prospect feels seen instead of stalked.

If you joined Topa recently and have access to LinkedIn Signals, this gets a whole lot easier… it tracks who interacts with a chosen person’s posts and who posts a chosen keyword, every day, on autopilot.

Otherwise you can just do this manually.

So take some time and think about all the signals you wanna look out for.

Then, answer below…

👉🏻 What’s your niche and “signal(s)” that will show if your prospects are in motion?

## The homework reply close (Joey, 2026-10-09)

Joey: "this kind of homework reply tactic might work. We're getting them to do something based on
what we discussed to get them taking action. Don't want to overuse but it's ONE tactic we can
deploy in communities. Travis does this a lot."

How it is built here:
1. Tell them to go and think about the thing the post just taught.
2. "Then, answer below…"
3. One pointed question whose answer is their own homework: their niche plus the signals they will watch.

Joey's definition (same day): homework is "go do/apply/implement/work on the topic I explained so
you UNDERSTAND IT and start using it… and then report below with your answers." It should NOT be
"go reach out to 10 ppl and then report back", which is too much work with too long a turnaround.
"A simple 10 min task writing in their journal" is the right size.

It blends two of the options offered (niche in the comments, and one small task) but the reader
does the thinking and Dan promises nothing in return.

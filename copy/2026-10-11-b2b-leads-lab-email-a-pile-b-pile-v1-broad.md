Happy Sunday.

Old school copywriter Gary Halbert had this thing about mail.

He said everyone sorts it into two piles standing over the bin.

The A-pile is stuff that looks personal.

The B-pile is everything that obviously wants to sell you something.

The A-pile always gets opened… the B-pile… good luck.

His trick was dead simple… plain envelope, real stamp, no teaser copy on the outside.

Nothing that screams "I want your money."

Now think about your LinkedIn DMs for a sec.

Your prospects are doing the EXACT same sort.

One glance at the preview and they already know which pile you're in.

And most outreach? It announces itself as a pitch before they even tap on it.

The connection request that opens with your job title and a value prop.

The follow-up that reads like a brochure.

It's all B-pile, every time.

Here's something worth chewing on though.

A PACKAGE that lands on someone's actual desk?

That's never B-pile.

Nobody bins a parcel without ripping it open first.

But whether it's a parcel, a DM, or an email…

The question to ask yourself BEFORE you send anything is simple.

Does this LOOK personal… or does it look like a pitch?

Go check your last five outreach messages right now and be honest about which pile they'd land in.

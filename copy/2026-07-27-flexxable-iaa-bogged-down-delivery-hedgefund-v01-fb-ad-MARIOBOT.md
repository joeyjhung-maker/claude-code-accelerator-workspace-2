I copied this model from hedge fund managers... and the first thing it killed was the 11pm client texts, the bug fixes, and the knot in my stomach every time I closed a new deal.

Because that's the bit nobody warns you about when you start an agency.

Winning clients is supposed to feel good.

You closed the deal, you should be buzzing.

But instead there's this weird dread that kicks in... because you know what comes next.

The build, the back and forth, the notification badges piling up on your phone like a bloody rash that won't clear.

Client messages at all hours asking why something's broken.

You fixing it at midnight because if you don't, they'll be fuming by morning.

And the worst part is you DID the work, you delivered, and they're still unhappy because the thing you built needs constant babysitting... and guess who the babysitter is.

So you're stuck in this loop where more clients should mean more money... but actually just means more of YOUR time gets eaten alive.

Every new deal tightens the vice a little more.

That's the model most agency owners are trapped in right now, especially the ones building AI tools, chatbots, automations, whatever the trendy thing is this week.

You're basically signing up to be an unpaid tech support line with a nice logo.

I ran a version of this for years with my ad agency.

Different service, same trap.

Then I saw how hedge fund managers actually structure their deals... and it rewired how I think about agency revenue completely.

They don't build anything for the client, they don't maintain anything, they just take a percentage of the upside they generate, and that's the entire relationship.

So I asked myself... what if I could do that with local businesses?

Turns out, you can.

Every business you'll ever talk to is sitting on a database of old leads.

People who enquired weeks, months, sometimes years ago... and never bought.

The business owner wrote them off ages ago, and now they're just dead rows in a CRM somewhere.

You drop an AI employee into that database, it reaches out by SMS, starts real convos with those old prospects, and books appointments automatically.

You don't build the client a website, you don't manage their ads, and you don't configure some custom chatbot and then spend three weeks squashing bugs while they send you screenshots at 9am.

You install the bot, point it at their dead leads, and collect 30-50% of every sale it generates.

There's nothing to maintain, nothing to troubleshoot, and nothing for the client to message you about at stupid o'clock... because you didn't build them a thing.

You just turned their forgotten leads into revenue they weren't getting before.

That's the whole delivery.

One text sent, then silence, and your phone stays clean.

And because you're working leads the client already paid to acquire, there's no ad budget involved either, no CPLs, no campaign management, no algo anxiety.

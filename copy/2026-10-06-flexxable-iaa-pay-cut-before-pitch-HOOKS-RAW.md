## STEP 1 — EMOTIONAL EXCAVATION

The core emotion here is **the quiet humiliation of being pre-devalued**. Not losing a client. Not getting outbid. Something worse: walking into a pitch and discovering that your price was already slashed before you opened your mouth. The identity threat is devastating: "I am no longer the person who sets the price. Someone — or something — re-tagged me while I wasn't looking, and the buyer treats that lower number as obvious." The specific experience that makes this feel personal is the moment a familiar brief lands — same scope, same deliverables, same expected results — but the budget line is 40% lighter, and nobody apologizes for it because to them it's just what this costs now. The contradiction that's impossible to scroll past: your skill didn't drop, your work didn't shrink, the outcome they want didn't change... but your fee got crossed out anyway. The most vicious angle is the **yellow sticker** feeling — being marked down like clearance merchandise before the buyer even meets you. You're not competing on price. You've already been priced. **NAME: "The crossed-out fee ad."**

## STEP 2 — 10 HOOKS

**Hook 1**
Your fee got crossed out before the client even met you.

**Dominant vehicle:** Visceral accusation — states the wound as a fact the reader hasn't consciously named yet.
**Question it leaves open:** How is that possible? Who crossed it out, and when?

---

**Hook 2**
Someone slashed your rate before you walked into the pitch, and nobody told you.

**Dominant vehicle:** Betrayal framing — "nobody told you" implies it was done behind your back.
**Question it leaves open:** Who did it, and how did they decide the new number?

---

**Hook 3**
The budget was already cut before you saw the brief... and your name was nowhere near it.

**Dominant vehicle:** Exclusion — you weren't even in the room when your pay was decided.
**Question it leaves open:** What force is setting budgets before providers are even contacted?

---

**Hook 4**
Your quote hasn't left your mouth yet, but your price has already been marked down.

**Dominant vehicle:** Timing violation — the markdown happened before the conversation even started.
**Question it leaves open:** What marked it down, and how does the buyer already "know" the lower number?

---

**Hook 5**
A familiar brief just landed in your inbox with your old fee crossed out and a smaller number stuck over it.

**Dominant vehicle:** Concrete scene — the yellow-sticker image rendered as a moment the reader can see.
**Question it leaves open:** Why is the scope identical but the fee shrunken, and who decided that?

---

**Hook 6**
The brief looks the same, the deliverables are the same, and your fee has been gutted before you typed a single word.

**Dominant vehicle:** Repetition-then-betrayal — "same, same, same... gutted." The pattern break is the violence.
**Question it leaves open:** What changed between the last time you quoted this work and now?

---

**Hook 7**
You haven't pitched yet and you've already taken a pay cut.

**Dominant vehicle:** Blunt, short, bodily — "pay cut" lands like a slap. Maximum compression.
**Question it leaves open:** How can you take a pay cut on a job you haven't even quoted?

---

**Hook 8**
Somewhere between your last project and your next pitch, your rate got re-tagged like clearance merchandise.

**Dominant vehicle:** Metaphor with mouthfeel — "re-tagged like clearance merchandise" is humiliating and tactile.
**Question it leaves open:** What happened in that gap that re-priced you without your knowledge?

---

**Hook 9**
The strangest thing is happening to agency pitches right now, and most providers won't notice until the budget line hits them in the teeth.

**Dominant vehicle:** Dread + delayed discovery — "won't notice until" implies the damage is already done.
**Question it leaves open:** What is the "strangest thing," and why can't providers see it coming?

---

**Hook 10**
Nobody called to say your rate dropped. The next brief just showed up lighter, like it was obvious.

**Dominant vehicle:** Quiet brutality — the absence of announcement is the cruelty. "Like it was obvious" twists the knife.
**Question it leaves open:** What made the buyer treat a lower fee as the default without any negotiation?

**TEST 4 — FRAGMENT CHECK:** All hooks are complete sentences with subjects. No neo-noir fragments. Read aloud: natural, conversational flow. ✓

**TEST 5 — VICIOUSNESS CHECK:** Hooks 1, 7, 8, and 10 all produce a flinch. "Crossed out before the client even met you" is accusatory and specific. "Re-tagged like clearance merchandise" is humiliating. "Like it was obvious" is quietly devastating. ✓

## TOP 3

⚡ **Hook 7 — "You haven't pitched yet and you've already taken a pay cut."**
Most dangerous because it's the shortest, hardest, and most impossible to argue with. "Pay cut" is a phrase every worker dreads, and attaching it to a pitch that hasn't happened yet creates a cognitive dissonance that demands resolution. It greases the slide into the body instantly because the reader needs to understand HOW.

⚡ **Hook 1 — "Your fee got crossed out before the client even met you."**
Most dangerous because "crossed out" is visual, physical, and violent — you can see the red line through your number. The timing ("before the client even met you") is the gut punch. It names the exact wound this ad exists to press on.

⚡ **Hook 10 — "Nobody called to say your rate dropped. The next brief just showed up lighter, like it was obvious."**
Most dangerous because it captures the specific, quiet cruelty of the experience — no confrontation, no negotiation, just a smaller number treated as self-evident. "Like it was obvious" is the most emotionally vicious phrase in the entire set because it implies the buyer didn't even think your old rate was worth discussing.

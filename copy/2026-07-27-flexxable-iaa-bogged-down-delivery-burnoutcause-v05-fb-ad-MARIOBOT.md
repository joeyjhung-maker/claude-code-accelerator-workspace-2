You know exactly what I mean.

You land a new client and for about 48 hours it feels great.

Then the messages start.

The "quick question" at 9pm that turns into a 45-minute thread, the screenshot of something that looks slightly off with zero context and a question mark, the voicemail you find Sunday morning from someone who sounds like they're about to cancel.

And it's every client doing this at once, overlapping, in a rotation that never lets up.

Your phone becomes this thing you low-key dread picking up.

The work itself isn't even the hard part, it's that every client you serve needs ONGOING stuff from you, fixes, updates, tweaks, explanations, builds that break, campaigns that need babysitting.

The more clients you sign, the heavier it gets.

Which is a pretty brutal trap when you think about it… because the whole point of signing more clients was supposed to be MORE freedom, not less.

So what if the delivery just… wasn't there?

Not "streamlined." Not "delegated to a VA." Actually gone.

That's what made me rethink the entire agency model.

Old leads they already paid for, prospects who enquired weeks or months ago and went cold.

You don't build anything for the client, you don't manage a campaign, and there's no website to tweak, no funnel to debug, no ad account to monitor.

You install it, point it at leads that are already sitting in their CRM doing nothing… and you collect 30-50% of the profit on every sale it generates.

There's nothing to maintain, which means there's nothing for a client to message you about at 10pm on a Tuesday.

No build means no bugs, and no ongoing service means no "can you just quickly..." requests piling up in your DMs every week.

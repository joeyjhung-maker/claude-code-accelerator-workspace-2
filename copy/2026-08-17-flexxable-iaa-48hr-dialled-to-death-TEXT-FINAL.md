## The argument

Reader's inner sentence:

> *"I've been pitching the part of the business they'll defend. The part they've already given up on is sitting there unclaimed."*

Sidekick Hijack, stacked with the zero-downside argument — see [[argument-bank]]. The client's own sales team is the wave; the ad farms what it discards rather than competing with it. *"You write them off anyway"* is the line that kills price before price exists.

## What the three hooks are testing

| Hook | Door | Opens on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Result | The retainer, plus AI doing the work |
| 2 | The client's behaviour | Dialling then binning — trash into a deal |
| 3 | The mistake | Trash-to-treasure against good-work-for-peanuts |

Hook 3 is the only one carrying an argument rather than a result, and its beat 2 sets up the body's opening move — Dan pitching a build and getting a NO.

## Known and accepted

Flagged during the build, decided by Joey, recorded so the account read has context rather than rediscovering them.

- **The mistake is never named.** Hook 3 promises "this ONE deal-killing mistake" and the body shows it without labelling it. Deliberate tease; the risk is the loop reads as unclosed for skim readers.
- **8 dials, not 16.** Source says 8 a day for two days. Ad runs 8 in 48 hours throughout, so it is internally consistent.
- **The ecom paragraph claims the front of the queue.** The brief bans speed-to-lead in this ad because the angle is the back of the queue. Kept anyway as a second proof point.
- **The close carries a three-item list** (find, close, fulfil).
- **No blue-ocean line.** Nothing in the ad says nobody else is running this position.

If this one underperforms, those five are the first things to test against — not new angles.

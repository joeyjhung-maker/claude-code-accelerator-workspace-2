## Why it won
Joey: *"Really liking how these Bill emails are coming out… very nice tone for partner emails as they don't have Dan's full lingo for his own list."*

The Partner Voice is a **different instrument** from Dan's own-list voice. Dan = British-inflected, jargon-dense, swaggering (right for people who know him). Partner Voice = a **trusted friend making an introduction to strangers** — understated, jargon-free, endorsement-led. Mixing them up is the #1 way JV emails flop. ## The editing instincts that made it sing (from Joey's surgical edits to the Dan-origin email)
These are the moves Joey made to my draft. Apply them proactively going forward:

1. **Open with reader-relevance, not the story cold.** Joey added a first line that calls out WHO it's for ("If you don't consider yourself an AI expert… Dan's story might resonate") before starting the story. Give the reader a reason to care in the first line; don't open on the subject cold.
2. **Stack a SECOND layer of tension before resolving.** My draft fixed the problem too fast (grind → per-result offer → done). Joey inserted a second problem AFTER the first fix — "he still had to run the ads… one bad day wiping out a month's profit… the money was good but the STRESS was still there." THEN AI resolves it. Don't resolve the arc too early; stack the remaining problem so the payoff lands harder.
3. **Dial numbers DOWN for believability.** Joey changed "more than TEN retainer clients" → "3 retainer clients." A smaller, credible multiple beats an impressive-but-suspicious one.
4. **Show the realization as a lived thought, not a stated fact.** My declarative "AI could do the delivery work" → Joey's "What if he combined his OFFER making skills with AI doing all the delivery." Put us inside the moment of insight (mind-movie), don't just report it.
5. **Make the model a simple "all he does is X, then Y" formula.** Joey reshaped the offer logic into "All he does is make a great offer no one says no to… then have AI do the delivery." Feels doable.
6. **Inject ONE concrete proof number at the peak of the model explanation** — even in a proof-light email — as a parenthetical punch: "(He recently ran a campaign that did $1.03M and it paid out $222,600!)".
7. **Add a "by the end you'll know exactly how to X" takeaway promise before the CTA.** Joey added "By the end of the training, you'll know exactly how to land more deals without 'selling' and finally get paid what you're worth." (Webinar-reg best practice — cash the value into a concrete walk-away.)
8. **Relocate a point to its strongest position and cut the duplicate.** Joey moved the "you don't need to be a tech genius" idea to the OPENING line (strong position) and CUT my P.S. that repeated it. Don't make the same point at both ends.
9. **Cut mechanism-adjacent/redundant clauses to keep the emotional focus.** Joey cut "the client carried almost zero risk" and kept "Dan could finally scale without burning out" — focus on the human payoff, not the mechanical aside.
10. **Register + warmth:** "live class" / "training" over "webinar" (less salesy); warm human sign-off ("Hope to see you there!") over a clipped "- Bill".

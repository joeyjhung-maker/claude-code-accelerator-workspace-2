# The Lock

The destination for a brief. A brief is "locked" when every dimension RHYMES
— mechanism, image, star-ingredient, spokesperson, binary, proof, pain/solution
— and they all converge on the product as the inevitable answer.

When it's locked, the product stops being a product and becomes the obvious
conclusion of a worldview. The reader doesn't feel sold to. They feel shown how
the world works, and the product is just what you'd obviously use once you see it.

Source: the "MOST IMPORTANT" section of the 37 Rapid-Fire A-lister tips.
Related: [[The Script]] [[Memory Loop]] — lives at the /brief stage.

**This is the dream state, not a bar.** Full lock is what we AIM for — the
north star for a brief. But lots of good ads never fully lock, and we do NOT
discount an ad, mechanism, or seed for failing to reach it. Use the test to see
how close you are and to point at what to tighten, then ship anyway. Aim here;
don't gate on it.

## The one test that matters
Say the whole thing in ONE breath — product, mechanism, proof, enemy, solution
— in 3–4 sentences someone at a dinner party would find compelling.

- If every clause implies the next and they nod before you finish → locked.
- If you keep needing "and also…" or "but the other thing is…" → a dimension
  isn't rhyming. Find where the connection breaks.
- If a listener could predict the ending from sentence one → locked. That's not
  boring, that's inevitability. Each "of course" is a dimension clicking in.

The olive oil, in one breath: "The only olive oil grown on land untouched by a
single pesticide in 35 years — a former UN no-man's-land in Cyprus where the
trees fight so hard to survive they produce the highest polyphenols ever
measured. Most premium olive oil is dead. This is the world's only living oil."
No cold-pressed. No extra-virgin. All the gray claims became supporting detail.

## What "not locked" feels like (the diagnostics)
- **You keep adding "and also."** Locked copy says "which means" / "because" /
  "which is why." Every element FOLLOWS the last, never gets ADDED to it.
- **Mechanism and star-ingredient feel like separate decisions.** In a lock the
  ingredient IS the mechanism — the bee propolis penetrates, the volcanic rock
  purifies, the untouched land makes the trees fight. If the star ingredient
  doesn't EMBODY the mechanism, it'll always feel forced.
- **The spokesperson could be swapped out.** In a lock they're irreplaceable —
  their specific background is what makes the discovery possible. (Ties to the
  brief's Spokesperson field + [[dan-truth-constraint]].)
- **You're defending the mechanism, not building the world.** "I know this
  sounds crazy, but… studies show…" = the worldview isn't self-supporting. In a
  lock the proof is another chapter of the same story, not a defence bolted on.
- **Pain and solution don't share a root metaphor.** Sealed cells → penetrated
  cells. Dirty brain → clean brain. Dead oil → living oil. Invert the pain
  metaphor and it SHOULD produce the solution metaphor. If it doesn't, the root
  isn't aligned. (The copper-pillowcase miss: pain was "pressure on your face,"
  mechanism was "copper ions" — two stories, no rhyme.)

## Watch the direction the rhyme points
A rhyme can be perfect and still point the wrong way. "Cannibal brain" rhymed
beautifully — toward hopelessness, and hopelessness has no product at the end.
"Dirty brain" rhymes toward SOLVABLE (dirty → cleanable, gunk → flushable). The
pain rhyme and the solution rhyme must CONVERGE on the product.

## How to get there (the loading, then the rotation)
1. **Load everything before committing to anything.** Every ingredient origin,
   the spokesperson's full backstory, the market's live conversations, the
   historical suppressions, the competitors' exact claims. One interesting find
   = one dial position; you need five or six dials before a lock can appear.
2. **Find the shade of color** — the one irreducible fact that tightens your gut.
   The thing NO competitor can say. If it could sit in a rival's ad and still
   work, it's gray. Turn the saturation on the color all the way up.
3. **Name the detail's natural metaphor — does it rhyme with the mechanism?**
   Bee stings → penetrates the cell. Volcanic rock → purifies the water. If the
   natural metaphor maps to the mechanism of action, you're locking. Don't force
   it — try three or four pairings; the click is the rhyme.
4. **Check the spokesperson** — does their background naturally PRODUCE the
   insight or EMBODY the mechanism?
5. **Generate the binary** — dead vs alive, horse pills vs micro-strip. The bad
   side = life without the mechanism; the good side = life with it.
6. **Run the one-breath test** (above).
7. **Check the pain and benefit cascades stay in the root metaphor** — no new
   concept smuggled in on either side.

## The feeling
A slope, not a staircase — each line tilts you into the next. One idea expressed
seven ways, not seven claims. Inevitable, not clever: discovery, not persuasion.

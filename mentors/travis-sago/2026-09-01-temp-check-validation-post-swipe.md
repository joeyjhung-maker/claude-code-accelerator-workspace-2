## What the post is for

A validation post, not a sales post. Its entire job is testing real appetite for something BEFORE
committing time to build it — Travis explicitly states he'll cancel the idea if interest isn't
there. This is "map vs terrain" (test the assumed audience appetite live) and "qualify commitment
before cash" (Make 'Em Beg, Ch.3) applied to a whole audience at once instead of a 1:1 conversation.

## The skeleton

1. Named mechanism, versioned like software ("X: Y (Version 1.0)") — implies real, tested,
   iterating IP, not a random idea.
2. Opens on an almost-unbelievable RESULT claim, pre-framing the reader's skepticism before
   stating it ("I know this'll sound unbelievable, but—"). Curiosity/novelty opener, not
   pain-first — see the analysis below for why this works.
3. A philosophy beat before the offer — his stated worldview on how business should work (do the
   work once, get ongoing benefit forever), not a product pitch.
4. The mechanism itself stays completely withheld — not even loosely explained. "Useful but
   incomplete," pushed further than usual: even the plan isn't shown, only its result.
5. Two-tier access structure (a perk for existing payers, a challenge/invite path for everyone
   else) — rewards loyalty AND creates an aspirational path for others.
6. **The core mechanic: a 1–10 "temperature check" with MIRRORED ANCHORS** — the exact same vivid,
   funny phrase at both ends of the scale, meaning flipped:
   > 🤮 1 = "I'd rather spend a week at my mother-in-law's than participate"
   > ❤️ 10 = "I'd spend a week at my mother-in-law's just so I could participate"
   This is the new, specific technique worth reusing — it makes an abstract gut-check scale funny
   and concrete without needing to define what a "10" objectively means, and the shared reference
   point makes the whole range legible in one read.
7. Real stated stakes, not fake urgency: "if there's not enough serious interest, I'll can the
   idea" — a genuine consequence tied to the answer, which raises response rate and honesty.
8. Minimizes the cost of responding ("take 2 secs") — a small compliance action, not a big ask.

## Why it works (four jobs from one poll)

- Tests real appetite before building anything (map vs terrain, live).
- Qualifies commitment before presenting the actual offer (Santa/Rudolph principle, scaled).
- Segments the whole audience for free — 8s/9s/10s become a warm outreach list, 1s/2s tell him who
  not to bother pitching.
- Visible high answers in the comments pre-sell latecomers scrolling past — the audience does the
  convincing (same engine as the binary consensus-poll example in
  [[travis-call-engagement-principles]], just on a gradient instead of two options).

## Why the opener isn't pain-first (worth citing as a live example of an existing rule)

He leads with an audacious, almost-unbelievable RESULT about himself, not a pain call-out. He
pre-frames the reader's skepticism ("I know this'll sound unbelievable... but—") before the claim
lands, then stacks specific proof (no ads, no content treadmill, a decade-plus) to back a claim
that started sounding impossible. This is the "general openness/pure intrigue" door from the
vicious-hook principles in `brand/hooks.md`, applied to a post opener rather than an ad hook — and
it's a clean, real-world confirmation of the pain-is-amplifier-not-opener rule already standing in
this workspace, worth citing as a concrete example next time that rule needs one.

## The one-line version
Ask your group for help designing the paid tier. Then DM everyone who answers, within minutes, two sentences and one question: "Right now it's $49, would you like to be the 3rd VIP?"
About 50% say yes.

## The setup he did it in
- Leads On Demand (Travis + Tan). Freemium, three tiers: $0 Standard, $9 Premium, $49 VIP.
- About 600 members. Close to 100 on the $9 tier, which was already converting well.
- Goal: 100 on the $49 VIP. Rolled out at $49 and wanted to validate the offer first.
- The VIP page is just the Skool plans page. No sales page.

> PS Help us make an AWESOME VIP Level Please! Comment below 👇

Why each part is there:
- **"$100 to $300 a month" is a deliberate price anchor.** Not "I'm thinking $49." He builds the ballpark first, so the real price lands as a bargain. He quizzes the call on why he used that range. The answer from the call: anchor.
- **"I'll give our first helpful VIP LEADers a WAAAAY better deal though"** stops them answering with "here's what I'd pay." They answer with what they'd want.
- **A to H menu.** Multiple choice is easy to comment on. "Mix and match" invites pile-ons.
- **A single pick beats "all of the above" (F).** He got a few answers naming one item alone (E, Cold Outreach Mojo). "This one thing is worth $100–300 a month to me" is a stronger buying clue to him than a blanket F.
- **No G's.** Nobody picked "none of the above."
- Also written like a human asking a favour. He says AI-written help-me posts won't read this way.

Result: 32 comments, about half Travis's own replies saying thank you. About 16 people voted.

## Step 2 — the Tap (DM, ASAP after they comment)
Reward the behaviour with attention. Reply to the comment with a thanks. Then DM within minutes. "Not two days from now, not five weeks from now."

> Thank you for the help on the VIP advice!!! We think the same. Right now I've got it priced at $49. Would you like to be the 3rd VIP???

Two sentences, one question. Real thread shown on screen:
- 2:07pm Travis sends the tap. 2:11pm: "Send me the link homie."
- 2:22pm "thank you #3!" + the Skool plans link. 2:23pm: "Cool! I'm in."
- Next tap (2:26pm): same text, "...Would you like to be the 4th VIP?" Reply: "Yes. Count me in."

Details:
- "We think the same" = their own vote is mirrored back to them. They already said it's worth $100–300.
- Numbered scarcity ("3rd VIP", "4th VIP"). First one jumped in before Travis had even sent the link.
- Sometimes he pauses after the thank-you and lets them reply before he asks. This time he didn't.
- He didn't send the link in the tap. He sent it after the yes.
- About 50% conversion, said plainly ("full frontal transparency"). Everyone who said no was nice about it: busy, mid-auction. He doesn't argue. A no is a prompt for another post showing how it fits.
- Variation: "We'd like to be in the first 10 VIPs" works less well than the specific number. Could run up to 100.

## Where the A–H menu came from
"Very scientific": he posts a lot and watches what sticks.
- Cold outreach posts got big engagement. He knows who that is: people with no list or audience, who rely on cold outreach.
- Tactical front door ("traffic on demand", "cold outreach") into the strategic living room.
- Second pain after they get traffic: their stuff doesn't sell. So "make your offer sell."
- "I'm not thinking of my product. I'm thinking about their journey."
- Possible spin-off: a separate Cold Outreach Mojo tier for beginners with no audience.

## The doctrine under it
- **Not knowing the answer is a gift.** Don't guess. Go to the market and ask. "The most expensive thing I can do is say I know this will work." Asking also bonds the group.
- **Consistency over big swings.** One $49 sale a day = ~$1,500 in month one. Six months of one a day = ~$9K/month. A year = ~$18K/month, minus cancels, plus refills. That's over $100K/year. Compare: it'd take over $1M at 10% to throw off $9K a month. ("Parabolic" later.)
- **Feed the babies.** Every property needs a daily feed. Sprints are fine (about 4 a day for 7 days gets you the month).
- **Anything new = a reason to talk.** New bonus, a call coming up Tuesday, an auction that just ran. Each one is a conversation opening. For a new bonus: ask the group what it'd sell for on its own, then tap "it's now in VIP, only $49, want to be the first eyeballs?"
- **Hire a $20–25/hour person to do the tapping.** You (or they) dream up the posts. No sales samurai needed. "Leverage for non-geniuses like me."
- **Human beats AI here.** "When everybody's zigging, how can I zag?" Vulnerability ("can you help me?") is hard for AI to fake.
- **Many more low-ticket-recurring owners than big-ticket.** About 10x, especially in 2026 (from the post).

## Why a community beats an email-list continuity
(Travis plus another call member, last few minutes. Captions don't name the second speaker.)
- In a community, members talk to each other. Nobody on a plain email list does.
- Churn is partly refilled by the group itself: it sells for you.
- Trick: tell VIPs to put "VIP:" at the front of their post titles. Non-VIPs see it, see the upgrade button, and upgrade.
- The other speaker: "You can never pre-sell enough." The community is pre-selling for you.

## Hopper Millions Cliff Notes (overview module)

**Core teaching**
A "hopper" is a Facebook group (or any warm community) used to pre-sell an offer over time instead of pitching cold. Instead of telling people what to believe, you build a multi-week experience where they prove the concept to themselves through small assigned actions, then convert on the back of that self-proof. The whole system is really just "preselling the five piece sandwich" — problem, personality/philosophy, plan, price, product.

**Named framework/terms**
- **Crock Pot Hopper** — a long-running, low-heat group (main community, e.g. an existing FB group) you can scoop sales out of anytime by dropping a T1 invite post. Good default for testing a new/newish offer.
- **Microwave Hopper** — a shorter, hotter, segmented spin-off group built around one specific offer/topic, used once a crock pot offer is proven (e.g., made 5 figures) and you want to scale it into a dedicated push. Also used when the crock pot is too busy (posts get buried fast) or the topic is off-brand for the main group.
- **Five Piece Sale Sandwich** — the two "bread" pieces are present pain (top) and price (bottom); the "meat" is the plan (what's new/different); the "condiments" are personality and philosophy.
- **CDDC (Clearly Different and Desirable Concept)** — the plan/meat must be immediately, visibly different from what the prospect is already doing — different enough to draw as a before/after a "fifth grader" could understand in seconds, not something that needs 5 minutes of explaining.
- **T1 / T2 / T3** — tiered invite/offer posts (T1 = initial invite/insight post; T3 = the pitch/price reveal), used to segment interest and cherry-pick before a full rollout.
- **The Domino** — the one specific action/belief you need people to prove to themselves before they'll buy (e.g., "send this template to 5 people and see what happens").

**Concrete actionable rules**
- If testing a new or newish offer, run it in your existing crock pot first — don't build a new group until it's proven.
- Approve new group members in a batch, not one by one, so everyone starts warm together.
- Put your welcome/orientation video in the pin post; lay out the whole 3-part plan map without pitching the product yet.
- Ask for a small compliance action early (a comment, a "yes" reply) to build momentum and boost the algorithm's reach.
- Give people a task that lets them *prove the breakthrough to themselves* rather than just telling them it works.
- Publicly celebrate anyone who completes the task — social proof drives the next round of compliance.
- Be willing to remove/threaten to remove inactive members from a free group; it signals standards and warms up laggards.
- Read the comments, not just the posts — that's where you learn who's serious, what objections exist, and what to write about next.
- Before rolling out an offer to everyone, soft-test with a "would you be interested — reply yes" post, then privately reach out to the first few "yes" repliers to get a small T3 batch working before full rollout.
- Multiply results: whatever a campaign made once, you can typically run it ~4x a year with a fresh (or partly returning) audience.
- A microwave group doesn't have to be paid or converted into a crock pot — you can abandon it after extracting the sale wave, or duplicate the format for the next run.

**Notable examples**
- The "30 Year Wealth Shortcut" microwave hopper: one single test post in the crock pot put 5 people into a $22,500 offer (~$112K); the full microwave rollout brought in ~30 people at $30K each (~$900K).
- Hopper members compared to a "cold potato thrown into a crock pot" — Facebook notification behavior (likes, comments) treated as a literal warming-up signal before someone reaches out to buy.

## The 5 Ps in Pre-Selling

**Core teaching**
Most sellers try to jump straight from stating the *problem* to pitching the *product*, and that jump fails almost every time. Between problem and product there are three more "stepping stones" to pre-sell — personality, philosophy, and especially the plan and the price — each pre-sold *before* the pitch dramatically raises conversion, ideally to the point where the actual product barely needs discussing on the call.

**Named framework/terms**
- **The 5 Ps**: Problem (the floater — motivates them to show up, doesn't have to be first), Personality (~10% of conversion weight — shown by helping with a small challenge, not "look how cool I am" posts), Philosophy (your stance on life/business — repels the wrong people, attracts the right ones), Plan (the biggest lever — a concrete, contrasted, different way forward), Price (rarely pre-sold, but doing so can push conversion to 90–100%).
- **Jumping the creek** — the core metaphor: problem and product are opposite banks; each P is a stepping stone/boulder. Skipping straight from problem to product = "splash" (objections, stalling, "let me think about it").
- **The Band Moment / conversion point** — not the moment of excitement over a small win, but the moment they're run up against the *next* challenge they can't clear alone — that's where real conversion happens.
- **Dramatic Difference** — you can't call something "different" in isolation; it only registers against a stated contrast to what they've already tried (their past failed attempts).

**Concrete actionable rules**
- Always find out what the prospect has already tried before presenting your plan.
- Pre-sell the plan as early and as far "before they get in the boat" as possible.
- Lay out your plan in 3 steps; ask directly "what do you like best about that plan?" — if they say yes to the plan, you're 50–60%+ likely to close.
- If someone says no to the plan itself, don't push — you're probably not the right fit for them.
- Use small, concrete symptom-language tests ("would you like to see less of X / more of Y?") to find which framing gets the most hand-raisers.
- Never rely on abstract authority words — "proven process," "proprietary system," "differentiator formula." Replace with plain language describing the literal mechanism.
- To help someone find their own dramatic difference: ask what specifically they changed that got results, rather than vague claims.
- Testimonials often unsell rather than sell — unless the testimonial explains *why* the breakthrough happened.
- Not everyone should convert — if someone needs the plan broken into every micro-step, they may be a harder-fit client; acceptable filter, not a funnel flaw.

**Notable examples**
- The "helicopter" story: a prospect who'd tried leg exercises, karate-kicking, and digging under a wall finally says yes to "flying over it" (the plan) — people need to exhaust their old plan's failures before a new plan feels believable.
- A partner's video pre-sold the plan across a couple of posts before the T3 pitch existed; 50 comments came in, only 8 T3s were sent, 5 people closed (~60% close rate) — but price was never pre-sold, so results could have been higher still.

## Module 1: Clearly Different Desirable Concept (CDDC) & T1 Idea Stimulator Worksheet

**Core teaching**
Before writing any copy, map out what's actually stopping your prospect (their tangible, felt constraint), the false assumptions holding that constraint in place, your breakthrough (a reversal or challenge of that assumption), and the resulting implications — external and internal — of removing the constraint. The implications are where your CDDC lives, and where your T1 ideas and hopper names are born. Specificity ("skin on the bones") is what actually converts.

**Named framework/terms**
- **CDDC (Clearly Different Desirable Concept)** — replacement for "the big idea." Doesn't need to be huge or novel, just visibly different and desirable versus what the prospect is doing now. If it takes 5 minutes to explain, it isn't one.
- **The Constraint** — the tangible thing that's actually stopping the prospect (tactile/specific, not abstract).
- **Assumptions** — beliefs the prospect holds about the constraint and about how to remove it; these are the actual source of the "problem."
- **The Breakthrough (leverage)** — almost always a direct challenge or reversal of the prospect's core assumption.
- **Implications** — the downstream external and internal consequences of the breakthrough once achieved; the well T1 ideas and hopper names are drawn from.
- **"Six Friends" of specificity** — What, Who, Where, When, Why, How — six angles to make an offer "finger-pointy" instead of abstract. Combining 2–3 multiplies power.
- **"Put skin on the bones"** — don't sell the mechanism, sell the specific, tactile outcome.
- **Bullseye vs. Fringe targeting** — start messaging at your ideal, narrow "bullseye" client; fringe/general messaging pulls in fringe people but the center of the bullseye never comes out for broad messaging.

**Concrete actionable rules**
- Work the worksheet in order: what stops them → their assumptions about the constraint → their assumptions about removing it → your breakthrough → the implications (external + internal) → dramatize implications tactilely → mine implications for T1s and hopper names.
- Never sell the mechanism — always point to a specific, visualizable outcome or number.
- Pick ONE (or combine 2) of the six friends to sharpen any offer statement; "How" is used most often but is actually the weakest of the six.
- Target your true ideal client (bullseye) even if the addressable pool looks smaller.
- To find who your audience actually wants to sell to, ask them directly.
- Sending 10 specific, narrow messages over time will out-convert one broad, general message.
- Get very specific on licensing/partnership terms if IP-licensing is part of the model — vague deals create risk.
- Prefer favoring your partner in ongoing revenue-share (e.g., 30% forever) over squeezing a bigger one-time cut (70% for one year).

**Notable examples**
- The Rudolph breakdown: Rudolph's shiny nose (the constraint) is assumed shameful; Santa's breakthrough reframes it as the very thing that guides the sleigh (CDDC); implications are external (all reindeer love him) and internal (self-respect, history).
- A general "relationship handbook" that wouldn't sell became a focused product once targeted at "women looking for commitment" with the insight "respect and love get entangled in a man's mind" — $1M in sales the first year.

## Module 2: The 3 Step UN-Plan

**Core teaching**
Define the offer strictly from the customer's point of view, not your own product's feature set — nobody actually wants "more leads" or "more sales," they want the underlying transformation. Reduce that transformation into a memorable 3-step plan (a "hammer" reframed as an exciting journey), and design a welcome video that hangs that plan on a sticky story rather than logic.

**Named framework/terms**
- **3 Step UN-Plan** — Step 1 must be counterintuitive/unusual/pattern-breaking (breaks a false belief); Step 2 is a positive step toward the vision; Step 3 is the celebratory transformation if they follow through.
- **The Hammer** — your core mechanism/method. Boring on its own; never lead with it — lead with its implications.
- **Breakthrough Exercise** — "If you had 2 minutes to give your secret, which almost always works, what would it be? Fuck all the ___, just ___."
- **Stick-to-your-brain story/framework** — the welcome video needs an emotional story or metaphor to hang the 3-step plan on.
- **Fans and Fortunes Magic** — the proof mechanism: identify the small, almost-magical thing you already do that works, which becomes the "domino" the customer needs to prove to themselves.
- **Problem, Promise, Proof** — the weakest link in most sales arguments is proof; testimonials alone are weak. The strongest proof is a demonstration the prospect performs on themselves.

**Concrete actionable rules**
- Work backwards from the real desired impact/identity shift, not "more sales/leads."
- Draft the 3-step plan before building any content: Step 1 must contradict conventional wisdom in the niche.
- Cap the plan at 3 steps; 4 is tolerable, avoid 6–10 step plans.
- Identify the "hammer" explicitly, but never open marketing with it — brainstorm its implications instead.
- Test implications directly with your list/group as simple yes/no interest questions before building anything.
- Look for hidden "magic" you already do that you've stopped noticing — these under-sold mechanical details often make the best hooks.
- When following up with past "no"s, ask about the problem they still have, not "did you decide to buy."
- Avoid painting the prospect's shame/internal failure too directly — reframing onto a less shame-laden external cause converts better.
- Don't overload a niche/offer with every possible service type — narrow the delivery model.

**Notable examples**
- The 30 Year Wealth Shortcut hopper used "Get control of assets → Cash flow → Live free" as its 3-step plan, hung on a welcome-video story about a man who started a sailing school with no money by borrowing someone else's boats.
- On proof: echoed via the Star Wars analogy — Luke doesn't believe in the Force just because Obi-Wan says so; he builds belief incrementally through small proofs before facing Vader.

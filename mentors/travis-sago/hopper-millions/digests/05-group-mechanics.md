## Module 5: Catch Up Call and 3 Main Principles of the Hopper

**Core teaching**
A hopper (or "crock pot") group is a warm, curated group used to funnel a small, qualified slice of a larger audience into an offer. Once a main community gets big enough that only the same handful of people ever engage, the fix isn't more content — it's carving out a smaller, self-selected group defined by a clear qualifier, which restores real engagement and gives clean, fresh terrain to test offers. Three underlying principles determine whether a hopper converts: sustained attention on one topic, layered proof, and leaving the audience wanting more.

**Named framework/terms**
- **Hopper / Crock pot group** — the smaller, purpose-built group people are funneled into to be sold to over time; "crock pot" = slow-simmer, always-warm, semi-permanent.
- **Sales hopper** — a more structured, time-boxed version built around a specific launch (e.g., a 30-day arc), versus the crock pot's ongoing, evergreen nature.
- **CAP (Child, Adult, Parent)** — Travis's buying-psychology model, from transactional analysis. A buyer runs three "tapes" at once: Child (emotional, reacts to an overt benefit), Adult (logical, wants proof/kitchen-table-logic), Parent (judges good/bad, wants a clear differentiator). See `../../psm-btem/synthesis.md` for the full breakdown (Call 5, "CAP Sales Mojo").
- **Hell island / Heaven island** — shorthand for the negative consequence state vs. the desired outcome state.
- **Fans and fortunes** — a demonstration/proof mechanism where the audience *sees* something work in real time, producing an implicit realization rather than being told.
- **Implicit proof / kitchen table logic** — implicit proof is proof the prospect concludes for themselves by watching something happen; kitchen table logic is proof that "just makes sense" without needing hard data.
- **AUM (Assets Under Management) analogy** — frames the hopper strategy on the hedge-fund manager model: get control of *other people's* existing assets (email lists, FB groups, audiences) rather than spending years building your own, then take a performance-based cut.
- **Bass-Ackwards method** — start from the desired end result and work backward, rather than building forward from where someone currently is.
- **Theory of constraints (Eli Goldratt)** — local optimization is meaningless unless it improves the whole system's throughput — caution against vanity-metric thinking disconnected from actual sales.

**Concrete actionable rules**
- When a large group goes stale, split off a smaller "crock pot" hopper defined by a binary qualifier the audience self-identifies with.
- Pick the qualifier from a problem your *existing, longer-tenured* people have that brand-new people don't — not a generic "advanced" label.
- Position the new group bluntly: state clearly who it's for and who it's not for.
- Use simple application questions on group entry as a signal — approve people even if they skip the questions.
- You don't need volume — six figures from a 300-person group is achievable.
- To position a new group internally, use pacing metaphors (dating, gardening, cooking, sports) that are genuinely native to your own life.
- Three unkillable principles, in order: (1) extend attention span on one topic; (2) prove the point, ideally with implicit/demonstrated proof; (3) always leave them thirstier than when they arrived.
- On "I'm already in another program" objections: ask whether it's time/energy/money, get them to articulate whether the other thing worked, let them reach their own conclusion.
- Watch price resistance empirically — if raising price measurably hurts conversion, it may not be worth pushing.

## Module 6: Invite Posts to Your Hopper Group

**Core teaching**
The invite/hand-raiser post is the single artifact that gets the right people to opt into your hopper group. Write it (or its close cousin, the T3) *before* the T1 outreach posts — the act of writing the invite forces clarity on the offer and exposes missing pieces (proof, benefit) before anything gets published. A strong invite post: attention-grabbing identity-based lead → objection pre-handling → a "brain-gasm" insight backed by credible third-party proof → intensification/benefit stacking → objection-busting close with a low-friction CTA.

**Named framework/terms**
- **T1 / T2 / T3** — T1 = initial outreach/awareness post, T2 = a deeper follow-up, T3 = the "offer"/invite-style post that gets someone to take an action.
- **The lead** — the opening of the post; sole job is to get attention, not sell yet.
- **Brain-gasm / the insight** — the single "aha" moment the whole post delivers, a reframe that reorganizes how the reader sees their situation.
- **Intensification** — stacking additional proof/benefit points after the core insight to deepen impact before the ask.
- **Useful but incomplete** — giving genuinely valuable, functional help that gets a small result on its own, but stops short of solving the whole problem — creates internal proof and a natural reason to want the next step.
- **The lion-with-a-thorn analogy** — correct sales sequencing: don't walk up to someone in pain and start talking about your product; acknowledge and diagnose their pain, ask what they've tried, then offer help.
- **Binary vs. psychographic targeting** — target by provable facts/assets rather than vague self-identity language.
- **"You already have this" (asset-recognition close)** — showing someone that most/all of the pieces they need already exist in their life/business; they're missing only one small piece.
- **Whale vs. minnow ("chum the water for the whales")** — speak to your most advanced/valuable prospects even if it repels lower-level people.
- **Licensing for leads** — attach your product as a bonus into someone else's existing funnel/list in exchange for a royalty or lead share.

**Concrete actionable rules**
- Write the T3 before the T1s — clarifies the offer and reveals what's missing before anything goes live.
- Don't make invite posts short by default — under-explaining is a bigger risk than over-explaining when many groups compete for attention.
- If a post/insight worked once, revisit and re-run variations of the same core insight.
- Build insights from a "stop doing X, start doing Y" contrast template when the shift is surprising; back it with credible, already-trusted reference points.
- Target higher-ticket/advanced offers by provable assets/behavior, not vague psychographic labels.
- To sell a "who" upgrade, talk explicitly about the bigger opportunity even if it repels lower-tier people.
- For licensing/bonus plays: find an existing product/asset and offer it as a bonus into someone else's funnel for a revenue share.
- When teasing content release, use time pressure tied to real momentum, not an arbitrary deadline.

## Module 7: Detachment and Having FUN with your Hopper

**Core teaching**
Grip too tightly on outcomes and you (and your group) stop enjoying the process, which hurts both retention and conversion. Leaders who stay light and humorous when things go sideways demonstrate the certainty and leadership people want to buy into — members often remember how they *felt* in your group more than whether they got the exact promised result.

**Named framework/terms**
- **"Twenty years from now" reframe** — most day-to-day setbacks won't matter in 20 years (or even 20 days); don't over-invest emotional energy in them.
- **Turning disadvantage into advantage** — when something looks like a problem, actively reframe it as a feature instead of treating it as damage control.
- **CAP is everywhere** — the Child/Adult/Parent lens applies not just to big posts but to small daily interactions: comment replies, video intros, personal conversations.
- **Tapper / tapper link** — a Messenger-based CRM mechanism: a link opens Messenger to a managed inbox, letting a team member or bot handle and track hand-raiser responses rather than personal DMs.
- **Business page vs. personal profile posting** — set up a Facebook Business Page to avoid Facebook flagging multiple people/IPs logging into one personal account.

**Concrete actionable rules**
- When something goes wrong publicly, name it openly and reframe it as a benefit rather than hiding or over-apologizing.
- Don't over-schedule structured groups to please people who want a rigid agenda — ask what they specifically need.
- Use interim/mini posts between main content drops to give quick value and gauge what resonates.
- Handle blowback or friction in the comments personally and visibly — demonstrates leadership.
- Videos/content can run long as long as CAP keeps getting reintroduced to sustain attention.
- Never call a hopper's timed offer a "challenge" if failure would look like public failure — frame as "prove it to yourself" so partial results still count as a win.
- Avoid mass-pasting identical links/comments rapidly (Facebook restriction risk) — vary delivery.
- Move team members who help manage posting onto a linked business page, not personal profiles.
- Avoid group/offer names that risk tripping platform income-claim policies.

## Module 8: Figuring out your Hopper's Timeline

**Core teaching**
There's no fixed formula for how long to run a hopper before making the offer — the right pace is set by your own operational capacity and the audience's visible engagement level, not an arbitrary calendar date. The clearest statement of "who before what": the highest-leverage change available to almost any business is changing *who* you sell to, not changing what you sell.

**Named framework/terms**
- **Noah's Ark opener** — a lead style that starts by naming a real, current threat/change happening in the market's environment before pivoting to your solution — "warning them of the flood," not manufacturing fear.
- **"Change the who, not the what"** — the most expensive, hardest lever to pull is changing *what* you offer; the cheapest, highest-leverage lever is changing *who* you offer it to, followed by *when* and *where* you deliver it.
- **Binary vs. psychographic asset targeting** — define your ideal prospect by concrete, provable assets/history rather than vague identity language.
- **Peanut butter and jelly analogy** — the prospect already has the bread and jelly (leadership, audience, offer, results); you're just supplying the missing peanut butter.
- **Bull's-eye focus** — speak precisely to your most ideal, narrow target rather than broadening the message.
- **Excitement ≠ conversion** — a raised hand is real signal but not proof of purchase intent.

**Concrete actionable rules**
- Don't gate the group's launch to a fixed calendar date — gate it to operational bandwidth and audience engagement thresholds.
- Ask the group directly how many have completed the current step, and let their answer determine when the next piece releases.
- Once marketing assets are validated in one hopper run, reuse them: open a fresh, separate group for the next cohort.
- Consider running two hopper types in parallel: an evergreen crock pot plus a time-boxed sales hopper for a specific launch.
- Publicly celebrate *early* micro-actions more than final results — the first domino matters more for momentum than the eventual sale.
- Never respond to non-participation with public shaming — celebrate the people who did act.
- When critiquing publicly, use self-deprecating or third-party framing rather than direct criticism.
- To reposition an existing successful offer, first ask "can I just change the who?" before considering a new product, channel, or timing.

**Notable example**
A business turning point: stopping the chase of a 60,000-person cold prospect list and refocusing on the existing customer list instead — "changing the who" — described as one of the biggest breakthroughs.

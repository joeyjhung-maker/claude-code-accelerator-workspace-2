# Partner Voice — the JV email template

*A reusable voice for writing emails FROM a JV partner, TO that partner's own list, promoting OUR client's (e.g. Dan/Flexxable's) offer or webinar. Born from the Bill McIntosh × Buildy webinar sequence (Jul 2026), which Joey flagged as the right tone for partner sends. Use this for every future JV partner, swapping the partner, the expert, and the audience bridge.*

Related: [[JV-map]] [[flexxable-voice-and-emphasis]] [[copy-rubric]] [[always-use-mariobot-to-write]]

## The one big idea
You are NOT writing in the client/expert's voice. You're writing in the **partner's** voice — a trusted host introducing and vouching for a friend (the expert) to people who **have never heard of the expert or any of their lingo.** Most JV emails fail because they import the expert's insider jargon and swagger onto an audience that doesn't have the decoder ring. Partner Voice strips all of that.

## The 10 rules of Partner Voice

1. **Whose voice: the PARTNER's, not the expert's.** The partner (host) is the trusted narrator. The expert is talked ABOUT, in third person — "my friend Dan," "Dan's story," "I've known Dan for years."

2. **Strip ALL of the expert's insider jargon.** The partner's list doesn't know the coined terms. For Flexxable/Dan, that means NONE of: Sleeping Beauty Android, Prince Charming SMS, Offer of the Century, Coffee Dates, Profit Partner (model), Androids, Hulk, Tech Terminators, DBR. Translate every one to plain language: *sales meetings, deals, the system, partnerships, take a cut, get paid on results.* Rule of thumb: if the partner's audience wouldn't already use the word, it doesn't go in the email.

3. **Match the PARTNER's register — don't import the expert's tics.** Dan writes British/Aussie-inflected: "peeps," "mate," "bloke," "gonna." Bill does NOT. Bill is warm, understated, neutral-conversational. When onboarding a new partner, capture THEIR actual voice from their own content and write to that — never default to the client's.

4. **Understated, never hypey.** Curiosity and belief come from **specificity + paradox + honesty**, not volume. BANNED: "secret," "SECRET," "mind-blowing," "game-changer," "you won't believe," "this changes everything," exclamation-spam. The quieter and more genuinely-curious the partner sounds, the more the reader leans in.

5. **The endorsement is the unique asset.** The partner's personal vouch is the ONE thing no other sender can replicate — use it, genuinely and sparingly: "it's rare I put my name on something," "I've known him for years," "he's the real deal, not another guru selling theory."

6. **Bridge to the partner's SPECIFIC audience.** Speak to what THAT list already believes and struggles with. (Buildy: they can already build with AI but can't get paid → "building was never what got Dan paid; the money's in the offer.") The bridge is different for every partner — write it to their people's known pain/identity.

7. **Never dunk on the partner's existing model or audience.** The offer is an ADDITION that serves their people, never a takedown of what they already do. (See copy-rubric, 2026-07-03.)

8. **Open with a hook, never proof.** Curiosity, a story, a belief-buster, or a reframe — never open on a testimonial or a stat. Proof comes AFTER trust (house rule).

9. **Structure that works:** hook → build → reveal/vouch the expert → bridge to their audience's situation → CTA to the event. (Story-led openers work especially well for re-engaging mid-sequence.)

10. **CTA: verb-first, outcome-driven button + event logistics.** First word is an action verb ("Register…", "Reserve…", "Get…"), pointed at the reader's outcome, followed by the event line ("We go live [when]").

## Subject lines — grade them, don't wing them (standing step)
Subject lines are HOOKS, not body copy — grade every one against `brand/hooks.md` (the 8 vicious-hook principles) + the copy-rubric's hook-intensity gate. The body is calm; the subject line must GRAB. Do NOT write them in the understated body voice — that's why average SLs happen.
- Apply the craft: **relevance/reader-call-out in the first words · specificity (a real number reads as information) · a real open loop · charged-but-real words · reads like information, not an ad · one curiosity thread · caveman-plain.**
- Run the **product-swap test**: if the line could sell a mattress or a supplement too, it's too generic — kill it.
- **PARTNER CALIBRATION:** keep the craft, pull INTENSITY back from "vicious" to "understated-magnetic." No "secret / shocking / you won't believe / game-changer / !!!". The grab comes from specificity + open loop + reader-relevance in a grounded, friend-telling-you-something voice.
- **Accuracy gate:** never fabricate a number/timeframe for punch (e.g. "15 years" when the real story is ~7). And never dunk on the partner's audience ("broke," etc.) to make a line land.
- **LENGTH: keep them short — aim ≤8 words.** (Confirmed default, Joey 2026-07-21: the 8-word versions beat the longer ones cold — short reads like a person texting you, long reads like a marketer. Provoke, don't explain.)
- Output a spread of angles (reader-call-out / specific-number / open-loop / belief-challenge), then pick the ones that pass product-swap + accuracy.

## Quick checklist (run before sending a partner email)
- [ ] Written in the partner's voice, third-person about the expert?
- [ ] Zero expert-insider jargon? (grep for the coined terms and kill them)
- [ ] Partner's own register — none of the client's slang tics?
- [ ] Understated, zero hype words?
- [ ] Personal endorsement used (genuinely)?
- [ ] Bridge tailored to THIS partner's audience?
- [ ] No dunking on the partner's existing model?
- [ ] Hook-first open (not proof)?
- [ ] Verb-first, outcome-driven CTA + event logistics?

## Campaign-specific rules that rode along (NOT part of Partner Voice itself)
These applied to the Bill×Buildy *reg-email* sequence but are separate levers — decide per campaign:
- **Mechanism hidden** (Day 1–2): no old-leads/DBR/SMS reveal; outcomes only. (This is a curiosity-sequence choice, not inherent to Partner Voice.)
- **No "Profit Partner model"** branded term (retired for this campaign).

## Adapting for a new JV partner
1. Capture the new partner's real voice from their own content (emails, videos) — that becomes rule #3 for them.
2. Identify their audience's specific belief/pain — that's the bridge (rule #6).
3. Keep rules #1, #2, #4, #5, #7–#10 as-is.
4. Draft with mariobot (see [[always-use-mariobot-to-write]]), primed on this template + the exemplars, then judge against [[copy-rubric]].

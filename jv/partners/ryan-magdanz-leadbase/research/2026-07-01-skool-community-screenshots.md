# Raw research — Leadbase Skool community screenshots (2026-07-01)

19 screenshots pulled from `~/Downloads/Leadbase skool research/`, Joey's own captures of the Leadbase Free Skool group. This is the audience the ROYA/Hulk campaign will run to. Compiled only — no campaign angle decided yet.

Each entry: post author, topic, what it reveals. Quotes are the members' own words.

## Beginner needing some advice — Jack Hodges (NDIS/disability lead gen, Australia)
Landed first client ($2,250 AUD / 15 leads) after ~3 months, quit Pro for a while then restarted. Burned ~$750 of budget, getting leads ~$100 each via FB lead forms, but many unqualified/won't answer. Considering switching niches entirely.
- Direct quote on isolation: "I'm fairly on my own here as I don't have anyone around me trying to make money online and sometimes I wonder if I'm loosing the plot 😅"
- Direct quote on doubt: "Am I getting cold feet too early? Has anyone had success in this niche or a similar health/disability service type niche?"
- Tahir Miah (community helper, appears across nearly every thread) talked him out of switching niches: "Personally, I wouldn't switch niches yet... it sounds less like a niche problem and more like a qualification problem," pointed to refining the qualification questions instead of chasing a new niche.

## Conversion Problem — Nick Santoro (insurance/final expense)
Getting clicks but barely any leads from an "Instant Form." Deep back-and-forth with the community (Samuel Demers, Ariful Islam) about number/order of qualifying questions, A/B testing form titles, tracking drop-off. Mentions "@Ryan Magdanz https://insuranceian.com" as a referenced example — confirms Ryan's own site (Insurance Ian) is treated as the in-community gold standard/swipe.

## Generated 10,000 leads but poor client acquisition — Khai Huyen Nguyen (local services, Austin TX)
Built a lead-gen system in a hackathon (HVAC/plumbing/drywall/10+ trades), very cheap to run (~$30-40/month in software), ~10,000 Austin-area leads sitting in a database (60% warm). **The bottleneck isn't lead generation — it's client acquisition.** Cold email and Meta ads both underperformed. This is a recurring split in the community: people are good at the generation side and stuck on the sales/client side, or vice versa (see "It's doable" below — Artur Szymczyk is the mirror case, good at closing, stuck on qualification/tech).

## Getting Paid Upfront — Justin Banner (cold outreach, various niches)
Running into resistance getting cold-sourced clients to pay before delivery. Strong response thread:
- Mehran Khan: "Setup Fee + Performance" hybrid model to lower the trust barrier for paid-ads-sourced clients.
- Tahir Miah's detailed answer: sells lead packages upfront via Stripe prepay, frames it explicitly as "buying a set number of leads" not "marketing services" (concrete, not abstract), keeps first package small (20-25 leads) to lower commitment, checks in around the 20-lead mark to review quality before reload.
- Antony Stagg: floated $1,000 of his own cashflow (on a credit card) to fund the first client's leads rather than asking for money upfront — with non-negotiables (solid buyer, good balance sheet/rep, weekly-in-arrears payment, 48hr scrub rate).
**This "will they pay upfront" objection is one of the most repeated pain points across the whole dataset** — also shows up in "Thy won't pay me in advance" below.

## Introduction — Anna Ozon (healthcare/home-care leads, Brazil, ex-nurse)
Beginner, picked healthcare/nursing-home niche specifically because she's a nurse and knows the industry. Tahir Miah's advice: don't over-build the funnel before talking to enough buyers — "the fact you're already in the industry is a huge advantage... I'd be having as many conversations as possible with home care agencies and nursing homes to understand exactly what kind of leads they want... A funnel is much easier to build once you know what the market is actually asking for." Recurring theme: build-before-you-validate is a common beginner mistake the community actively corrects.

## It's Doable. — Artur Szymczyk (solar, hybrid retainer→PPL)
Switched from retainer/commission-based work to pay-per-lead. Two example deals: 2x20 leads @ $130 = $5,200 revenue; 1x10 leads @ $130 = $1,300. Cost per qualified lead ~$30-90 (60% ad spend / 20% tax / 20% profit split). Frustrated with retainer clients treating him "more like an employee than a supplier" (Ryan Magdanz reacted "THIS" to that line) — validates the retainer-vs-PPL contrast Ryan pitches in his videos as something the community itself independently voices, not just his marketing copy. Still stuck on lead qualification mechanics (instant forms vs. quiz funnels, conversion API, wants an "easy life" with pre-qualified leads and no extra manual calling).

## Lead Agency Owner — Struggling With Lead Quality From Instant Form — Akshan Shah (car detailing)
New to the space, 2/10 form-fills converting. Community consensus: Instant Forms bring low-intent/low-friction leads by design; fixes suggested = add a review-step "Higher Intent" setting, 2-3 qualifying questions, fast (5-10 min) follow-up, or move to landing-page forms once there's ad/creative volume to justify it.

## Lead Gen 101 — What are your main struggles when it comes to Ads? — Caru Risi (poll/discussion, 30 comments)
Ryan Magdanz personally engaged: "LET US KNOW GUYS WE WANT TO HELP :)" Struggles named by members: making ads work financially (volume vs. quality trade-off between Meta and Google), creative-format confusion (aspect ratios, advantage+ vs manual), **connect/answer rate on phone follow-up** (Karla Karlsen: "70%+ leads... calls, texts, emails get no response... with calling within the 1st couple of minutes"), targeting/audience-broad-vs-narrow uncertainty as platforms push broad/algorithmic targeting.

## Mistakes I made with my first client — Kyle Johannessen (jewelry ads background, new to lead gen)
Detailed self-critical post: ran funnel + form campaign simultaneously too early (couldn't isolate what worked), assumed OTP-verified Meta leads = quality (they weren't — "bottom of the barrel leads"), over-promised delivery volume before the funnel was tested, pressure built, ultimately refunded the client and parted on good terms. Tahir Miah's reply is notable community culture: "A lot of people only post the wins, but posts like this are usually where the real learning happens... The fact you refunded the client and parted on good terms says a lot." **The community explicitly values honest "what went wrong" posts, not just win-posting** — relevant for how a campaign should sound if it wants credibility with this crowd (bragging-only copy would read as inauthentic to people who've seen this norm).

## Cross-cutting patterns (compiled observations, not a campaign angle)

- **Experience spans wide**: true $0 beginners (Jack Hodges, Anna Ozon, Corey Feldman, Sylvia Landeros) through experienced media buyers pivoting in (Billy Porter, $20M spend) through people already running PPL at real revenue (Artur Szymczyk, Nathan Lear) through a builder who went and made his own SaaS (Jack Hunter). Not a single skill tier.
- **The retainer-agency-refugee story is real and recurring**, not just Ryan's marketing narrative — Karel Stoy, Nathan Lear, and Jack Hunter are all independent members who lived a version of "retainer agency → something more ownable," unprompted.
- **The single most repeated concrete pain point is lead qualification/quality** (Jack Hodges, Nick Santoro, Artur Szymczyk, Akshan Shah, Kyle Johannessen, Grace Weaver all hit this from different angles) — Instant Forms specifically get called out as a quality trap repeatedly.
- **The second most repeated pain point is the "will the client pay upfront" trust gap** (Justin Banner, Crixo Rey both ran full threads on this; Ryan personally answered one).
- **Isolation/self-doubt is present and explicit**, not just implied — Jack Hodges' "I don't have anyone around me trying to make money online... wonder if I'm loosing the plot" is the clearest single line, and a live search for "struggling" inside the group surfaced a long list of similarly-themed thread titles (Market struggling / Still struggling / Struggling to close / From Struggling to Winning / etc.) — this appears to be a dominant emotional register in the free community, not a one-off.
- **Community culture rewards honesty over highlight-reels** — Kyle Johannessen's mistakes post and Grace Weaver's refund post were both praised more than criticized; Tahir Miah explicitly said "a lot of people only post the wins... posts like this are usually where the real learning happens."
- **Tahir Miah functions as an unofficial senior peer/mentor** across nearly every thread sampled — thoughtful, structured, non-judgmental answers. Ryan Magdanz himself is also directly present and responsive (reacting "THIS," "YES," "This is awesome," personally answering objection-handling questions, personally DMing swipe files to hundreds of commenters).
- **Financially fluent audience** — comfortable quoting cost-per-lead, revenue splits, commission percentages, ad-spend ratios. Not a "what is Facebook Ads" crowd.
- **Global, English-speaking, dispersed**: Australia, Brazil, Germany, UK, Canada (implied), South Africa, Spain, US all represented across just 19 screenshots.
- **Verticals seen**: NDIS/disability (AU), home care/nursing (Brazil), solar, final expense/burial/life insurance, mortgage, roofing, HVAC, kitchen remodeling, car detailing, business loans/funding, local multi-trade (Austin hackathon build).
- **Noise flag**: "James Henry" posted an identical unsolicited ecommerce/dropshipping pitch as a comment on two unrelated threads — looks like spam/bot activity in the free community, not real member voice. Don't mistake for signal if it recurs in future screenshots.

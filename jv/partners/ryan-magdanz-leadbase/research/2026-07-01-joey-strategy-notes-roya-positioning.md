# Strategy notes from Joey — avatar buckets + ROYA positioning (2026-07-01)

Joey's own strategic thinking, captured as-is before it gets built into copy. Not yet a finalized campaign angle — raw positioning material.

## Two avatar buckets for the Leadbase Skool campaign
1. **Newbies getting started** in PPL/lead gen.
2. **Agency owners** — running any kind of agency, OR already doing PPL specifically.

## Bucket 2 — "how ROYA is better" (the Lawrence case study)
Lawrence is a member of Joey's own community (not Leadbase) — a PPL guy who did Dan's course. No interest in stopping lead gen. Wants to bolt DBR (Done-By-Rep?) / Speed-to-Lead onto his leads so they convert better for the businesses he sells them to — solving the problem *after* handoff. Higher CVR% for his buyers = his own margins increase too (he can likely charge more or retain clients longer).

- **The real problem for Lawrence:** sales teams always blame lead quality. In reality, the agency's leads are often fine — the buyer's sales team is bad at speed-to-lead / follow-up, running leaky systems. Lawrence gets blamed for something downstream of his control.
- Lawrence doesn't want to fulfil the DBR/speed-to-lead layer himself — so he partnered with Dan. Structure: white-label add-on to Lawrence's business, Joey's side takes a %. Alternative structure: if Joey's side has clients needing leads, hand them to Lawrence to fulfil.
- **Hypothesis:** more established agency owners inside Ryan's Leadbase group may want the same deal. The pitch shape: *"You already have clients and a network. We do the tech + delivery. You keep the relationship, make more money, focus on what you're already good at."*

## Bucket 1 — smaller/newer lead-genners
- Widespread burn: leads getting more expensive, harder to get, market flooded with cheap/low-quality competition (explicitly named: "Indian agencies selling crap leads").
- Even good fulfillment doesn't stop client complaints — buyers blame "bad leads," non-converting leads, laziness, not picking up the phone (this matches the Skool screenshot data almost exactly — see [2026-07-01-skool-community-screenshots.md](2026-07-01-skool-community-screenshots.md)).
- **The framing:** getting a client AND keeping a client are both hard-mode in PPL.
- **Hypothesis:** ROYA works as a foot-in-the-door offer — get the win with ROYA first, THEN offer lead gen after trust is established. Solves both the acquisition problem and the retention problem in one motion, rather than trying to solve them inside the PPL relationship itself.

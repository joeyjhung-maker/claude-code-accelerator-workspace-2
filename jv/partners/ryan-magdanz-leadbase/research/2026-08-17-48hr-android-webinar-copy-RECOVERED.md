## The 48-Hour arc — slides 63–77, verbatim

This is the whole discovery story, in order. Slide numbers are the original deck's.

> **[63]** But… Databases are "Finite"
>
> **[64]** I had my "foot in the door"...
>
> **[65]** But I wanted long term passive income from my partners
>
> **[66]** I didn't want to offer them leads either, and spend more on PPL
>
> **[67]** What if instead of targeting OLD leads… we went after FRESH leads?
>
> **[68]** Use AI + SMS to book appointments the *moment* a lead comes in — not just from old databases.
>
> **[69]** NOPE. Our "larger" clients didn't like that idea.
>
> **[70]** They had HUMANS working these leads. Didn't matter how good my AI was — they weren't firing their team.
>
> **[71]** Plus… it was HARDER to charge per appointment or % of profit because they had just paid for Meta or Google for the lead
>
> **[72]**
> Me: "How long do your sales guys call the leads for, and how many times?"
> Client: "After 48 hours, we've dialed them 16 times. Then we move on."
> Me: "So they're pretty much dead after 48 hours?"
> Client: "Yeah — we try a few times a year, but they're dead to us."
>
> **[73]** Bingo.
>
> **[74]**
> Me: "If they're dead to you — would you API the leads to us after 48 hours, and pay per appointment set? We cover all costs."
> Client: "Hell yeah."
>
> **[75]** This was the breakthrough we needed.
>
> **[76]** We **always** START with what I call the 'Offer of the Century' — 'DBR campaigns' on old leads. Performance Based. Clients never say no.
>
> **[77]** THEN we upsell into the 48-Hour AI "Android".
> **DBR opens the door → The 48-Hour android is the recurring revenue.**

**Why this arc is the strongest asset in the deck:** the mechanism is revealed through two lines of
real dialogue, not explained. Slides 72–74 are a scene. The objection (69–71) is stated *before* the
solve, so the answer lands as a discovery rather than a pitch.

## The DBR half that sets it up — slides 35–62, condensed verbatim

Needed because 63–77 only makes sense as the *second* move.

> **[35]** Late 2024. I looked at my own education business.
> **[36]** We were spending $50,000–$100,000+ per month on ads. Over 100K peeps on my list.
> **[37]** Roughly 20% had bought from me before — I knew they'd buy again.
> **[38]** But I didn't have the patience or desire to hire more staff. We tried commission-only closers. Total disaster.
> **[39]** AI was just becoming a thing… and a student of mine had an idea…
> **[41]** Jamie's pitch: "Send your leads an SMS. If they reply, I'll build you an AI bot that behaves like you — books a strategy session or gets them to a webinar."
> **[42]** We ran it. It crushed. North of $500,000 from one campaign.
> **[44]** So I picked up the phone to my old PPL rolodex — clients sitting on big databases
> **[45]** I sent them a Loom of what worked on my own list… showed them the actual AI conversations.
> **[46]** Gave 'em an offer: "Do you have any old leads we can turn into sales? Performance basis only — if you don't get paid, we don't get paid."
> **[48]** Everyone said yes. It CRUSHED.
> **[50]** A debt-consolidation company spending multiple 6-figures/month on leads… Only spoke to HALF of them. The rest got dialed 16 times. Then written off as dead.
> **[51]** We took 319 of those dead leads and fed them to our "Sleeping Beauty Android" — 47 responded. 21 booked appointments. Each sale worth $4,000–$7,000.
> **[52]** $600,000+ in 90 days — from leads they'd completely given up on. (And I earned a % on all of it)
> **[54]** How does a bot get a response 16 calls couldn't? ✅ Opens so personal they think "oh yeah, I remember" ✅ Disarms the "I'm too busy" wall ✅ Qualifies them naturally — feels like chatting, not selling ✅ Ties the callback to a real person — so show rates soar
> **[55]** We spent 2.5 years and tens of thousands of dollars tweaking & testing the psychology — and this is why we're getting 30–60% RESPONSE rates
> **[62]** It worked so well for so many people I coined this the: "Offer of The Century"

Then the $1.03M headline lands *after* the 48-hour reveal:

> **[78]** Let's talk about this "No Ad Spend" Campaign which SO FAR has made $1,030,000+ — My cut: $222,600.

## The 48-Hour Android in the email copy (Ryan's list, July 2026)

From the open-cart email that named all seven androids — the tightest one-line description we have:

> **3. 48 Hour Android** — Works the leads the sales team already gave up on. Found money sitting
> right there that nobody's touching.

Its sibling line matters too, because the two get confused constantly:

> **2. Speed 2 Lead Android** — Hits every new lead in seconds, around the clock. Never get blamed
> for "bad leads" again.

**Keep these straight in copy:** Speed 2 Lead goes to the *front* of the queue (minute one).
48-Hour goes to the *back* (hour 49, after the humans quit). Same tech, opposite position in time —
and the 48-Hour one is the one that doesn't threaten anybody's job.

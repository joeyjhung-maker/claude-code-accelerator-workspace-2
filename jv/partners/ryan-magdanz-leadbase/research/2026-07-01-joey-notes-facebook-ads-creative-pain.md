# Strategy notes from Joey — Facebook/Meta ads creative-volume pain (2026-07-01)

Joey's own market knowledge, captured as-is. A third pain-point pillar alongside lead qualification/quality and the upfront-payment trust gap already documented — this one is about the operational burden of running the ads themselves, not what happens after the lead is captured.

## The pain, in Joey's words
- Meta changes constantly. The Andromeda update specifically now requires launching hundreds of new creatives to feed the engine with creative diversity — the algorithm rewards volume/variety of creative, not a handful of evergreen winners.
- Ad costs are always rising, which squeezes PPL profit margins directly — Ryan's own pitch is "buy a lead for $50, sell for $100"; if the cost side of that spread creeps up, the margin compresses unless creative/targeting efficiency improves to compensate.
- It's very hard to keep up, and resource-intensive to do properly — this isn't a one-time setup, it's an ongoing operational treadmill.
- AI-generated ads are "fine" but risky — they can bomb hard if done badly. AI lowers the cost of producing volume but doesn't remove the skill/QA requirement; bad AI creative at scale is still bad creative at scale.
- Scaling makes this worse, not better: more clients = more ads needed = more of this exact problem, compounding rather than flattening as the business grows.
- On top of raw volume, there's a constant treadmill of testing hooks, angles, and creative formats — this never resolves into a "done" state.

## Why this matters for both avatar buckets
- **Newbies:** already resource- and time-constrained (per the Currency & Sharks Sharks tab: "lack of time," "uncertainty around the first few steps"). Creative volume demands compound an existing scarcity, not a new one — they don't have a team or budget to produce hundreds of creatives.
- **Established agency owners:** this is a concrete, mechanical version of the "ceiling they can't see clearly" theme already surfaced in the Build-a-Buyer-Elite profile for this segment (see [2026-07-01-build-a-buyer-elite-established-agency-owner.md](2026-07-01-build-a-buyer-elite-established-agency-owner.md)). Creative production capacity doesn't scale down per-client as they add clients — it scales up, linearly or worse. More success directly creates more of this specific pain.

## Live Skool proof (2 more screenshots, 2026-07-01) — this pillar confirmed directly

These two close the loop — direct, named, quantified member evidence for the exact pain Joey described above, from both the paid and free communities.

### Help with Ads — Olle Sjöström (Leadbase **Pro**, 912 members, Mar '28, Ads)
Full post: *"I'm struggling hard with consistency when scaling Facebook ads and I'm hoping someone here has cracked this. I ran a small test batch Monday and Tuesday and was getting leads under $20. Felt great. Then I launched the exact same ads for a bigger client with more budget and CPL shot up to $100. Out of that bigger batch I identified 7 clear winners, or so I thought. Moved them into a dedicated scale campaign like I've seen recommended here, and now those same ads that were getting me leads for under $15 are sitting at $70-80. It feels like every time I try to scale what's working, the algorithm completely resets on me. What am I missing? Would really appreciate it if someone who's been through this could hop on a quick call with me and walk me through what I'm doing wrong. **Happy to pay for your time if needed.**"* Tagged @Ryan Barbieri, @Ryan Magdanz, @Andrew Sharpe directly.

Follow-up detail (to Andrew Sharpe, who asked his daily budget): *"$300/day across 7 ads in dynamic creative. Problem is Meta keeps feeding budget to the wrong ones. One ad has 547 reach and $95 CPL while another gets 3 leads at $12 CPL but barely gets any spend. On the bigger campaign I'm running 9 ad sets at $60/day each ($540/day total) with 10 dynamic creatives per ad set, so 90 images total. Spent $1,315 so far and got 26 leads at $50.60 average CPL. The issue is the same as the scale campaign. Meta is dumping spend into images that get clicks but zero leads. One image got 1,145 impressions, 153 clicks and 0 leads... Meanwhile I have images sitting at $2-10 CPL that are barely getting shown. Would it be okay to: pausing 3 entire ad sets that aren't performing, then going into each of the 6 surviving ad sets and removing the dead images from the dynamic creatives. Yeah it'll reset learning phase but I'd rather have a few days of re-learning than keep burning $700 on images that will never convert."*

This is the single most concrete, quantified example of the "scaling breaks the algorithm" pain in the whole dataset — a real operator running 90 images across 9 ad sets, willing to **pay a stranger for a call** to fix it. Confirms this hits people already past the beginner stage (Pro community, real budget, real client), not just newbies.

### Help With Roofing Ads? — Ben Behmer (Leadbase **Free**, Jan '7, Ads)
Full post: *"Hey, curious if anyone is crushing with roofing meta ads in here? I working with a client and we have done about $2,000 in ad spend (50% perspective funnels, then 50% to meta lead forms to try even lower friction)...but we've come out with only 5 leads... yikes. We've tried a ton of different creative types (videos made with captions.ai, revid.ai, and photos/statics made with Canva, nano banana, midjourney, just project photos, chat gpt, etc), and campaign structures, and a couple of different offers (from free anti moss treatments with a new roof, new roofs starting at $5,997), etc... all to seemingly no result."*

**Brandon Raziano's reply is the key line — direct, unprompted member confirmation of the AI-creative-risk point Joey raised:** *"I have a couple years of FB Ads experience, but what I can tell you immediately is that its your creative. People buy from people, and with obvious AI videos...theres no trust. People like seeing PROOF, and not fake employees, fake houses, and fake roofs being worked on. I'd suggest you get the roofers some rayban meta camera glasses and throughout the day they just click the glasses and you can clip up some videos pretty easily."*

Christina Robbins adds a cross-vertical confirmation it's not just roofing: *"That's rough, but honestly not uncommon right now. We're seeing the same thing happen in solar as well. Meta performance has been inconsistent across a few verticals, especially when demand isn't event-driven."* She's bridging the gap with layered outbound (call/email/SMS on intent-qualified homeowner data) rather than relying purely on lead forms — a workaround, not a fix.

Caru Risi (recurring helpful voice in Free, parallel to Tahir Miah) reframes it as an approach problem, not just a creative problem: *"Roofing on Meta usually isn't a creative issue, rather than the approach and urgency. Homeowners don't wake up wanting a new roof unless there's damage or urgency."*

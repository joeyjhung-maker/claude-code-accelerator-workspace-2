## What's actionable for THIS campaign

### 1. Post-webinar CTA mechanism
Travis: don't rely on a single DM channel. Give them SMS (a real number + QR code, easy to whip up), Facebook DM, AND email as options — "the more ways you got them to do it, the better... you're trying to get the hot leads." Multi-channel capture, not one bottleneck.

### 2. Price anchoring without stating the exact number
Don't post the raw price ($4,500-$5,500 range, 12-pay option). Instead:
- Anchor with a relatable comparison, not a number — Travis's own line: "it's what most people would spend, like, a family of four on a weekend vacation." Same anchor, reads as $1k to one person and $10k to another, without naming it.
- Have 2-3 packages so you're never quoting ONE flat price on the spot — "depending on what you need, we can plug you into that."
- Real number that IS said out loud: the $500 "skin in the game" test fee (see #6 below) — that's the one number that gets stated plainly, everything above it stays soft.

### 3. No fake scarcity — vet for fit instead
Travis: don't say "I'm only taking a handful of people." Instead frame it as a fit-check — his own phrasing: "we're gonna make sure we go together like peanut butter and jelly, rather than peanut butter and mustard... we just don't make a good sandwich together, so we're not gonna let everybody in." Filters without manufacturing urgency. The registration link itself is the real backup plan (you've already got their data).

### 4. THE central persuasion angle: "delivery bogged down" contrast
This is Travis's biggest strategic point. Spend real time contrasting:
- The OLD way: hard to sell a $25k package or $2k/mo retainer, AND once sold, constant service delivery pain — "DMs out the wazoo," bugs, ongoing support, clients calling mad.
- OUR way: "we can just demo... the AI does all the work, and we make a ton of money... we don't have to deliver anything." Front-end offer, minimal delivery burden.
Named term: **"delivery bogged down."** Don't just list value-stack bullets — walk through the CONTRAST, back and forth, old pain vs. new relief. Add the emotional mind-movie: "what's your first roofer gonna say when their calendar's suddenly blacked out?"

### 5. Never do a flat value-stack list for the close
Dan reacted strongly here: "I hate that." Travis's fix: walk them through a JOURNEY/bridge narrative instead of "you get this module, you get that module" — "you're gonna come on, we're gonna find the best fit, then you go get your first coffee date, here's how, here's the exact scripts... you want to lay out that bridge, and they almost feel like they've walked across it." **Directly relevant to the still-unbuilt webinar close section ("ADD CLOSE" placeholder in the deck) — this is the shape it should take, not a bullet stack.**

### 6. The actual close script Dan is planning to use/teach
Confirmed, verbatim shape: "It's a test, it's performance-based, all it is, is 500 bucks to get your skin in the game. We spend a lot more time and money than that, but we need skin in the game from you. If you're comfortable, we'll run this for two weeks. If it doesn't work, no harm, no foul. If it does, we can talk numbers."
Also confirmed as the live pitch for the direct-mail/whiskey-bottle campaign, already running and working ("people are so happy to get on a call... some are pissed off, but that's the signal something's working").

### 8. Stay on live for extended Q&A after the pitch
Travis: the webinar itself isn't the closing device, it's the pre-sell — the real close happens in Q&A/follow-up. "How many people are still on there when you end the webinar? Those are all the sales you didn't get." Dan dislikes this but Travis is firm it drives conversions. Worth planning for a long live Q&A block, not a quick wrap.

### 9. Bigger structural option: 3-webinar sequence (not required, but on the table)
Travis's own history: benefits webinar → how-it-works webinar → the actual close/cart-day webinar, with the LAST one being the true closing device (he cites Bob Proctor staying live 12 hours on close day, Mindvalley doing the same, both doing multi-million-dollar closes this way). Could also bring successful ROYA students onto the close-day session for live crosstalk/endorsement ("bring some of your successful students, have some crosstalk with people who've actually gone through it").

### 10. Voice/copy note — directly addressed to Joey
Travis's strongest opinion, and the one Dan explicitly flagged for Joey by name: **make copy so obviously human that nobody could mistake it for AI.** His argument: as AI-written copy floods everyone's inbox, sounding unmistakably human is the new differentiator — "when everybody zigs, you gotta zag." His examples: weather-based personal asides ("hotter than the devil's ass out here"), guessing a real personal detail about a prospect to prove attention, provocative direct questions. He also says getting some ANGRY replies alongside excited ones is a good sign, not a bad one — it means the copy has real edge, not just polite blandness. His diagnosis of most competitors: "written by smart people" but bland because they lack personality/texture.
⚠️ **This is in some tension with the Script's ban on invented backstory/results and the Dan-truth constraint** — any "spice" added needs to still be true to Dan, not fabricated color for its own sake. Flag for Joey to weigh, not an automatic license to invent.

### 11. Benchmark number
Bill's team reportedly got 750 people to show up live on a recent webinar using AI-personalized registration messages/SMS — a target number if we want a comparable benchmark for this campaign's attendance.

# The Argument Bank — cold traffic only

*Living doc. Every strong argument we find that works on a STRANGER goes here. Add, don't replace.*

Related: [[2026-08-17-headline-bank-240-live-statics]] [[hooks]] [[_organizing-swipes]] [[Promotion]]

## Scope — read this before adding anything

**Cold acquisition only.** The reader does not know us, does not want anything from us, and did not ask. That is the only situation this bank covers.

In awareness terms: **unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware.** The moment an argument assumes the reader already knows the brand, it does not belong here.

Doesn't go in:
- Product-aware and most-aware plays — testimonials, offers, discount theatre, timeline proof. Those are retargeting and list work.
- Warm-list and JV arguments — a partner's audience is borrowed trust, which is a different game.
- Organic social structures — they win on engagement, and agree-or-argue in the comments is worth nothing in a paid ad.

The sweep found the same line independently: *"Product-aware and most-aware are straightforward — testimonials and offers. Unaware, problem-aware and solution-aware are where you MANUFACTURE the belief that makes the sale possible."* That manufacturing is what this file is for.

## What counts as an argument

Not a headline. Not a format. The piece of logic the ad makes a stranger accept — written as **their own inner sentence**.

> *"Melts belly fat"* is the claim.
> *"My fat is a substance stuck IN me, not a verdict ON me, and substances can be removed"* is the argument.

The argument transplants between offers. The picture doesn't.

**How to add one:** belief line (their inner sentence) → why a cold reader accepts it → the one thing to steal → source. If you can't write the belief as a sentence a stranger would think in their own head, you haven't found the argument yet.

**Craft rules live in [[hooks]]** — the 8 vicious-hook principles, and the rule that you find the argument before you write the hook.

# PART 1 — Ours

## "The better your content, the worse it converts"
**Dan Henry — the teacher's brain.** The one that started this file.

**Belief:** *"The thing I'm best at is the thing that's killing me — and I never would have guessed."*

**Why it works cold:** a stranger has no reason to believe a promise, but he cannot ignore a contradiction about himself. The paradox is stated flat, with no hedge, so he has to ask "wait, why?" before any proof shows up. Then the cause gets a proprietary name — **the teacher's brain** — which turns his own competence into the hidden trap.

Structure: paradox hook → "I know that sounds backwards, hear me out" → credibility stat → "here's what I discovered" → restate the paradox with specifics → "so I started digging into why" → named-mechanism reveal → explain it → "here's the thing though, you can have both" → CTA.

**Steal:** open on the naked contradiction, not the result. Prove the ratio AFTER the paradox lands. Name the root cause as a diagnosis, not a marketing term — test it against *would a non-marketer feel personally called out?*

Stated flat elsewhere in the same campaign: *"Most webinar scripts are built to teach. Teaching kills conversions."*

→ [[2026-08-10-dan-henry-teachers-brain]] · used in [[2026-08-10-flexxable-iaa-nice-guy-trap-fb-ad-WINNER]]

## "One embarrassing number, three doors into it"
**Dan Henry — pizza delivery to $40M.**

**Belief:** *"Something specific went wrong for him and I need to know what it was."*

**Why it works cold:** the proof stat opens a loop before the pitch exists. *"My first webinar converted at 48% on a warm list… then it dropped like a rock to less than 1%."* Oddly specific, self-deprecating, unfakeable-looking — a stranger reads that as information, not advertising. Three headlines then carry the same open into three different doors: transformation (pizza → $40M), identity (*"I DELIVERED PIZZAS FOR 7 YEARS. ONE WEBINAR SCRIPT GOT ME OUT."*), diagnosis (*"IF YOUR WEBINAR ISN'T CONVERTING, THE SCRIPT IS THE PROBLEM."*).

**Steal:** don't make one hook do transformation + identity + diagnosis at once. Isolate the proof stat as its own unit — it works as an overlay or comment, not baked into the headline — then run 2–3 headlines that each pick ONE door. Cheap to test, core asset stays constant.

→ [[2026-08-05-dan-henry-pizza-to-40m-webinar-script]]

## "The asset I already own is worth more than the thing I'm about to buy"
**Flexxable / IAA — the CRM reframe.**

**Belief:** *"There's money already sitting in my business that I've been walking past."*

**Why it works cold:** a stranger will not accept a growth promise, but he will accept a fact about property he already owns. *"Your client's CRM is probably worth more than their entire ad budget this year, and nobody's told them yet."* World-level, not proof-led, and it makes the reader the one who spots the goldmine. Sidesteps the tired escape-the-grind open entirely.

**Steal:** the existing-asset-they've-forgotten reframe. Travels to any database-reactivation offer. Cold framing only — for a JV partner's list it needs reworking, see [[jv-additive-framing]].

→ [[2026-07-06-crm-worth-more-than-ad-budget-hook]]

## "They have nothing to lose, which is why they pay me so much"
**Flexxable / IAA — dead leads as zero downside.**

**Belief:** *"Of course they'd say yes — it costs them nothing they haven't already written off."*

**Why it works cold:** the stranger's real objection isn't price, it's *"my clients would never go for that."* This kills it without ever mentioning price. Dan's own line: *"these old leads are DEAD to the business owner — they have nothing to lose — which is why they pay me so much. Easiest. Sell. Ever."*

**Steal:** when your offer works on an asset the buyer has already mentally written off, say so out loud. The absence of downside IS the argument.

→ `clients/flexxable/winning-ads.md` (Ad 2)

## "I'm not stuck because I'm bad at this — I'm stuck because the money is trapped"
**Client Accelerators — the ceiling reframe.**

**Belief:** *"It isn't my competence. There's a specific number sitting on the table right now."*

**Why it works cold:** a stranger being told he's leaving money on the table hears an insult and starts arguing. This removes the self-blame in the same breath as the callout — *$2,000/DAY … NOT BECAUSE YOUR ADS SUCK* — so he stays open. Then it turns the feeling into arithmetic: *$2,000 × 40 DAYS = $80,000 TRAPPED.*

**Steal:** rule out the reader's own self-blame theory before you offer yours. And translate "stuck" into an exact number sitting on the table *right now* — trapped money beats missed opportunity as a visual every time.

→ [[2026-03-18-client-accelerators-income-ceiling-reframe]]

## "I've probably been told it's X. It's not X."
**Chalkboard native — the belief-break step.**

**Belief:** *"My current explanation for why I keep failing is wrong."*

**Why it works cold:** a stranger arrives with a theory already installed about why he's failed. Educate him without removing it and he files your mechanism under "another thing that won't work." Structure: Question → Show problem → **Break belief** → Educate. Step 3 is the one most ads skip — *"not the acid," "PPIs don't reach it," "70–80% get no relief."*

**Steal:** never jump from problem straight to mechanism. Kill the old theory first. The reveal only feels earned once it's gone.

→ [[2026-07-20-chalkboard-native-question-problem-belief-educate]]

## "The way I work IS the problem — and someone has compressed it to 83 minutes"
**Zalo Kabche — old way vs new way.**

**Belief:** *"That left column is literally my week."*

**Why it works cold:** a stranger doesn't recognise your offer, but he recognises his own week instantly. The left column is the qualifying device — *ignored proposals, price wars, 60-hr grinds, another $500 project* — written as the exact phrases he'd use, not as "problems." If that's his life he has identified himself as your buyer before you've claimed anything. The right column then only has to beat **his week**, not beat a competitor: *Send AI Demo (3 min) → Send Video Pitch (5 min) → Deploy & Get Paid (75 min).*

**Steal:** write the old-way column as their week. And time-stamp the new way — *"3 steps"* is a claim, *"75 min"* is a demo.

→ [[2025-12-23-zalo-kabche-old-way-vs-new-way-3-step-system]]

## "I don't want the new price — I want out of the old model"
**AI agency price drop.**

**Belief:** *"I do not want to keep doing what I'm currently doing."*

**Why it works cold:** a discount means nothing to a stranger who hasn't been sold on leaving. So the ad swaps the unit economics first — *"Still charging $500 for websites?"* → *$5K for a 1-hour install* — and only then drops $999 → $37.

**Steal:** lead a price-drop with the old business model you're helping them escape, not the new number. The struck-through price only works because he was sold on leaving one line earlier.

→ [[2025-12-23-ai-agency-price-drop-urgency]]

## "This person has nothing to gain from telling me this"
**The Practitioner Pattern — persuasion by disinterest.**

**Belief:** *"No one is selling me here, so I can believe it."*

**Why it works cold:** the stranger's first thought on any ad is *"you're selling me,"* and every claim after that gets discounted. A narrator with no stake removes the motive to lie — *"I have no affiliation with them. I just see what works across dozens of families every year."* This is a cold-only device: to someone who already knows the brand, claiming no stake is confusing or dishonest. The most unaware-heavy library in the sweep runs the same move at scale — Rosabella's network of publisher-styled pages, 70% top-of-funnel, product almost never in frame.

**Paired move — ONE word, hammered.** That ad says "position" 15 times and "flat" 19 times, until the reader cannot see his own situation any other way. The word works literally and figuratively at once.

→ [[2026-08-13-practitioner-pattern-longform-unaware]]

## "The thing I'm best at is the ceiling"
**The Nice Guy Trap.**

**Belief:** *"I don't need more clients. I need permission to stop optimising for the wrong thing."*

**Why it works cold:** surface complaint is *"too many clients, not enough profit."* The real mechanism is that the agency model ties income to labour and headcount — no amount of skill moves that ceiling, and every dollar is rented from someone else's business. A stranger recognises the complaint; the argument is what he hasn't heard.

**Steal:** the naming test. The best mechanism names are **internal** — a reflex or wiring inside the reader, not an external trap the market set — and read as a diagnosis, not an insult. Fixable, not a character flaw. If the name could headline an ad for a different profession, it isn't specific enough.

→ [[hooks]]

## "Someone smarter, in a bigger game, already solved this"
**Flexxable — the hedge fund analogy.**

**Belief:** *"This isn't a marketing idea, it's a borrowed one from people who play for higher stakes."*

*"I copied this agency model from what Hedge Fund Managers do… Then added A.I. because I wanted A.I. to do the work."*

**Steal, with the caveat we learned the hard way:** an analogy angle needs connective tissue. Explain WHY the analogy holds before it can carry weight, and delay naming the expert until the idea has landed. Naming first makes it a credential; naming after makes it proof.

→ `clients/flexxable/winning-ads.md` (Ad 1) · [[hedge-fund-angle-needs-connective-tissue]]

# PART 2 — The DR supplement sweep (Aug 2026)

15 cold-traffic arguments reverse-engineered from 2,370 live statics across 12 brands, Meta Ad Library 2026-08-02/03. Their numbers, not verified by us. Source: `named-mechanism/_assets/2026-08-17-winning-static-ad-types-SOURCE.md`. Raw headlines: [[2026-08-17-headline-bank-240-live-statics]].

*Four of the original 19 were product-aware or most-aware and are out of scope — listed at the bottom of this file.*

Ranked strongest first. My read, except where noted.

## TIER 1

### "The thing I'm already doing has a hidden cost — and this fixes the cost without asking me to stop"
**The Sidekick Hijack.** Their own verdict: the strongest strategic move in the entire sweep.

**Why it works cold:** you don't compete with the wave, you **farm its side effects**. It never attacks the reader's decision, so it never triggers a defence — and it inherits a huge pre-qualified cold audience for free, because the wave's own ad budget did the audience-building.

- *"Lose Fat, Not Definition."* — never argues against GLP-1. Argues about what GLP-1 **costs** you.
- *"Lose Fat, Not Strength."* — same skeleton, one word swapped. That's how you test which loss the market actually fears.
- *"TEETH PROBLEMS ON GLP-1?"* — a side effect so specific most users didn't know it was a category.

**Formula:** {THE WAVE} {SPECIFIC SIDE EFFECT}? + {PRODUCT} as the fix — never as the alternative.

**Build:** name the wave in your market. List every complaint its users have. One ad per complaint.

**Breaks when:** you attack the wave's premise. Tell them their decision was stupid and you've lost someone who already paid for it.

### "I've been fighting the wrong enemy — so my failure was never my fault"
**The Reframe Bomb.** Absolution and mechanism in one frame. Same family as the teacher's brain.

- *"THAT BELLY DIDN'T COME FROM BEER. IT CAME FROM LOW TESTOSTERONE."*
- *"High Cortisol could be why you're snapping at everyone"* — a character flaw reframed as a hormone. Their pick for the most forgiving sentence in the library.
- *"IT WAS HORMONAL"* — reassigns a lifetime of diet failure.

**Formula:** IT'S NOT {WHAT THEY BLAME}. IT'S {NAMED CAUSE}. Flat fact.

**Test:** finish *"You think it's ___, but it's actually ___."* If the first blank isn't something your market says **unprompted**, you haven't found it.

**Breaks when:** the cause feels invented rather than discoverable. *"Your mitochondrial signalling cascade"* loses to *"cortisol."*

### "My flaw is not me. It's a substance IN me. And substances can be removed."
**The Visceral Mechanism.** Extraction theology.

**Why it works cold:** it kills the willpower objection **structurally**, not with copy. A hose does the work, not discipline. The stranger never has to promise to try harder — which is the promise he's already broken and won't make again.

- *"SOMETIMES IT'S NOT FAT, IT COULD BE STUCK POOP."* — a colon built from balloon animals, brown section packed and stuck.
- *"MELTS STUBBORN BELLY FAT STORAGE"* — fat-cell spheres burning under a blue flame. The verb performed literally, in frame.

Two unrelated brands independently landed on **balloons** for trapped internal volume — because balloons promise release without effort.

**Formula:** render {SYMPTOM} as {SUBSTANCE or OBJECT}, product {CAUSE-VERB}-ing it via {APPARATUS}, stamped with a {TIMEFRAME}.

**Breaks when:** the product merely stands next to the problem. It must participate.

### "I can have the result without giving up the thing I actually enjoy"
**Keep-the-Vice.** Removes the sacrifice objection — which they name as the real reason most people never buy.

**Why it works cold:** every past failure the stranger has had involved giving something up. This promises the outcome with the vice intact, and shows the vice in frame being enjoyed.

- *"NO MORE CRASH DIETING"* — names the sacrifice being cancelled, not the benefit delivered.
- *"Healthy can taste this good."* — the vice IS the format. The gummy is the permission.

**Build:** ask five customers what they refused to quit. One ad per vice.

**Breaks when:** your mechanism isn't genuinely orthogonal to the vice. Then it's refunds.

## TIER 2

**"The mainstream option I was about to choose has a flaw I didn't know about"** — *The Category Hijack.* Borrows a bigger category's entire search demand and wins on ONE shortcoming. *"GOODBYE TRT. HELLO MARS MEN."* / *"FINASTERIDE WITH MARS MEN"* — hijack as **combination**, which converts people mid-treatment who won't quit / *"ONCE YOU INJECT, YOUR BODY STOPS MAKING ITS OWN."* Build: **write the sentence your prospect says at 11pm when they're scared of the mainstream option.** That fear is your hijack. Breaks if you name a brand — attack the category mechanism, not a trademark.

**"These scattered annoyances aren't separate. They're ONE thing, and it has a name."** — *The Diagnostic Checklist.* The stranger diagnoses himself; nothing is sold, so nothing is defended against. Ten most specific annoyances in their own words, one villain, villain in the headline, no product in frame — the click is the conversion. Breaks on generic signs: *"low energy"* is a category, not a sign.

**"They're talking to ME specifically. Not people like me — me."** — *The Segment Callout.* *"we want 5 men who realized that 'i'm just tired' isn't a personality"* segments by **self-awareness**, not demographics. A mushroom coffee brand claiming cannabis users; a greens gummy claiming the MTHFR gene mutation; an antifungal claiming steel-toe-boot workers. The format that finds cold pockets competitors haven't noticed exist.

**"I can see the difference with my own eyes. Nothing to take on faith."** — *Us-vs-Them.* Converts a claim into an observation, which is what a stranger will accept and a claim isn't. **Find the one number your competitors won't print. Print it next to their blank.** That blank space is the most persuasive object in the ad. Breaks if your winning axis is price — you're teaching the market to leave the moment someone undercuts.

**"That's literally my exact problem, in the words I use for it."** — *The Symptom-Question Poster.* Two to five words. *"WORKOUT-RESISTANT CHEST FAT?"* — not "chest fat"; WORKOUT-RESISTANT pre-answers *"I should just train harder."* Write twenty in an afternoon, one per symptom: the cheapest possible read on which door a cold market walks through, and the winner tells your video team what to build.

**"All of it. That's all of it on one page. And one thing is causing all of it."** — *The Multi-Symptom Chant.* Where the checklist teaches, the chant hammers — built for recognition velocity, so it catches every stranger whose particular symptom is anywhere in the list. Eight complaints from the support inbox, three words each, stacked under the villain's name. Then watch which brand-new door people quote back in the comments — that's the next ad. Breaks when it gets rhythmic: triplets go AI-flavoured fast.

## TIER 3 — the wrappers

**"A real person typed this to real people."** — *Notes App Confession.* Total feed camouflage; the sell arrives after the stranger is already committed to the story. *"SORRY TO WHOEVER SEES THIS AND IT'S TOO LATE…"* **If your designer touches it, you've destroyed the asset** — a logo, a font or a clean crop converts it back into an ad.

**"There's a character living inside my body and I want it evicted."** — *Talking-Object Mascot.* Problem-polarity beats solution-polarity: a villain visibly *enjoying* your body primes outrage, and the after-panel pays it off as revenge. Longest-lived family in the sweep, 72d median.

**"There are fakes of this, which means the real one is worth faking."** — *Copycat Callout.* A trust play disguised as a public-service announcement — the fraud-alert framing stops a cold scroll, then converts the alarm into preference. Almost nobody in supplements runs this position.

**"Somebody's finally showing it instead of tastefully implying it."** — *The Raw Specimen.* Full-bleed clinical photo, no design, no type, no product; the image shocks, the text diagnoses. Instantly qualifies its reader because only a sufferer keeps looking. Highest platform-rejection risk in the deck — isolate it in its own ad set.

**"I'm being shown what's happening inside me. This is medical, not marketing."** — *Anatomy Authority.* Belief compresses from "I hope this works" to "of course this works" the moment the picture makes sense. 85% of anatomical renders sit at problem-aware — the most awareness-locked style in the set, and pure cold-traffic territory.

# PART 3 — Rules that govern all of them

**Made-For-Me.** Mass-market product, every ad reads like it was made for exactly one person. If your target wouldn't feel it was made for them, the ad failed — no matter how clever the visual.

**Text names, visual dramatises.** Text names the cause literally. Visual carries the metaphor. Never the reverse. A cold viewer must leave knowing what's causing his symptom and what it's doing.

**The verb test.** Headline verb and image are the SAME event. If your verb is DRAINS, the picture shows draining. Otherwise it's a caption on a stock photo.

**Emotion through visuals, never on the nose.** *"17 days later, he lasted 17 minutes"* with a satisfied wife. Nobody says the emotional thing out loud.

**The wrapper is permission.** Clay, cartoon, sculpture, hand-drawn note — production style isn't decoration, it's what lets you show a truth a photograph couldn't get away with. Cartoon disarms, so you can go darker.

**Ugly is a strategy, not a budget.** The handwritten note and the finger over the lens are engineered. They read as information rather than advertising, and buy the two seconds a polished ad never gets.

**Their words, not yours.** Not *digestive irregularity* — **stuck poop**. Not *gynecomastia* — **man boobs**. Not *incomplete evacuation* — **skid marks**. Mirror language, bathroom language, locker-room language: the phrases they use with themselves and would never say to a doctor. Same instinct as caveman language in [[hooks]].

**Craft outlives offers.** Median days live: Cartoon Mascot 72d · Native iPhone 66d · Sidekick Hijack 61d · Visceral Mechanism 60d · Unaware Diagnostic 54d — against Offer Theater at 38d. The formats doing the hardest cold work are the ones brands leave running.

**When a headline wins, don't retire it. Re-shoot it.** 52% of headline-and-brand combos run against two or more images. RYZE ran *FOR WEED SMOKERS* against 19 images across 6 rendering styles.

**Six words.** Median on-image headline never moves off 5–6 words at any awareness level.

**Price stamps at the cold end.** Only 6% of unaware ads carry a price or discount — but 22–23% of problem-aware and solution-aware ads do. A price on a mid-funnel cold ad is normal practice now, not a mistake.

**The one they published against themselves.** They expected cause-naming to buy longer run times. It didn't — 51 days vs 52. Cause-clarity is still right because it makes an ad *comprehensible* to a cold viewer, which is the point. It just isn't what keeps an ad alive, and they said so.

## The teardown method — three questions before anyone talks layout

1. **WHAT is being expressed?** The BELIEF, as the prospect's inner sentence. Not the claim.
2. **HOW?** The central metaphor and what it borrows power from. Who the prospect is allowed to be in the image. What the eye sees first (premise) and last (conclusion). **What does this wrapper permit that a photograph would not?**
3. **WHY this expression?** Which awareness level. What the image does that copy can't. **What the prospect would never let you SAY but will happily let you SHOW.** Which objection dies without a word spent on it.

They rebuilt all 16 extracted families for a different offer — different gender, symptom, price point — and all 16 transferred. The rule that made it work: **abstract at the JOB level, never the surface level.** Their sexual-payoff ads aren't a "sexual" family, they're the DREAM-STATE PAYOFF family. A testosterone market's fantasy is virility; a gut market's is ordering the pasta without fear. Same slots, different fill.

# PART 4 — Cold arguments that died

**"Clarity early always beats curiosity."** True for cold paid traffic — a stranger bounces from confusion, so revealing the mechanism plainly and early reduces drop-off. It is the wrong medicine anywhere else, and we tried to apply it to a warm JV list where the withhold was the engine. Keep it as a cold-only rule. → [[2026-07-21-clarity-first-webinar-reorder-wrong-for-warm-jv]]

**"Pain-led opens."** Pain is white noise now. Lead cold with curiosity, novelty, a promise or a world-level hook; pain is the AMPLIFIER once attention is earned. Opt in per ad. → [[pain-is-amplifier-not-opener]]

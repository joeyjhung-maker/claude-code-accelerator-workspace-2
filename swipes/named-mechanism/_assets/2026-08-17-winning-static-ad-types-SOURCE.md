## **What one frame has to do**

The standard: 3 seconds, sound off, cold scroller. When they flick past, they still leave with WHO it's for, WHAT the problem is, WHAT causes it - and a feeling.

#### **The Made-For-Me Test**

Mass-market product, yet every ad reads like it was made for exactly one person. If your target would not feel it was made for them, the ad has failed - no matter how clever the visual.

#### **Cause-clarity: text names, visual dramatizes**

The TEXT names the cause literally and says what it is doing to you. The VISUAL carries the metaphor. Never the reverse. A cold viewer must leave knowing what is causing his symptom and what it is doing - if either lives only in the picture, the ad fails.

#### **The verb test, single-frame edition**

The headline's verb and the image are the SAME event. 'SUCKS OUT' = a hose visibly sucking. 'DRAINS' = something visibly draining. If the image does not literally perform the verb, you have a caption sitting on a stock photo.

#### **Emotion through visuals, never on the nose**

'17 days later, he lasted 17 minutes' with a satisfied wife. Nobody says the emotional thing out loud. The picture carries the payload and the viewer does the last inch of the work themselves.

#### **The wrapper is permission**

Clay, cartoon, sculpture, balloon, hand-drawn note - the production style is not decoration, it is what lets you show a truth a photograph could never get away with. Cartoon disarms, so you can go darker.

#### **Ugly is a strategy, not a budget**

A handwritten note screenshot and a finger over the camera lens are engineered. They read as information rather than advertising, and they buy the two seconds a polished ad never gets.

## **UNAWARE**

No product. No symptom the reader has already named. The job is to get a stranger to stop, self-diagnose, and walk away believing they have a problem they did not know they had. Hardest rung to write, and the one that opens the biggest audiences.

### **01** **The Diagnostic Checklist**

**pain doorsmechanism**

**The belief it installs:** These scattered annoyances I have been ignoring are not separate. They are ONE thing, it has a name, and I am looking at the list of my own life.

Takes 5-10 symptoms the reader already lives with, groups them under a single named cause, and lets them diagnose themselves. Nothing is sold. The ad reads as information, which is exactly why it gets read.

**RUNS AT: NativePath, Mars Men, Happy Mammoth, Grüns, RYZE, Primal Viking**

**Happy Mammoth**32 days live

'10 SIGNS CORTISOL IS HIGH' - one villain named, ten doors opened at once. Whichever sign the reader owns, the ad has them.

**NativePath**62 days live

'WHAT I WISH I NOTICED SOONER' on torn notebook paper, three photos pinned with yellow sticky notes - Hands first / Nails changed / Skin looked thinner - and a footer that only whispers the mechanism: 'One protein may be part of the story after 50.' No product in frame. The scrapbook wrapper makes a sales argument feel like a friend's confession.

**Mars Men**93 days live

'MEN OVER 35 NOTICE THESE?' - the segment is in the headline, so the reader is qualified before the list starts.

**Primal Viking**81 days live

'IF YOUR FINGERNAILS COULD TALK' - Healthy Nail vs High Cortisol Nail, side by side, with the ridges and discoloration labeled. A diagnostic the reader can run on their own hand in the two seconds they are looking at it. No product anywhere in frame.

**Grüns**31 days live

'This is a rabbit. These are its poops.' - the checklist inverted into a single visual joke. You know exactly what it means before you read a word.

**FORMULA**

## **PROBLEM-AWARE**

They know the symptom. They see it in the mirror. What they do NOT have is a cause - and installing the cause is the whole job. This is the biggest and most valuable rung on the ladder, and where the best visual work in every library lives.

### **04** **The Raw Specimen**

**pain doorsvisuals**

**The belief it installs:** That is exactly what mine looks like. Somebody is finally showing the thing instead of tastefully implying it.

A clinical close-up of the actual affliction, full-bleed, no design, no type, no product. It stops the scroll on pure recognition and pure discomfort - and it instantly qualifies its reader, because only a sufferer keeps looking.

**RUNS AT: Veloma, Primal Viking, RYZE, Botanique**

**Veloma**118 days live

A full-frame photo of an inflamed groin rash. No headline, no logo, no product, no design of any kind - and it has been running 118 days. The copy underneath does all the naming: 'That itch in your groin? It's not just irritation. It's fungal. And it's spreading.' Textbook division of labor - the image shocks, the text diagnoses.

**FORMULA**

A tight, unretouched clinical photograph of {THE AFFLICTION AT ITS MOST RECOGNIZABLE}, full bleed, with the cause named entirely in the copy beneath.

### **08** **The Multi-Symptom Chant**

**pain doorsmechanism**

**The belief it installs:** All of it. That is all of it on one page. And it is one thing causing all of it.

### **Law 4 - The same headline gets many images**

This is the one that changed how we read the whole data set. We first assumed that ads sharing a headline were Meta variant duplicates. They are not. Primal Viking runs **MELT AWAY MAN BOOBS** as 3D Pixar reindeer AND as flat-cartoon gorillas - different creature, different palette, different bullet count, different aspect ratio, same six words.

**52% of headline-and-brand combinations run against two or more different images** (676 of 1295), and 71 of those swap the rendering style outright.

**RYZE ran one headline against 19 different images across 6 rendering styles** - cartoon, photo-lifestyle, studio product, infographic, sculpture and claymation. That is not a brand repeating itself. That is a brand holding the message fixed and testing the visual world against it, which is precisely the experiment this training tells you to run. The evidence that it works is that they are still running it.

**What to do with it:** when a headline wins, do not retire it and write a new one. Re-shoot it. Half this market has already worked that out.

### **How to read every number in this section**

One caveat that genuinely limits these figures. **Format families are single-label, and real ads overlap.** Primal Viking's reindeer before/after is a Cartoon Mascot AND a Before-After Diptych; it got filed under one. So treat family counts as directional, not exact. The awareness, style and mechanism tags are far more reliable - those are closed questions with one right answer. Family is a judgement call forced into one box.

Duplicates were removed upstream by pixel hash and by perceptual similarity within each brand, so every row here is a visually distinct creative. We deliberately did NOT collapse ads that share a headline - as Law 4 shows, those are the image tests, and they are the most interesting thing in the set.

### **Law 6 - Headlines are short everywhere**

Median on-image headline length, in words: UNAWARE **5** · PROBLEM-AWARE **6** · SOLUTION-AWARE **6** · PRODUCT-AWARE **6** · MOST-AWARE **5**. It never moves off 5-6 regardless of awareness level. Whatever you think you need to say on the image, it is about six words.

### **Law 7 - The one that did NOT hold up**

We expected ads naming the cause in the text to run longer. They do not: **51 days median for cause-named (n=435) versus 52 days for cause-absent (n=562)**. One day apart on a \~50 day median is noise.

Cause-clarity is still the right rule - it is what makes an ad *comprehensible* to a cold viewer, and comprehension is the point. But it is not what keeps an ad alive, and we are not going to pretend the data says otherwise. What DOES track with longevity is mechanism visualization and format craft (Laws 1 and 5).

## **The reverse-engineering method**

### **The Reading comes before the Spec**

Anyone can clone a layout and ship a dead ad. What transfers between brands is not the picture - it is the psychological argument underneath it. So every teardown answers three questions in order, and only then moves to how it was built.

1.  **WHAT is being expressed?** Not the surface claim - the BELIEF installed, written as the prospect's own inner sentence. 'Melts belly fat' is the claim. 'My fat is a substance stuck IN me, not a verdict ON me, and substances can be removed' is the belief. The belief is the thing you can transplant.
2.  **HOW is it expressed?** The central metaphor and what it borrows power from - medical imagery, art history, childhood materials, courtroom before/after logic. Who the prospect is allowed to be in the image. What the eye sees first (the premise) and last (the conclusion). And critically: what does this WRAPPER permit that a photograph would not?
3.  **WHY this expression?** Which awareness level it serves, what job the image does that copy cannot, what the prospect would never let you SAY but will happily let you SHOW, and which objection dies without a word being spent on it.

Then, and only then, the spec: layout zones, the money shot, the text layer, the color code, the timeframe stamp.

### **Proof that it transfers**

We ran this exact process on Primal Viking's library, extracted 16 format families, and rebuilt all 16 for a completely different offer (Resilia - a gut and parasite softgel aimed at a different gender, a different symptom, a different price point). All 16 transferred. The ones that looked non-transferable at first were failures of abstraction on our end, not failures of the format.

The rule that fixed it: **abstract at the JOB level, never the surface level.** Primal Viking's sexual-payoff ads are not a 'sexual' family - they are the DREAM-STATE PAYOFF family. They went sexual because a testosterone market's fantasy is virility. A gut market's fantasy is ordering the pasta without fear. Same family, same slots, different fill.

## **The Testing System - what to test, and how**

A video ad bundles a dozen decisions into one asset. When it fails you learn nothing about which decision failed, and you wait days for the next attempt. A static isolates ONE variable, costs an afternoon, and answers within a week. That gap in feedback speed is the whole argument for getting good at statics - not because images beat video, but because images are how you find out what the video should say before you pay anyone to produce it.

### **What makes all six work: their words, not yours**

Every axis above is only as good as the language you fill it with. The brands winning here do not write *digestive irregularity*. They write **stuck poop**. Not *gynecomastia* - **man boobs**. Not *facial oedema* - **alcohol face**. Not *incomplete evacuation* - **skid marks**.

That is what lets an ad reach millions and still feel written for one person. The product is mass-market. The words are stolen from the market's own mouth - mirror language, bathroom language, locker-room language. The phrases they use with themselves and would never say to a doctor.

**Where to get it:** reviews, Reddit threads, your own support inbox, the comments under a competitor's ad. You are not looking for what they want. You are looking for how they SAY it. Then you put that exact phrase on an image and find out whether it was the right one - which costs you an afternoon instead of a production week.

### **How to actually run it**

1.  **Fix everything but one variable.** Same offer, same layout skeleton, same product shot. Rotate only the door, or only the villain, or only the image world. If two things move, you have learned nothing.
2.  **Ship in batches of one axis.** Twenty Symptom-Question Posters, one per door, all in the same format. Not twenty different formats - that is a format test wearing a door test's clothes.
3.  **Judge on survival, not opinion.** Let them run. Anything past 30 days told you something true. Mars Men runs **Lose Fat, Not Definition** / **Not Strength** / **Not Shape** as three ads one word apart, and lets the calendar pick.
4.  **Breed the winners across axes.** Take the winning door and run it through the other five - new villain, new segment, new visual world. One insight becomes twelve ads.
5.  **Only then build the video.** You hand your video team a proven door, a proven villain, a proven payoff, a proven voice and a proven visual world. You are no longer guessing what the ad should say. You are deciding how to dramatize what already tested.

**An animated ad is a chain of statics. Test the frames first. Animate only what already won.**

## **How to actually build one**

**Everything in this deck can be made by one person in an afternoon. Here is the production doctrine, learned the expensive way on a 70-static build.**

### **Step 1 - Pick the family from the JOB, not the mood**

Decide the awareness rung first, then let Law 2 pick your style. Problem-aware and you need authority? Anatomy render. Problem-aware and the truth is humiliating? Clay, cartoon, or a photoreal prop gag. Unaware and you need camouflage? Native photo or a notes screenshot. Most-aware? Text on a colour field. You are not choosing a look, you are choosing a permission level.

### **Step 2 - Write the six words first**

Law 5 says the median headline is 5-6 words at every rung. Write that line before you touch an image, and make the verb in it something a camera could actually film. If your headline verb is DRAINS, the picture has to show draining. Headline and image are the same event or you have a caption sitting on a stock photo.

### **Step 3 - Know what the generator is good at, and what it will always botch**

This is the part that costs people days. From a 70-image build at roughly a 76% first-pass rate, every persistent failure fell into the same classes:

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| \*\*The generator handles\*\* | \*\*The generator reliably fails\*\* |
| Whole scenes and materials. Short display text, quoted with exact spelling, under about six words. Type framed as a physical object ("puffy sculpted clay letters reading ..."). Digits. | Long clinical words in small or serif type. Analog clock hands. Exact counts and grids (a 14-square calendar, five bottles). Strikethroughs and set operations - one item always escapes. Nested quotation marks. Progressive size change across panels in one frame. |

**Route by class before you generate.** Display numbers as digits, never as words - 14, not FOURTEEN. Analog clocks become digital. Grids, lists, strikethroughs and small-serif banners: generate a blank base plate and draw the precision layer in post with real fonts. Progressive sequences: generate each panel separately and assemble.

### **Step 4 - Three laws that will save you a regeneration cycle**

1.  **Colour changes only at line boundaries.** Ask for one word inside a line in red and you get PARA in dark and SITES in red. Put the pop word on its own stacked line and specify each line as entirely one colour.
2.  **On-image text primes the objects near it.** Ask for the words "DOWN TO THE EGGS" and your softgels will render as literal gold eggs. When your copy names a noun the picture must not become, over-specify that object and QC it specifically.
3.  **Text near multiple identical products duplicates onto them.** In multi-product shots, state that the text floats printed on nothing and the packs are unlabeled.

### **Step 5 - The prompt skeleton**

A {MATERIAL WORLD} advertisement scene. {PRODUCT, with a reference image

attached for label fidelity}. The product {CAUSE-VERB}s {SUBSTANCE: colour,

viscosity} out of {BODY PART or character} via {APPARATUS}.

Substance is {AMBER/MUSTARD}, thick, glossy.

Expression: {defeated / relieved}. Background: {neutral surface}, soft light.

Text at top in {material} letters: "{HEADLINE, EXACT SPELLING}"

  - each line is one single colour.

Text at bottom: "{TIMEFRAME LINE}".

No other text anywhere. Aspect {4:5 / 1:1 / 9:16}.

Product labels come from a reference image, never from description. One verb, one substance, one apparatus per image. Close every prompt with the no-other-text line.

### **Step 6 - QC before spend**

Read every render as a cold viewer with the sound off: who is this for, what is the problem, what causes it. Then check spelling on every word, count anything countable, and confirm the product label is right. A static with a typo in it is a static that will run for months with a typo in it.

### **The honest cost picture**

Roughly three in four images come out usable first try when you route by class up front. The other quarter are fixes, not disasters - and they are almost always one of the failure classes above, which means they were predictable before you spent the credit.

**An animated ad is a chain of statics. So test the frames first, and only animate what already won.**

Every static isolates ONE variable at image cost. That is the entire argument for doing this before you spend on video: a video ad bundles a dozen decisions together, so when it fails you learn nothing about which decision failed. A static tests one thing, and the answer is unambiguous.

## **Winner detection - reading spend like a P\&L**

  - **Days running is the honest metric.** Meta kills what does not convert. Anything alive past 30 days is spending money that someone has decided is worth spending. In this sweep, 1,927 of 3,939 statics cleared that bar.
  - **Variant count reveals scaling.** When the same creative appears in 6, 9, or 14 separate ad units, the brand is deliberately pushing budget behind it. Mars Men's 'Symptoms of High Cortisol' runs in 14 - that is a winner they are pressing.
  - **Format repetition across unrelated brands is market-level validation.** The apology letter runs at five brands in five markets. The MISSING poster runs at two. When a format crosses markets it is not a gimmick, it is a formula.
  - **Read the shape of the library before you read the ads.** A brand weighted to the top of the funnel is buying new customers and is worth studying for message. A brand weighted to offers and discounts is milking a list and will teach you nothing about angles. Check the mix first so you do not waste a week swiping the wrong library.

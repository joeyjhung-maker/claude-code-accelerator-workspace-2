# Swipe: Dan Henry — "Pizza Delivery to $40M in Webinar Sales" (rags-to-riches proof + named-script mechanism)

Related: [[_organizing-swipes]] [[hooks]]

**Seen running:** 2026-07-19, 2026-07-19, 2026-08-05 (three hook variants live in the same ~2.5-week window — still running as of the last capture)

**Mechanism:** Every variant opens on the same proof stat as a comment-style overlay — "My first webinar converted at 48% on a warm list... then it dropped like a rock to less than 1%" — before the headline even loads. That's the curiosity gap: a real, specific, embarrassing number with no explanation attached. Three different headlines then carry that same open into three different angles:
- Hook 1 (rags-to-riches): "GET THE EXACT SCRIPT THAT TOOK DAN HENRY FROM DELIVERING PIZZAS TO $40M IN WEBINAR SALES"
- Hook 2 (identity/rags-to-riches, first person): "I DELIVERED PIZZAS FOR 7 YEARS. ONE WEBINAR SCRIPT GOT ME OUT." — "Not a new offer. Not a new audience. Just a script built differently."
- Hook 3 (diagnosis): "IF YOUR WEBINAR ISN'T CONVERTING, THE SCRIPT IS THE PROBLEM." — "Most webinar scripts are built to teach. Teaching kills conversions."

All three route to the same free 2-hour workshop CTA.

**Why it works:** the comment-overlay proof stat does the heavy lifting for every variant — it's unfakeable-looking (oddly specific, self-deprecating) and creates the "wait, why did it drop?" question before any pitch starts. The three headlines are really testing WHICH door the reader wants to walk through to get that answer: the transformation story (pizza→$40M), the identity story (I did this, so can you), or the diagnosis (something specific in YOUR script is broken). Same mechanism, three entry points.

**The one thing to steal:** don't force one hook to do transformation + diagnosis + identity at once. Isolate the proof stat as its own unit (works as an overlay/comment, not baked into the headline), then run 2-3 headline variants that each pick ONE door into it. Cheap to test, keeps the core proof asset constant.

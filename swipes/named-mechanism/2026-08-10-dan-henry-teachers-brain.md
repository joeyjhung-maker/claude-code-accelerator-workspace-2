# Swipe: Dan Henry — "The Better Your Webinar Content, The Worse It Converts" (paradox hook + named mechanism)

Related: [[Promotion]] [[hooks]]

**Mechanism:** Paradox-first hook states a flat contradiction ("the better X, the worse Y") with no hedge — the reader has to ask "wait, why?" before any proof shows up. A few lines later, the cause gets a proprietary name ("the teacher's brain") that reframes the reader's own strength as the hidden trap. Structure: paradox hook → "I know that sounds backwards, hear me out" → real credibility stat → "here's what I discovered" → restated paradox with specifics → "so I started digging into why" → named-mechanism reveal → explain the mechanism → "here's the thing though, you can have both" (objection-defuse) → CTA bridge.

**Why it worked:** it doesn't sell the outcome, it sells the ARGUMENT — the reader's own competence (teaching well) is diagnosed as the cause of their problem, which lands as a personal insight, not an ad claim. The named mechanism makes the insight ownable and shareable in a way a generic claim can't be.

**The one thing to steal:** open on the naked contradiction, not the result. Prove the ratio/outcome AFTER the paradox lands, not before. Name the root-cause mechanism as if it's a diagnosis, not a marketing term — test it against "would a non-marketer feel personally called out?"

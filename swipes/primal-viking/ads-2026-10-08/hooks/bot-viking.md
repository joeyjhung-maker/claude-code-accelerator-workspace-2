

## STEP 1 — EMOTIONAL EXCAVATION

**CORE EMOTION:** The quiet humiliation of watching yourself soften, slow down, and surrender to "normal aging" while suspecting, somewhere deep, that men aren't supposed to fall apart this early. It's not fear of death. It's the shame of becoming irrelevant, physically useless, a man whose body quit on him decades before it should have.

**IDENTITY THREAT:** You're not a man anymore. You're a retired version of one. You're the guy who gets winded carrying groceries, who needs a nap by 2 p.m., who looks in the mirror and sees someone his father would have been embarrassed by. The Viking contrast doesn't just make you curious. It makes you ashamed, because a man 1,000 years ago with no gym, no protein powder, and no doctor was harder than you at 57 than you are at 45.

**SPECIFIC BELIEF:** "Men just break down after 40-50. That's biology. That's how it works." This ad detonates that belief by showing them men who were literally going to war in their late 50s, in conditions that would kill a modern man in a week.

**IMPOSSIBLE-TO-SCROLL-PAST CONTRADICTION:** A 57-year-old man rowing across the North Sea and swinging an axe in combat. In an era with no medicine, no supplements, no technology. Meanwhile you threw out your back reaching for the remote.

**MOST VICIOUS ANGLE:** The modern 50-year-old man is softer, weaker, and more broken than a man from 1,000 years ago who had every reason to be dead by 40. You didn't age. You decayed. And the reason is what you stopped eating.

**AD NAME:** "The 57-year-old axe man." That image, a 57-year-old Viking carrying a shield and axe into battle, must be in the first line of most hooks. It IS the ad.

The difference wasn't genetics. It was what they ate.

## STEP 3 — QUALITY GATE

**TEST 1 (Weakest Interpretation):** Every hook's weakest read still contains: an old Viking doing something physically impossible for a modern man, plus an open question about why. None reduce to "here's a health tip." ✅

**TEST 2 (Compression):** Hooks 6 and 8 were tightened. Hook 5 trimmed from three lines to two. All others wrung dry. ✅

**TEST 3 (Escalation):** "Going to war" escalated to "swinging an axe in hand-to-hand combat." "Cold weather" escalated to "minus-40." "An old Viking" escalated to "a 57-year-old Viking with a shield on his back and an axe in his hand." Each hook pushed to its most visceral, concrete version. ✅

**TEST 4 (Fragment Check):** All sentences have subjects and flow naturally. "They should." in Hook 2 is a complete sentence (subject + verb). "Nobody asks how." is complete. No neo-noir fragmentation. ✅

**TEST 5 (Viciousness Check):** Hooks 1, 4, 7, 9, and 12 all produce a genuine wince. The contrast between "axe in his hand" and "ibuprofen to get out of bed" is cruel. "You eat like a domesticated animal" is an identity attack. "You eat the cattle" reframes the reader's entire diet as the reason they're falling apart. ✅

---

## ⚡ TOP 3 MOST DANGEROUS

⚡ **Hook 1** — The ibuprofen line is a gut punch. Every man over 45 has had that morning. Placing it directly after the image of a 57-year-old rowing across the North Sea with a weapon creates a contrast so brutal the reader cannot pretend it's not about him. Most dangerous because it's impossible to read without feeling personally attacked.

⚡ **Hook 12** — "Vikings ate the reindeer. You eat the cattle." This reframes the reader's entire modern life as domesticated decay. It's not an insult, it's a mirror, and that's worse. The cattle/reindeer contrast is the entire ad philosophy compressed into two lines. Most dangerous because it makes the reader feel like livestock.

⚡ **Hook 4** — "You're 47 and your knees hurt on the stairs" is savagely specific. Nearly every man in this demo has felt exactly that this week. Placing it after a 57-year-old fighting hand to hand across an ocean makes it feel pathetic in a way that can't be argued with. Most dangerous because it names a private, daily humiliation and holds it next to something heroic.

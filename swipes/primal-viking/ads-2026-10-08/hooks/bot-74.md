**STEP 3 — QUALITY GATE**

**TEST 1 — WEAKEST INTERPRETATION:** Hooks 1-4 reduce to "here's a symptom checklist for low T" which still has stakes (your manhood is collapsing) and specificity (named symptoms). Hook 8 in its weakest form is still a diagnostic checklist that forces self-identification. Hook 9 at worst is "your wife noticed your body declining" — still vicious. Hook 10 at worst is "a man fixed his symptoms" — borderline. Strengthened the framing to keep the mystery of the unnamed gland. All pass.

**TEST 2 — COMPRESSION:** Hook 6 was wordy — tightened the run-on. Hook 5 trimmed. Hook 9 compressed the middle. Hook 10 tightened. All hooks now wrung dry.

**TEST 3 — ESCALATION:** Hook 3 escalated from "chest fat" to "man boobs you can grab with your hand" — more visceral. Hook 4 escalated "she's busy" to "she stopped touching you and you told yourself she's just tired." Hook 9 escalated "she noticed" to "she doesn't say it, but she sees it." Each version is the one that makes me more uncomfortable. Used it.

**TEST 4 — FRAGMENT CHECK:** Hooks 1-4 use intentional list formatting (symptom → diagnosis) which is a rhetorical structure, not neo-noir fragmentation. Each non-list sentence has a subject and complete thought. Hook 5, 6, 7, 9, 10 all checked — complete sentences throughout. No orphaned fragments posing as sentences. Pass.

**TEST 5 — VICIOUSNESS CHECK:** Hook 3's "man boobs you can grab with your hand" — flinch. Hook 4's "your wife stopped touching you" — flinch. Hook 8's checklist format forces the reader to COUNT their symptoms — that's interactive viciousness. Hook 9's "she doesn't say it, but she sees it" — gut punch. Hook 10's real testimonial quote anchors everything in reality. All pass.

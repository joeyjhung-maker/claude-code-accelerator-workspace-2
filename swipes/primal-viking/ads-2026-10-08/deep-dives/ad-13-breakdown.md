## Full script transcription

No voiceover. No speech. The ad is 100% text overlay on a looping gym clip plus a mood music bed. The text above is the whole script.

## 1. Hook (first 3 seconds)

- VISUAL: Man hammering battle ropes in a gym. Constant motion, effort, "trying hard" image.
- VERBAL: None.
- TEXT: "LOW TESTOSTERONE MEN:" plus the start of the bullet list, "- M*sturbate".
- Type: call-out plus curiosity gap. Text does all the heavy lifting. The visual is an irony prop: he's working hard, the text says he's still broken.

### Hook effectiveness score: 8.5/10

Why it scores high:
- Instant self-ID. Names the avatar in the first line.
- Taboo list items (masturbation, porn) make a thumb stop. Things men don't say out loud.
- Bundled symptoms make "that's me" hit several times in one glance.
- Works with sound off and with no read time lost, since the text is there at frame 0.

Why not 10:
- Wall of text on frame 0. No pacing, nothing to anticipate.
- The call-out is blunt and diagnostic ("LOW TESTOSTERONE MEN") so it can read as a label some men reject.
- Asterisk censoring cheapens it slightly and may read as spam.

## 2. Target avatar

Men who suspect something is off: anxious, unfocused, unmotivated, can't pull women, stuck in porn or masturbation habits, poor emotional control. Assumes the viewer already half-believes testosterone could be the cause, or is open to it.

## 3. Awareness stage

Problem-aware, leaning unaware-to-problem-aware. It reframes a spread of behaviour problems as one cause (low testosterone). It names no solution or product.

## 4. Angle and mechanism

- Angle: one hidden cause behind a cluster of male failures (low testosterone). Shame and insecurity entry.
- Mechanism: not named in the video. The curiosity gap is "if you want to know why". The mechanism is held back for the landing page. The primary text carries it: "The Aromatase Trap" (fat cells like a factory, food as a chemical war on biology).
- Reads as a two-part system: video opens the "why" loop, copy names the villain mechanism, quiz page closes it.

## 5. Script structure

Listicle of symptoms, then curiosity CTA. Hook (0:00 call-out) / body (symptom list, effectively simultaneous) / close ("Link in bio if you want to know why").

## 6. Proof and objection handling

None in the video. No testimonial, number, credential, or product. Objection handled implicitly: "why am I like this?" answered with "it's one thing". Market skepticism inferred: men are tired of generic advice (lift, cut carbs, generic zinc, all failing per the primary text).

## 7. Emotional angle

Shame and frustration first, curiosity second, hope of a single fix third. Carried by the text, not the visuals or delivery. The sad music bed supports shame.

## 8. Visual approach

Single stock-feel or native gym clip, no cuts. Lo-fi. Passes the "doesn't look like an ad" test as an organic motivational/fitness repost. No logo, no product, no brand element.

## 9. CTA

"Link in bio if you want to know why". Soft, curiosity-based, low friction (click, then a quiz). Destination is an emoji quiz page (primalviking.com/pages/emoji-quiz-reindeer-long). No urgency or scarcity. Note "link in bio" is an organic-style CTA; in a Meta ad the button does the real work.

## 10. Format

9:16, 360x640, 11.98s, sound-off viable (all info is text), Reels/Stories/Shorts-native.

## What makes it work

1. Organic-native disguise. Looks like a creator post, not an ad, so it dodges ad blindness.
2. Taboo-forward call-out list. Hits shame topics men won't engage with elsewhere, creating strong self-recognition and share/comment pull.
3. Single-cause reframe plus withheld "why". One cause for eight problems is a cheap, powerful curiosity gap that sends the click to a quiz that qualifies and builds the mechanism.
4. Cheap to make. One clip plus text; easy to produce dozens of variants (swap list items, swap clip).
5. Four months live suggests it's a stable or iterated winner (longevity signal, not a confirmed one).

## What could be improved

1. Frame 0 is a wall of text. Staggered or stacked reveals (4 to 5 words at a time) could hold attention longer, per Joey's own video text rule.
2. No sound design hook. The sad drone bed does nothing to stop a scroll; no VO.
3. No product or brand cue anywhere. Pure top-of-funnel. No bridge from "why" to reindeer organs, so all the lift rides on the quiz page.
4. "Link in bio" is the wrong CTA language for a paid placement.
5. Gym clip is generic and slightly mismatched to a list about sexual habits and social failure. A visual tied to the first bullet or to the "trap" mechanism would reinforce the hook.
6. Asterisk censoring may hurt trust or trigger review.
7. No hint of solution type; some viewers will drop at the click.

SHORTEN ONE LINE. Write 12 options, numbered, nothing else. No explanations.

THE LINE (the first line of the ad):
"A 52-year-old Viking rowing for days through frigid open water, hauling blood-crusted axes up jagged shoreline could take out men half his age and conquer nations."

THE LINE AFTER IT (do not rewrite, your line must lead straight into it):
"But today, that same 52-year-old man needs ibuprofen to get out of bed."

THE PROBLEM: the ad is now SUNG as a sea shanty. At 27 words with two long clauses stacked before the verb, the line is too wordy to sing. The singer has to cram it. It needs to be shorter and land on strong beats, without losing the pictures.

WHAT MUST SURVIVE (as much of it as the word count allows):
- "A 52-year-old Viking" opens the line. The age has to be there, the next line calls back to it.
- The ordeal: rowing for days through freezing open sea.
- The weapon picture: blood-crusted axes.
- The payoff: at 52 he could still beat men half his age and conquer nations.
- The charged words. Keep "blood-crusted", "half his age", "conquer". Do not swap them for softer words.

RULES:
- 12 to 18 words each. Count them.
- It can be one sentence, or two short lines a crowd could sing back.
- Put the verb early. No long run of clauses before we find out what he did.
- Plain hard words, one or two syllables where possible. Easy to sing, easy to hear the first time.
- Same voice as the ad: a man telling this at a bar, dry, a bit savage.
- No new facts, no named places, no dates, no numbers other than 52. No rhyme forced in.

MAKE THEM GENUINELY DIFFERENT:
- three that keep ALL four pictures (rowing, cold sea, axes, half his age/conquer) in the fewest words
- three that drop the shoreline and keep rowing + axes + the payoff
- two that keep only the sea and the payoff
- two written as two short sung lines
- two that start with what he could DO ("could still...") and put the ordeal second

WRITE OPEN-LOOP LINES FOR THE TOP OF AN AD. Write 12 options, numbered. Nothing else, no commentary.

THE AD OPENS (do not rewrite these lines):
"A 52-year-old Viking rowing for days through frigid open water, hauling blood-crusted axes up jagged shoreline could easily fight hand to hand against men half his age and conquer nations.
But today, that same 52-year-old modern man needs ibuprofen to get out of bed.
The difference wasn't genetics. It was what they ate.
[SLOT A]
And in the next 60 seconds, I'll show you what ancient men ate every single day that modern men never touch…
Why your energy, your drive, your ability to stay lean, and your confidence in the bedroom all quietly stopped at the same time…
And how to get them back without prescriptions, without injections, and without another useless supplement collecting dust on your counter.
[SLOT B]
To answer this, we have to look at the life that body had to survive."

THE JOB: an open loop. One short line that plants a question the viewer must keep watching to close: something surprising is coming later, and the best part is at the end. It makes him stay through the middle of a three-minute video. The kind of line a storyteller drops with a grin before he gets going: nobody saw the next part coming, and wait until you hear the last bit.

WHAT THE AD ACTUALLY PAYS OFF LATER (an open loop must point at something real in the ad, never at something that isn't there):
- It names four things the Vikings ate (liver, heart, kidney, testicle) plus a herb, and gives each one job. The LAST ones are the funniest: the kidney line ends with "half of Normandy had his jawline", and the herb was a wedding-night gift that kept him "swinging hard on the field and in the bedroom".
- The animal turns out to be a reindeer, not a cow.
- Near the end it says what a man notices first, then next, then "your wife notices before you say a word".
- So good loops tease: the animal nobody guesses, the last thing on the list, the one his wife will thank him for, what it explains about Normandy, what happens after the first few weeks.

RULES:
- 10 to 22 words each. One or two short sentences.
- Same voice as the ad: a man telling this at a bar, dry, a bit savage, a grin in it.
- Tease, never reveal. Do not say reindeer, organs, liver, kidney, or capsules.
- No "you won't believe", no "shocking", no "doctors hate". No numbers. No "cure" or "treat".
- Mark each option [A] or [B] for the slot it fits. Give six for each slot.
- Make them genuinely different: the animal, the last item, the wife, the bedroom, Normandy, the first few weeks, "it isn't what you think", "the last one is the reason".

WRITE ONE BRIDGE. Write 12 options, numbered, nothing else. No explanations.

THE GAP: an ad tells the story of a 57-year-old Viking and what each organ he ate did for him. Then it jumps straight to the product with no transition, and it feels abrupt. The last story line and the product line are:

"And Rhodiola, the herb they gave on wedding nights, made sure he could keep swinging hard on the field and in the bedroom.

[BRIDGE GOES HERE]

That's Primal Viking, freeze-dried reindeer liver, heart, and kidney, with beef testicle and Arctic herbs."

WHAT THE BRIDGE MUST DO: carry the reader from THEN (the Viking, a thousand years ago, eating the whole animal) to NOW (the reader can get the same thing). It turns the story into an offer in one move. After the bridge, "That's Primal Viking" should feel like the answer to a question the bridge just raised.

You may EITHER write a single bridge line to sit before "That's Primal Viking..." OR rewrite the opening of the product line so the bridge is built into it (then write the full line, keeping the ingredient list exactly: freeze-dried reindeer liver, heart, and kidney, with beef testicle and Arctic herbs).

RULES: 8 to 22 words for a bridge line. One sentence. Same voice as the ad: a man telling this at a bar, dry, a bit savage. Nobody is going hunting reindeer or eating raw kidney, a joke about that is welcome. No numbers except "two capsules". No "imagine", no "what if", no questions. Ellipsis or comma, never a dash.

Give a spread: the "you're not going to hunt a reindeer" angle, the "a thousand years later" angle, the "everything he ate, in two capsules" angle, the "someone finally put it back" angle, and a few funny ones.

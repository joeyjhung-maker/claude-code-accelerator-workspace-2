REWRITE ONE BEAT OF AN AD. Write 8 options, numbered 1 to 8, nothing else.

THE AD opens:
"A 57-year-old Viking was still rowing across the North Sea with a shield on his back and an axe in his hand.
A 57-year-old modern man needs ibuprofen to get out of bed.
The difference wasn't genetics. It was what they ate.
Picture the life that body had to survive."

THEN THIS LINE, which is the one to rewrite:
"Rowing for days through freezing open water, hauling iron weapons up a beach, then fighting hand to hand against men half his age."

THEN: "That kind of body doesn't run on chicken breast and multivitamins."

THE WEAK PART: "hauling iron weapons up a beach". It's a summary, not a picture. Nobody feels it. Maximise it. Make the reader FEEL what it takes for a 57-year-old body to get off a longship and up a beach to a fight: the cold water he jumps into, the weight of what he's carrying, wet sand and stones under him, soaked wool, salt, hands that have been on an oar for days, and then he has to run uphill and swing an axe at a man of 25 who wants him dead.

YOU MAY TAKE LIBERTIES. This is storytelling, not a history paper. It needs to be visceral and a bit funny, instantly understood, and nothing so wrong it jolts the reader out of the story. No named people, sagas, battles or dates. No numbers except his age (57) and the other man's age if you want it.

TONE: a man telling this at a bar. Savage, physical, dry humor. Concrete nouns and verbs, no adjectives doing the work. The reader should wince and half laugh.

FORMAT: each option replaces the one weak line with ONE to THREE sentences, 30 to 55 words total. Keep the rowing at the start and the fight with a younger man at the end, and make the beach the big middle. One sentence per line. No staccato fragment lists, every line carries a new picture. Ellipses or commas only, never a dash. It must still flow into "That kind of body doesn't run on chicken breast and multivitamins."

Make the 8 genuinely different: one on the weight he carries, one on the cold water, one on his hands after days on the oar, one on the run up the beach, one that contrasts with what a modern 57-year-old finds hard (stairs, a suitcase, getting off the sofa), one through the 25-year-old waiting at the top, one funniest, one most brutal.

## Gaps (where a spec ad can beat what they run)
1. **It is one ad, 27 times.** Sale banner, symptom checklist, doctor name, two testimonials, discount. No story, no timeline, no scene, no humour, no world-level hook. Anything with a different shape is new to this account.
2. **Their best mechanism is not in a single ad.** The archaea story (an ancient organism that slows your gut down on purpose so it can feed longer) lives only on the sales page. The ads stop at "fiber is a traffic jam." The flinch is sitting unused.
3. **Every ad opens on pain or on price.** Nothing opens on curiosity. They lead with the checklist everyone in this market leads with.
4. **Nobody is in the picture.** Bottle statics. No avatar the prospect can see himself or herself in.
5. **Video is thin.** 3 of 28 cards. One 43-second testimonial, one long doctor video, one 7-second clip.
6. **Segments they are not naming:** GLP-1 constipation, the laxative-weekend cycle, men (the funnel says "flat and sexy", the reviews and the FAQ say husbands, sons and dads), people who have been stuck for decades.

## Where I'd aim the spec ad
Three seeds, not copy. Pick one and it goes to a brief.

- **S1. The squatter.** World-level curiosity. The oldest kind of life on earth, the same organism found in boiling springs and salt lakes, lives in the gut and slows it down on purpose. Fiber feeds it. Uses the mechanism they already own and never run. Video, voice-over, product as hero.
- **S2. The laxative Saturday.** Story or timeline. The weekend planned around the bathroom: Friday dose, Saturday at home, exhausted by Sunday, and round again. Then Day 1, Day 3, Week 2 from the speeds buyers actually report. Fits the timeline-ad process.
- **S3. The shot nobody warned you about.** Segment play. GLP-1 users who got the weight loss and lost the bathroom. Fresh audience, one buyer already says it in the reviews.

My pick: **S1.** It is the biggest gap, it uses their own material so nothing is invented, and it gives a first frame nobody else in the account has.
